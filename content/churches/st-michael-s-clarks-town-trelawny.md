# St. Michael's, Clarks Town
**Trelawny** · Church · active

St. Michael’s Anglican Church serves the town of Clarks Town, a key farming and market center in central Trelawny. It was founded in the late 1800s for the communities formed by freed families after Emancipation in 1838. Despite facing hurricanes and other changes over the years, the church has remained a constant and active presence. Today, it continues to be a strong place of worship and a source of community support in Clarks Town.

## History
St. Michael’s Anglican Church was established in the late 19th century to serve the growing population of Clarks Town. After Emancipation in 1838, many formerly enslaved families left coastal estates to establish new homes and farms in this inland area. In response, the Anglican Diocese sent clergy to provide religious guidance and community support. By the 1880s, a permanent stone church was completed and dedicated to St. Michael the Archangel, a figure symbolizing strength and justice. It quickly became a central institution for worship, education, and community gatherings, helping to stabilize the new free village.

## Architecture
St. Michael’s is a classic rural Anglican church, built from local limestone in a simple Gothic Revival style. It features a rectangular nave, a small chancel, and pointed-arch windows with colored glass. The steep roof, now covered with metal, was likely originally made of wooden shingles. Inside, the church has an open-timber roof, wooden pews, and a simple pulpit. The surrounding churchyard, with graves of the town's early families, contributes to the site's quiet and historic atmosphere.

## Clergy
St. Michael’s has always been part of a Trelawny Anglican circuit, sharing clergy with churches in Albert Town, Wait-A-Bit, and Warsop. Early priests traveled long distances to lead services, offer moral guidance, and support local education. The church was instrumental in providing basic literacy and religious instruction when formal schools were scarce. After Jamaica’s Independence in 1962, the congregation remained active through youth groups, the Mothers’ Union, and community events. Lay readers were essential in maintaining regular worship when ordained clergy were serving multiple congregations.

## Notable Facts
St. Michael’s has endured several major hurricanes, including Hurricane Charlie (1951) and Hurricane Gilbert (1988), which caused damage to the roof and windows. Each time, the congregation, with support from the Diocese, carried out the necessary repairs. Further restoration work in more recent years has helped preserve the building.
