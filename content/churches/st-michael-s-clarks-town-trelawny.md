# St. Michael's, Clarks Town
**Trelawny** · Church · active

St. Michael's Anglican Church — often recorded historically as "Swanswick" — serves Clark's Town, a market community in central Trelawny. A stone Anglican church was built here in 1838 on land given for a mission within the Swanswick Estate; five years later, in 1843, the estate's proprietor G.M. Clarke donated 30 acres of adjoining land to establish the free village that became the town. Clark's Town is named for the Rev. John Clark, the Baptist missionary, rather than for the estate owner. By 1891 the congregation — still listed in the Handbook of Jamaica under the name "Swanswick" — numbered 210 registered members.

## History
Trelawny was created as a parish in 1770 out of St. James. Its economy was built on slavery. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church. Emancipation came in 1834; full freedom followed on 1 August 1838 — the same year the Swanswick stone Anglican church was raised, in a landscape where freed people were about to begin settling independent homes on land beyond the cane-pieces.

In 1843, G.M. Clarke, owner of Swanswick Estate, donated thirty acres of land to establish a free village adjoining the mission church. The settlement that grew there was named Clarks Town — after the Baptist missionary Rev. John Clark rather than the estate-owning Clarkes, according to local tradition — and for many years remained hemmed in by surrounding sugar cane properties. The 1891 Handbook of Jamaica lists the church under its original parish name, Swanswick, with Rev. C. Melville serving 210 registered members, 43 baptisms, and 4 marriages. The town expanded only from the 1940s onwards, when the Government acquired the Hyde and Gibraltar properties for subdivision.

## Architecture
St. Michael's is a cut-stone Anglican church of 1838, in the simple Gothic Revival tradition of the era. The Diocese lists the church under its full heritage name "St. Michael's Church, Swanswick, Clarks Town, Trelawny."

## Clergy
Rev. C. Melville served at Swanswick (Clark's Town) in 1891 per the Handbook of Jamaica. The church is currently the St. Michael's Cure within the Trelawny Deanery of the Diocese's Montego Bay Region.

## Hurricane Melissa (October 2025)
Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on 28 October 2025. The Jamaica Gleaner, in its 16 November 2025 feature "The past in ruins," reported that the hurricane tore away large sections of the roof of St. Michael's Anglican Church at Clark's Town, causing severe structural damage — the exposed interior and torn roof were photographed for the feature. Trelawny was named by Diocesan officials as one of the worst-affected deaneries, alongside St. Elizabeth and Westmoreland. As of late 2025, the church required major roof reconstruction and structural assessment.

## Notable Facts
The stone church of 1838 and the free village of 1843 together form an unusually well-documented post-Emancipation pairing: the church preceded the free village, and the village grew outward from the mission. The naming of Clark's Town after the Baptist missionary Rev. John Clark — while the Anglican church on the site is itself named St. Michael's — captures the denominational complexity of the early free-village landscape of western Jamaica.
