# All Saints', Fellowship
**Portland** · Church · active

All Saints' Anglican Church serves the community of **Fellowship** in Portland parish, a small farming district in the southern interior of the parish in the hill country approaching the Blue Mountains. The dedication to **All Saints** — commemorating the full company of saints on **1 November** — is one of the traditional Anglican dedications.

## History
**English rule (1655) and the founding of Portland (1723).** Under English rule from **1655**, the future Portland coast remained largely Maroon territory in the Blue Mountains and John Crow Mountains. **Portland parish was created in 1723** by order of the then Governor, **Henry Bentinck, 1st Duke of Portland** (Governor 1722–1726), and named in his honour. The British built **Fort George** at Port Antonio in 1729 on the western flank of the harbour to defend the new settlement against Maroons and pirates.

**The First Maroon War (1728–1739) and Nanny.** The **Windward Maroons** under **Quao** and **Nanny** controlled the Blue Mountains and John Crow Mountains. **Nanny Town** was captured in 1734 by forces under Colonel Brooks. The **1739 treaty** with the Leeward Maroons (Cudjoe) and **1740 treaty** with the Windward Maroons granted **Nanny a 500-acre land grant** that forms the present site of **Moore Town** (also called New Nanny Town). **Nanny of the Maroons** became Jamaica's only female National Hero in October 1975, of Ashanti origin.

**Slavery and the planter church.** Sugar estates dominated the fertile coastal lowlands and river valleys; coffee thrived in the hills. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds; its vestry was composed of the planter class.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion** in the western parishes, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union** to "wipe non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Christ Church**, the parish church at Port Antonio, was built around **1840** (land indentured 1836; designed by **Annesley Voysey**). Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb** (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools across Jamaica.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall, and the banana revolution.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. In **1870**, **Captain Lorenzo Dow Baker** purchased bananas in Port Antonio market; the first shipment of **1,450 stems** reached Jersey City in **1871**, launching the international banana trade. Baker's **Boston Fruit Company** became the **United Fruit Company in 1899**, and Port Antonio grew into one of the busiest ports in the Caribbean.

**Foundation of All Saints'.** The Anglican Diocese established All Saints' as a mission chapel to serve the Fellowship interior community. The community name **"Fellowship"** reflects a Jamaican naming tradition of aspirational or devotional community names, particularly common in post-Emancipation settlements.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** The **1903 hurricane** devastated Portland's banana industry and damaged Christ Church. Tourism developed at Port Antonio from the late 19th century, with **Errol Flynn** owning an estate at Navy Island from the 1940s. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found All Saints' a settled rural mission.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Hurricane Melissa brought serious rain impacts to this part of Portland.

## Architecture
All Saints' is a small masonry chapel in the southern interior of Portland, in the high-rainfall hill country near the Blue Mountains. The building sits in a landscape of extraordinary rainfall and steep terrain, with the associated landslide and flooding risk that characterises Portland's interior valleys.

## Clergy
Fellowship has been served within the **Portland deanery** under shared clergy arrangements. The interior location means the church depends significantly on lay leaders for its regular pastoral life.

## Notable Facts
Community names like **Fellowship** are found throughout Jamaica's interior, particularly in communities founded by freed people after Emancipation who expressed their aspirations for their new communities in the names they chose. Portland's interior communities south of Port Antonio are in the approach country to the Blue Mountains wilderness.
