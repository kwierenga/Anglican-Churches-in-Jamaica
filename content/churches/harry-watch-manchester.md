# Harry Watch (Anglican Chapel of Ease)
**Manchester** · Chapel · active

The Anglican chapel of ease at Harry Watch serves a small community in the rural interior of Manchester parish. The chapel is administered as a chapel of ease of the St. Barnabas', Mile Gully cure, alongside St. Peter's Whitby. The cure rectory is at Mile Gully P.O., Manchester (telephone 965-4527); the cure was recently listed as **VACANT**.

## History
Harry Watch is one of the small upland farming districts of Manchester, in the rolling country around Mile Gully — a market town and ecclesiastical centre that has been an Anglican focal point of the parish since the early nineteenth century, the period in which the parish itself was created (Manchester, 13 December 1814). The Anglican Diocese established the Harry Watch chapel of ease as one of the small mission outposts of the St. Barnabas' Mile Gully cure, which serves the dispersed congregations of the Mile Gully hinterland.

The "chapel of ease" designation in the diocese listing distinguishes Harry Watch from a full mission station — a chapel of ease is a building "for the ease of parishioners who cannot conveniently reach the parish church," a term inherited from the established Church of England's parochial system. The Diocese has long included a small number of chapels recorded in the directory by community name rather than by a saint dedication; the dedication may be informal, lost to documentary record, or simply known locally.

The Mile Gully cure is also notable for its proximity to the famous abandoned St. George's Anglican Church — the "Duppy Church" — that stands roofless near the town and has become a national monument and well-known site of local folklore.

## Architecture
The Harry Watch chapel is a small rural building of the kind erected by the Diocese for chapel-of-ease use across rural Manchester. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal storm surge while remaining exposed to wind and rainfall during major storm passages.

## Clergy
Harry Watch is served from the St. Barnabas', Mile Gully cure rectory; pastoral oversight is shared across the cure's network of mission stations and chapels of ease. The cure has recently been listed as vacant.
