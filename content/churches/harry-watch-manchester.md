# Harry Watch (Anglican Chapel of Ease)
**Manchester** · Chapel · active

The Anglican chapel of ease at **Harry Watch** serves a small community in the rural interior of Manchester parish. The chapel is administered as a chapel of ease of the **St. Barnabas', Mile Gully** cure, alongside St. Peter's Whitby. The cure rectory is at Mile Gully P.O., Manchester (telephone 965-4527); the cure was recently listed as **VACANT**.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1814).** Manchester was created by **Act 55 George III. c. 23** of **1814** in response to a petition of 29 November 1814 by residents of **Mile Gully**, May Day, and Carpenter's Mountain. The Act came into effect on **13 December 1814**, taking the "hill country" of eastern St. Elizabeth and the western portions of Vere and Clarendon. The new parish was named after **William Montagu, 5th Duke of Manchester** (Governor 1808–1827); **Mandeville**, the capital, was named after his son, **Viscount Mandeville**.

**Slavery, the parish church, and the Sam Sharpe Rebellion (1831).** Manchester's hill-country economy was built on coffee, supplemented by sugar in lower zones, both worked by enslaved African labour. **St. Mark's Anglican Church, Mandeville** — the parish church — was completed and consecrated in **1820**; its organ loft was used as a jail during the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**. Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation, 1865, disestablishment.** Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Maidstone** in Manchester (1840) was among Jamaica's earliest Moravian-led free villages. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of the Harry Watch chapel of ease.** Harry Watch is one of the small upland farming districts of Manchester, in the rolling country around Mile Gully — a market town and ecclesiastical centre that has been an Anglican focal point of the parish since the early nineteenth century. The Anglican Diocese established the Harry Watch chapel of ease as one of the small mission outposts of the **St. Barnabas' Mile Gully cure**, which serves the dispersed congregations of the Mile Gully hinterland. The 1900 **Handbook of Jamaica** records "Mile Gully" as a cure under **Rev. E. Clarke**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

The "chapel of ease" designation in the diocese listing distinguishes Harry Watch from a full mission station — a chapel of ease is a building "for the ease of parishioners who cannot conveniently reach the parish church," a term inherited from the established Church of England's parochial system. The Diocese has long included a small number of chapels recorded in the directory by community name rather than by a saint dedication; the dedication may be informal, lost to documentary record, or simply known locally. The Mile Gully cure is also notable for its proximity to the famous abandoned **St. George's Anglican Church** — the "Duppy Church" — that stands roofless near the town and is a Jamaica National Heritage Trust site.

**Twentieth century: Kendal, bauxite, Independence.** The **Kendal train tragedy** of **1 September 1957** at Kendal Manchester claimed almost 200 lives — Jamaica's worst rail accident; Kendal village lies a short distance from the Mile Gully country. The **bauxite industry** transformed Manchester from 1957 onward. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found Harry Watch a settled chapel of ease.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of the Harry Watch chapel after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
The Harry Watch chapel is a small rural building of the kind erected by the Diocese for chapel-of-ease use across rural Manchester. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal storm surge while remaining exposed to wind and rainfall during major storm passages.

## Clergy
Harry Watch is served from the St. Barnabas', Mile Gully cure rectory; pastoral oversight is shared across the cure's network of mission stations and chapels of ease. The cure has recently been listed as vacant.
