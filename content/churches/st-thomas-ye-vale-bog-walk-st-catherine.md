# St. Thomas-ye-Vale, Bog Walk
**St. Catherine** · Church · active

St. Thomas-ye-Vale Anglican Church serves Bog Walk, a community in the Rio Cobre gorge on the main road between Spanish Town and the north coast of Jamaica. Confirmed at its Bog Walk Church Road location, the church is described by reviewers as a warm and welcoming congregation with a fantastic family feel. Bog Walk is a community defined by the spectacular Rio Cobre gorge that the main road passes through — one of the most dramatic landscapes on any Jamaican main road.

## History
**Spanish Villa de la Vega (1534) and English conquest (1655).** The Spanish founded **Villa de la Vega** in 1534 — Jamaica's capital from 1538 until **1872**. Penn and Venables's English expedition captured Jamaica in May **1655**.

**Founding parishes — including St. Thomas-in-the-Vale.** Under English rule from 1655, **St. Catherine** was one of Jamaica's original parishes. **St. Thomas-in-the-Vale** was established as a separate early parish covering the upland valley north of Spanish Town; the parish was merged into expanded St. Catherine under **Law 20 of 1867** (Governor Sir John Peter Grant's reduction from 22 to 14 parishes). Bog Walk sat in the old St. Thomas-in-the-Vale parish.

**Slavery and the planter church.** Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds. **The Diocese of Jamaica** was established by Letters Patent on **24 July 1824**; **Christopher Lipscomb** was consecrated first Bishop in 1824.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838), and the founding of St. Thomas-Ye-Vale (1839).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **St. Thomas-Ye-Vale Anglican Church was established in 1839** — the year following full Emancipation — to serve the Bog Walk community at the entrance to the Rio Cobre gorge, a natural bottleneck on the main road between the Spanish Town lowlands and the north coast. **Sligoville** in St. Catherine became Jamaica's **first free village** in 1838, founded with land provided by **Howe Peter Browne, 2nd Marquess of Sligo**, then Governor of Jamaica. Bishop Lipscomb (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall, 1907 earthquake.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. Capital moved from Spanish Town to **Kingston in 1872**. The church was destroyed by the **14 January 1907 Kingston earthquake** and restored in **1909**.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** In 2016 the church celebrated its **177th anniversary** by honouring community members in an event reported in the *Jamaica Gleaner*. The "**Ye Vale**" element in the name preserves an archaic English form of "the vale" — a memory of the Spanish-era valley name "Vale of St. Thomas in the East" that was rendered in pre-modern English as "St. Thomas ye Vale." Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Thomas-ye-Vale a settled town parish.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Bog Walk's position in the Rio Cobre gorge makes it one of the more flood-vulnerable inland communities on the main north-south road.

Bog Walk developed as a community at this gorge, with the church serving travellers and local residents for nearly two centuries.

## Clergy
Bog Walk has been served within the St. Catherine deanery. The church's 'great family history' noted by a reviewer suggests a congregation with deep multigenerational roots in the community.

## Notable Facts
The Rio Cobre gorge at Bog Walk is one of Jamaica's most dramatic inland landscapes, where the river has cut through the limestone hills to create a narrow gorge that the main road follows closely. Flooding in the gorge during hurricanes regularly closes the main road, isolating north St. Catherine and St. Ann from Spanish Town and Kingston. The church at Bog Walk has witnessed many such flood events across its history. The unusual 'Ye Vale' in the church's name preserves an archaic English form that gives St. Thomas-ye-Vale a distinctively antique identity among Jamaican Anglican church names.
