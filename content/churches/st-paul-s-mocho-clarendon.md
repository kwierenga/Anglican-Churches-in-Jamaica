# St. Paul's, Mocho
**Clarendon** · Church · active

St. Paul's Anglican Church serves Mocho, a remote community in the highland interior of Clarendon near the Manchester border. Mocho is known for being in some of the most isolated hill country in Jamaica's central interior — the name itself has become synonymous in Jamaican idiom with extreme remoteness, 'Mocho' being used colloquially to mean a faraway or backwoods place.

## History
Mocho sits in the high interior of Clarendon, in the hills approaching the central ridge of Jamaica near the Manchester and St. Elizabeth boundaries. The Anglican Diocese established St. Paul's to serve this remote community. The church has maintained an Anglican presence in one of the most isolated rural communities in the central parishes.

## Architecture
St. Paul's is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural Clarendon in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. Set in the rural interior of Clarendon. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
Mocho has been served within the Clarendon deanery under shared clergy arrangements. The remote location means the church depends substantially on lay leaders for its regular pastoral life.

## Notable Facts
Mocho's cultural significance in Jamaican idiom far exceeds its geographical size. 'Out a Mocho' — meaning extremely remote or faraway — is a widely understood Jamaican expression, making Mocho one of the most colloquially famous place names on the island despite its small size and isolation. The Anglican church's presence here represents the Diocese's commitment to maintaining worship even in the island's most remote communities.
