# St. Stephen's, Thornton
**St. Thomas** · Church · active

St. Stephen's Anglican Church serves Thornton, a community in the northern interior of St. Thomas parish, in the hill country approaching the Blue Mountains. Thornton is a small farming community at higher elevation, and the church has provided Anglican ministry to the local community for several generations. The dedication to St. Stephen the Deacon, the first Christian martyr, is found in several Jamaican Anglican churches across the island.

## History
Thornton sits in the northern interior of St. Thomas, in the elevated terrain on the approaches to the Blue Mountains. This part of St. Thomas is significantly cooler than the south-coast plain and has a distinct agricultural character — coffee growing has historically been important in the Blue Mountain foothills of St. Thomas. The Anglican Diocese established St. Stephen's to serve the Thornton community, providing a ministry presence in the higher-elevation communities of the parish interior.

## Architecture
St. Stephen's is a masonry chapel in the elevated interior of St. Thomas, near the Blue Mountain foothills. The building's higher elevation creates greater wind exposure during hurricane passages and places it in a zone of significant orographic rainfall as moisture-laden air rises against the Blue Mountain massif.

## Clergy
Thornton has been served within the St. Thomas deanery under shared clergy arrangements. The interior location means the church depends on lay leaders for its regular pastoral life between clerical visits.

## Notable Facts
The northern interior of St. Thomas, on the Blue Mountain flanks, is one of the less-visited parts of the parish but has a distinct character shaped by coffee cultivation and the cooler highland climate. The Blue Mountains of St. Thomas form part of the same massif that produces Jamaica's famous Blue Mountain coffee. Hurricane Ivan (2004) caused landslide damage across the Blue Mountain foothills. Hurricane Melissa (October 2025) brought heavy rain and wind to the interior of St. Thomas; the Thornton community was in the affected zone.
