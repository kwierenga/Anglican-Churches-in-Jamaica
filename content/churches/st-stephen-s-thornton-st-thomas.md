# St. Stephen's, Thornton
**St. Thomas** · Church · active

St. Stephen's Anglican Church serves **Thornton**, a small farming community in the northern interior of St. Thomas parish, in the hill country approaching the **Blue Mountains**. The dedication to **St. Stephen the Deacon**, the first Christian martyr (feast day **26 December**), is found in several Jamaican Anglican churches.

## History
**English rule (1655) and the founding parish.** Under English rule from **1655**, St. Thomas was one of Jamaica's original parishes — originally **"St. Thomas-in-the-East"** with the first rector appointed in **1666**.

**Sugar economy and slavery.** St. Thomas was one of Jamaica's great sugar parishes — but the northern interior near the Blue Mountains had a distinct agricultural character. **Coffee growing** has historically been important in the Blue Mountain foothills of St. Thomas. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union** to "wipe non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb** (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**The Morant Bay Rebellion (11 October 1865).** On **11 October 1865**, **Paul Bogle** — a small farmer and Native Baptist deacon of **Stony Gut** — led a protest march to the Morant Bay courthouse. Governor **Edward John Eyre's** brutal response: **439 executed**, **600+ flogged**, **1,000+ homes burned**. Bogle was hanged on **24 October 1865**; **George William Gordon** was hanged on **23 October 1865**. **Both were declared National Heroes in 1965**.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall.** Jamaica became a **Crown Colony in 1866**. Law 20 of 1867 absorbed **St. David** into St. Thomas. The Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

**Foundation of St. Stephen's.** Thornton sits in the northern interior of St. Thomas, in the elevated terrain on the approaches to the Blue Mountains. This part of St. Thomas is significantly cooler than the south-coast plain. The Anglican Diocese established St. Stephen's to serve the Thornton community, providing a ministry presence in the higher-elevation communities of the parish interior.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** Internal self-government came in **1944**. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** fulfilled the aspirations for which Bogle and Gordon died. **Hurricane Ivan (2004)** caused landslide damage across the Blue Mountain foothills.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Hurricane Melissa brought heavy rain and wind to the interior of St. Thomas; the Thornton community was in the affected zone.

## Architecture
St. Stephen's is a masonry chapel in the elevated interior of St. Thomas, near the Blue Mountain foothills. The building's higher elevation creates greater wind exposure during hurricane passages and places it in a zone of significant orographic rainfall as moisture-laden air rises against the Blue Mountain massif.

## Clergy
Thornton has been served within the **St. Thomas deanery** under shared clergy arrangements. The interior location means the church depends on lay leaders for its regular pastoral life between clerical visits.

## Notable Facts
The northern interior of St. Thomas, on the Blue Mountain flanks, is one of the less-visited parts of the parish but has a distinct character shaped by **coffee cultivation** and the cooler highland climate. The **Blue Mountains** of St. Thomas form part of the same massif that produces Jamaica's famous **Blue Mountain coffee**. The dedication to **St. Stephen** — proto-martyr of the Christian church, stoned to death in Jerusalem (Acts 7), feast day **26 December** — is shared with St. Stephen's at St. Margaret's Bay in Portland.
