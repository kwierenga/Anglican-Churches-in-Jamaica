# St. Thomas', Race Course
**Clarendon** · Church · active

St. Thomas' Anglican Church serves **Race Course**, a community in the southern coastal plain of Clarendon, between May Pen and the south coast. The church is administered as a mission of the **St. Peter's Alley / Lionel Town** cure, alongside St. John's Portland Cottage and St. Saviour's Milk River.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1664).** Clarendon parish was created in **1664**, nine years after the English seizure of Jamaica from Spain (**1655**), and named for **Sir Edward Hyde, 1st Earl of Clarendon** (1609–1674), Lord High Chancellor of England under Charles II. The original parish church was the **Church of the White Cross** at "The Cross" — Jamaica's third Anglican church. **St. Paul's Chapelton** was built as a chapel of ease in **1666** and became the parish church when the Cross Church was destroyed in **1815**.

**Vere parish and the southern plain (1673–1867).** **Vere** was carved out as a separate sugar-plain parish in **1673**, taking in the southern coastal plain — including Race Course. **St. Peter's Alley** (1671) was Vere's parish church and remains the third-oldest Anglican church in Jamaica. Vere was reabsorbed into Clarendon in **1867**, and **Law 20 of 1867** designated **May Pen** as chief town.

**Slavery, Sam Sharpe, and Emancipation.** The sugar estates of the Vere plain depended on the labour of thousands of enslaved Africans. One of Jamaica's earliest slave rebellions erupted at **Suttons Plantation in 1690**: some 500 enslaved people rose in revolt, and many of those who escaped joined the Maroon communities eventually led by **Cudjoe** into the Cockpit Country. During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**, a vigilante alliance that sought to "wipe the non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of St. Thomas' Race Course.** Race Course sits in the southern coastal plain of Clarendon, in the flat agricultural landscape between May Pen and the south coast. The name "Race Course" is a reminder that horse racing was a significant leisure activity in colonial Jamaica, and several communities developed around what were originally horse racing venues. St. Thomas' has served the Race Course community for several generations as a mission of the Lionel Town circuit. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**, and St. Thomas' Race Course belongs to that broader expansion phase. The Find a Grave project records 61 memorials in the churchyard.

**Twentieth century: bauxite, May Pen, Independence.** Chapelton lost its status as parish capital in **1938**, with administrative functions transferring to **May Pen**. **Alcoa** began bauxite operations in Clarendon in **1959**; the **Jamalco** alumina refinery at Halse Hall opened in **1972**. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Thomas' a settled mission of the Lionel Town circuit.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of St. Thomas' Race Course after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. Thomas' is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural Clarendon in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. The southern coastal-plain position creates exposure to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
St. Thomas' Race Course is served as a mission of the St. Peter's Alley / Lionel Town cure, alongside St. John's Portland Cottage and St. Saviour's Milk River. The cure rectory is at Lionel Town, Clarendon.

## Notable Facts
Horse racing was one of the most popular leisure activities in colonial Jamaica, and several communities across the island bear "Race Course" names — reminders of the turf culture that was central to the social life of the planter class. **St. Thomas' feast day** (**21 December**, the day of "Doubting Thomas") falls at the winter solstice — the shortest day of the year in the northern hemisphere — an ancient astronomical significance that gives Thomas churches a particular character in the liturgical calendar.
