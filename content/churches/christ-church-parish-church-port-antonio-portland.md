# Christ Church (Parish Church), Port Antonio
**Portland** · Parish Church · active

Christ Church is the Anglican parish church of Portland, standing on elevated ground on Harbour Street in **Port Antonio** with a commanding view over the town's famous twin harbours. Its land was indentured on **3 September 1836** by **John Dean** and **Jane Brown** to the Bishop of Jamaica and the Rector of Portland, and the church was completed around **1840** — placing its foundation at the hinge of Emancipation, when Jamaica was moving from the 1834 Abolition Act, through the 1834–1838 apprenticeship period, to full freedom on **1 August 1838**.

## History
**English rule (1655) and the founding of Portland (1723).** Under English rule from **1655**, the future Portland coast remained largely Maroon territory. **Portland parish was created in 1723** by order of the then Governor, **Henry Bentinck, 1st Duke of Portland** (Governor 1722–1726), and named in his honour. The British built **Fort George** at Port Antonio in 1729 on the western flank of the harbour to defend the new settlement against Maroons and pirates.

**The First Maroon War (1728–1739) and Nanny.** Portland's interior — the Blue Mountains and the John Crow range — is the homeland of the **Windward Maroons**. The British signed a treaty with them on **20 April 1740**, ending decades of war and granting **500 acres** to the followers of **Nanny**. The community established on that grant, **Moore Town** (or New Nanny Town), remains the principal Windward Maroon settlement. **Nanny became Jamaica's only female National Hero** in October 1975. Sugar estates dominated the fertile coastal lowlands and river valleys, while coffee thrived in the hills of the Blue Mountains and the John Crow range.

**Slavery and the planter church.** The enslaved population on these estates produced the wealth that made Port Antonio a commercial port. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds — its vestry composed of the planter class, its clergy funded by the colonial government.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), the apprenticeship period, and the building of Christ Church.** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union** to "wipe non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. On **3 September 1836** — during the apprenticeship period that followed the 1834 Emancipation Act — **John Dean** and **Jane Brown**, acting for the residents of the parish, indentured a parcel of land to Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb** and the Rector of Portland, **Rev. Griffiths**, for the erection of a church. The building was designed in the **neo-Romanesque** style by the English architect **Annesley Voysey**, who arrived in Jamaica in 1837 and died on **5 August 1839** before construction was complete. Christ Church was finished around **1840** and is one of the few Jamaican Anglican churches whose architect is known by name. Voysey is buried inside the church he designed.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall, and the banana revolution.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. After Emancipation, Portland's economy was transformed by the banana trade. In **1870**, **Captain Lorenzo Dow Baker** purchased bananas in Port Antonio market on a return voyage from Venezuela — the first successful shipment of **1,450 stems** reached Jersey City in **1871**, launching the international banana trade. Baker's **Boston Fruit Company**, founded with Andrew Preston, became the **United Fruit Company in 1899**, and Port Antonio grew into one of the busiest ports in the Caribbean. **Captain Baker donated the brass lectern that still stands in Christ Church**.

**1903 hurricane and Independence.** The **hurricane of 1903** severely damaged the building; it was restored around **1911**. Tourism developed at Port Antonio from the late 19th century, with **Errol Flynn** owning Navy Island from the 1940s. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found Christ Church at the centre of a parish whose identity was shaped by the banana trade, the Maroon heritage, and the north-coast beauty.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Hurricane Melissa struck Jamaica's north coast, and Christ Church's storm exposure on its harbour-front hilltop placed it within the impact zone for diocesan recovery.

## Architecture
Christ Church is predominantly a **brick** structure, with stone and masonry used for decorative purposes — the **neo-Romanesque** style designed by **Annesley Voysey** in 1837–39. The building is rectangular in plan with a tower of four storeys housing the belfry and clock. Voysey's burial and the **Baker lectern** are among the documented interior features; memorial tablets span from the 1790s through the 1960s and include Major William Henry Plant and the architect himself. The restoration around **1911**, after the 1903 hurricane, stabilised the fabric that survives today.

## Education
The Anglican mission to educate expanded across Portland after Emancipation, part of Bishop **Lipscomb's** wider diocesan effort to provide schools for the freed population. Church schools were established alongside chapels in the coastal settlements and interior valleys.

## Clergy
As the parish church of Portland, Christ Church has been the seat of the Portland deanery's senior clergy, providing oversight of the mission network from the banana-estate coast to the remote hill settlements of the John Crow range. The 1900 **Handbook of Jamaica** records the Portland cure with its full network of stations.

## Notable Facts
Christ Church is one of the few Jamaican Anglican churches whose architect is documented by name: **Annesley Voysey** designed it in neo-Romanesque style and is buried inside, having died in 1839 before construction was complete. The brass lectern was donated by **Captain Lorenzo Dow Baker**, the Massachusetts sea captain whose 1870 Port Antonio banana purchase launched the international banana trade and, through the Boston Fruit Company and its successor United Fruit, shaped the economy of twentieth-century Port Antonio. Portland is the home of Jamaica's only female National Hero, **Nanny of the Maroons**, whose Windward Maroon community at Moore Town traces its title to the **1740 treaty grant**. The 1903 hurricane severely damaged Christ Church; restoration around 1911 — and further work supported by the Jamaica National Heritage Trust and the Digicel Foundation — has preserved the fabric.

## Recent coverage

In April 2015 Christ Church marked its 175th anniversary; the [Jamaica Gleaner](http://past.jamaica-gleaner.com/article/news/20150704/christ-church-celebrates-175-years) reported that the 1840 hand-made-brick building, with its chiming clock and bell still ringing across Port Antonio, retained about 120 active members and remained "arguably the oldest place of worship and one of the most significant buildings in the parish."
