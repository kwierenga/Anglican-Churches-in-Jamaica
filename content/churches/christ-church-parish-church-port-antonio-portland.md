# Christ Church (Parish Church), Port Antonio
**Portland** · Parish Church · active

Christ Church is the Anglican parish church of Portland, standing on elevated ground on Harbour Street in Port Antonio with a commanding view over the town's famous twin harbours. Its land was indentured on 3 September 1836 by John Dean and Jane Brown to the Bishop of Jamaica and the Rector of Portland, and the church was completed around 1840 — placing its foundation at the hinge of Emancipation, when Jamaica was moving from the 1834 Abolition Act, through the 1834–1838 apprenticeship period, to full freedom on 1 August 1838.

## History
Portland was a plantation parish on Jamaica's north-eastern coast. Sugar estates dominated the fertile coastal lowlands and river valleys, while coffee thrived in the hills of the Blue Mountains and the John Crow range. The enslaved population on these estates produced the wealth that made Port Antonio a commercial port. The Anglican Church served as the established church of this system — its vestry composed of the planter class, its clergy funded by the colonial government.

On 3 September 1836 — during the apprenticeship period that followed the 1834 Emancipation Act — John Dean and Jane Brown, acting for the residents of the parish, indentured a parcel of land to Bishop Christopher Lipscomb and the Rector of Portland, Rev. Griffiths, for the erection of a church. The building was designed in the neo-Romanesque style by the English architect Annesley Voysey, who arrived in Jamaica in 1837 and died on 5 August 1839 before construction was complete. Christ Church was finished around 1840 and is one of the few Jamaican Anglican churches whose architect is known by name. Voysey is buried inside the church he designed.

After Emancipation, Portland's economy was transformed by the banana trade. In 1870, Captain Lorenzo Dow Baker purchased bananas in Port Antonio market on a return voyage from Venezuela — the first successful shipment reached Jersey City in 1871, launching the international banana trade. Baker's Boston Fruit Company, founded with Andrew Preston, became the United Fruit Company in 1899, and Port Antonio grew into one of the busiest ports in the Caribbean. Captain Baker donated the brass lectern that still stands in Christ Church. The hurricane of 1903 severely damaged the building; it was restored around 1911.

Portland's interior — the Blue Mountains and the John Crow range — is the homeland of the Windward Maroons. The British signed a treaty with them on 20 April 1740, ending decades of war and granting 500 acres to the followers of Nanny. The community established on that grant, Moore Town (or New Nanny Town), remains the principal Windward Maroon settlement. Nanny became Jamaica's only female National Hero in October 1975.

Jamaica's independence on 6 August 1962 found Christ Church at the centre of a parish whose identity was shaped by the banana trade, the Maroon heritage, and the north-coast beauty that drew Errol Flynn to Port Antonio in the 1940s.

## Architecture
Christ Church is predominantly a brick structure, with stone and masonry used for decorative purposes — the neo-Romanesque style designed by Annesley Voysey in 1837–39. The building is rectangular in plan with a tower of four storeys housing the belfry and clock. Voysey's burial and the Baker lectern are among the documented interior features; memorial tablets span from the 1790s through the 1960s and include Major William Henry Plant and the architect himself. The restoration around 1911, after the 1903 hurricane, stabilised the fabric that survives today.

## Education
The Anglican mission to educate expanded across Portland after Emancipation, part of Bishop Lipscomb's wider diocesan effort to provide schools for the freed population. Church schools were established alongside chapels in the coastal settlements and interior valleys.

## Clergy
As the parish church of Portland, Christ Church has been the seat of the Portland deanery's senior clergy, providing oversight of the mission network from the banana-estate coast to the remote hill settlements of the John Crow range.

## Notable Facts
Christ Church is one of the few Jamaican Anglican churches whose architect is documented by name: Annesley Voysey designed it in neo-Romanesque style and is buried inside, having died in 1839 before construction was complete. The brass lectern was donated by Captain Lorenzo Dow Baker, the Massachusetts sea captain whose 1870 Port Antonio banana purchase launched the international banana trade and, through the Boston Fruit Company and its successor United Fruit, shaped the economy of twentieth-century Port Antonio. Portland is the home of Jamaica's only female National Hero, Nanny of the Maroons, whose Windward Maroon community at Moore Town traces its title to the 1740 treaty grant. The 1903 hurricane severely damaged Christ Church; restoration around 1911 — and further work supported by the Jamaica National Heritage Trust and the Digicel Foundation — has preserved the fabric. Hurricane Melissa (28 October 2025) struck Jamaica's north coast, and Christ Church's storm exposure on its harbour-front hilltop placed it within the impact zone for diocesan recovery.
