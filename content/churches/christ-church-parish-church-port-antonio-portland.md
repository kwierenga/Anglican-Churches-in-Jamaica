# Christ Church (Parish Church), Port Antonio
**Portland** · Parish Church · active

Christ Church is the Anglican parish church of Portland, standing on elevated ground on Harbour Street in Port Antonio with a commanding view over the town's famous twin harbours. Dedicated on 3 September 1836 by the Bishop of Jamaica — two years before full Emancipation — it was built at one of the most transformative moments in Jamaican history: the transition from slavery to freedom. Visitors describe it as "a true gem" whose "culture and history takes you back to a time when everything was much slower and treasured."

## History
Portland was a major plantation parish on Jamaica's north-eastern coast. Sugar estates dominated the fertile coastal lowlands and river valleys, while coffee thrived in the hills of the Blue Mountains and the John Crow range. The enslaved population on these estates produced the wealth that built Port Antonio as a commercial port. The Anglican Church served as the established church of this system — its vestry composed of the planter class, its clergy funded by the colonial government.

Christ Church was dedicated on 3 September 1836, during the "apprenticeship" period that followed the 1834 Emancipation Act — a transitional system that effectively extended enslavement for four more years. Full freedom came on 1 August 1838, just two years after the church's dedication. The timing places Christ Church at the exact hinge point of Jamaican history: consecrated under slavery's shadow, it opened its doors to a congregation that would soon include the freed.

After Emancipation, Portland's economy was transformed by the banana trade. In the 1870s, Captain Lorenzo Dow Baker began exporting bananas from Port Antonio to Boston, creating an industry that brought considerable prosperity to the town. The United Fruit Company's operations made Port Antonio one of the busiest ports in the Caribbean by the early twentieth century. Christ Church served the growing community of merchants, banana traders, and families alongside the descendants of the freed population.

Portland's interior — the Blue Mountains and the John Crow range — was home to the Windward Maroons, led by National Hero Nanny of the Maroons, who won their freedom through the 1740 treaty with the British. Moore Town, the surviving Windward Maroon community, lies in Portland's interior.

Jamaica's independence on 6 August 1962 found Christ Church at the centre of a parish whose identity was shaped by the banana trade, the Maroon heritage, and the beauty of the north coast that would later draw Errol Flynn and the beginnings of Jamaica's tourism industry.

## Architecture
Christ Church is built of stone and wood on a hilltop site overlooking Port Antonio's East and West Harbours. The colonial heritage architecture, elevated position, and harbour views make it one of the most distinctive parish churches on Jamaica's north coast.

## Education
The Anglican mission to educate extended across Portland's communities after Emancipation. Church schools were established alongside chapels in the coastal settlements and interior valleys. Portland has 27 Anglican churches — the fifth-highest concentration of any parish.

## Clergy
As the parish church of Portland, Christ Church has been the seat of the Portland deanery's senior clergy, providing oversight of the mission network from the banana-estate coast to the remote hill settlements of the John Crow range.

## Notable Facts
The dedication date of 3 September 1836 is one of the few precisely documented Anglican church dedications in Jamaica. Portland is home to the Windward Maroon community at Moore Town, led by Nanny of the Maroons — the only female National Hero. The twin harbours of Port Antonio made the town famous: Errol Flynn settled here in the 1940s. Hurricane Melissa (October 2025) struck the Portland north coast, and Christ Church was in the heart of the impact zone.
