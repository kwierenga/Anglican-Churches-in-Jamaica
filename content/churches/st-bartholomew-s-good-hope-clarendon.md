# St. Bartholomew's, Good Hope
**Clarendon** · Church · inactive

St. Bartholomew's Anglican Church serves **Good Hope** in interior Clarendon, a mission of the **All Saints' Crofts Hill** cure circuit alongside St. Peter's Rhoden Hall and St. Michael's Arthur's Seat. St. Bartholomew's Good Hope is distinct from St. Bartholomew's Frankfield — there are two Bartholomew dedications in Clarendon, reflecting the parish's large size and the need for multiple cure circuits across the interior.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1664).** Clarendon parish was created in **1664**, nine years after the English seizure of Jamaica from Spain (**1655**), and named for **Sir Edward Hyde, 1st Earl of Clarendon** (1609–1674), Lord High Chancellor of England under Charles II. The original parish church was the **Church of the White Cross** at "The Cross" — Jamaica's third Anglican church. **St. Paul's Chapelton** was built as a chapel of ease in **1666** in the upland interior, and became the parish church when the Cross Church was destroyed in **1815**. **Vere** was carved out as a separate sugar-plain parish in **1671** and was reabsorbed into Clarendon in **1867**; **Law 20 of 1887** designated **May Pen** as chief town.

**Slavery, Sam Sharpe, and Emancipation.** Clarendon was one of Jamaica's great sugar parishes — the fertile Rio Minho plain held the great estates of the Suttons, Pennants, Ballards, and Dawkins, and the wealth of the parish was built on the labour of enslaved Africans. One of Jamaica's earliest slave rebellions erupted at **Suttons Plantation in 1690**: some 500 enslaved people escaped to form the Maroon communities eventually led by **Cudjoe** into the Cockpit Country. During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**, a vigilante alliance that sought to "wipe the non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of the Good Hope mission.** Good Hope sits in the interior of Clarendon, in the farming landscape of the parish — its name belongs to the wider Jamaican post-Emancipation tradition of giving aspirational names to free settlements (Good Hope, New Hope, Providence, Refuge, Mount Pleasant). The Anglican Diocese established St. Bartholomew's to serve this community alongside the Rhoden Hall and Arthur's Seat missions of the Crofts Hill cure. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

**Twentieth century: bauxite, May Pen, Independence.** Chapelton lost its status as parish capital in **1938**, with administrative functions transferring to **May Pen**. **Alcoa** began bauxite operations in Clarendon in **1959**; the **Jamalco** alumina refinery at Halse Hall opened in **1972**. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Bartholomew's a settled mission of the Crofts Hill cure.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of St. Bartholomew's Good Hope after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. Bartholomew's is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural Clarendon in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. Set in the rural interior of Clarendon. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
The cure has been listed as **VACANT** in recent diocesan records. **William Willis** (SM) assists the Crofts Hill / Good Hope circuit. The Crofts Hill rectory (telephone 966-7609) serves the Good Hope cure.

## Notable Facts
**St. Bartholomew the Apostle** is said to have been martyred by being flayed alive — making him the patron of tanners, leatherworkers, and butchers. Two churches bearing his name in the same Jamaican parish (Good Hope and Frankfield) are an unusual distinction. The feast of St. Bartholomew (**24 August**) falls during the heart of the Atlantic hurricane season.
