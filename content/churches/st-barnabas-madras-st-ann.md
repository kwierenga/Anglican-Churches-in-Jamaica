# St. Barnabas', Madras
**St. Ann** · Church · active

St. Barnabas Anglican Church serves the quiet farming district of **Madras** in central St. Ann. It was established in the late 1800s for the communities formed by freed families in the hills after Emancipation in 1838. Today, St. Barnabas continues to be a place of worship and a living link to the history of the Madras community.

## History
**Pre-English settlement: Sevilla la Nueva (1509–1534).** St. Ann has a deeper colonial past than almost any other Jamaican parish. **Sevilla la Nueva**, the first permanent European settlement in Jamaica and the colony's first Spanish capital, was founded in **1509** by Juan de Esquivel near present St. Ann's Bay and served as Spanish capital until **1534**. **Christopher Columbus** had been marooned at St. Ann's Bay for over a year (1503–1504).

**English rule (1655) and the planter church.** Under English rule from **1655**, St. Ann became a major **sugar and livestock** region — the coastal plain to sugar estates and the interior plateau to "**pens**" (livestock properties). The Madras region was previously dominated by sugar and livestock plantations where the population was enslaved. Until **1870**, the Church of England was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**Sam Sharpe Rebellion and the Colonial Church Union (1831–32).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, **Hamilton Brown** of Brown's Town led a Colonial Church Union mob that destroyed the Brown's Town Baptist chapel in 1832 — the **Colonial Church Union** sought to "wipe the non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth." **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation (1838) and free villages.** Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. The freed population established new villages in the **Madras, Linton Park, and Brittonville** areas. Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb** (1824–1843) had begun extending Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835. **Sturge Town** (1839, founded by the English Quaker abolitionist Joseph Sturge) and **Clarksonville** (1835, founded by Baptist missionary Rev. John Clark, named for the abolitionist Thomas Clarkson) were among Jamaica's earliest free villages — both in St. Ann (NLJ, *History of St. Ann*).

**Disestablishment (1870) and the founding of St. Barnabas'.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. Anglican clergy from Alexandria and Moneague began visiting the nascent settlements at Madras, holding services in homes and temporary spaces. By the **1880s**, a permanent stone chapel was completed and dedicated to **St. Barnabas**, the biblical figure known for encouragement (the apostles named him "son of encouragement"). This church became a central hub for baptisms, education, and the spiritual life of the new free communities.

**Twentieth century: bauxite, Independence.** Bauxite mining began in St. Ann around **1952** (Reynolds, Kaiser). **Hurricane Charlie (1951)** and **Hurricane Gilbert (1988)** damaged the roof and windows; each time the community, with support from the Diocese, carried out the necessary repairs. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Barnabas a settled rural mission.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of St. Barnabas Madras after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. Barnabas is a modest, sturdy church built from local limestone in the rural Gothic Revival style. It features a simple rectangular nave, a small chancel, and narrow pointed-arch windows. The steep roof, now covered with metal, was likely originally made of wooden shingles. Inside, the church has an open-timber roof, wooden pews, and a simple pulpit. The surrounding churchyard, with graves of generations of local families, contributes to the site's serene and historic atmosphere.

## Clergy
St. Barnabas has always been part of the **St. Ann Deanery**, sharing clergy with other missions in Alexandria, Brittonville, Moneague, and Claremont. Early priests travelled long distances to lead worship and support local schooling and family life. Lay readers were essential in maintaining regular services when ordained clergy were serving multiple congregations.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to **St. Barnabas** — the early-Christian apostle whom Acts 4:36 describes as "Son of Encouragement," missionary companion of Paul, and traditional founder of the Church of Cyprus — has a feast day on **11 June**. The "encouragement" theme of Barnabas's apostolic identity carried particular meaning for a community of freed families establishing themselves in the post-Emancipation hills.
