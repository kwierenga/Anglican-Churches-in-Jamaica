# St. Barnabas', Madras
**St. Ann** · Church · active

St. Barnabas Anglican Church serves the quiet farming district of Madras in central St. Ann. It was established in the late 1800s for the communities formed by freed families in the hills after Emancipation in 1838. Despite facing storms and other challenges over the years, the church has maintained its role in the district. Today, St. Barnabas continues to be a place of worship and a living link to the history of the Madras community.

## History
St. Barnabas Anglican Church was founded in the late 19th century, as freed people established new villages in the Madras, Linton Park, and Brittonville areas after Emancipation. The region was previously dominated by sugar and livestock plantations where the population was enslaved. Anglican clergy from Alexandria and Moneague began visiting these nascent settlements, holding services in homes and temporary spaces. By the 1880s, a permanent stone chapel was completed and dedicated to St. Barnabas, a biblical figure known for encouragement. This church became a central hub for baptisms, education, and the spiritual life of the new free communities.

## Architecture
St. Barnabas is a modest, sturdy church built from local limestone in the rural Gothic Revival style. It features a simple rectangular nave, a small chancel, and narrow pointed-arch windows. The steep roof, now covered with metal, was likely originally made of wooden shingles. Inside, the church has an open-timber roof, wooden pews, and a simple pulpit. The surrounding churchyard, with graves of generations of local families, contributes to the site's serene and historic atmosphere.

## Clergy
St. Barnabas has always been part of the St. Ann Deanery, sharing clergy with other missions in Alexandria, Brittonville, Moneague, and Claremont. Early priests traveled long distances to lead worship and support local schooling and family life. The church was pivotal in providing basic literacy and religious education, which were crucial in the post-Emancipation era. After Jamaica’s Independence in 1962, it continued its mission through the Mothers’ Union, youth programs, and community events. Lay readers were essential in maintaining regular services when ordained clergy were serving multiple congregations.

## Notable Facts
St. Barnabas has endured several major hurricanes, including Charlie (1951) and Gilbert (1988), which damaged the roof and windows. Each time, the community, with support from the Diocese, carried out the necessary repairs. Ongoing maintenance has preserved the building's structure and function. Today, St. Barnabas Anglican Church remains active, hosting weekly services, weddings, and funerals, standing as a testament to the enduring faith and resilience of the Madras community.
