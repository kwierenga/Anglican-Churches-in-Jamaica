# St. Matthias', Middleton
**St. Thomas** · Church · active

St. Matthias' Anglican Church serves Middleton, a community in St. Thomas parish.

## History
Middleton is one of the small farming districts of St. Thomas, in the parish whose colonial-era plantation landscape stretched from the coast at Morant Bay up into the Blue Mountain foothills and the slopes of John Crow Mountain. The Anglican Diocese established St. Matthias' as a mission chapel to serve this community as part of its rural network across St. Thomas, the parish that — with the 1865 Morant Bay Rebellion led by Paul Bogle — became one of the most consequential sites in Jamaica's nineteenth-century history. St. Thomas Parish Church itself was rebuilt as a brick structure in 1865 after the original eighteenth-century building at Church Corner had fallen into dilapidation; mission stations across the parish were extended in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries to serve the parish's small-farming and post-Emancipation communities.

The dedication to St. Matthias is a distinctive Anglican choice. Matthias was the apostle chosen by lot, as recorded in Acts 1, to replace Judas Iscariot among the twelve — an especially fitting patron for a small mission in a parish whose history includes the post-1865 reconstitution of community life. His feast day is 14 May (in the contemporary Anglican calendar) or 24 February (in older usage).

No substantive foundational date or detailed architectural history specifically for St. Matthias' Middleton has been identified in the public heritage sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. Matthias' is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for mission stations across the St. Thomas interior. The position creates exposure to wind and rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
St. Matthias' Middleton is served within the Diocese's Eastern Jamaica Region.
