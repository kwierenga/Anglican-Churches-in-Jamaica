# St. Matthias', Middleton
**St. Thomas** · Church · active

St. Matthias' Anglican Church serves **Middleton**, a small farming district in St. Thomas parish.

## History
**English rule (1655) and the founding parish.** Under English rule from **1655**, St. Thomas was one of Jamaica's original parishes — originally **"St. Thomas-in-the-East"** with the first rector appointed in **1666**.

**Sugar economy and slavery.** Middleton sits in the parish whose colonial-era plantation landscape stretched from the coast at Morant Bay up into the Blue Mountain foothills and the slopes of John Crow Mountain. Great estates — Stokes Hall, Golden Grove, Albion, Holland — dominated the landscape, worked by enslaved Africans throughout the colonial period. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union** to "wipe non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb** (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**The Morant Bay Rebellion (11 October 1865).** On **11 October 1865**, **Paul Bogle** — a small farmer and Native Baptist deacon of **Stony Gut** — led a protest march to the Morant Bay courthouse. Governor **Edward John Eyre's** brutal response: **439 executed**, **600+ flogged**, **1,000+ homes burned**. Bogle was hanged on **24 October 1865**; **George William Gordon** was hanged on **23 October 1865**. **Both were declared National Heroes in 1965**. St. Thomas Parish Church itself was rebuilt as a brick structure in **1865** after the original eighteenth-century building at Church Corner had fallen into dilapidation.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall, and the founding of St. Matthias'.** Jamaica became a **Crown Colony in 1866**. Law 20 of 1867 absorbed **St. David** into St. Thomas. The Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. The Anglican Diocese established St. Matthias' as a mission chapel to serve the Middleton community as part of this expansion. Mission stations across the parish were extended in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries to serve the parish's small-farming and post-Emancipation communities.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** Internal self-government came in **1944**. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** fulfilled the aspirations for which Bogle and Gordon died.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. **Hurricane Ivan (2004)** had earlier caused widespread damage across St. Thomas.

## Architecture
St. Matthias' is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for mission stations across the St. Thomas interior. The position creates exposure to wind and rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
St. Matthias' Middleton is served within the Diocese's **Eastern Jamaica Region**.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to **St. Matthias** is a distinctive Anglican choice. Matthias was the apostle chosen by lot, as recorded in **Acts 1**, to replace Judas Iscariot among the twelve — an especially fitting patron for a small mission in a parish whose history includes the post-1865 reconstitution of community life. His feast day is **14 May** (in the contemporary Anglican calendar) or **24 February** (in older usage).
