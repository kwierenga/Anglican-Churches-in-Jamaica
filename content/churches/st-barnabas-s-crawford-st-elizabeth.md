# St. Barnabas', Crawford
**St. Elizabeth** · Church · unknown

St. Barnabas' Anglican Church serves Crawford in St. Elizabeth, listed as a co-cure with St. John's Black River in the St. Elizabeth deanery. Crawford is a community in the interior of St. Elizabeth near the Black River area. The church extends Anglican ministry in this part of the parish alongside the main Black River cure, which also includes missions at St. Stephen's Arlington and St. Boniface Pondside.

## History
Crawford sits in the interior of St. Elizabeth near Black River. The Black River/Crawford cure is based at 88 High Street, Black River (tel: 965-2380-1) and is served by Carlton Tulloch with Wesley M. Wiggan (SM) and Sharon Hall-Nisbeth (AE) as assistants.

## Architecture
St. Barnabas' is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural St. Elizabeth in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. Set in the rural interior of St. Elizabeth. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
The Black River/Crawford cure is served by Carlton Tulloch from 88 High Street, Black River.

## Notable Facts
Crawford is a Scottish surname — common in Jamaica, reflecting the significant Scottish presence in colonial proprietorship. St. Barnabas' feast day (11 June) falls in the early rainy season in Jamaica, at the edge of hurricane season. Barnabas' dedication in a community named after a Scottish family suggests the church was established by or named after Scottish estate owners in this part of St. Elizabeth.
