# St. Barnabas, Kencot
**St. Andrew** · Mission · unknown

St. Barnabas' Anglican Church serves the Kencot community in St. Andrew, one of two Anglican churches in this western urban neighbourhood. Alongside St. Clement's also recorded in Kencot, St. Barnabas' reflects the Diocese's dual presence in this densely settled residential and commercial area on the western edge of the metropolitan zone. The dedication to Barnabas — the 'son of encouragement' — gives this urban community church a patron celebrated for building up and supporting others.

## History
Kencot is a neighbourhood in the western metropolitan area of Kingston/St. Andrew, and the two Anglican churches here — St. Clement's and St. Barnabas' — reflect the area's population density and the Diocese's commitment to multiple worship points in larger urban communities. St. Barnabas' serves its part of the Kencot community.

## Architecture
St. Barnabas is a masonry church of modest proportions, representative of Anglican rural church building in St. Andrew. The structure balances practicality with the dignity appropriate to a place of worship, featuring a straightforward nave with local stone or concrete block construction typical of Jamaica's interior church-building tradition. Set in the rural interior of St. Andrew. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
Kencot's Anglican churches have been served within the western St. Andrew deanery circuit under shared clergy arrangements.

## Notable Facts
The two Anglican churches in Kencot — St. Clement's and St. Barnabas' — are approximately 1.5 km apart, reflecting the urban density of the area. Barnabas' feast day (11 June) is one of the Apostles' days in the Anglican calendar, and his association with encouragement and generous practical support makes him a fitting patron for an urban community church.
