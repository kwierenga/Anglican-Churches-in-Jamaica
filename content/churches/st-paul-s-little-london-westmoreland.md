# St. Paul's, Little London
**Westmoreland** · Church · active

St. Paul's Anglican Church serves Little London, a town of nearly 10,000 people in western Westmoreland, midway between Negril and Savanna-la-Mar. By the 1891 Handbook of Jamaica, it was a substantial parish — 450 registered members, 152 baptisms, and 10 marriages under Rev. B. A. S. MacCalla, described as "a pronounced High Churchman" whose "teaching and ritual was of the true Catholic character." On 28 October 2025, Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall at New Hope, approximately 3.5 kilometres from St. Paul's — placing the church and its congregation essentially at ground zero of the most destructive hurricane in Jamaica's recorded history.

## History
Little London sits in the flat alluvial plains of western Westmoreland, one of Jamaica's most important sugar parishes — by 1722, at least 73 estates operated in the parish. The estates surrounding Little London were worked by enslaved Africans throughout the colonial period. The nearby Masemure estate (269 enslaved in 1811) is historically significant as the site where the western arm of Tacky's Revolt erupted on 25 May 1760, when enslaved Africans under the command of Apongo — an African military leader — rose in rebellion. One of his lieutenants, Simon, killed the managing attorney John Smith, signalling the start of an uprising in which approximately 1,200 self-liberated Africans attacked eight plantations in Westmoreland and two in Hanover. This was part of the most significant slave rebellion in the West Indies between the 1733 St. John uprising and the 1791 Haitian Revolution. Meylersfield estate, also nearby, held 506 enslaved people in 1823.

After Emancipation in 1834 and full freedom on 1 August 1838, freed families settled in Little London and along the coast. The Anglican Church established a presence as part of its post-emancipation expansion. The earliest documented burials at St. Paul's churchyard — the Hudson family — date to the 1880s, and by 1891 the church had 450 members under Rev. MacCalla, who was ordained deacon in 1889 and served as rector from 1890 to 1892 before moving to Holy Trinity, Montego Bay. By 1900, Rev. R. L. Reid served "St. Paul's and Negril" jointly.

The broader Westmoreland labour history shaped the communities St. Paul's served. The Frome sugar factory, consolidated by Tate and Lyle and opened in January 1939, became the site of the 1938 Frome labour riots — in which 14 died and hundreds were injured — a catalyst for Jamaica's trade union movement and the political parties that led the country to independence on 6 August 1962.

Hurricane Melissa made landfall at New Hope on 28 October 2025 as a Category 5 storm with 295 km/h sustained winds — the first Category 5 to make direct landfall on Jamaica in recorded history. Across Jamaica, approximately 150,000 structures were damaged, 24,000 destroyed, and total damage was estimated at US$8–15 billion. Little London, 3.5 km from landfall, was among the most severely impacted communities.

## Architecture
St. Paul's is a masonry church in the centre of Little London. The churchyard contains 147 documented burials spanning from the 1880s to the present.

## Education
St. Martin's Church of England School at Broughton, a neighbouring district within the Little London area, is the verified Anglican school connection. Little London High School was founded in 1970.

## Clergy
Rev. Opal Beharie (Supplementary Minister) is currently assigned to St. Paul's. The 1891 Handbook records Rev. B. A. S. MacCalla (450 members); the 1900 Handbook lists Rev. R. L. Reid serving "St. Paul's and Negril."

## Notable Facts
The dedication to St. Paul — the Apostle to the Gentiles — is consistent with the Anglican tradition of dedicating mission churches to apostolic figures. Paul's identity as one who crossed boundaries to build new communities mirrors the church's own founding purpose. His feast day is 29 June. St. Paul's proximity to the Masemure estate — where Apongo's 1760 rebellion erupted — and to the Frome factory — where the 1938 labour riots catalysed Jamaica's independence movement — places the church at two of the most significant sites of resistance in Jamaican history.
