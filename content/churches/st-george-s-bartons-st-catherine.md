# St. George's, Bartons
**St. Catherine** · Church · active

St. George's Anglican Church serves Bartons, a community in the interior of St. Catherine parish. Bartons is a small farming district in the hill country, and St. George's has provided Anglican ministry here for several generations. The dedication to St. George, patron of England and the most internationally celebrated of the soldier-saints, is found in several Jamaican Anglican churches.

## History
Bartons sits in the interior of St. Catherine, in the hill country north-west of the main road corridor. The Anglican Diocese established St. George's to serve this community as part of its mission network across the interior. The church has served the Bartons farming community through the transformations of the parish's agricultural economy.

## Architecture
St. George's is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural St. Catherine in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. Set in the rural interior of St. Catherine. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
Bartons has been served within the St. Catherine deanery under shared clergy arrangements.

## Notable Facts
St. George's Day (23 April) is celebrated as the feast of the patron saint of England, though George was a Palestinian soldier-martyr whose historical connection to England is entirely legendary. His presence in Jamaica through multiple Anglican church dedications reflects the transplanting of English cultural and religious traditions across the British colonial world.
