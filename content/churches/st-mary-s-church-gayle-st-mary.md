# St. Mary's Church, Gayle
**St. Mary** · Church · active

St. Mary’s Anglican Church at Gayle serves one of the larger communities in the interior of St. Mary parish, a market town set in the hill country south of Port Maria. Gayle is an important agricultural service centre for the interior of the parish, with a weekly market and a range of community institutions. The Anglican church has been part of Gayle’s community for well over a century, providing worship and pastoral care to the farming families of the surrounding district.

## History
Gayle developed as an interior market town for the hill country communities of central St. Mary, growing in importance as a service and trading point for the coffee and later banana-growing districts of the parish interior. The Anglican Diocese established St. Mary’s at Gayle as part of its mission network across the post-Emancipation interior communities of the parish. The church has served generations of Gayle’s market families and the surrounding farming population through the economic changes of the 19th and 20th centuries.

## Architecture
St. Mary’s Gayle is a masonry church in the hill country of interior St. Mary, set in the Gayle community. The building reflects the functional construction style of Anglican interior churches in Jamaica, suited to a hill-country community at moderate elevation. The terrain of interior St. Mary creates landslide and flooding risk during heavy rainfall, particularly on the steeper slopes above the market towns.

## Clergy
Gayle has been served within the St. Mary deanery, grouped with other interior and coastal congregations under shared clergy arrangements. As a market town church, it has maintained a more substantial regular congregation than smaller mission chapels, supporting a full round of worship, festivals, and community occasions.

## Notable Facts
Gayle’s interior position gives it some protection from direct coastal storm surge but exposes it to the heavy orographic rainfall that the Blue Mountain ridge system generates in interior St. Mary. The parish is among Jamaica’s most rainfall-intensive, and Gayle and the surrounding hill communities regularly experience road closures and landslide damage during major hurricane events. Hurricane Melissa (October 2025) brought serious rain and wind impacts to interior St. Mary; the Gayle community and congregation were affected.
