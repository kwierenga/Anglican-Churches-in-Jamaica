# Holy Trinity, Retreat
**St. Mary** · Church · active

Holy Trinity Anglican Church in Retreat, St. Mary overlooks the Rio Nuevo valley and stands as the parish's first Anglican church. Built around 1835 on former sugar estate land donated by planter Henry Rigg, it served as mother church to other Anglican congregations. During Hurricane Melissa (2025), the Rio Nuevo area suffered severe damage including blocked bridges and damaged homes, exposing the church's coastal-hill location to intense weather despite no specific structural reports.

## History
Holy Trinity Anglican at Retreat is the main Anglican church for the Rio Nuevo area, built on land Henry Rigg donated in 1828 and completed around 1835. This timing places its construction during Jamaica's transition from slavery to emancipation. The surrounding Rio Nuevo valley holds layered history as a major Taíno settlement site and location of the decisive 1650s Spanish-English battle that secured English control of Jamaica, creating a landscape shaped by Indigenous, colonial, and plantation eras.

## Architecture
Holy Trinity is a Georgian-style church constructed of brick, stone, concrete and wood, built directly on Rigg's former sugar estate. Early 20th-century photographs show a well-proportioned structure that served as the community's spiritual anchor. Although a early-2000s fire damaged the interior requiring concrete replastering, the exterior preserved its original Georgian character. The church forms part of a historic landscape including nearby Taino and battle sites, making it a cultural corridor element.

## Clergy
As St. Mary's first Anglican church, Holy Trinity evolved from plantocracy origins to broader community service. The intergenerational congregation of approximately 80 members maintains active programs including Sunday School, women's and youth groups. It serves as mother church to four other Anglican churches in St. Mary, connecting clergy across the region. Through generations, the church has accompanied the community through emancipation, independence, and into contemporary challenges.

## Notable Facts
Holy Trinity represents multiple historical layers: built near major Taino settlements and the decisive Rio Nuevo battle site, completed post-emancipation on plantation land. It survived a serious early-2000s fire that required interior reconstruction. During Hurricane Melissa (2025), the Rio Nuevo area experienced severe damage with blocked bridges and destroyed homes, though specific church damage wasn't documented. The church now symbolizes Jamaica's resilience through slavery, emancipation, independence, and modern climate challenges.
