# St. Thomas', Bath
**St. Thomas** · Church · active

St. Thomas' Anglican Church at Bath is one of the most historically significant Anglican churches in Jamaica, confirmed at its Bath location and described by reviewers as the reputed first place of worship in what became the Incorporated City of Bath. Bath is a spa town in the eastern interior of St. Thomas, famous across Jamaica for its mineral hot springs, and the church here is deeply connected to the town's long history as a place of healing and genteel resort culture. One reviewer records that during the 1865 Morant Bay Rebellion, one of the church's rectors was slain and the parish documents were placed in a bottle and buried in a vault behind the church.

## History
Bath was established as a spa town in the late 17th century when the mineral hot springs of the Rio Grande Valley were developed as a therapeutic resort. Bath Botanical Garden, founded in 1779, is one of the oldest botanical gardens in the western hemisphere. The Anglican church at Bath served the town's genteel population — planter families, visitors taking the waters, and the local community — across more than three centuries. The record of a rector slain during the 1865 Morant Bay Rebellion connects the church directly to one of the most traumatic events in Jamaican history; the burying of parish documents in a vault behind the church is a detail preserved in living community memory. The church's status as the 'first place of worship' in Bath speaks to its foundational role in the town's history.

## Architecture
Bath is an inland spa town, and the church sits in the interior of St. Thomas rather than on the coast. No detailed architectural survey is available, but the building's long history — including the account of parish documents buried in a vault behind the church during the 1865 Rebellion — confirms a substantial masonry structure that has served the community for well over two centuries.

## Clergy
Bath has been served within the St. Thomas deanery. The church's long history includes the extraordinary record of a rector killed during the 1865 Rebellion, a testament to the danger clergy faced during that period of colonial upheaval. The church has maintained an active congregation through the community of Bath, which remains a modest inland town despite the decline of the spa resort culture.

## Notable Facts
Bath's mineral hot springs are fed by geothermal activity associated with the Blue Mountains, and the waters were historically attributed with healing properties for skin conditions, gout, and rheumatism. Bath Botanical Garden, established in 1779, received plants from across the Caribbean and tropical world, including the breadfruit trees brought from Tahiti by Captain Bligh in 1793 — one of the most famous episodes in Jamaican botanical history. The rector killed in 1865 and the buried parish documents form one of the most vivid specific details in any St. Thomas Anglican church's history. Hurricane Ivan (2004) caused severe flooding in the Rio Grande Valley area.
