# St. Thomas', Bath
**St. Thomas** · Church · active

St. Thomas' Anglican Church at **Bath** is one of the most historically significant Anglican churches in Jamaica, described by reviewers as the reputed first place of worship in what became the Incorporated City of Bath. Bath is a spa town in the eastern interior of St. Thomas, famous across Jamaica for its **mineral hot springs**. One reviewer records that **during the 1865 Morant Bay Rebellion, one of the church's rectors was slain and the parish documents were placed in a bottle and buried in a vault behind the church**.

## History
**English rule (1655) and the founding parish.** Under English rule from **1655**, St. Thomas was one of Jamaica's original parishes — originally **"St. Thomas-in-the-East"** with the first rector appointed in **1666**.

**Bath as a spa town and Bath Botanical Garden (1779).** **Bath was established as a spa town** in the late 17th century when the mineral hot springs of the Rio Grande Valley were developed as a therapeutic resort. **Bath Botanical Garden, founded in 1779**, is one of the oldest botanical gardens in the Western Hemisphere. The garden received plants from across the Caribbean and tropical world, including the **breadfruit trees brought from Tahiti by Captain Bligh in 1793** — one of the most famous episodes in Jamaican botanical history. Bath's healing waters were attributed with therapeutic properties for skin conditions, gout, and rheumatism. The Anglican church at Bath served the town's genteel population — planter families, visitors taking the waters, and the local community — across more than three centuries.

**Sugar economy and slavery.** St. Thomas was one of Jamaica's great sugar parishes. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union** to "wipe non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb** (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**The Morant Bay Rebellion (11 October 1865) — and the rector slain at Bath.** On **11 October 1865**, **Paul Bogle** — a small farmer and Native Baptist deacon of **Stony Gut** — led a protest march to the Morant Bay courthouse. Governor **Edward John Eyre's** brutal response: **439 executed**, **600+ flogged**, **1,000+ homes burned**. Bogle was hanged on **24 October 1865**; **George William Gordon** was hanged on **23 October 1865**. **Both were declared National Heroes in 1965**. The record of a rector slain during the Rebellion connects this church directly to one of the most traumatic events in Jamaican history; the burying of parish documents in a vault behind the church is a detail preserved in living community memory. The church's status as the "first place of worship" in Bath speaks to its foundational role in the town's history.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall.** Jamaica became a **Crown Colony in 1866**. Law 20 of 1867 absorbed the **old parish of St. David** into St. Thomas. The Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** Internal self-government came in **1944**. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** fulfilled the aspirations for which Bogle and Gordon died. **Hurricane Ivan (2004)** caused severe flooding in the Rio Grande Valley area.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Hurricane Melissa brought heavy rain to the Bath area.

## Architecture
Bath is an inland spa town, and the church sits in the interior of St. Thomas rather than on the coast. No detailed architectural survey is available, but the building's long history — including the account of parish documents buried in a vault behind the church during the 1865 Rebellion — confirms a substantial masonry structure that has served the community for well over two centuries.

## Clergy
Bath has been served within the **St. Thomas deanery**. The church's long history includes the extraordinary record of **a rector killed during the 1865 Rebellion**, a testament to the danger clergy faced during that period of colonial upheaval. The church has maintained an active congregation through the community of Bath, which remains a modest inland town despite the decline of the spa resort culture.

## Notable Facts
**Bath's mineral hot springs** are fed by geothermal activity associated with the Blue Mountains, and the waters were historically attributed with healing properties for skin conditions, gout, and rheumatism. **Bath Botanical Garden**, established in **1779**, received plants from across the Caribbean and tropical world, including the **breadfruit trees brought from Tahiti by Captain Bligh in 1793**. The rector killed in 1865 and the buried parish documents form one of the most vivid specific details in any St. Thomas Anglican church's history. The dedication to **St. Thomas the Apostle** (feast day **21 December**; **3 July** in some traditions) — the apostle who doubted then proclaimed "My Lord and my God" — is the parish-name dedication.
