# St. Mark's, Hopewell
**St. James** · Church · active

St. Mark's Anglican Church serves Hopewell, a community in St. James parish. (Note: a separate St. Mark's at Hopewell exists in **Westmoreland** parish — these are two distinct Anglican churches in two different parishes whose communities share the common Jamaican estate name "Hopewell.")

## History
The St. James Hopewell is a small farming district whose name belongs to the wider Jamaican post-Emancipation tradition of giving aspirational names — Hopewell, New Hope, Providence, Refuge — to communities established by freed people after 1838. The Anglican Diocese established St. Mark's as a small mission chapel to serve this community as part of the broader rural church-planting expansion that the diocese carried out across St. James from the late nineteenth century onward.

The dedication to St. Mark the Evangelist — author of the second Gospel, traditionally associated with Alexandria, with a feast day on 25 April — is shared with the Manchester Parish Church (St. Mark's Mandeville, consecrated 1820) and other Jamaican Anglican churches.

No substantive foundational date or detailed architectural history specifically for St. Mark's Hopewell, St. James has been identified in the public heritage sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. Mark's is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for mission stations across the St. James interior. The position creates exposure to the major hurricanes that periodically strike Jamaica's western parishes — Gilbert (1988), Ivan (2004), and most recently Hurricane Melissa (October 2025), whose Category 5 landfall in Westmoreland brought damaging winds across St. James.

## Clergy
St. Mark's Hopewell is served within the Montego Bay Region's St. James Deanery network.
