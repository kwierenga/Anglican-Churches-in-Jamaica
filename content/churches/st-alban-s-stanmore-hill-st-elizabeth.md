# St. Alban's, Stanmore Hill
**St. Elizabeth** · Church · Active

St. Alban's Anglican Church at **Stanmore Hill** is one of the documented Anglican congregations of St. Elizabeth recorded in the 1891 *Handbook of Jamaica*, with **431 registered members** that year under the rectorship of Rev. **A. P. Kennedy** — who simultaneously served the larger Gilnock cure with 780 members. By 1900 Kennedy still served both Gilnock and St. Alban's as a single combined cure.

## History
**Founding of the parish (c.1664).** St. Elizabeth was one of the original Jamaican parishes, established under Sir Thomas Modyford and named in honour of **Lady Elizabeth Modyford**, wife of the first Governor of Jamaica (1664–71). Prior to **1703**, the parish stretched across the territory of present-day Westmoreland, Hanover, and Manchester; in 1703 Westmoreland was created from the western portion, and in **1814** the final subdivision created Manchester from the southern section. The original parish capital was **Lacovia** — courts were initially heard alternately at Lacovia and Black River, until **Black River** became the sole capital. The parish church, **St. John the Evangelist** at Black River, is recorded in some sources as founded in 1837 (though structural elements may date earlier).

**Slavery and the sugar economy.** Under English rule, sugar and slavery defined the parish economy: prominent estates included **Holland, Vineyard, and Fullerswood**. The parish also received in the seventeenth century a small group of Scottish settlers descended from the **Darien expedition** — a failed Scottish colonial venture in Panama whose survivors made their way to southern St. Elizabeth. Cattle-rearing on the **Pedro Plains** was a Spanish-era inheritance preserved into the British period. **Cudjoe**, leader of the Leeward Maroons, signed the **Peace Treaty of 1739** at the Peace Cave in St. Elizabeth's Cockpit Country, securing Maroon land rights — and the Maroon community of **Accompong Town** still exists in the parish today. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**The Sam Sharpe Rebellion (December 1831).** The Christmas Rebellion of 1831, led by the Baptist deacon **Samuel Sharpe** and ignited at the Kensington Estate in St. James on **27 December 1831**, spread through the western parishes; some 60,000 enslaved people were involved. Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation.** The British Parliament passed the Slavery Abolition Act in 1833; Emancipation came in 1834, and full freedom followed at the end of the apprenticeship period on **1 August 1838**. After 1838, freed people settled the upland districts of southern St. Elizabeth as small farmers — Stanmore Hill, like Mount Hermon, Mayfield, and other interior settlements, belongs to that wave of post-Emancipation free settlement.

**1865, 1870, and the 1891 Handbook record.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865, Crown Colony rule replaced the planter Assembly (1866); the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica. The 1891 Handbook of Jamaica records St. Alban's at Stanmore Hill with **431 registered members, 79 baptisms, and 9 marriages**, under **Rev. A. P. Kennedy** — who simultaneously served Gilnock (780 members), making the combined Gilnock–St. Alban's cure one of the larger pastoral charges in St. Elizabeth. By the 1900 Handbook, the cure was formally recorded as **"Gilnock & St. Alban's"** under Kennedy. Bishop Enos Nuttall's tenure (1880–1916) saw the Diocese grow from 26 rural mission stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

**Twentieth century: bauxite, modernisation, Independence.** The bauxite industry developed in St. Elizabeth from **1943**, with **ALPART** later headquartered at Nain. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Alban's a long-established mission. In **February 2026** the church received **J$1.3 million** from the wound-up Mavis Fraser-Davis Trust to support pupils at the affiliated St Alban's Primary School and to establish a scholarship honouring the late educator (Gleaner, 19 Feb 2026).

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. St. Elizabeth was severely affected, with major Anglican losses including St. Luke's Balaclava and St. Thomas' Lacovia. The specific status of St. Alban's Stanmore Hill after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. Alban's is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for mission stations across the St. Elizabeth uplands in the late nineteenth century. The Diocese of Jamaica's online directory image collection includes a photograph of the church (attachment_id 875). The interior position offers some shelter from coastal storm surge while remaining exposed to wind and rainfall during major storms.

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** **St. Alban** — the first recorded Christian martyr of Britain (3rd or 4th century) — was a Romano-British layperson at Verulamium (modern St Albans, Hertfordshire) who sheltered a fugitive Christian priest, converted, and was beheaded after surrendering himself in the priest's place. His feast day is **22 June**. The dedication is a quiet thread connecting this small Stanmore Hill congregation to the very origins of Christianity in the British Isles.

**The educational mission.** **St Alban's Primary School**, affiliated with the church, received a **J$1.3 million** legacy in February 2026 from the Mavis Fraser-Davis Trust to support pupils and to establish a scholarship honouring the late educator — a recent example of the Anglican educational tradition that the Diocese has carried since the post-Emancipation expansion under Bishops Lipscomb (over 50 schools by the 1830s) and Nuttall (the diocese ran 263 elementary schools island-wide by 1900).
