# St Alban's, Stanmore Hill
**St. Elizabeth** · Church · active

All Saints Anglican Church is an old church in the cool hills of Malvern, built in the 1800s as the area grew after Emancipation in 1838. It became a center of worship for families who settled in the mountains and for students at Munro College and Hampton School. The church has survived strong hurricanes, repairs, and many changes from the time of slavery through Jamaica’s Independence in 1962. Today, it remains an important part of the Malvern community and continues to serve both residents and students.

## History
All Saints Anglican Church began in the early 19th century, when the Anglican Diocese expanded into the Santa Cruz Mountains. Before Emancipation in 1838, the area around Malvern was sparsely settled, with most people living and working on surrounding estates. After slavery ended, freed families moved into the hills to build farms and communities of their own. The growing population led to the founding of schools such as Munro College (1856) and Hampton School (1858), both Anglican institutions. A permanent stone church was completed in the 1860s and became the parish center for worship, education, and community gatherings.

## Architecture
All Saints is a strong limestone church built in the Victorian Gothic style. It has a tall west tower with a belfry, a long rectangular nave (where the congregation sits), and a small chancel at the eastern end. The steep roof, once covered with wooden shingles and now with metal, fits well with the rainy mountain climate. Inside, the church has an open wooden roof, smooth stone floors, and wooden pews. Large pointed windows with colored glass brighten the space, and the churchyard holds many old graves of teachers, clergy, and residents from the 1800s onward.

## Clergy
The church has always been part of the Black River Deanery and was served by many well-known Anglican priests, some of whom also worked in the nearby schools. Clergy like Canon W. R. Campbell played important roles in both education and worship. Students from Hampton and Munro attended services here, and school events were often held in the church. After Jamaica gained Independence in 1962, the church continued to support youth programs, choirs, and Mothers’ Union activities. Lay readers helped keep the church active during times when clergy were shared across the parish.

## Notable Facts
All Saints has survived many natural disasters. Hurricanes Charlie (1951) and Gilbert (1988) damaged the roof and tower, but repairs were made by parishioners and the Diocese. The church also came through Hurricane Melissa (2025) without any major reported damage. Today, it remains well maintained and continues to host school services, weddings, funerals, and community events. All Saints Anglican Church stands as a strong symbol of faith, history, and education in the Malvern hills, connecting Jamaica’s past with its present.
