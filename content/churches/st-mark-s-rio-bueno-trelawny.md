# St. Mark's, Rio Bueno
**Trelawny** · Church · active

St. Mark’s Anglican Church serves the historic seaside community of Rio Bueno, one of Jamaica's earliest harbors. The church was established in the 1800s, an era when the area was dominated by the plantation system and later transformed after Emancipation in 1838. Despite facing hurricanes and social changes, the congregation has persevered through repairs and rebuilding. Today, it remains a small but resilient part of the community's worship and historical landscape.

## History
Rio Bueno was a bustling harbor in the 18th and early 19th centuries, shipping sugar and rum from nearby plantations that relied on enslaved labor. During slavery, the church primarily served the white planter class and free people of color in the port town. After Emancipation in 1838, as freed families settled in the surrounding areas, the Anglican Diocese expanded its mission to serve them. A chapel dedicated to St. Mark was active by the 1860s, becoming a hub for both the maritime community and new residents. It quickly grew into a central place for worship, baptisms, and community gatherings in the evolving post-settlement period.

## Architecture
St. Mark’s is a quaint stone church built in the simple Gothic Revival style adapted for Jamaica's north coast. It features thick limestone walls, a small rectangular nave, and pointed-arch windows designed to catch the sea breeze. The roof, now clad in metal, was likely originally wooden shingles, with the change helping it withstand salt air and storms. Inside, the church is furnished with wooden pews, a simple altar, and an open-timber ceiling. The churchyard overlooks the historic harbor, contributing to the site's peaceful and timeless atmosphere.

## Clergy
St. Mark’s has always been part of a north coast Anglican circuit, sharing clergy with larger parishes in Discovery Bay, Duncans, and Falmouth. Early priests traveled along the coast to lead services and provide pastoral care to the fishing and farming families. The church played a key role in local education, offering basic literacy and religious instruction when schools were scarce. After Jamaica’s Independence in 1962, it continued its mission through the Mothers’ Union and community outreach. Lay readers were vital in maintaining regular services when ordained clergy were assigned to multiple congregations.

## Notable Facts
St. Mark’s coastal location has made it vulnerable to many hurricanes, including Charlie (1951) and Gilbert (1988), which required significant repairs to the roof and windows. Each time, the community and diocese collaborated on restoration. The harsh marine environment also demands ongoing maintenance, such as repainting and roof work. There are no reports of damage from Hurricane Melissa (2025). Today, St. Mark’s continues to hold services and community gatherings, serving as a quiet but enduring landmark that connects modern Rio Bueno to its deep historical roots.
