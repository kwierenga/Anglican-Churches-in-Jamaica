# St. Mark's, Rio Bueno
**Trelawny** · Church · active

St. Mark's Anglican Church is the pale-blue seaside church of Rio Bueno, a small historic port village on the Trelawny–St. Ann border. Founded in 1833 — the year of the Slavery Abolition Act and the year before Emancipation — it stands on one of the most contested pieces of shoreline in the early Jamaican historical record: Rio Bueno has a long-standing (and disputed) claim to be the site of Christopher Columbus's first Jamaican landing, and Rio Bueno Harbour is the deepest natural harbour in Jamaica. By 1891 the church had 293 registered members under Rev. C. R. Chandler, making it one of the larger congregations in the Trelawny deanery.

## History
Rio Bueno grew during the early English colonial period as a way-stop for mariners and overland travellers, with taverns, guesthouses, and inns lining the shore. Columbus's landing in Jamaica is sometimes placed at Rio Bueno (though Discovery Bay, a few kilometres east in St. Ann, also makes the claim). In the plantation era it served as a shipping point for sugar and rum from the surrounding estates, its harbour defended by Fort Dundas on the rise above the bay. The 1835 lithograph of Rio Bueno by James Bartholomew Kidd records the town near the height of its commercial life.

St. Mark's was founded in 1833 — one year before Emancipation took formal effect and five years before full freedom on 1 August 1838. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church. The 1891 Handbook of Jamaica records Rio Bueno as one of the principal Anglican churches of Trelawny, with Rev. C. R. Chandler serving 293 registered members, 59 baptisms, and 7 marriages that year — figures comparable to Falmouth.

The town's commercial life waned in the later nineteenth century, and by the turn of the twentieth Rio Bueno was "almost deserted" (as one Gleaner feature later described it). It was resurrected in the mid-twentieth century by the construction of a bauxite-shipping wharf. The Baptist Church on the hill above the bay is associated with William Knibb, the English Baptist abolitionist. Rio Bueno served as a filming location for the 1965 film *A High Wind in Jamaica*. Princess Alice, Countess of Athlone (1883–1981, aunt of King George VI), was a frequent visitor to the church during her residence in Jamaica.

## Architecture
St. Mark's is a small stone church in the simple Gothic Revival adaptation characteristic of Jamaican north-coast Anglican churches. Distinctively painted pale blue, it has a rectangular nave, a small chancel, and pointed-arch windows. The churchyard overlooks the harbour.

## Clergy
Rev. C. R. Chandler served as clergyman at Rio Bueno in 1891 (293 members per the Handbook). The church has been served within the Trelawny Deanery under shared clergy arrangements with the wider cure.

## Hurricane Melissa (October 2025)
Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on 28 October 2025. Trelawny was among the most affected parishes in the west, though Rio Bueno's specific damage status has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Notable Facts
Rio Bueno Harbour is the deepest natural harbour in Jamaica. The town is one of two rival claimants for the site of Columbus's first Jamaican landing (with Discovery Bay). Fort Dundas, the town's surviving fortification, and St. Mark's 1833 foundation make Rio Bueno one of the more historically dense half-kilometres of coastline in the island.
