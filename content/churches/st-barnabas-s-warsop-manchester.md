# St. Barnabas', Warsop
**Manchester** · Church · inactive

St. Barnabas' Anglican Church serves Warsop in northern Manchester, the fifth church in the Albert Town cure circuit. Warsop is a community in the northern interior of Manchester, in the hill country near the Albert Town area. The name Warsop derives from a town in Nottinghamshire, England — Warsop in the Sherwood Forest area — reflecting the practice of English estate owners naming their Jamaican properties after English towns.

## History
Warsop is a community in northern Manchester near Albert Town. It is part of the Albert Town cure, the most northerly cure circuit in the Manchester deanery, serving the communities of the upper Manchester hills near the Trelawny border.

## Architecture
St. Barnabas' is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural Manchester in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. Set in the rural interior of Manchester. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
The Albert Town cure covering Warsop is served from the Albert Town rectory.

## Notable Facts
Warsop in Nottinghamshire is in the historic Sherwood Forest area, associated with the Robin Hood legend. The Jamaican Warsop most likely takes its name from an English estate owner from the Nottinghamshire area. Barnabas, whose name means 'son of encouragement,' was the companion who vouched for Paul to the suspicious Jerusalem church after Paul's conversion — an act of advocacy that shaped the entire trajectory of early Christianity.
