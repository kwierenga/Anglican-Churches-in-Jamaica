# St. Simon's, Comfort Hall
**Manchester** · Church · active

St. Simon's Anglican Church serves Comfort Hall, in the western hills of Manchester some **6 km north-west of Mile Gully**. Public listings (Lonely Planet) describe the church and its surrounding community as a quiet rural area in the cool, tranquil character of the high western Manchester hills. Comfort Hall is administered as a mission of the **St. Barnabas' Mile Gully cure**, which has been listed as **VACANT** in recent diocesan directories.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1814).** Manchester was created by **Act 55 George III. c. 23** of **1814** in response to a petition of 29 November 1814 by residents of Mile Gully, May Day, and Carpenter's Mountain. The Act came into effect on **13 December 1814**, taking the "hill country" of eastern St. Elizabeth and the western portions of Vere and Clarendon. The new parish was named after **William Montagu, 5th Duke of Manchester** (Governor 1808–1827); **Mandeville**, the capital, was named after his son, **Viscount Mandeville**.

**Slavery, the parish church, and the Sam Sharpe Rebellion (1831).** Manchester's hill-country economy was built on coffee, supplemented by sugar in lower zones, both worked by enslaved African labour. **St. Mark's Anglican Church, Mandeville** — the parish church — was completed and consecrated in **1820**; its organ loft was used as a jail during the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**. Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation, 1865, disestablishment.** Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Maidstone** in Manchester (1840) was among Jamaica's earliest Moravian-led free villages. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of St. Simon's: the Mile Gully cure.** Comfort Hall sits in the western hills of Manchester, in some of the coolest and most remote community areas of the parish. The Anglican Diocese established St. Simon's to serve this isolated hill community as part of the Mile Gully cure, which also encompasses St. Peter's Whitby, the Harry Watch chapel of ease, St. Lawrence's Devon, and the famous abandoned **St. George's Anglican Church** ("Duppy Church") — a national monument. **Bishop Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. The 1900 Handbook records "Mile Gully" as a cure under Rev. **E. Clarke**.

**Twentieth century: Kendal, bauxite, Independence.** The **Kendal train tragedy** of **1 September 1957** at Kendal Manchester claimed almost 200 lives — Jamaica's worst rail accident. The **bauxite industry** transformed Manchester from 1957 onward. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Simon's a settled hill mission within the Mile Gully cure.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of St. Simon's Comfort Hall after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. Simon's is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural Manchester in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries. The remote hill-country location means the church depends substantially on lay leadership.

## Clergy
- **1900** — Rev. **E. Clarke** (rector at Mile Gully).
- Today — administered as a mission of the St. Barnabas' Mile Gully cure (currently vacant).

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** **St. Simon the Zealot**, one of the twelve apostles, distinguished from Simon Peter by his political designation. His feast day is **28 October** (shared with St. Jude Thaddaeus, in the Anglican calendar's joint feast of "Simon and Jude"). Simon is the most obscure of the apostles in the New Testament — his choice as patron for a small hill-country chapel gives the church a quietly lesser-known apostolic identity.

**Comfort Hall.** Comfort Hall's name is one of the more aspirationally named communities in Jamaica — expressing the hope of ease and peace after the hardships of plantation life or post-Emancipation settlement. The cool, quiet character of the community described by reviewers suggests the name has been borne out in the community's character.
