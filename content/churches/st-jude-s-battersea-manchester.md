# St. Jude's, Battersea
**Manchester** · Church · active

St. Jude's Anglican Church serves Battersea in the highland country of northern Manchester. The church is administered as a mission of the **Christ Church, Christiana cure**, alongside St. John the Divine, Alston. The cure rectory is at P.O. Box 15, Christiana P.O., Manchester (telephone 964-2274). The 1900 Handbook of Jamaica records "Christiana" under **Rev. J. Cass**. (Note: a separate "St. Jude's Anglican, Battersea" is recorded in St. Ann parish — that is a different church in a different parish, not to be confused with this Manchester entry.)

## History
**Founding of the parish (1814).** Manchester was created by **Act 55 George III. c. 23** of **1814** in response to a petition of 29 November 1814 by residents of Mile Gully, May Day, and Carpenter's Mountain. The Act came into effect on **13 December 1814**, taking the "hill country" of eastern St. Elizabeth and the western portions of Vere and Clarendon. The new parish was named after **William Montagu, 5th Duke of Manchester** (Governor 1808–1827); **Mandeville**, the capital, was named after his son, **Viscount Mandeville**.

**Slavery, the parish church, and the Sam Sharpe Rebellion (1831).** Manchester's hill-country economy was built on coffee, supplemented by sugar in lower zones; both depended on enslaved African labour. **St. Mark's Anglican Church, Mandeville** — the parish church — was completed and consecrated in **1820**, and its organ loft was used as a jail during the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**. Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation, 1865, disestablishment.** Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Maidstone** in Manchester (1840) was among Jamaica's earliest Moravian-led free villages. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of St. Jude's: the Christiana cure.** Battersea is a small farming community in the upland country around Christiana, one of several Manchester districts whose names were transferred from London or other English towns by colonial-era estate proprietors (Battersea being a south-London neighbourhood). The Battersea district sits in the cool, elevated zone that supported coffee and provision farming after Emancipation; the post-1838 small-farmer movement created the kind of scattered upland congregations that the Anglican Diocese reached through chapel-of-ease and mission-station foundations attached to the larger cure churches like Christ Church Christiana. Bishop Enos Nuttall's tenure (1880–1916) saw the Diocese grow from 26 rural mission stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. The 1900 Handbook records the Christiana cure under Rev. **J. Cass**.

**Twentieth century: Kendal, bauxite, Independence.** The **Kendal train tragedy** of **1 September 1957** at Kendal Manchester claimed almost 200 lives — Jamaica's worst rail accident. The **bauxite industry** (Alcan from 1957, Kaiser later) employed over 3,000 by the 1960s. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Jude's a settled mission within the Christiana cure.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of St. Jude's Battersea after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. Jude's is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for highland mission stations across northern Manchester. The elevated position creates wind exposure during storm passages.

## Clergy
- **1900** — Rev. **J. Cass** (rector at Christiana, with St. Jude's as a mission station of the cure).
- Today — served as a mission of the Christ Church Christiana cure within the Mandeville Region's Manchester Deanery.

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** **St. Jude** — Jude Thaddaeus, one of the twelve apostles, often confused in tradition with Judas Iscariot — became the patron of "lost and hopeless causes" in medieval popular devotion: because his name was so similar to the betrayer's, people avoided invoking him; with few petitioners, he was thought available and eager to help. His feast day is **28 October** (shared with Simon the Zealot). The joint feast of "Simon and Jude" appears in several Anglican mission dedications across the island.
