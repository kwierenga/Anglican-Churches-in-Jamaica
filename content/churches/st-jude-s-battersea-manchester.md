# St. Jude's, Battersea
**Manchester** · Church · active

St. Jude's Anglican Church serves Battersea, a community in the highland country of northern Manchester. The church is administered as a mission of the Christ Church, Christiana cure, alongside St. John the Divine, Alston. The cure rectory is at P.O. Box 15, Christiana P.O., Manchester (telephone 964-2274). (Note: a separate "St. Jude's Anglican, Battersea" is recorded in St. Ann parish — that is a different church in a different parish, not to be confused with this Manchester entry.)

## History
Battersea is a small farming community in the upland country around Christiana, one of several Manchester districts whose names were transferred from London or other English towns by colonial-era estate proprietors. The Battersea district sits in the cool, elevated zone that supported coffee and provision farming after Emancipation; the post-1838 small-farmer movement created the kind of scattered upland congregations that the Anglican Diocese reached through chapel-of-ease and mission-station foundations attached to the larger cure churches like Christ Church Christiana.

The dedication to St. Jude — apostle Thaddaeus, identified in the New Testament as one of the twelve and traditional patron of "lost or hopeless causes" — is a distinctive Anglican choice; St. Jude's feast day (28 October) is shared with St. Simon the Zealot, and the joint feast of "Simon and Jude" appears in several Anglican mission dedications across the island.

## Architecture
St. Jude's is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for highland mission stations across northern Manchester. The elevated position creates wind exposure during storm passages.

## Clergy
St. Jude's Battersea is served as a mission of the Christ Church Christiana cure within the Mandeville Region's Manchester Deanery.
