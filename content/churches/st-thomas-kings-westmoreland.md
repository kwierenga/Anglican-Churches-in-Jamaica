# St. Thomas', Kings (Whitehouse)
**Westmoreland** · Church · active

St. Thomas' Church, Kings Whitehouse, is the Anglican church of the coastal fishing town of Whitehouse in southern Westmoreland. Its church sign — bearing the arms of the Diocese of Jamaica and the Cayman Islands — formally identifies the building as "St. Thomas' Church, Kings Whitehouse," combining the traditional district name (Kings) with the town name (Whitehouse). Services are held Sundays at 8:00 am and Morning Prayer on the first and third Thursdays at 7:30 am. The church is paired with St. Thomas' Bluefields as a single cure in the Westmoreland Deanery.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1703).** Westmoreland was officially formed in **1703** from the western portion of the older parish of St. Elizabeth, named for its position at the western part of the island. The parish capital, **Savanna-la-Mar**, was founded around 1730. In 1723 the new parish of Hanover was carved out of Westmoreland's northern section.

**Slavery and the sugar economy.** Under English rule, sugar and slavery accelerated in Westmoreland: an inventory in **1722** numbered the estates at **73**. The Beckford family (Richard, Peter, William) were among the most prominent planters; modern Petersfield is named for Peter Beckford. The **Sam Sharpe Rebellion** of 1831, ignited at Kensington Estate in St. James on **27 December 1831**, spread directly through the western parishes, with some 60,000 enslaved people involved; Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832**. **Emancipation** came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**.

Whitehouse is a settlement of around 4,000 residents on the south-western coast of Westmoreland, known traditionally for its fishing industry — fresh seafood from Whitehouse is distributed to businesses across Jamaica. The community lies near Kings Pen, a locality with a distinctive Maroon heritage: per the Jamaica National Library and community histories, Kings Pen was founded by Maroons who had originally come from Cattawood Springs in Portland and joined forces with Cudjoe, leader of the Leeward Maroons, in 1730 — five years before the First Maroon War reached its most intense phase and nine years before the 1739 treaty granted the Leeward Maroons formal freedom and land. The community retained much of its African cultural heritage in consequence.

The Anglican presence in this part of Westmoreland was historically centred on Bluefields a few kilometres to the west. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church; after the disestablishment Law 30 of 1870, the Church reconstituted itself as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica, and Bishop Enos Nuttall's episcopate (1880–1916) expanded the Diocese's rural station network from 26 in 1880 to 122 by 1911. St. Thomas' Kings/Whitehouse does not appear in the 1891 Handbook of Jamaica's Anglican returns for Westmoreland — which records the principal churches of Savanna-la-Mar and other cures of the period — suggesting its foundation (or formal organisation) came after 1891, consistent with the Nuttall-era rural expansion pattern.

Jamaica's independence on 6 August 1962 found Whitehouse a fishing village whose Anglican congregation was part of the coastal Westmoreland ministry. In recent decades Whitehouse has developed a tourism dimension alongside its fishing economy.

## Architecture
No substantive architectural description of St. Thomas' Kings has been identified in the public heritage sources reviewed. The church stands in the town centre of Whitehouse, at 18.084, -77.954.

## Clergy
The Diocese of Jamaica and the Cayman Islands Westmoreland Deanery directory lists the "St. Thomas' Bluefields & St. Thomas' Kings" cure as **vacant**. Pastoral responsibility and worship continue under neighbouring clergy and lay leadership.

## Hurricane Melissa (October 2025)
Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope, Westmoreland, on 28 October 2025. The Diocese named St. Thomas' Bluefields — the cure's sister church — as one of four Anglican churches that "stood little chance" against the storm. The specific status of St. Thomas' Kings/Whitehouse after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed, but the southern Westmoreland coast sustained catastrophic damage across the board.

## Notable Facts
The church is officially signed "St. Thomas' Church, Kings Whitehouse" — an unusually explicit double-naming that preserves both the traditional district name and the modern town name on the same board. Kings Pen's Maroon heritage traces to a 1730 founding by fugitives from Portland who joined Cudjoe's Leeward Maroon community. Whitehouse's fishing industry is central to the town's identity and to the broader south-western coast economy. The dedication to Thomas the Apostle — "Doubting Thomas" of John 20:24–29 — has a feast day of 3 July.
