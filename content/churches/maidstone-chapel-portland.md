# Maidstone Chapel
**Portland** · Chapel · active

Maidstone Chapel is recorded in our inventory as an Anglican chapel in the **Maidstone** district of Portland, in the parish's eastern interior. (Note: a separate Maidstone heritage site is in **Manchester** — a Moravian-founded free village dating from 1840 — and is sometimes confused with this Portland entry. The two are distinct communities in different parishes.)

## History
**English rule (1655) and the founding of Portland (1723).** Under English rule from **1655**, the future Portland coast remained largely Maroon territory. **Portland parish was created in 1723** by order of the then Governor, **Henry Bentinck, 1st Duke of Portland** (Governor 1722–1726), and named in his honour. The British built **Fort George** at Port Antonio in 1729. The original Spanish-era settlement of the area was minimal; English development followed the 1723 parish foundation.

**The First Maroon War (1728–1739) and Nanny.** The **Windward Maroons** under **Quao** and **Nanny** controlled the Blue Mountains and John Crow Mountains. The **1740 treaty** granted **Nanny a 500-acre land grant** that forms the present site of **Moore Town**. **Nanny of the Maroons** became Jamaica's only female National Hero in October 1975.

**Slavery and the planter church.** The parish became a sugar-producing zone manned by African enslaved labour. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union** to "wipe non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Christ Church Port Antonio** was completed around **1840** under Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb**.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall, and the banana revolution.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. After Emancipation, Portland's economy shifted dramatically when, in **1871**, the American **Captain Lorenzo Dow Baker** exported the first shipment of **1,450 stems** of bananas from Port Antonio to Boston — a moment that displaced sugar and made Portland the centre of Jamaica's banana economy for the late 19th and early 20th centuries. Baker's Boston Fruit Company became **United Fruit Company in 1899**.

**Foundation of the Maidstone Chapel.** The Anglican Diocese established a network of mission chapels across Portland's interior to serve the small farming and banana-labouring communities of the parish. Maidstone Chapel — like the small chapels at Comfort Castle, Birnamwood, Sherwood Forest, and others scattered across the Portland interior — represents that wider mission presence. No substantive foundational date or detailed building history for the Portland Maidstone Chapel has been identified in the public heritage sources reviewed.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** The **1903 hurricane** damaged Christ Church Port Antonio and devastated the banana industry. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found Maidstone Chapel a settled rural worship point.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Hurricane Melissa brought heavy rain to Portland's interior; the Maidstone area was in the affected zone.

## Architecture
Maidstone Chapel is a small rural building of the kind erected by the Diocese for chapel use across rural Portland. The hill-country position of the Portland interior creates exposure to the heavy rainfall typical of the parish — Portland is one of the wettest parishes in Jamaica.

## Clergy
The chapel has been served within the **Eastern Jamaica Region** of the Diocese, grouped with the cure churches and missions of the Portland Anglican network.

## Notable Facts
Many of Portland's chapels were established during the post-1870 Nuttall expansion to serve dispersed banana-economy communities. The **Maidstone** in Manchester (1840) — sometimes confused with this Portland Maidstone — was a Moravian-led free village rather than an Anglican settlement, illustrating the variety of denominational foundations in post-Emancipation Jamaica.
