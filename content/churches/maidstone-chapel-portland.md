# Maidstone Chapel
**Portland** · Chapel · active

Maidstone Chapel is recorded in our inventory as an Anglican chapel in the Maidstone district of Portland, in the parish's eastern interior. (Note: a separate Maidstone heritage site is in **Manchester** — a Moravian-founded free village dating from 1840 — and is sometimes confused with this Portland entry. The two are distinct communities in different parishes.)

## History
Portland was created as a parish in 1723 by order of the then Governor, the Duke of Portland, and named in his honour. The original Spanish-era settlement of the area was minimal; English development followed the 1723 parish foundation, and the parish became a sugar-producing zone manned by African enslaved labour. After Emancipation, Portland's economy shifted dramatically when, in 1871, the American Captain Lorenzo Dow Baker exported the first shipment of 1,450 stems of bananas from Port Antonio to Boston — a moment that displaced sugar and made Portland the centre of Jamaica's banana economy for the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries.

The Anglican Diocese established a network of mission chapels across Portland's interior to serve the small farming and banana-labouring communities of the parish. Maidstone Chapel — like the small chapels at Comfort Castle, Birnamwood, Sherwood Forest, and others scattered across the Portland interior — represents that wider mission presence. No substantive foundational date or detailed building history for the Portland Maidstone Chapel has been identified in the public heritage sources reviewed.

## Architecture
Maidstone Chapel is a small rural building of the kind erected by the Diocese for chapel use across rural Portland. The hill-country position of the Portland interior creates exposure to the heavy rainfall typical of the parish — Portland is one of the wettest parishes in Jamaica.

## Clergy
The chapel has been served within the Eastern Jamaica Region of the Diocese, grouped with the cure churches and missions of the Portland Anglican network.
