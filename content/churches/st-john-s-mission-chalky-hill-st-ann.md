# St. John's Mission, Chalky Hill
**St. Ann** · Mission · active

St. John's Mission serves the community of **Chalky Hill**, a rural district in St. Ann in the hilly interior south of Ocho Rios — a small agricultural community in the ridge country between the north-coast tourist corridor and the southern agricultural plain. St. John's was established as an Anglican mission chapel to serve this dispersed hill community.

## History
**Pre-English settlement: Sevilla la Nueva (1509–1534).** St. Ann has a deeper colonial past than almost any other Jamaican parish. **Sevilla la Nueva**, the first permanent European settlement in Jamaica and the colony's first Spanish capital, was founded in **1509** by Juan de Esquivel near present St. Ann's Bay and served as Spanish capital until **1534**. **Christopher Columbus** had been marooned at St. Ann's Bay for over a year (1503–1504).

**English rule (1655) and the planter church.** Under English rule from **1655**, St. Ann became a major **sugar and livestock** region — the coastal plain to sugar estates and the interior plateau to "**pens**" (livestock properties). The Chalky Hill area sits in the hill country of interior St. Ann, an area shaped by the post-Emancipation settlement of freed families who moved into the interior highlands after 1838. Until **1870**, the Church of England was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**Sam Sharpe Rebellion and the Colonial Church Union (1831–32).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, **Hamilton Brown** of Brown's Town led a Colonial Church Union mob that destroyed the Brown's Town Baptist chapel in 1832 — the **Colonial Church Union** sought to "wipe the non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth." **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation (1838) and free villages.** Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. The freed population settled the hills of interior St. Ann, establishing small farms on lands that had previously been part of the plantation system. **Sturge Town** (1839, founded by the English Quaker abolitionist Joseph Sturge) and **Clarksonville** (1835, founded by Baptist missionary Rev. John Clark, named for the abolitionist Thomas Clarkson) were among Jamaica's earliest free villages — both in St. Ann (NLJ, *History of St. Ann*). Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb** (1824–1843) had begun extending Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**Disestablishment (1870) and the Chalky Hill mission.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. The Anglican Diocese extended its mission network into these interior communities, and St. John's was planted as a mission chapel to serve Chalky Hill and the surrounding district. As a mission rather than a parish church, it has always operated within the ministry circuit of the larger St. Ann congregations, with its life sustained largely by dedicated lay leadership.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** Bauxite mining began in St. Ann around **1952** (Reynolds, Kaiser); tourism grew at Ocho Rios. **Hurricane Gilbert (1988)** caused serious damage across the parish interior. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. John's a settled mission within the Diocese.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Hurricane Melissa brought heavy rain and wind to interior St. Ann; Chalky Hill and the mission congregation were in the affected zone.

## Architecture
St. John's Mission is a small chapel of simple construction typical of Anglican mission buildings in the St. Ann interior. The building is set in the hilly terrain of Chalky Hill, in a landscape of small farms and wooded ridges. The interior hill position gives the mission some shelter from direct coastal storm surge, but the ridge country of interior St. Ann is subject to landslides and road closures during heavy rainfall events associated with tropical storms and hurricanes.

## Clergy
As a mission chapel, St. John's Chalky Hill has been served within the **St. Ann's Bay or Ocho Rios clergy cluster** of the Diocese, grouped with other interior and coastal St. Ann congregations under shared ministry arrangements. The mission's pastoral life has depended heavily on lay leaders — wardens, Mothers' Union members, and readers — who maintain regular worship between clerical visits. The church is confirmed active in diocesan records.

## Notable Facts
The Chalky Hill area of St. Ann is part of the interior ridge landscape that lies between the tourist-facing north coast and the quieter agricultural south of the parish. Communities like Chalky Hill represent a St. Ann largely invisible to visitors who come for the beaches and attractions of Ocho Rios, yet these interior hill communities have deep roots in the post-Emancipation history of the parish. The dedication to **St. John** — most commonly the Evangelist (feast 27 December), occasionally the Baptist (24 June) — is one of the most common Anglican dedications.
