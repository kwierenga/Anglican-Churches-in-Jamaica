# St. Margaret's, Liguanea
**St. Andrew** · Church · active

St. Margaret's Anglican Church stands on Old Hope Road in Liguanea, Kingston 6, serving as the mother church of a grouped parish that includes St. Joseph's, The Grove and St Cyprian's, August Town. Founded in 1938, it was the only Anglican church in the area when the University College of the West Indies opened on the adjacent former Mona Estate in 1948, and it became the spiritual home for the first generation of UWI students. Over more than eight decades, it has grown from a neighbourhood church into a regional hub hosting diocesan events and welcoming visitors from across Jamaica and the diaspora. The current rector is the Very Rev. Franklyn Jackson.

## History
St. Margaret's was established in 1938, during the inter-war suburban growth of eastern St. Andrew as the Liguanea area transformed from sugar estates and cattle pens into a residential and commercial district. The name Liguanea derives from the Arawak/Taino word for iguana — archaeologist Philip Allsworth-Jones has identified seventeen Taino sites on the Liguanea Plain, confirming substantial pre-Columbian settlement long before European contact.

The Liguanea Plain was densely covered with sugar estates worked by enslaved Africans throughout the colonial period. Hope Estate, founded by Major Richard Hope in the 1660s on over 2,600 acres, was one of the island's largest and most technologically advanced slave plantations; the government acquired it in 1881, and it became the Hope Botanical Gardens — the largest in the Caribbean. Cherry Garden Estate, nearby, was owned by Colonel Ezekiel Gomersall, one of Jamaica's largest slave owners. It was on this estate that George William Gordon (1820–1865) was born, the illegitimate son of Anna Rattrey, an enslaved woman. Gordon would later be executed after the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and declared a National Hero of Jamaica. By the time St. Margaret's was founded in 1938, these estates had long been subdivided into the residential and commercial plots that now characterise Liguanea, but the area's identity is inseparable from its plantation past.

In 1948, when the University College of the West Indies opened on the former Mona Estate, St. Margaret's was the only Anglican church in the area. The church became the natural home for Anglican students and faculty, including several who went on to distinguished careers. The rector at the time, Harold Critchlow, served until 1972 and later became Dean of St. Michael's Cathedral, Barbados.

Jamaica's independence on 6 August 1962 accelerated the transformation of the Liguanea area. Today it is home to Vale Royal (the Prime Minister's residence), Jamaica House (the PM's office), and King's House (the Governor-General's residence). St. Margaret's has reflected this changing profile while maintaining a strong parish identity, and it celebrated its 85th anniversary in November 2023 with a banquet at The Jamaica Pegasus Hotel.

## Architecture
St. Margaret's is a substantial, buff-coloured church with a red-roofed nave, a gabled west end, and pointed-arch lancet windows — blending traditional Gothic Revival elements with mid-twentieth-century materials and proportions. Set back from Old Hope Road behind a parking forecourt and palm trees, it has a solid but welcoming presence. Parish halls and ancillary rooms extend from the main worship space, hosting youth groups and diocesan conferences. A columbarium and landscaped memorial areas provide permanent resting places for ashes.

## Education and Community
In 1992, the STEP Centre (School for Therapy, Education and Parenting of Children with Multiple Disabilities) opened in the church hall, providing specialist education and care. The church frequently hosts regional and diocesan gatherings, including international Anglican delegate meetings, and has served as a civic venue for events such as the Peace Corps Jamaica 60th anniversary celebration. St. Margaret's maintains an active online presence, livestreaming Sunday services and posting devotional content that keeps diaspora members closely connected to parish life.

## Clergy
The Very Rev. Franklyn Jackson serves as rector of the grouped parish, with assistant Rev. Melrose Wiggan. Harold Critchlow, rector during the formative decades when UWI opened, was instrumental in establishing the church as the Anglican centre of the Mona corridor. A Jamaican Chief Justice has been noted among its members, reflecting the parish's influence in national life.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to St. Margaret of Antioch — a young Christian virgin martyred under Roman persecution in the early fourth century — connects the parish to one of the most popular saints in the English church. Over 250 churches are dedicated to her in England, most famously St. Margaret's, Westminster, the parish church of the British Houses of Parliament. Her feast day is 20 July. The Liguanea Club, founded in 1910 near the church, celebrated its centenary in 2010. That George William Gordon, National Hero, was born on the nearby Cherry Garden Estate to an enslaved mother gives the Liguanea area a particular resonance in Jamaica's story of slavery, resistance, and nationhood.
