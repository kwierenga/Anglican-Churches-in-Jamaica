# St. Peter's, Petersfield
**Westmoreland** · Church · active

St. Peter's Anglican Church serves Petersfield, one of the larger interior communities in Westmoreland, situated along the main road between Savanna-la-Mar and Montego Bay. Petersfield grew as a market and service community for the surrounding sugar estates and, after Emancipation, became a centre for the small-farmer settlements that emerged across the interior plain. St. Peter's has been the Anglican anchor for this community for several generations, offering worship, pastoral care, and a connection to the wider Diocese.

## History
Petersfield takes its name from the estate history of the district and grew into a recognisable town with the decline of the plantation system after Emancipation. Freed families settled along the road and in the surrounding farms, and the town developed a small commercial character serving the interior of Westmoreland. The Anglican Church established St. Peter's as a mission church to serve this dispersed rural population, becoming an important complement to the parish church at Savanna-la-Mar. The church's history runs parallel to the social transformation of Westmoreland from a plantation economy to a smallholder and commercial one across the 19th and 20th centuries.

## Architecture
St. Peter's is a masonry chapel of modest proportions typical of Anglican rural churches in the Westmoreland interior. The building is functional in character, designed for a congregation of modest means in an agricultural community. Its setting along the main road gives it visibility as a community landmark. The interior furnishings are simple, and the church is maintained by the congregation as a centre for worship and community gatherings, including harvest festivals and seasonal celebrations that remain important in Westmoreland's agricultural communities.

## Clergy
Petersfield has been served within the Westmoreland Anglican circuit, with clergy based at St. George's in Savanna-la-Mar overseeing a cluster of mission churches including St. Peter's. As an interior church away from the coast, it is somewhat less exposed to direct hurricane damage than the coastal congregations, though inland flooding and wind damage have affected it during major storms. The church's ministry is sustained in significant part by dedicated lay leaders, consistent with the pattern across Westmoreland's smaller Anglican communities.

## Notable Facts
Petersfield sits on slightly elevated ground compared to the coastal plain, giving it marginally better protection against surge flooding, though it remains exposed to wind damage during major hurricanes. Hurricane Charlie (1951) tracked directly over this part of Westmoreland and caused widespread structural damage. Hurricane Gilbert (1988) brought significant destruction to interior communities. Hurricane Melissa (October 28, 2025) reduced St Peter's Church to ruins. The Jamaica Gleaner reported the church 'lies in ruins after Hurricane Melissa tore through the area,' with an accompanying photograph confirming structural collapse. Rector Canon Hartley Perrin — also Custos of Westmoreland — described conducting a funeral under a tent in a parishioner's front yard because the church no longer stood: 'It is heartbreaking. Families make all the plans — the programmes, the music — but suddenly the church isn't there.' Perrin, who was due to retire after 47 years of ministry, said he would spend his final months guiding the congregation through recovery. Petersfield is in the area of direct landfall, making this one of the most severely impacted Anglican sites in Westmoreland.
