# St. Silas', Mt. Airy
**Westmoreland** · Church · active

St. Silas' Anglican Church serves the community of Mt. Airy in the interior of Westmoreland parish, in the upland farming country between Savanna-la-Mar and the parish's hinterland with St. James and Hanover. The church belongs to the dispersed network of small late-nineteenth and early-twentieth-century mission stations that the Diocese of Jamaica established across the post-Emancipation Jamaican countryside. The church does not appear in the 1891 Handbook of Jamaica's Anglican clergy returns, nor in the 1900 Handbook — placing its foundation firmly in the great rural mission expansion of Bishop Enos Nuttall (Bishop of Jamaica 1880–1916), under whose tenure the Diocese grew from 26 rural mission stations in 1880 to 122 by 1911.

## History
Westmoreland was created as a parish in 1703 from the western portion of the older parish of St. Elizabeth, named after Westmorland — the north-western English county that the colonial naming committee chose to commemorate. The new parish capital, Savanna-la-Mar, retained its Spanish-era name (literally "the savannah by the sea") on the south coast. From its creation, Westmoreland was one of Jamaica's leading sugar and rum parishes: the lowland plains and river valleys held some of the most productive estates of the British Caribbean, all worked by enslaved African labour.

The parish's nineteenth-century history was shaped most decisively by the Sam Sharpe Rebellion of December 1831, also known as the Christmas Rebellion or the Baptist War. The uprising — led by the Baptist deacon Samuel Sharpe and ignited at the Kensington Estate in St. James on the night of 27 December 1831 — spread rapidly through the western parishes; some 60,000 enslaved people across St. James, Trelawny, Hanover, and Westmoreland joined the rising. Suppressed brutally by colonial militias and Maroon allies of the planters, the rebellion ended with Sharpe's hanging in Montego Bay on 23 May 1832. But its consequence was decisive: within two years the British Parliament passed the Slavery Abolition Act (1833), Emancipation came in 1834, and full freedom followed at the end of the apprenticeship period on 1 August 1838. Sharpe is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

After Emancipation the freed people of Westmoreland settled the upland districts of the parish in search of provision land — communities like Mt. Airy, Cornwall Mountain, Sheffield, and Kentucky belong to that wave of post-1838 free settlement. Bishop Spencer's diocese had already begun extending the Anglican network to the new free villages, but it was Bishop Enos Nuttall's tenure, beginning 1880, that systematised the rural mission expansion. By the 1900 Handbook of Jamaica, the Westmoreland Deanery's Anglican clergy were: C. H. Davis and J. J. C. Ormsby (Sav-la-Mar); S. J. Vaughan (Trinity); S. Negus (Bluefields & Kings); R. L. Reid (St. Paul's & Negril); W. Heaver (Petersfield & Darliston); and E. D. Tinling (New Road and 5 Mission Stations). St. Silas' was likely one of the mission stations attached to one of these cures during this period of expansion.

The dedication to Silas (also called Silvanus) — companion of the Apostle Paul through the second missionary journey, named in Acts 15:40 and in the Pauline epistles — is characteristic of the smaller mission-station foundations of the later nineteenth century, when dedications to Pauline companions and the lesser apostles were widely used for mission chapels rather than to single saintly patrons.

## Architecture
St. Silas' is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for mission stations across the Westmoreland uplands in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries: a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small adjoining churchyard. The interior hill-country position offers some shelter from coastal storm surge, but the western Jamaican parishes have repeatedly been struck by major hurricanes — and Mt. Airy's ridge-and-valley terrain creates significant wind exposure during major storm passages.

## Clergy
St. Silas' Mt. Airy is served within the Montego Bay Region's Westmoreland Deanery. As one of the smaller mission stations in a deanery whose cure churches are in Sav-la-Mar, Petersfield, Darliston, Grange Hill, and the New Road circuit, St. Silas' has historically depended on shared clergy and on the sustained ministry of lay leaders.

## Notable Facts
**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025)** — Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on 28 October 2025, the strongest hurricane to make landfall on Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Interior Westmoreland communities experienced destructive winds and heavy rain; the Diocese of Jamaica reported approximately 50 church buildings across the affected western parishes destroyed or significantly damaged. The specific status of St. Silas' Mt. Airy after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

**The Silas dedication** — Silas (Silvanus) is one of the Pauline companions named in Acts and in 1 and 2 Thessalonians. He is traditionally associated with Paul's second missionary journey through Asia Minor and Greece. A dedication to Silas rather than to one of the twelve apostles or a major patron saint reflects the late-nineteenth-century Anglican preference for using "missionary companion" figures as patrons of new rural mission stations — an apt theological choice for a chapel founded as part of an evangelistic expansion across the post-Emancipation Jamaican countryside.
