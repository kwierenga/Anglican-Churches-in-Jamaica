# St. Mary's Chapel, Lawrencefield
**St. Andrew** · Chapel · active

St. Mary's Chapel is a historic Anglican chapel serving the **Lawrencefield** community in St. Andrew, situated on land that was part of the colonial estate system of the parish. **Lawrencefield Estate** was the property at which the privateer-turned-Lieutenant-Governor **Sir Henry Morgan** died on 25 August 1688.

## History
**English conquest (1655), Henry Morgan, and St. Andrew parish (1664).** Penn and Venables's English expedition captured Jamaica from Spain in May **1655**. Governor D'Oyley invited the Brethren of the Coast to base at Port Royal in 1657, and **Henry Morgan** — the Welsh privateer who raided Portobello, Maracaibo, and Panama City — used Port Royal as his base before being knighted by Charles II and serving as Lieutenant Governor. **He died at Lawrencefield Estate on 25 August 1688** and was buried in the Palisadoes cemetery before the 1692 earthquake claimed his grave. **St. Andrew parish was established in 1664** with a 600-acre glebe granted in 1667 by Charles II.

**Port Royal, the 1692 earthquake, and the founding of Kingston (1693).** The **Great Earthquake of 7 June 1692** destroyed two-thirds of Port Royal — Morgan's grave slid into the harbour and has never been found; the new city of **Kingston** was formally established on **22 July 1692**, with the Assembly constituting Kingston as a parish in **1693**.

**Slavery, Diocese, Sam Sharpe, Emancipation, disestablishment.** Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church. The **Diocese of Jamaica** was established by Letters Patent on **24 July 1824**; **Christopher Lipscomb** was consecrated first Bishop in 1824. During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. Following emancipation, the chapel's role transformed significantly: it became a cornerstone for the newly freed community. Bishop Lipscomb (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

**1872 capital, 1907 earthquake, 1923 KSAC.** The capital moved from Spanish Town to **Kingston in 1872**. The **14 January 1907 earthquake** destroyed much of Kingston; under Nuttall's £50,000 reconstruction programme, ten churches were consecrated within a single month in **January 1911**. The **Kingston and St. Andrew Corporation (KSAC)** unified the two parishes for civic administration in **1923**.

**Foundation of St. Mary's Chapel.** St. Mary's Chapel was established to serve the Lawrencefield estate and surrounding community in St. Andrew. Its primary initial function was to serve the spiritual needs of the planter family, overseers, and the enslaved population of the estate. After Emancipation it provided a formal and enduring space for worship, baptisms, marriages, and funerals for the freed community, helping to foster a distinct village identity separate from the plantation system.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Mary's a chapelry within the wider Anglican parish structure.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Lawrencefield in eastern St. Andrew was well away from the worst of the storm's western-Jamaica impact; no specific structural damage to the chapel has been reported.

## Architecture
St. Mary's is a modest, functional structure, likely constructed of stone or rubble masonry. Its architecture follows the simple, unadorned pattern typical of Jamaican estate chapels: a rectangular nave with a small, distinct chancel, topped by a timber-frame roof clad in zinc or wood shingles. The interior is characterised by simple, well-kept furnishings.

## Clergy
As a chapel-of-ease, St. Mary's has historically not maintained a full-time rector. During the slavery era, it was served intermittently by chaplains. Post-emancipation, it relied on curates and visiting priests. In the modern era, its clergy have been part of a rotational ministry, shared with other small missions within a regional deanery.

## Notable Facts
**Lawrencefield Estate** is most famously remembered as the property where **Sir Henry Morgan** — Welsh privateer, Lieutenant-Governor of Jamaica, and the man whose 1671 raid on Panama City made him the most celebrated buccaneer of the age — died on **25 August 1688**. The dedication to **St. Mary the Virgin** is one of the most universal in the Anglican calendar, with feasts on **25 March** (Annunciation) and **15 August** (Falling Asleep).
