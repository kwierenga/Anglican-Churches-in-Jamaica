# St. Philip's, Whitfield Town
**St. Andrew** · Church · active

St. Philip's Anglican Church serves Whitfield Town, a community in the western part of the Kingston/St. Andrew metropolitan area. Whitfield Town is a residential neighbourhood in the urban zone near the St. Andrew/Kingston boundary, and St. Philip's has served its community for several generations. The dedication to St. Philip the Apostle — who brought Nathanael to Christ — is found in several Jamaican Anglican churches.

## History
Whitfield Town developed as a residential neighbourhood in the western urban expansion of Kingston and St. Andrew. The Anglican Diocese established St. Philip's to serve this community as the metropolitan area grew through the 20th century. The church sits in a neighbourhood that has been part of the broader inner-city landscape of west Kingston.

## Architecture
St. Philip's is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural St. Andrew in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. Set in the rural interior of St. Andrew. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
Whitfield Town has been served within the St. Andrew urban deanery circuit, connected to the Kingston metropolitan ministry network.

## Notable Facts
Whitfield Town is in the zone of western Kingston/St. Andrew that has been associated with significant social and economic challenges in Jamaica's post-independence urban history. The Anglican church's presence in this community reflects the Diocese's commitment to serving all parts of the metropolitan area, including those facing the greatest social pressures.
