# All Saints', Mt. Providence
**Clarendon** · Church · active

All Saints' Anglican Church serves **Mount Providence**, a community in the upland farming country of central Clarendon north of the parish capital. The church is administered as a mission of the **St. Paul's, Chapelton** cure, alongside St. Mark's Beckford Kraal, St. James' Rock River, and St. Luke's Wood Hall. The cure rectory is at Chapelton P.O., Clarendon (telephone 785-0782).

## History
**Founding of the parish (1664).** Clarendon parish was created in **1664**, nine years after the English seizure of Jamaica from Spain (**1655**), and named for **Sir Edward Hyde, 1st Earl of Clarendon** (1609–1674), Lord High Chancellor of England under Charles II. The original parish church was the **Church of the White Cross** at "The Cross" — Jamaica's third Anglican church and the site of Jamaica's first House of Assembly elections. **St. Paul's Chapelton** was built as a chapel of ease in **1666** in the upland interior, and became the parish church when the Cross Church was destroyed in **1815**. **Vere** was carved out as a separate sugar-plain parish in **1671**; Vere was reabsorbed into Clarendon in **1867**, and **Law 20 of 1887** designated **May Pen** as chief town.

**Slavery and the Suttons rebellion.** Clarendon was one of Jamaica's great sugar parishes. The fertile Rio Minho plain held estates bearing the names of their English founders — **Suttons, Pennants, Ballards** — while the buccaneer **Henry Morgan** owned Morgan's Valley and Danks. The **Dawkins family** alone accumulated seventeen plantations covering over 25,000 acres in Clarendon between 1665 and 1833. The wealth of the parish was built on the labour of enslaved Africans. One of Jamaica's earliest slave rebellions erupted at **Suttons Plantation in 1690**: some 500 enslaved people escaped and joined Spanish-speaking free Africans, forming the Maroon communities eventually led by **Cudjoe** into the Cockpit Country.

**Sam Sharpe and the Colonial Church Union (1831).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**, a vigilante alliance that sought to "wipe the non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth." Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation, free villages, disestablishment.** Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. The freed population of Clarendon established free villages and provision grounds across the interior. The "Providence" element of the name **Mount Providence** belongs to a wider Jamaican post-Emancipation tradition of giving aspirational, scripturally resonant names to new free settlements — names like Providence, New Hope, Refuge, and Mount Pleasant express the hope of divine care that defined the founding generation of free villages after 1838. Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb** (1824–1843) had begun extending Anglican schools across the parish — by 1835 he reported 142 schools with 8,500 scholars across Jamaica. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of the Mt. Providence mission.** The Anglican Diocese established All Saints' as a small mission station of the **St. Paul's Chapelton** cure to serve this community within the diocese's late-nineteenth-century rural church-planting expansion. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

**Twentieth century: bauxite, May Pen, Independence.** Chapelton lost its status as parish capital in **1938**, with administrative functions transferring to May Pen. **Alcoa** began bauxite operations in Clarendon in **1959**; the **Jamalco** alumina refinery at Halse Hall opened in **1972**. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found All Saints' Mt. Providence a settled mission within the Chapelton cure.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of All Saints' Mt. Providence after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
All Saints' is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for mission stations across the Clarendon interior. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal storm surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major hurricane passages.

## Clergy
All Saints' Mt. Providence is served as a mission of the St. Paul's, Chapelton cure within the Mandeville Region's Clarendon Deanery.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to **All Saints** — commemorating the full company of recognised and unrecognised saints on **1 November** — is the most general dedication in the Anglican tradition and is a common choice for mission chapels intended to gather a scattered farming community without claim to a single saintly patron.
