# St. Paul's, Moore Town
**Portland** · Church · active

St. Paul's Anglican Church at **Moore Town** serves one of Jamaica's most historically significant communities — a **Maroon town** in the Rio Grande Valley of Portland, officially recognised as one of the **four surviving treaty Maroon communities** of Jamaica (alongside Accompong, Charles Town, and Scott's Hall). Moore Town is the main town of the **Windward Maroons** of Portland, a community that maintained its freedom and distinct culture through resistance, treaty, and continuous habitation from the 17th century to the present.

## History
**English rule (1655) and the founding of Portland (1723).** Under English rule from **1655**, the future Portland coast remained largely Maroon territory. **Portland parish was created in 1723** by order of the then Governor, **Henry Bentinck, 1st Duke of Portland** (Governor 1722–1726), and named in his honour. The British built **Fort George** at Port Antonio in 1729.

**The First Maroon War (1728–1739), Nanny, and the founding of Moore Town.** Moore Town's Maroons are descendants of enslaved Africans who escaped from plantations and established free communities in the mountains of eastern Jamaica. The **Windward Maroons**, led by the legendary **Nanny of the Maroons** — now a Jamaican National Hero — controlled the Blue Mountains and John Crow Mountains. After an earlier **Nanny Town was captured in 1734** by forces led by Colonel Brooks (National Library of Jamaica), the Maroons rebuilt under new leadership. In **1746 Nanny signed a separate peace treaty with the English and received a 500-acre land grant** that forms the present site of Moore Town — this followed Cudjoe's Leeward treaty of March 1738/9 and Quao's Windward treaty of June 1739. Moore Town (also called **New Nanny Town**) became the administrative centre of the Windward Maroon territory.

**Slavery and the planter church.** Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds. The Anglican Diocese later established St. Paul's to serve Moore Town's community, navigating the complex relationship between the established church and a community with its own independent religious and cultural traditions.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union** to "wipe non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Christ Church Port Antonio** was completed around **1840** under Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb**.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall, and the banana revolution.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. In **1870**, **Captain Lorenzo Dow Baker** purchased bananas in Port Antonio market; Baker's Boston Fruit Company became **United Fruit Company in 1899**, and the Rio Grande valley became one of the most important banana-growing zones in the British Caribbean. Google Places confirms St. Paul's at its Moore Town location.

**National Hero recognition (1975) and Independence.** Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found Moore Town a settled treaty Maroon community. **Nanny became Jamaica's only female National Hero in October 1975**, formally recognising her Windward Maroon leadership.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Hurricane Melissa brought extraordinary rainfall to Portland's interior; the **Rio Grande Valley** and Moore Town were severely affected.

## Architecture
St. Paul's is a masonry church in the **Rio Grande Valley**. The valley setting between the Blue Mountains and John Crow Mountains creates extraordinary rainfall — Moore Town is one of the wettest inhabited places in Jamaica. The church's construction reflects the requirements of a building in a high-rainfall, high-humidity valley environment. The surrounding mountains create flooding and landslide risk during hurricane passages.

## Clergy
Moore Town has been served within the **Portland deanery**. The unique character of the Maroon community has shaped the relationship between the Anglican church and its Moore Town congregation across many generations, with the church operating within a community that has its own distinct spiritual and cultural traditions alongside Anglican worship.

## Notable Facts
**Moore Town** was previously known as **New Nanny Town**. After an earlier Nanny Town was captured in 1734, the Maroons rebuilt under new leadership, and in **1746 Nanny signed a separate peace treaty** with the English and received a **500-acre land grant** that forms the present site of Moore Town. Today Moore Town is governed by a **Colonel**, an honorary Maroon title. **Nanny of the Maroons** — first and only female National Hero of Jamaica, of Ashanti origin — is commemorated here. The Rio Grande Valley is subject to catastrophic flooding during major rainfall events.
