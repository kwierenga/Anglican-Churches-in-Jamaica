# St. Thomas', Manchioneal
**Portland** · Church · active

St. Thomas' Anglican Church serves **Manchioneal**, a fishing and farming community at the far eastern end of Portland parish's accessible coast — at the point where Portland's coastal road reaches the edge of the **John Crow Mountains** wilderness. Reviewers note stained glass windows and a serene ambiance in a community of warm and welcoming character.

## History
**English rule (1655) and the founding of Portland (1723).** Under English rule from **1655**, the future Portland coast remained largely Maroon territory. **Portland parish was created in 1723** by order of the then Governor, **Henry Bentinck, 1st Duke of Portland** (Governor 1722–1726), and named in his honour. The British built **Fort George** at Port Antonio in 1729.

**The First Maroon War (1728–1739) and Nanny.** The **Windward Maroons** under **Quao** and **Nanny** controlled the Blue Mountains and John Crow Mountains — including the wilderness immediately above Manchioneal. The **1740 treaty** granted **Nanny a 500-acre land grant** that forms the present site of **Moore Town**. **Nanny of the Maroons** became Jamaica's only female National Hero in October 1975.

**Slavery and the planter church.** Sugar estates dominated the fertile coastal lowlands and river valleys; coffee thrived in the hills. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union** to "wipe non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Christ Church Port Antonio** was completed around **1840** under Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb**.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall, and the banana revolution.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. In **1870**, **Captain Lorenzo Dow Baker** purchased bananas in Port Antonio market; Baker's Boston Fruit Company became **United Fruit Company in 1899**.

**Foundation of St. Thomas'.** Manchioneal developed as a coastal community at the eastern extremity of the Portland coastal plain, with the John Crow Mountains pressing close to the sea at this point and limiting further coastal settlement to the east. The Anglican Diocese established St. Thomas' as part of its coastal mission network in Portland, named for the apostle known for his questioning faith. The community's fishing tradition and its position as the last significant settlement before the wilderness of the John Crow Mountains gave it a distinct character within the parish.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** The **1903 hurricane** damaged Christ Church Port Antonio and devastated the banana industry. Tourism grew at Port Antonio. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Thomas' a settled eastern coastal mission.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. **Hurricane Ivan (2004)** had earlier struck this part of the Portland coast with severe force; Hurricane Melissa tracked along the north coast and Manchioneal was in the storm's direct path.

## Architecture
St. Thomas' is a coastal church at the eastern end of the Portland coast. Reviewers describe stained glass windows and a serene atmosphere, with the church set in the tranquil surroundings of Manchioneal. The coastal position creates storm surge vulnerability during hurricane passages, and the proximity of the John Crow Mountains means the community also faces heavy rainfall and potential landslide risk from the steep terrain immediately behind the coast.

## Clergy
Manchioneal has been served within the **Portland deanery**, connected to the Port Antonio circuit. The remote coastal location means the community depends significantly on local leadership to sustain its congregation.

## Notable Facts
**Manchioneal** sits at the point where Portland's accessible north coast effectively ends, with the **John Crow Mountains** creating a barrier to further coastal development to the east. The John Crow Mountains are one of Jamaica's most pristine and inaccessible wilderness areas (and were the heartland of Nanny's Windward Maroon territory), and Manchioneal is the gateway community to this landscape. The dedication to **St. Thomas the Apostle** (feast day **21 December** in the Anglican calendar; **3 July** in some traditions) — the apostle who doubted then proclaimed "My Lord and my God" — is shared with the parish to the south, **St. Thomas-in-the-East**.
