# St. Barnabas', Port Morant
**St. Thomas** · Church · active

St. Barnabas' Anglican Church serves **Port Morant**, a coastal community at the eastern end of St. Thomas parish. Port Morant was historically one of the main shipping ports of eastern St. Thomas, with a natural harbour that served the sugar estates of the **Plantain Garden River valley**.

## History
**English rule (1655) and the founding parish.** Under English rule from **1655**, St. Thomas was one of Jamaica's original parishes — originally **"St. Thomas-in-the-East"** with the first rector appointed in **1666**.

**Sugar economy and slavery.** Port Morant developed as the principal port of eastern St. Thomas, shipping the sugar produced in the Plantain Garden River valley. Great estates — **Stokes Hall, Golden Grove, Albion, Holland** — dominated the landscape, worked by enslaved Africans throughout the colonial period. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds. **Captain Bligh** landed at Port Morant with **breadfruit plants** brought from **Tahiti in 1793**, following his second voyage after the famous Mutiny on the Bounty. The breadfruit was introduced to Jamaica as a food source for the enslaved population.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union** to "wipe non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb** (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**The Morant Bay Rebellion (11 October 1865).** On **11 October 1865**, **Paul Bogle** — a small farmer and Native Baptist deacon of **Stony Gut** — led a protest march to the Morant Bay courthouse. Governor **Edward John Eyre's** brutal response: **439 executed**, **600+ flogged**, **1,000+ homes burned**. Bogle was hanged on **24 October 1865**; **George William Gordon** was hanged on **23 October 1865**. **Both were declared National Heroes in 1965**.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall.** Jamaica became a **Crown Colony in 1866**. Law 20 of 1867 absorbed **St. David** into St. Thomas. The Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

**Foundation of St. Barnabas'.** The Anglican Diocese established St. Barnabas' to serve the Port Morant community — dedicated to the apostle celebrated for his encouragement of others and his generosity to the early church. The church has served the port community and its surrounding districts through the transitions from plantation agriculture to the post-colonial economy.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** The banana trade transformed the eastern St. Thomas economy from the late 19th century. Internal self-government came in **1944**. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** fulfilled the aspirations for which Bogle and Gordon died. **Hurricane Ivan (2004)** made landfall near Port Morant and caused extraordinary destruction across eastern St. Thomas.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Port Morant's position makes it one of the most exposed communities to hurricanes; the congregation experienced direct storm impact.

## Architecture
St. Barnabas' is a coastal church on the south-east corner of Jamaica. Port Morant's position makes it one of the most exposed communities to hurricanes approaching from the east — Hurricane Ivan (2004) made landfall near this part of the coast and caused catastrophic damage in the area.

## Clergy
Port Morant has been served within the **St. Thomas deanery**. As a coastal port community with a longer settlement history than many interior villages, it has maintained an active Anglican congregation through the fishing and farming community.

## Notable Facts
**Port Morant** is notable in Jamaican history as the location where **Captain Bligh** landed with breadfruit plants brought from Tahiti in **1793**, following his second voyage after the famous Mutiny on the Bounty. Port Morant's natural harbour and its position at the eastern extremity of St. Thomas made it a significant port for the sugar economy. The dedication to **St. Barnabas** — the apostle whom Acts 4:36 describes as "Son of Encouragement," missionary companion of Paul, with a feast day on **11 June** — is particularly fitting for a port community of arrivals and departures.
