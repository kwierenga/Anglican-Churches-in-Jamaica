# St. Stephen's, Maryland
**St. Andrew** · Church · active

St. Stephen's Anglican Church serves Maryland, a community in northern St. Andrew in the hill country above the suburban fringe of the city. Maryland is a small residential and agricultural district in the elevated terrain of the parish, and St. Stephen's has provided Anglican ministry here for several generations. The dedication to St. Stephen the Deacon, the first Christian martyr, is found in several Jamaican Anglican churches.

## History
Maryland sits in the northern hills of St. Andrew, at a position that gives it a character shaped more by hill farming and rural living than by the urban economy of the city below. The Anglican Diocese established St. Stephen's to serve this community, providing a worship point in the northern hill country beyond the suburban fringe.

## Architecture
St. Stephen's is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural St. Andrew in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. Set in the rural interior of St. Andrew. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
Maryland has been served within the northern St. Andrew deanery circuit under shared clergy arrangements.

## Notable Facts
The name Maryland in this St. Andrew context is one of several North American colonial place names found in Jamaica, reflecting the cultural connections between the British colonies in the Caribbean and mainland North America. Maryland state and Maryland Jamaica share a colonial-era naming tradition that testifies to the interconnected world of the 18th-century British Empire.
