# St. Mary's, Southfield
**St. Elizabeth** · Church · active

St. Mary's Anglican Church serves Southfield, a hill-country market community on the Santa Cruz Mountain plateau in southern St. Elizabeth, on the road between Junction and the south coast. Southfield "cemented itself as a vegetable producing area" during the nineteenth century, and recorded a production of **12 million pounds of tomatoes** in 1949–50 (NLJ).

## History
**Founding of the parish (c.1664).** St. Elizabeth was one of the original Jamaican parishes, established under Sir Thomas Modyford and named in honour of **Lady Elizabeth Modyford**, wife of the first Governor of Jamaica (1664–71). Prior to **1703**, the parish stretched across the territory of present-day Westmoreland, Hanover, and Manchester; in 1703 Westmoreland was created from the western portion, and in **1814** the final subdivision created Manchester from the southern section. **Lacovia** was the original parish capital before Black River became the sole capital. The parish church, **St. John the Evangelist** at Black River, dates structurally from around 1700.

**Slavery and the sugar economy.** Under English rule, sugar and slavery defined the parish economy: prominent estates included Holland, Vineyard, and Fullerswood. **Cudjoe**, leader of the Leeward Maroons, signed the **Peace Treaty of 1739** at the Peace Cave in St. Elizabeth's Cockpit Country, securing Maroon land rights — and the Maroon community of **Accompong Town** still exists in the parish today. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**The Sam Sharpe Rebellion (December 1831).** The Christmas Rebellion of 1831, led by the Baptist deacon **Samuel Sharpe** at the Kensington Estate in St. James on **27 December 1831**, spread through the western parishes; some 60,000 enslaved people were involved. Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation and post-1838 settlement.** The British Parliament passed the Slavery Abolition Act in 1833; Emancipation came in 1834, and full freedom followed at the end of the apprenticeship period on **1 August 1838**. Freed people settled the upland districts of southern St. Elizabeth as small farmers — Southfield belongs to that wave of post-Emancipation free settlement. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865, Crown Colony rule replaced the planter Assembly (1866); the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of St. Mary's: Nuttall's expansion.** Bishop Enos Nuttall's tenure as Bishop of Jamaica (1880–1916) saw the Diocese grow from 26 rural mission stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. The church does not appear in the 1891 Handbook of Jamaica's Anglican clergy returns, consistent with a foundation date in the late phase of Nuttall's expansion.

**Twentieth century: vegetables, bauxite, Independence.** During the nineteenth and twentieth centuries Southfield established itself as a vegetable-producing area despite the predisposition for drought; in **1949–50** the district recorded production of **12 million pounds of tomatoes**, an exceptional output for a single rural community (NLJ). The bauxite industry developed in St. Elizabeth from **1943**, with **ALPART** later headquartered at Nain. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Mary's a settled rural mission.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. St. Elizabeth was severely affected, with major Anglican losses including St. Luke's Balaclava and St. Thomas' Lacovia. The specific status of St. Mary's Southfield after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. Mary's is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for mission stations across the St. Elizabeth uplands. The Santa Cruz Mountain plateau position creates wind exposure during storm passages.

## Clergy
St. Mary's Southfield is served within the Montego Bay Region's St. Elizabeth deanery network.

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** **St. Mary the Virgin** — mother of Jesus, commemorated on multiple Anglican feast days including the Annunciation (**25 March**) and the "Falling Asleep" / Assumption (**15 August**) — is one of the most common Anglican dedications. (St. Mary the Virgin is the namesake of Jamaica's neighbouring St. Mary parish.)

**Tomato production.** Southfield's 1949–50 output of 12 million pounds of tomatoes represents one of the most striking examples of post-Emancipation peasant agricultural success in Jamaica — a small farming community on a drought-prone plateau achieving substantial commercial output.
