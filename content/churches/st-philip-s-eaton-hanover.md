# St. Philip's, Eaton
**Hanover** · Church · active

St. Philip's at Eaton is a mission church of the Hanover Deanery within the Montego Bay Region of the Diocese of Jamaica and the Cayman Islands. Like St. Agnes' at Grange, it is attached to the principal churches of the deanery — St. Mary's, Lucea; Holy Trinity, Green Island; and St. Bartholomew's, Dalmally — and serves a small rural community in Hanover's interior.

## History
Hanover parish was carved out of St. James in 1723 and became one of the major sugar-producing parishes of the Jamaican north-west. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds; disestablishment that year reconstituted it as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

The 1865 and 1870 Jamaica Almanacs record the Hanover Anglican establishment as Lucea, Green Island, Newport (Island Curate), and Church Hill (missionary station). The 1891 Handbook lists the main churches as Lucea, Green Island, and St. Saviour's, with mission stations at Church Hill, Dalmally, Chester Castle, and Hopewell. Eaton is not recorded in any of these nineteenth-century lists, indicating that St. Philip's was established after 1891 — most likely during Bishop Enos Nuttall's great expansion of rural mission stations (the Diocese grew from 26 such stations in 1880 to 122 by 1911). No founding date has been verified.

The dedication is to Philip the Apostle, one of the twelve disciples, whose feast day falls on 1 May (shared with James the Less). The Philip of Acts — deacon, evangelist, and baptiser of the Ethiopian eunuch — is a separate figure whose feast day is 11 October; the Eaton dedication is ordinarily understood to refer to the Apostle.

## Architecture
St. Philip's is a modest rural parish church. No substantive architectural description is recorded in the public heritage sources reviewed.

## Clergy
As a mission church of the Hanover Deanery, St. Philip's is served under shared clergy arrangements with the principal churches of the cure.

## Notable Facts
Hurricane Melissa (28 October 2025) made landfall in Westmoreland and caused severe damage across Hanover. Across the Diocese of Jamaica and the Cayman Islands, some 50 church buildings were destroyed or significantly damaged; the most prominent confirmed loss in Hanover was the 100-year-old Ramble Anglican Church, reported by the Jamaica Gleaner as demolished. The specific status of St. Philip's after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources.
