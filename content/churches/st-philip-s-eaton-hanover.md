# St. Philip’s, Eaton
**Hanover** · Church · active

St. Philip’s Anglican Church serves the community of Eaton in the interior of Hanover, in the rolling hill country between the north coast and the south-coast agricultural districts. Eaton is a small farming community whose name reflects the English estate-naming conventions of Jamaica’s plantation era. St. Philip’s has provided Anglican worship and pastoral care to this modest inland community for several generations, functioning as a local mission chapel within the Hanover deanery and offering families in the district a place for worship, baptisms, burials, and community gathering.

## History
The Eaton district developed from the plantation landscape of interior Hanover, with the estate name surviving long after the sugar economy collapsed. The Anglican Diocese extended its mission network into Hanover’s interior communities in the post-Emancipation period, establishing mission chapels to serve freed families who settled away from the coastal towns. St. Philip’s was founded as part of this outreach, dedicated to the apostle Philip, and has served the Eaton community across the generations since.

## Architecture
St. Philip’s is a small masonry chapel of simple construction characteristic of Anglican mission buildings erected in Hanover’s rural interior. The building is set within a modest churchyard. Its inland hill-country position gives it some natural shelter from direct coastal storm surge, but the rolling terrain of interior Hanover is not immune to wind damage and localised flooding during major hurricanes, particularly as storm systems channel and intensify in narrow valleys.

## Clergy
Eaton has been served within the Hanover Anglican circuit under shared clergy arrangements with other interior congregations. As a small mission chapel, the church’s week-to-week life depends heavily on dedicated lay leaders — wardens, Mothers’ Union members, and lay readers — who maintain worship and community pastoral care. The church is confirmed active in diocesan records through its continued use for major life events in the Eaton community.

## Notable Facts
Interior Hanover, while less exposed to coastal storm surge than the shoreline communities, sits in a landscape of hilly terrain where hurricanes produce damaging winds and can trigger landslides on steeper slopes. The valleys channel and concentrate wind, sometimes producing locally severe conditions even when the storm centre passes some distance away. Hurricane Gilbert (1988) caused widespread damage to interior Hanover communities. Hurricane Ivan (2004) again affected the interior with high winds. Hurricane Melissa (October 2025) made landfall in western Jamaica and the interior communities, including Eaton, experienced damaging winds and heavy rain in the storm’s aftermath.
