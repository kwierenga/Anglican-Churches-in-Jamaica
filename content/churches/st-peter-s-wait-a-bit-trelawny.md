# St. Peter's, Wait-a-Bit
**Trelawny** · Church · active

St. Peter's Anglican Church serves Wait-a-Bit, a community in the southern interior of Trelawny at the edge of Cockpit Country. In the 1891 Handbook of Jamaica, Wait-a-Bit is recorded as a mission station with 100 registered members — one of the larger Anglican missions in the parish at that date. Today the church is part of the Albert Town cure, alongside St. Silas' Troy, St. Barnabas' Warsop, and the Albert Town rectory.

## History
Trelawny was created as a parish in 1770 out of St. James. The parish's interior was Cockpit Country — the Leeward Maroon heartland and a landscape of escape and resistance through the Maroon wars. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church. After Emancipation (1834) and full freedom on 1 August 1838, freed people settled across the Cockpit Country fringes, and the Anglican Diocese extended its network into the upland communities.

The 1891 Handbook of Jamaica lists Wait-a-Bit as a mission station with 100 registered members, 26 baptisms, and 1 marriage for the year — figures comparable to Albert Town (208) and Swanswick (210). No clergy name is given for the mission station itself; it was served by the incumbents of the principal Trelawny cures.

## Architecture
St. Peter's is a modest masonry chapel of the kind built by the Anglican Diocese across rural Trelawny in the later nineteenth century. No substantive architectural record has been identified in the public heritage sources reviewed.

## Clergy
Wait-a-Bit has historically been served within the Albert Town cure, which also covers Troy, Warsop, and the Albert Town rectory. This grouping reflects the dispersed Cockpit Country–fringe geography of Trelawny's upland Anglican network.

## Hurricane Melissa (October 2025)
Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on 28 October 2025. Trelawny was among the five most affected parishes. The specific status of St. Peter's Wait-a-Bit after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Notable Facts
Wait-a-Bit is among the most evocative community names in Jamaica. The name is believed to derive from the wait-a-bit thorn — a thorny interior shrub that catches travellers on their clothing, compelling them to stop and disentangle. It belongs to a small tradition of expressive Jamaican place names (Come See, Turn Back, Lookout, Rest and Be Thankful, and others) that describe a traveller's experience of the landscape. St. Peter's dedication — to the keeper of the keys of heaven, feast day 29 June — gives the rural chapel a Petrine emphasis fitting for a community whose very name is about being arrested in your tracks.
