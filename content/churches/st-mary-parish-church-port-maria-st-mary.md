# St. Mary (Parish Church), Port Maria
**St. Mary** · Parish Church · active

St. Mary Parish Church is the principal Anglican church of Port Maria, the capital town of St. Mary parish, situated on a beachfront site at the entrance to the town. The church occupies one of the most visually striking positions of any Anglican parish church in Jamaica, with the Caribbean Sea immediately behind it and the main coastal road in front. As the parish church of St. Mary, it is the oldest and most historically significant Anglican institution in the parish, having served the town’s community across more than two centuries of Jamaican history.

## History
Port Maria was established as the administrative and commercial capital of St. Mary parish during the British colonial period, and the Anglican church was built to serve the planter elite, merchants, and the broader population of the parish capital. The current building dates to the early 19th century, constructed using brick and stone in a manner characteristic of the period. The church served the full spectrum of St. Mary’s population through the plantation era, the Emancipation period, and into the 20th century, accumulating the parish’s records of baptisms, marriages, and burials across generations.

## Architecture
The church is built primarily of brick with stone details, in a style consistent with early 19th-century Jamaican Anglican architecture. Reviewers note that it retains most or all of its original brickwork, giving it an authentic historical character. Its beachfront site on the coastline of Port Maria harbour means the building is directly exposed to the Caribbean Sea, making it vulnerable to storm surge during hurricanes and to the long-term effects of salt air on the fabric of the building.

## Clergy
As the parish church of St. Mary, Port Maria has been the seat of the senior clergy of the St. Mary deanery within the Anglican Diocese of Jamaica. The church has had a long succession of rectors who also served as the senior Anglican presence across the wider parish, overseeing the network of mission chapels throughout St. Mary. The church remains active, with regular worship and pastoral services continuing into the present.

## Notable Facts
The church’s beachfront location is one of the most distinctive of any Anglican parish church in Jamaica — reviewers confirm it ‘actually sits on a beach front property’ with the Caribbean Sea immediately behind it. Port Maria harbour has exposed the church to direct hurricane impacts across its history. Hurricane Gilbert (1988) struck the north coast of St. Mary with severe force. Hurricane Melissa (October 2025) tracked along Jamaica’s north coast; Port Maria was directly in the storm’s path and the parish church’s beachfront position made it particularly vulnerable to surge damage.
