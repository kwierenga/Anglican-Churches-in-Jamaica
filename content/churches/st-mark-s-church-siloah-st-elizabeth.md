# St. Mark's Church, Siloah
**St. Elizabeth** · Church · active

St. Mark's Anglican Church serves Siloah, a community in the interior of St. Elizabeth parish. Siloah has a biblical name — Siloam was the pool in Jerusalem where Jesus healed a blind man in the Gospel of John — and is one of several spiritually named communities in St. Elizabeth. St. Mark's and St. Barnabas' are both recorded at Siloah, providing Anglican coverage for different parts of this interior community.

## History
Siloah sits in the interior of St. Elizabeth, in the farming landscape of the parish. The biblical name reflects the post-Emancipation tradition of naming communities with spiritually significant names, particularly names associated with healing and restoration. The Anglican Diocese established St. Mark's alongside St. Barnabas' to serve the Siloah community.

## Architecture
St. Mark's Church is a masonry church of modest proportions, representative of Anglican rural church building in St. Elizabeth. The structure balances practicality with the dignity appropriate to a place of worship, featuring a straightforward nave with local stone or concrete block construction typical of Jamaica's interior church-building tradition. Set in the rural interior of St. Elizabeth. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
Siloah has been served within the St. Elizabeth deanery. St. Mark's and St. Barnabas' together serve this interior community.

## Notable Facts
The Pool of Siloam in Jerusalem was the site of one of Jesus' most detailed healing miracles — he mixed mud with saliva, applied it to a blind man's eyes, and instructed him to wash in the Pool of Siloam, restoring his sight. The community of Siloah in St. Elizabeth carries this association with healing and transformation. The name is spelled variously as Siloah, Siloam, and Siloa across different sources.
