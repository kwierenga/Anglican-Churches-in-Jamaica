# St. Mark's Church, Siloah
**St. Elizabeth** · Church · active

St. Mark's Anglican Church serves Siloah, a community in the interior of St. Elizabeth parish. **Siloah has a biblical name** — Siloam was the pool in Jerusalem where Jesus healed a blind man (John 9) — and is one of several spiritually named communities in the parish. **St. Mark's and St. Barnabas'** are both recorded at Siloah, providing Anglican coverage for different parts of this interior community. The 1891 Handbook of Jamaica records "Siloah" with Rev. **A. A. Hedman**, **688 registered members**, 53 baptisms, and 21 marriages — making the wider Siloah Anglican presence one of the larger congregations in the parish.

## History
**Founding of the parish (c.1664).** St. Elizabeth was one of the original Jamaican parishes, established under Sir Thomas Modyford and named in honour of **Lady Elizabeth Modyford**, wife of the first Governor of Jamaica (1664–71). Prior to **1703**, the parish stretched across the territory of present-day Westmoreland, Hanover, and Manchester; in 1703 Westmoreland was created from the western portion, and in **1814** the final subdivision created Manchester from the southern section. **Lacovia** was the original parish capital before Black River became the sole capital. The parish church, **St. John the Evangelist** at Black River, dates structurally from around 1700.

**Slavery and the sugar economy.** Under English rule, sugar and slavery defined the parish economy: prominent estates included Holland, Vineyard, and Fullerswood. **Cudjoe**, leader of the Leeward Maroons, signed the **Peace Treaty of 1739** at the Peace Cave in St. Elizabeth's Cockpit Country, securing Maroon land rights — and the Maroon community of **Accompong Town** still exists in the parish today. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**The Sam Sharpe Rebellion (December 1831).** The Christmas Rebellion of 1831, led by the Baptist deacon **Samuel Sharpe** at the Kensington Estate in St. James on **27 December 1831**, spread through the western parishes; some 60,000 enslaved people were involved. Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation and post-1838 settlement.** The British Parliament passed the Slavery Abolition Act in 1833; Emancipation came in 1834, and full freedom followed at the end of the apprenticeship period on **1 August 1838**. The biblical name **Siloah** reflects the post-Emancipation tradition of giving spiritually significant names to communities established by freed people, particularly names associated with healing and restoration. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865, Crown Colony rule replaced the planter Assembly (1866); the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of St. Mark's: the 1891 and 1900 Handbook record.** The 1891 Handbook records **Siloah** under Rev. **A. A. Hedman** with **688 members, 53 baptisms, and 21 marriages** — the third-largest Anglican congregation recorded for St. Elizabeth that year (after Black River 852 and St. Mary 1,055). By the 1900 Handbook, the cure had been formally constituted as **"Lacovia & Siloah"** under Rev. **B. A. S. MacCalla**. St. Mark's at Siloah belongs to that wider Anglican presence, alongside the older St. Barnabas' Siloah.

**Twentieth century, Independence, hurricanes.** The 1893–94 logwood export boom shaped the parish economy — Siloah is described in the parish history as "St. Bess's sugar town" given its long-standing relationship with the Appleton estate and the sugar economy of the Black River valley. The bauxite industry developed in St. Elizabeth from **1943**, with **ALPART** later headquartered at Nain. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Mark's a settled mission within the wider Lacovia–Siloah Anglican circuit.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. St. Elizabeth was severely affected, with major Anglican losses including St. Luke's Balaclava and St. Thomas' Lacovia (the Lacovia–Siloah cure's mother church). The specific status of St. Mark's Siloah after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. Mark's Church is a masonry church of modest proportions, representative of Anglican rural church building in St. Elizabeth. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal storm surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy

- Today — served within the Montego Bay Region's St. Elizabeth deanery network.

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** **Mark the Evangelist** — author of the second Gospel, traditionally associated with Alexandria, with a feast day on **25 April** — is shared with the Manchester Parish Church (St. Mark's Mandeville, consecrated 1820) and other Jamaican Anglican churches.

**The Pool of Siloam.** The Pool of Siloam in Jerusalem (John 9:7–11) was the site of one of Jesus' most detailed healing miracles — he mixed mud with saliva, applied it to a blind man's eyes, and instructed him to wash in the Pool of Siloam, restoring his sight. The community of Siloah in St. Elizabeth carries this association with healing and transformation. The name is spelled variously as Siloah, Siloam, and Siloa across different sources.
