# St. Silas', Troy
**Trelawny** · Church · active

St. Silas' Anglican Church serves Troy, a community in the southern interior of Trelawny at the edge of Cockpit Country. St. Silas' was a principal Anglican cure in Trelawny by 1891 — the *Handbook of Jamaica* records **Rev. E. B. Key** as clergyman at St. Silas with **56 registered members, 19 baptisms, and 8 marriages** that year — and by the 1900 Handbook the cure had passed to **Rev. F. H. Perkins**. Today the church is part of the Albert Town cure covering Troy, Wait-a-Bit, Warsop, and the Albert Town rectory.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1770).** Trelawny was created in **1770** out of St. James, in response to settler petitions dating from 1733 that the parent parish was too large. The new parish was named after Governor **Sir William Trelawny**; its first capital was **Martha Brae**, replaced around **1790** by **Falmouth** (renamed for the governor's birthplace, Falmouth in Cornwall, England).

**Slavery, sugar, and the Maroons.** Trelawny became, in time, the parish with **the most sugar estates in Jamaica** — at its peak there were as many as 100 estates with over 40 sugar factories. The interior of the parish was **Cockpit Country**, the **Leeward Maroon heartland**: Cudjoe's Town (later Trelawny Town in St. James) was the principal Maroon settlement, protected by the **1739 Peace Treaty** that ended the First Maroon War. The **Second Maroon War of 1795–96** ended with the deportation of some 568 Trelawny Town Maroons to Nova Scotia, and their onward transport to Sierra Leone in 1800. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**The Sam Sharpe Rebellion (December 1831).** The Christmas Rebellion of 1831, led by the Baptist deacon **Samuel Sharpe** at the Kensington Estate in St. James on **27 December 1831**, spread directly into Trelawny. **Rev. William Knibb**, the Baptist abolitionist who ministered at Falmouth, played a major role in the campaign that secured the accelerated end of the apprenticeship in 1838. Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation, 1865, disestablishment.** The British Parliament passed the Slavery Abolition Act in 1833; Emancipation came in 1834, and full freedom followed at the end of the apprenticeship period on **1 August 1838**. After Emancipation, freed communities settled across the Cockpit Country edge, and the Anglican Diocese extended its network into these upland districts. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865, Crown Colony rule replaced the planter Assembly (1866); the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**1891 Handbook record.** The 1891 Handbook of Jamaica lists St. Silas as one of the six principal Anglican cures of Trelawny — alongside Falmouth, Swanswick (Clark's Town), Rio Bueno, Stewart Town, and Gibraltar — under **Rev. E. B. Key**. Its 56 registered members made it the smallest of the main cures but established enough to have its own resident clergyman. By the **1900 Handbook**, **Rev. F. H. Perkins** had succeeded Key as rector at St. Silas.

**Twentieth century, sugar decline, Independence.** By **1927** the Trelawny sugar estates had declined from a peak of 100 to just 16. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Silas' a settled cure mission within the Albert Town circuit.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. **Trelawny was among the five most affected parishes**. The specific status of St. Silas' Troy after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. Silas' is a modest masonry chapel of the kind built by the Anglican Diocese across rural Trelawny in the later nineteenth century. No substantive architectural record has been identified in the public heritage sources reviewed.

## Clergy

- Today — part of the Albert Town cure within the Trelawny Deanery of the Montego Bay Region.

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** **Silas** (also called Silvanus) — Paul's companion on his second missionary journey, whose most famous moment is singing hymns in a Philippian jail at midnight after an earthquake shook the prison doors open (Acts 16) — is one of the rarer Anglican dedications in Jamaica.

**Troy's classical name.** Troy is a classical naming choice — the ancient city of Troy from Homer's *Iliad* — reflecting the classical education of colonial proprietors. It belongs to a small Jamaican tradition of estate names drawn from antiquity (Adelphi, Albion, Bellas Gate, Hermitage).
