# St. Silas', Troy
**Trelawny** · Church · active

St. Silas' Anglican Church serves Troy, a community in the southern interior of Trelawny at the edge of Cockpit Country. St. Silas' was a principal Anglican cure in Trelawny by 1891 — the Handbook of Jamaica records Rev. E. B. Key as clergyman at St. Silas with 56 registered members, 19 baptisms, and 8 marriages that year — and today the church is part of the Albert Town cure covering Troy, Wait-a-Bit, Warsop, and the Albert Town rectory.

## History
Trelawny was created as a parish in 1770 out of St. James. Its interior was Cockpit Country — the Leeward Maroon heartland. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church. After Emancipation (1834) and full freedom on 1 August 1838, freed communities settled across the Cockpit Country edge, and the Anglican Diocese extended its network into these upland districts.

The 1891 Handbook of Jamaica lists St. Silas as one of the six principal Anglican cures of Trelawny — alongside Falmouth, Swanswick (Clark's Town), Rio Bueno, Stewart Town, and Gibraltar — under Rev. E. B. Key. Its 56 registered members made it the smallest of the main cures but established enough to have its own resident clergyman.

## Architecture
St. Silas' is a modest masonry chapel of the kind built by the Anglican Diocese across rural Trelawny in the later nineteenth century. No substantive architectural record has been identified in the public heritage sources reviewed.

## Clergy
In 1891, the Rev. E. B. Key served St. Silas' as its clergyman. The church is today part of the Albert Town cure within the Trelawny Deanery of the Montego Bay Region.

## Hurricane Melissa (October 2025)
Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on 28 October 2025. Trelawny was among the five most affected parishes. The specific status of St. Silas' Troy after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Notable Facts
Troy is a classical naming choice — the ancient city of Troy from Homer's *Iliad* — reflecting the classical education of colonial proprietors. The dedication to Silas (also called Silvanus) — Paul's companion on his second missionary journey, whose most famous moment is singing hymns in a Philippian jail at midnight after an earthquake shook the prison doors open (Acts 16) — is one of the rarer Anglican dedications in Jamaica.
