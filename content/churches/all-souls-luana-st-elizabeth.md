# All Souls, Luana
**St. Elizabeth** · Church · active

All Souls Anglican Church serves the rural communities of Brompton and Luana, located inland from Black River on St. Elizabeth's south coast. Established to provide local Anglican worship separate from Black River's main parish church, it grew alongside the area's historic estates and farming settlements. The churchyard's tombstones document generations of local families, from estate workers to community leaders. In recent years, the building has expanded its role to include community shelter and meeting space, demonstrating its ongoing significance beyond Sunday worship.

## History
All Souls was established to serve the growing population around Brompton and Luana, an area historically shaped by sugar estates and later small farming settlements along the south coast road between Black River and Middle Quarters. Parish historians note Brompton had a recognized Anglican church dedicated to All Souls by the 19th century, distinct from Black River's main parish church. Over time, the congregation drew worshippers from Luana housing schemes, rural lanes, and workers connected to the Luana wetlands and surrounding agricultural lands. Church registers and memorial inscriptions trace local family names linked to the area's properties, coastal trade, and inland estates across generations.

## Architecture
All Souls follows the familiar pattern of rural Jamaican Anglican churches, featuring a simple rectangular nave, small chancel, and low tower or bell cote marking the entrance. Its masonry walls, pitched roof, and regularly spaced pointed or segment-headed windows create a quietly Gothic character typical of 19th and early 20th-century Anglican chapels in the parish. The interior maintains an intimate scale with plain timber pews, central aisle, and small sanctuary with altar and rail, well-suited to its village congregation. The surrounding churchyard, with its mix of older and recent graves, serves as an outdoor archive of the Brompton and Luana community.

## Clergy
Historically, All Souls has been served by clergy from the wider Anglican network of St. Elizabeth, with rectors and curates responsible for multiple churches in the Black River region. Parish records indicate that clergy serving at St. John's in Black River and other south coast churches also conducted services and pastoral visits at All Souls. Lay leaders from the Brompton and Luana area have played crucial roles in sustaining regular worship, Sunday school, and outreach, particularly during periods without a resident priest. In the 21st century, clergy and laity have collaborated to use the church not only for liturgy but also for social support and emergency shelter during community hardships.

## Notable Facts
Parish histories recognize All Souls as one of the significant Anglican outposts that extended church life into St. Elizabeth's interior districts beyond the coastal town. The churchyard contains headstones spanning from the late 19th through the 20th centuries, featuring names associated with Brompton, Luana, and nearby estates, making it a valuable resource for genealogical research. Recent news coverage highlights how this church, like others named All Souls in the parish, has served as an emergency shelter during crises, illustrating its continued role as both place of worship and community refuge in times of need.
