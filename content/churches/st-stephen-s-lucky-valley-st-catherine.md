# St. Stephen's, Lucky Valley
**St. Catherine** · Mission · unknown

St. Stephen's Anglican Church serves Lucky Valley, a community in the high interior of St. Catherine parish near Woodside and the western hills. Lucky Valley is a farming community in elevated terrain, and the church has served this community for several generations. The aspiration embedded in the community name — Lucky Valley — is characteristic of the hopeful naming of post-Emancipation settlements in Jamaica's interior.

## History
Lucky Valley sits in the high interior of St. Catherine, in the hill country of the western part of the parish. The name Lucky Valley is characteristic of the post-Emancipation naming tradition in which freed people gave their new communities aspirational names expressing their hopes for prosperity and security. The Anglican Diocese established St. Stephen's to serve this community.

## Architecture
St. Stephen's is a masonry church of modest proportions, representative of Anglican rural church building in St. Catherine. The structure balances practicality with the dignity appropriate to a place of worship, featuring a straightforward nave with local stone or concrete block construction typical of Jamaica's interior church-building tradition. Set in the hilly interior of St. Catherine. The elevated terrain channels wind during storm passages and makes good drainage essential to the building's longevity.

## Clergy
Lucky Valley has been served within the St. Catherine deanery under shared clergy arrangements.

## Notable Facts
Lucky Valley's name is part of a tradition of aspirational Jamaican community names — alongside names like Friendship, Fellowship, Harmony, and Prosperity — that reflect the hopes of freed people who established new communities in the post-Emancipation period. These names stand in contrast to the colonial estate names that dominate the coastal plain, and represent a distinct naming culture rooted in the agency of freed communities.
