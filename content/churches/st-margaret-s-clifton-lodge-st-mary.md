# St. Margaret's, Clifton Lodge
**St. Mary** · Church · active

St. Margaret's Anglican Church at **Clifton Lodge** serves a community in the interior of St. Mary parish, one of two churches with this dedication in the parish (the other being St. Margaret's Long Road). St. Margaret's is identified as one of **Holy Trinity Retreat's four daughter chapels** alongside St. Matthew's Boscobel, St. John's Gayle, and St. Andrew's Labyrinth.

## History
**Pre-English era and the Battle of Rio Nuevo (1658).** **Christopher Columbus** sighted Jamaica in 1494 on his second voyage. The **Battle of Rio Nuevo (June 1658)** saw British forces under **Sir Edward D'Oyley** decisively defeat a Spanish expedition attempting to recapture Jamaica — effectively ending Spanish resistance to British rule.

**English rule (1655) and the planter church.** Under English rule from **1655**, St. Mary became a major sugar-producing parish. Clifton Lodge developed as a small interior community of St. Mary, its lodge-associated name reflecting the colonial estate landscape of the parish. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**Tacky's Revolt (1760).** On Easter Monday, **7 April 1760**, **Tacky's Revolt** broke out on **Frontier and Trinity estates** near Port Maria. Tacky — a **Fante royal** from the Gold Coast of West Africa — led a rising of self-emancipated **Coromantee** people intended to overthrow British colonial rule. Over **500 Black men and women** were killed in battle, executed, or took their own lives — the most significant slave rebellion in the British West Indies between the 1733 St. John insurrection and the 1791 Haitian Revolution.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union** to "wipe non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb** (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**Foundation: a daughter chapel of Holy Trinity Retreat.** **Holy Trinity at Retreat** — built around **1835** on land donated in 1828 by the planter **Henry Rigg** — is the oldest-standing Anglican church in St. Mary. The Anglican Diocese established St. Margaret's at Clifton Lodge as one of Holy Trinity's four daughter chapels, serving the post-Emancipation farming communities of this part of the parish interior.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** The **banana trade** transformed the St. Mary economy in the late 19th and 20th centuries. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Margaret's a settled daughter chapel.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Hurricane Melissa brought heavy rain and wind to interior St. Mary; the Clifton Lodge community and congregation were in the affected zone.

## Architecture
St. Margaret's Clifton Lodge is a small masonry chapel in the interior hills of St. Mary. The building sits in the hilly terrain characteristic of this part of the parish, subject to heavy rainfall and the landslide and road-closure risks that accompany major storm events in St. Mary's interior.

## Clergy
Clifton Lodge has been served within the **St. Mary deanery** under shared clergy arrangements as a daughter chapel of Holy Trinity Retreat. Lay leaders sustain the church's regular pastoral life between clerical visits.

## Notable Facts
The two **St. Margaret's** churches in St. Mary — at Long Road and Clifton Lodge — reflect the Diocese's use of repeated dedications across a parish network. Both serve small interior farming communities in different parts of the parish hill country. The dedication is most commonly to **St. Margaret of Antioch** (feast day **20 July**) — one of the most popular medieval saints — though some Anglican dedications honour **St. Margaret of Scotland** (feast day **16 November**), an 11th-century Scottish queen and patroness of education.
