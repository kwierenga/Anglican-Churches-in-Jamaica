# St. Paul's Church, Mile Gully
**Manchester** · Church · active

St. Paul's Anglican Church serves Mile Gully, a community in the interior of Manchester parish near the famous St. George's Church ruins — known locally as 'Duppy Church.' Mile Gully is a community in the hills of Manchester, and St. Paul's provides active Anglican ministry alongside the historic ruined church that stands as a landmark in the area.

## History
Mile Gully sits in the interior of Manchester, in the hill country of the parish. The proximity to the ruined St. George's Church — abandoned and roofless, with graves dating back centuries — gives Mile Gully a particular historical character. St. Paul's has served as the active Anglican worship point in the community, continuing the tradition of Christian worship in this area long after the old St. George's fell into disuse.

## Architecture
St. Paul's Church is a masonry church of modest proportions, representative of Anglican rural church building in Manchester. The structure balances practicality with the dignity appropriate to a place of worship, featuring a straightforward nave with local stone or concrete block construction typical of Jamaica's interior church-building tradition. Set in the rural interior of Manchester. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
Mile Gully has been served within the Manchester deanery. St. Paul's serves the active congregation while the ruins of St. George's stand as a historical landmark nearby.

## Notable Facts
Mile Gully is defined in local memory by the ruined St. George's Church — 'Duppy Church' — which stands roofless and full of old graves and has accumulated legends about ghostly church services heard by passers-by at night. The legend that music and congregational sounds are heard from the ruins late at night reflects a wider Jamaican tradition of duppy (ghost) stories attached to abandoned religious buildings. St. Paul's active congregation represents the continuation of Anglican life in a community shaped by this haunted landmark.
