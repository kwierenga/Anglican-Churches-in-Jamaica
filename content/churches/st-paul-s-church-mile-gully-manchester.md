# St. Paul's Church, Mile Gully
**Manchester** · Church · active

St. Paul's Anglican Church serves **Mile Gully**, a community in the interior of Manchester parish near the famous **St. George's Anglican Church ruins** — known locally as the **"Duppy Church."** Mile Gully provides active Anglican ministry alongside the historic ruined church that stands as a landmark in the area.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1814).** Manchester was created by **Act 55 George III. c. 23** of **1814** in response to a petition of 29 November 1814 by residents of **Mile Gully**, May Day, and Carpenter's Mountain — Mile Gully thus being one of the three founding petitioner districts of the parish. The Act came into effect on **13 December 1814**, taking the "hill country" of eastern St. Elizabeth and the western portions of Vere and Clarendon. The new parish was named after **William Montagu, 5th Duke of Manchester** (Governor 1808–1827); **Mandeville**, the capital, was named after his son, **Viscount Mandeville**.

**Slavery, the parish church, and the Sam Sharpe Rebellion (1831).** Manchester's hill-country economy was built on coffee, supplemented by sugar in lower zones, both worked by enslaved African labour. **St. Mark's Anglican Church, Mandeville** — the parish church — was completed and consecrated in **1820**; its organ loft was used as a jail during the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**. Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation, 1865, disestablishment.** Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Maidstone** in Manchester (1840) was among Jamaica's earliest Moravian-led free villages. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of St. Paul's: the Mile Gully cure.** Mile Gully sits in the interior of Manchester, in the hill country of the parish. The proximity to the ruined **St. George's** Church — abandoned and roofless, with graves dating back centuries (the structure is a national-monument site administered by the Jamaica National Heritage Trust) — gives Mile Gully a particular historical character. As St. George's fell into disuse, St. Paul's emerged as the active Anglican worship point in the community. The 1900 **Handbook of Jamaica** records "Mile Gully" as a cure under **Rev. E. Clarke**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

**Twentieth century: Kendal, bauxite, Independence.** The **Kendal train tragedy** of **1 September 1957** at Kendal Manchester claimed almost 200 lives — Jamaica's worst rail accident; Kendal village lies a short distance from Mile Gully along the old railway line. The **bauxite industry** transformed Manchester from 1957 onward. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Paul's a settled cure church.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of St. Paul's Mile Gully after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. Paul's Church is a masonry church of modest proportions, representative of Anglican rural church building in Manchester. The structure balances practicality with the dignity appropriate to a place of worship, featuring a straightforward nave with local stone or concrete block construction typical of Jamaica's interior church-building tradition. Set in the rural interior of Manchester. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
- **1900** — Rev. **E. Clarke** (rector at Mile Gully per the 1900 Handbook of Jamaica).
- Today — Mile Gully is served within the Manchester deanery; St. Paul's serves the active congregation while the ruins of St. George's stand as a historical landmark nearby.

## Notable Facts
Mile Gully is defined in local memory by the ruined **St. George's Church** — **'Duppy Church'** — which stands roofless and full of old graves and has accumulated legends about ghostly church services heard by passers-by at night (Jamaica Gleaner, "'Duppy Church' spooks Mile Gully residents," 31 Mar 2012). The legend that music and congregational sounds are heard from the ruins late at night reflects a wider Jamaican tradition of duppy (ghost) stories attached to abandoned religious buildings. St. Paul's active congregation represents the continuation of Anglican life in a community shaped by this haunted landmark.
