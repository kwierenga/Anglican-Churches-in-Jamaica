# St. James', Toll Gate
**Clarendon** · Church · active

St. James' Anglican Church serves Toll Gate, a community in southern Clarendon on the main road that historically ran from May Pen toward Porus and Mandeville. The church is administered as a mission of the St. Augustine's, Porus cure, alongside St. Stephen's Chantilly and (in our diocese-confirmed listing) under the Manchester Deanery rather than the Clarendon Deanery — although Toll Gate itself sits geographically in the Clarendon parish lowlands. The cure rectory is at Porus P.O., Manchester.

## History
Toll Gate takes its name directly from a colonial-era turnpike on the road network connecting central Clarendon to the Manchester highlands. Toll-gate communities of this kind grew up where travellers stopped, paid the road toll, and rested — and they often became natural focal points for surrounding farming districts. Like most of the small-mission Anglican churches of the central interior, St. James' Toll Gate was founded as part of the diocese's late-nineteenth-century rural church-planting expansion, attached to the larger St. Augustine's Porus cure that anchored Anglican ministry across the Manchester-Clarendon border country.

The dedication to St. James — "the Greater," brother of John the Evangelist and patron saint of pilgrims, with a feast day on 25 July — is found across the Anglican world; Jamaica's St. James Parish takes its name from this apostle, and several mission stations across the island bear his name.

## Architecture
St. James' is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for mission stations across the central Jamaican interior. The lowland position offers some shelter from coastal storm surge while remaining exposed to wind and rainfall during major storm passages.

## Clergy
St. James' Toll Gate is served as a mission of the St. Augustine's, Porus cure within the Mandeville Region's Manchester Deanery; the Porus circuit also covers St. Stephen's Chantilly.
