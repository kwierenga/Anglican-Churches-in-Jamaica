# St. James', Toll Gate
**Clarendon** · Church · active

St. James' Anglican Church serves **Toll Gate**, a community in southern Clarendon on the main road that historically ran from May Pen toward Porus and Mandeville. The church is administered as a mission of the **St. Augustine's, Porus** cure, alongside St. Stephen's Chantilly — making it part of the **Manchester Deanery** even though Toll Gate itself sits geographically in the Clarendon parish lowlands. The cure rectory is at Porus P.O., Manchester.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1664).** Clarendon parish was created in **1664**, nine years after the English seizure of Jamaica from Spain (**1655**), and named for **Sir Edward Hyde, 1st Earl of Clarendon** (1609–1674), Lord High Chancellor of England under Charles II. The original parish church was the **Church of the White Cross** at "The Cross" — Jamaica's third Anglican church. **St. Paul's Chapelton** was built as a chapel of ease in **1666** and became the parish church when the Cross Church was destroyed in **1815**. **Vere** was carved out as a separate sugar-plain parish in **1671** and was reabsorbed into Clarendon in **1867**; **Law 20 of 1887** designated **May Pen** as chief town.

**Slavery, Sam Sharpe, and Emancipation.** Clarendon was one of Jamaica's great sugar parishes — the fertile Rio Minho plain held the great estates of the Suttons, Pennants, Ballards, and Dawkins, and the wealth of the parish was built on the labour of enslaved Africans. One of Jamaica's earliest slave rebellions erupted at **Suttons Plantation in 1690**: some 500 enslaved people escaped to form the Maroon communities eventually led by **Cudjoe** into the Cockpit Country. During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**, a vigilante alliance that sought to "wipe the non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of the Toll Gate mission.** Toll Gate takes its name directly from a colonial-era turnpike on the road network connecting central Clarendon to the Manchester highlands. Toll-gate communities of this kind grew up where travellers stopped, paid the road toll, and rested — and they often became natural focal points for surrounding farming districts. Like most of the small-mission Anglican churches of the central interior, St. James' Toll Gate was founded as part of the diocese's late-nineteenth-century rural church-planting expansion, attached to the larger **St. Augustine's Porus** cure that anchored Anglican ministry across the Manchester-Clarendon border country. The 1900 **Handbook of Jamaica** records "Porus & Chantilly" as a cure under **Rev. W. Lund**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

**Twentieth century: bauxite, May Pen, Independence.** Chapelton lost its status as parish capital in **1938**, with administrative functions transferring to **May Pen**. **Alcoa** began bauxite operations in Clarendon in **1959**; the **Jamalco** alumina refinery at Halse Hall opened in **1972**. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. James' a settled Porus-cure mission.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of St. James' Toll Gate after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. James' is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for mission stations across the central Jamaican interior. The lowland position offers some shelter from coastal storm surge while remaining exposed to wind and rainfall during major storm passages.

## Clergy
St. James' Toll Gate is served as a mission of the St. Augustine's, Porus cure within the Mandeville Region's Manchester Deanery; the Porus circuit also covers St. Stephen's Chantilly. The 1900 Handbook recorded the Porus & Chantilly cure under **Rev. W. Lund**.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to **St. James the Greater** — apostle, brother of John the Evangelist, and patron saint of pilgrims, with a feast day on **25 July** — is found across the Anglican world; Jamaica's St. James Parish takes its name from this apostle, and several mission stations across the island bear his name.
