# St. Paul's, Keynsham
**St. Elizabeth** · Church · Active

St. Paul's Anglican Church serves Keynsham in St. Elizabeth, a community in the elevated farming landscape of the parish near the Manchester border. **Keynsham takes its name from Keynsham in Somerset, England** — a transferred place name characteristic of Jamaica's colonial-era plantation landscape, where estate proprietors often named their properties after their English home towns. The diocesan directory records this church and its parent cure within the parish's mission network. (Note: an earlier diocesan reference associated "St. Paul's, Keynsham" with the **Manchester** Deanery; the parish field of this row should be cross-checked against the current diocesan listing.)

## History
**Founding of the parish (c.1664).** St. Elizabeth was one of the original Jamaican parishes, established under Sir Thomas Modyford and named in honour of **Lady Elizabeth Modyford**, wife of the first Governor of Jamaica (1664–71). Prior to **1703**, the parish stretched across the territory of present-day Westmoreland, Hanover, and Manchester; in 1703 Westmoreland was created from the western portion, and in **1814** the final subdivision created Manchester from the southern section. **Lacovia** was the original parish capital before Black River became the sole capital. The parish church, **St. John the Evangelist** at Black River, dates structurally from around 1700.

**Slavery and the sugar economy.** Under English rule, sugar and slavery defined the parish economy: prominent estates included Holland, Vineyard, and Fullerswood. **Cudjoe**, leader of the Leeward Maroons, signed the **Peace Treaty of 1739** at the Peace Cave in St. Elizabeth's Cockpit Country, securing Maroon land rights — and the Maroon community of **Accompong Town** still exists in the parish today. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**The Sam Sharpe Rebellion (December 1831).** The Christmas Rebellion of 1831, led by the Baptist deacon **Samuel Sharpe** at the Kensington Estate in St. James on **27 December 1831**, spread through the western parishes; some 60,000 enslaved people were involved. Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation and post-1838 settlement.** The British Parliament passed the Slavery Abolition Act in 1833; Emancipation came in 1834, and full freedom followed at the end of the apprenticeship period on **1 August 1838**. Freed people settled the upland districts of southern St. Elizabeth as small farmers — Keynsham belongs to that wave of post-Emancipation free settlement on the parish's eastern boundary. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865, Crown Colony rule replaced the planter Assembly (1866); the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of St. Paul's: the 1900 Handbook record.** The 1900 Handbook of Jamaica records a substantial cure under Rev. **F. H. Perkins**: *"Keynsham, Balaclava & 6 Mission Stations"* — placing St. Paul's Keynsham as the head of a substantial cure of seven Anglican congregations across the upland St. Elizabeth–Manchester border country at the turn of the twentieth century. Bishop Enos Nuttall's tenure as Bishop of Jamaica (1880–1916) saw the Diocese grow from 26 rural mission stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

**Twentieth century, Independence, hurricanes.** Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Paul's an established cure within the upland St. Elizabeth network. The bauxite industry developed in St. Elizabeth from **1943**, with **ALPART** later headquartered at Nain.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. St. Elizabeth was severely affected, with major Anglican losses including St. Luke's Balaclava and St. Thomas' Lacovia. The specific status of St. Paul's Keynsham after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. Paul's is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for cures and mission stations across the St. Elizabeth uplands. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal storm surge while remaining exposed to wind and rainfall during major storms.

## Clergy
- **1900** — Rev. **F. H. Perkins** (rector of "Keynsham, Balaclava & 6 Mission Stations"). St. Paul's is now served within the Montego Bay Region's St. Elizabeth deanery network.

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** **St. Paul the Apostle** — author of much of the New Testament, missionary across the Mediterranean world, with feast days on **25 January** (his conversion) and **29 June** (with St. Peter) — is one of the most common Anglican dedications, and one of the more frequent choices for cure churches.

**Keynsham, Somerset.** The English Keynsham is a town between Bristol and Bath, and Jamaican Keynsham is one of many transplanted English place names in St. Elizabeth that record the eighteenth-century colonial naming practice.
