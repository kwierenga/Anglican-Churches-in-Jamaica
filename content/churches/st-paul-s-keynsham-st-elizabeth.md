# St. Paul's, Keynsham
**St. Elizabeth** · Church · unknown

St. Paul's Anglican Church serves Keynsham in St. Elizabeth, listed as a co-cure with St. Luke's Balaclava in the St. Elizabeth deanery. Keynsham is a community in the hill country of northern St. Elizabeth, near the Manchester border. The church extends Anglican coverage in this part of the parish alongside the main Balaclava cure.

## History
Keynsham sits in the hills of northern St. Elizabeth. The name derives from Keynsham in Somerset, England — a market town near Bristol known for its medieval abbey. The church is part of the Balaclava cure in the St. Elizabeth deanery.

## Architecture
St. Paul's is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural St. Elizabeth in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. Set in the rural interior of St. Elizabeth. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
The Balaclava/Keynsham cure has been listed as Vacant in recent diocesan records.

## Notable Facts
Keynsham in Somerset was historically significant as the site of a medieval Augustinian abbey, and the town sits on the River Chew between Bristol and Bath. The Jamaican Keynsham most likely takes its name from an estate owner from the Bristol/Somerset area — Bristol was a major hub of the transatlantic trade, and many Jamaican estate owners had Bristol connections.
