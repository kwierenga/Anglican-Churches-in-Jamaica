# St. Mary's, Montpelier
**St. James** · Church · active

St. Mary’s Anglican Church at Montpelier is a historic church in St. James, built in 1847 on a hill overlooking the former Montpelier sugar estate. Its site is deeply layered with history, having been part of an estate attacked during the 1831-32 Baptist War and potentially built on the foundation of a slave hospital. Today, this simple Gothic Revival stone church serves as both an active place of worship and a quiet heritage landmark, symbolizing the transition from a landscape of enslavement to one of community and faith.

## History
St. Mary’s stands on the grounds of the Montpelier estate, a major sugar plantation that was burned during the 1831-32 Baptist War—a pivotal enslaved people's uprising that accelerated the path to emancipation. Following the abolition of slavery, the Anglican Church expanded its mission work, laying the cornerstone for St. Mary's in 1847. Local tradition holds that the church was adapted from an earlier estate slave hospital, physically transforming a site of oppression into one of community and spirituality. Over generations, its churchyard has become the burial ground for local families, further weaving the church into the fabric of community memory.

## Architecture
St. Mary’s is a fine example of a simple, rural Gothic Revival chapel. Constructed from cut stone, it features a pointed-arch doorway, narrow pointed windows, and a large multi-part window at the rear. A small belfry tops the gable of the compact structure, which combines the nave and chancel into a single, continuous space. Historic photographs highlight its weathered stone and modest proportions, giving the hilltop church a poignant, rustic character that blends with its significant history.

## Clergy
St. Mary’s is part of the Montego Bay Region of the Anglican Diocese, grouped with other small country churches. Diocesan records from the mid-2010s list the Reverend Don T. Lewis as its priest, and a 2009 Gleaner report mentions the Reverend Anthony Ottey as a retired pastor, indicating long-standing pastoral ties to the community. The church remains an active parish, with recent funeral notices confirming that it continues to be a central venue for memorials and services for local families.

## Notable Facts
The church's location makes it a powerful historical symbol. The Montpelier estate was a key site of the Baptist War, directly linking the church to the struggle for freedom. The tradition of its foundation being a slave hospital, coupled with its 1847 construction date, makes St. Mary’s a profound example of a space of oppression being reclaimed for community and worship. It is recognized as a rural landmark in national heritage listings, and its cemetery serves as a valuable resource for genealogists, containing surnames common across western Jamaica.
