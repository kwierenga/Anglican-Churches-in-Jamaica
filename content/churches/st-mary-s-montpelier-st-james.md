# St. Mary's, Montpelier
**St. James** · Church · active

St. Mary's Anglican Church stands on a hill overlooking the former Montpelier sugar estate in the interior of St. James — one of the most historically significant sites in Jamaica. The cornerstone was laid in 1847, just nine years after full Emancipation, on the grounds of an estate that had been burned during the 1831 Christmas Rebellion — the largest slave uprising in the British Caribbean, which directly led to the abolition of slavery. Local tradition holds that the church was built on the foundation of the estate's slave hospital, physically transforming a site of suffering into one of worship. St. Mary's is the mother church of the Montpelier Cure, which includes St. Saviour's Chichester, All Saints' Chester Castle, St. Stephen's Cambridge, and St. Matthew's Catadupa, served by Rev. Don T. Lewis. It is recognised as a heritage landmark by the Jamaica National Heritage Trust.

## History
The Montpelier Estate — Old and New Montpelier — was owned by Lord Seaford (Charles Rose Ellis, 1st Baron Seaford), one of the most powerful absentee planters in the British Caribbean. At Emancipation in 1834, Old Montpelier held 373 enslaved people and New Montpelier 276, with Shettlewood Pen across the Great River in Hanover adding 146 more — over 1,000 enslaved people across the Seaford estates in this area alone. Lord Seaford received over £18,000 in government compensation for their freedom. The wealth of St. James was built on their labour: the parish was one of Jamaica's great sugar producers, and the Montpelier properties were among its largest operations.

On 27 December 1831, the Christmas Rebellion erupted at Kensington Estate in the hills above Montego Bay. Samuel Sharpe, a Baptist deacon, had organised enslaved people in a passive resistance strike that became an armed uprising involving up to 60,000 of Jamaica's 300,000 enslaved people. Old Montpelier was among the estates burned; a skirmish there on 28 December saw Colonel Grignon's militia outmanoeuvred by the rebels. The rebellion was eventually suppressed with brutal force — 14 whites and over 500 enslaved people died, and Sharpe was hanged on 23 May 1832. But the rebellion achieved its larger aim: it directly contributed to the passage of the Slavery Abolition Act in 1833. Sharpe is now a National Hero of Jamaica, and his image appears on the $50 note.

After full freedom on 1 August 1838, the Anglican Church expanded into the interior to serve the freed population. The cornerstone of St. Mary's was laid in 1847 on the hill overlooking the estate where the rebellion had been fought. Local tradition holds that the church incorporates the foundation of the estate's slave hospital — a powerful symbolic transformation of a site of enslaved suffering into a place of free worship. The 1891 Handbook of Jamaica records C. G. McGregor at Montpelier with 138 registered members. By 1900, McGregory served "Montpelier and 5 Mission Stations."

Jamaica's independence on 6 August 1962 marked the final chapter in the political transformation that the Christmas Rebellion had begun 131 years earlier.

## Architecture
St. Mary's is a fine example of a rural Gothic Revival chapel, constructed from cut stone on a hilltop site. It features a pointed-arch doorway, narrow pointed windows, and a large multi-part pointed window at the rear. A small belfry tops the western gable. The nave and chancel form a single continuous space. The weathered stone and modest proportions give the church a poignant character that reflects both its age and its setting on the former plantation.

## Education
The Anglican mission to educate was central to the Montpelier Cure's purpose after Emancipation. Schools were established alongside churches across the interior. Bishop Lipscomb established over 50 national schools (by 1835 he reported 142 schools with 8,500 scholars), and by 1900 the Diocese operated 263 elementary schools island-wide. The 1878 Jamaica Directory confirms a school at nearby Chichester with teacher N. Campbell, and the broader cure served communities where education for freed people was a revolutionary act.

## Clergy
Rev. Don T. Lewis serves as rector (retired) of the Montpelier Cure, assisted by Melvin Newsome and Cleve Llewellyn. The 1891 Handbook records C. G. McGregor; the 1900 Handbook lists C. G. McGregory. Rev. Anthony Ottey served as a long-standing pastor into retirement.

## Notable Facts
The site of St. Mary's — overlooking the very estate where the Christmas Rebellion was fought — makes it one of the most symbolically powerful church locations in Jamaica. The tradition that the church was built on the foundation of a slave hospital transforms a site of oppression into one of community and faith. The churchyard has become the burial ground for local families, weaving the church into the fabric of community memory across generations. St. Mary's feast day (the Blessed Virgin Mary) is 15 August (the Assumption) or 8 September (the Nativity of Mary) in the Anglican calendar.
