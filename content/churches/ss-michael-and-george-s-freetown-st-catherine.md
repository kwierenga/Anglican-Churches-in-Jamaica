# SS Michael and George's, Freetown
**St. Catherine** · Church · active

SS Michael and George's Anglican Church serves **Freetown**, a community in the rural interior of St. Catherine parish. Freetown is one of the post-Emancipation **free villages** of Jamaica's interior — communities that took the name "Freetown" or "Free Town" to celebrate the freedom granted in 1838.

## History
**Spanish Villa de la Vega (1534) and English conquest (1655).** The Spanish founded **Villa de la Vega** in 1534 — Jamaica's capital until **1872**. Penn and Venables's English expedition captured Jamaica in May **1655**.

**Founding parishes.** Under English rule from 1655, **St. Catherine** was one of Jamaica's original parishes. The neighbouring early parishes of **St. John, St. Dorothy, and St. Thomas-in-the-Vale** were merged into expanded St. Catherine under **Law 20 of 1867**.

**Slavery and the planter church.** Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds. **The Diocese of Jamaica** was established by Letters Patent on **24 July 1824**; **Christopher Lipscomb** was consecrated first Bishop in 1824.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838), and the free villages.** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. After full freedom, Baptist, Moravian, Presbyterian, and Anglican missionaries assisted the formerly enslaved in purchasing tracts of land, dividing them into parcels for sale to ex-slaves. Communities formed on these parcels — **Sligoville**, the first Jamaican free village (1835/1838); **Maidstone** in Manchester (1840, Moravian); Sutcliffe Mount; **Freetown**; and many others — became the social foundation of the post-Emancipation peasantry. **Sligoville** itself, in nearby St. Catherine, was founded with land provided by **Howe Peter Browne, 2nd Marquess of Sligo**, then Governor of Jamaica.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. Capital moved from Spanish Town to **Kingston in 1872**.

**Foundation of SS Michael and George's.** The Anglican Diocese established SS Michael and George's as a mission chapel to serve the free village of Freetown. The dual dedication is unusual in Jamaica's Anglican network: **"SS Michael and George"** combines **St. Michael the Archangel** (29 September) with **St. George the Martyr** (23 April) — a pairing not commonly found in the parish-church naming conventions of the diocese, suggesting a deliberate choice perhaps reflecting the dedication of the British **"Order of St Michael and St George"** (founded 1818) which was a recognised honour in colonial-era Jamaica. No substantive foundational date for SS Michael and George's at Freetown has been identified in the public heritage sources reviewed.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found SS Michael and George's a settled rural mission.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Hurricane Melissa brought wind and rain to interior St. Catherine.

## Architecture
SS Michael and George's is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for mission stations across the St. Catherine interior. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal storm surge while remaining exposed to wind and rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
SS Michael and George's Freetown has been served within the **Kingston Region's St. Catherine Deanery**.

## Notable Facts
The community name **Freetown** belongs to the broader Jamaican post-Emancipation tradition of free villages — settlements where formerly enslaved people purchased land and organised independent communities outside the plantation system. Sligoville in nearby St. Catherine was the most famous example. The dual dedication to **St. Michael and St. George** echoes the British **Order of St. Michael and St. George** (founded 1818), the imperial honour society for service in the Mediterranean and the colonies.
