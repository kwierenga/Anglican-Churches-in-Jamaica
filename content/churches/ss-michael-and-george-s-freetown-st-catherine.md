# SS Michael and George's, Freetown
**St. Catherine** · Church · active

SS Michael and George's Anglican Church serves Freetown, a community in the rural interior of St. Catherine parish.

## History
Freetown is one of the post-Emancipation free villages of Jamaica's interior — communities that took the name "Freetown" or "Free Town" to celebrate the freedom granted in 1838 with the end of the apprenticeship system that had followed formal Emancipation in 1834. After full freedom, Baptist, Moravian, Presbyterian, and Anglican missionaries assisted the formerly enslaved in purchasing tracts of land, dividing them into parcels for sale to ex-slaves. Communities formed on these parcels — Sligoville, the first Jamaican free village (1835); Maidstone in Manchester (1840, Moravian); Sutcliffe Mount; Freetown; and many others — became the social foundation of the post-Emancipation peasantry.

The Anglican Diocese established SS Michael and George's as a mission chapel to serve the free village of Freetown. The dual dedication is unusual in Jamaica's Anglican network: "SS Michael and George" combines St. Michael the Archangel (29 September) with St. George the Martyr (23 April) — a pairing not commonly found in the parish-church naming conventions of the diocese, suggesting a deliberate choice perhaps reflecting the dedication of the British "Order of St Michael and St George" (founded 1818) which was a recognised honour in colonial-era Jamaica. No substantive foundational date for SS Michael and George's at Freetown has been identified in the public heritage sources reviewed.

## Architecture
SS Michael and George's is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for mission stations across the St. Catherine interior. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal storm surge while remaining exposed to wind and rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
SS Michael and George's Freetown has been served within the Kingston Region's St. Catherine Deanery.
