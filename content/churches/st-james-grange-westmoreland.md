# St. James', Grange
**Westmoreland** · Church · active

St. James' Anglican Church serves the community of Grange in northern Westmoreland, close to the market town of Grange Hill. The district takes its name from the old estate nomenclature — a 'grange' being a farm or granary — common across Jamaica's colonial estate landscape. St. James' provides Anglican ministry to the farming families of the Grange community, functioning within the clergy cluster based around Grange Hill and the northern Westmoreland circuit.

## History
Grange is one of the communities of the hill country of northern Westmoreland, developed primarily in the post-Emancipation era when freed families settled the inland districts. The Anglican presence at Grange grew from the Diocese's mission outreach into the interior of the parish, part of the broader effort to serve dispersed rural communities that could not easily access the parish church at Savanna-la-Mar. St. James' has served as the local church for the Grange community across several generations, providing continuity of Anglican worship and pastoral care.

## Architecture
St. James' is a small masonry chapel of modest dimensions typical of Anglican mission churches in the northern Westmoreland hills. The building is set in a small churchyard in the Grange community, close to Grange Hill. Its construction reflects the practical needs of a small farming community rather than any architectural ambition. The hillside setting provides good natural drainage but exposes the church to hilltop winds.

## Clergy
Grange has been served within the northern Westmoreland clergy cluster, grouped with Holy Trinity Grange Hill and Transfiguration Mount Grace under shared ministry. Lay leaders sustain weekly worship and community activities, with visiting clergy conducting services, sacraments, and major pastoral occasions. The church's active status is confirmed through diocesan records and its documented use for community events.

## Notable Facts
The Grange area of northern Westmoreland lies within the foothills of the Dolphin Head range, which acts as a significant orographic barrier, causing weather systems to deposit heavy rainfall on the windward slopes. This makes the area prone to flooding and landslides during major storms. Hurricane Charlie (1951), Hurricane Gilbert (1988), and Hurricane Ivan (2004) all affected this part of the parish. Hurricane Melissa (October 2025) brought damaging winds and heavy rainfall to the northern Westmoreland hills; the Grange community and surrounding districts experienced road closures and property damage, and the congregation was directly affected.
