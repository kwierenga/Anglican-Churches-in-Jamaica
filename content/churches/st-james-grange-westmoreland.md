# St. James', Grange
**Westmoreland** · Church · active

St. James' Anglican Church serves the community of Grange in northern Westmoreland, near Grange Hill and the adjacent community of Glen Islay. The church is part of the Holy Trinity Cure, Grange Hill, under Rev. Michael Solomon. The nearby Glen Islay estate was owned in the mid-eighteenth century by John Wedderburn of Ballindean — described as the largest landowner in Jamaica with around 17,000 acres — whose purchase of an enslaved boy named Joseph Knight at Glen Islay led to the landmark Scottish court case that declared slavery incompatible with Scots law.

## History
Grange sits in the hill country of northern Westmoreland, approximately two kilometres north of the former Glen Islay sugar estate. The name "Grange" — an English word for a farmstead or granary — and "Glen Islay" — Scottish for "valley of Islay" — both reflect the planter origins of this landscape. Westmoreland was one of Jamaica's premier sugar parishes, and the estates surrounding Grange were worked by enslaved Africans throughout the colonial period.

Glen Islay's ownership history is documented in the UCL Legacies of British Slavery database. John Wedderburn of Ballindean (1729–1803), a Scot who arrived in Jamaica in 1747, owned the estate and became one of the island's largest landholders. In 1762, Wedderburn purchased an enslaved African boy aged twelve or thirteen at a Jamaican slave auction, naming him Joseph Knight. When Wedderburn returned to Scotland in 1769, he brought Knight as a personal servant. Knight later sued for his freedom in the case of *Knight v. Wedderburn* (1778), in which Scotland's Court of Session ruled 8–4 that "the state of slavery is not recognised by the laws of this kingdom." The ruling was a milestone in the legal challenge to slavery, predating Emancipation in the British Caribbean by over half a century.

By the 1820s, Glen Islay was owned by Benjamin Capon, who held 99 enslaved people and 66 head of stock. After Capon's death, the estate declined to 59 enslaved by 1833. His executors filed a compensation claim at Emancipation.

After full freedom on 1 August 1838, freed people settled across the interior of Westmoreland. The Anglican Church expanded into these communities through mission stations linked to the Grange Hill rectory, documented since at least 1869 when Rev. Henry Clarke served as rector. By the 1900 Handbook, R. E. Bennett served at Grange Hill.

## Education
The Anglican mission to educate is directly documented at Grange Hill. The Jamaica Information Service confirms that the Anglican church hall at Garden Hall served as the site of the Grange Hill Government School's inaugural day in January 1924. Sir Clifford Campbell (1892–1991), born in nearby Petersfield, served as principal of the school from 1928 to 1944 before becoming Jamaica's first Jamaican-born Governor-General (1962–1973) — serving through the independence era. Notable alumni include Justice Patrick Robinson (International Court of Justice) and the Hon. Marlene Malahoo Forte (Minister of Legal and Constitutional Affairs).

## Architecture
St. James' is a small masonry chapel of modest dimensions typical of Anglican mission churches in the northern Westmoreland hills.

## Clergy
St. James' is part of the Holy Trinity Cure, Grange Hill, which also includes Transfiguration Mount Grace and St. Luke's Cessnock. Current clergy: Rev. Michael Solomon, assisted by Rev. Ula Ruddock and Rev. Carol Haughton. Contact: The Rectory, Grange Hill P.O., tel. 918-8060.

## Notable Facts
The *Knight v. Wedderburn* case (1778) — arising from an enslaved boy purchased at Glen Islay, just two kilometres from St. James' — is one of the most significant legal challenges to slavery in British history. The Scottish court's ruling that slavery was incompatible with Scots law predated the Abolition of the Slave Trade Act (1807) by nearly thirty years and full Emancipation (1838) by sixty. That a church now stands in the landscape where Knight was enslaved embodies the transformation from bondage to freedom that defines Jamaica's history.
