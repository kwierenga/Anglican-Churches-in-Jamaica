# Church of the Resurrection, Duhaney Park
**St. Andrew** · Church · active

The Church of the Resurrection serves Duhaney Park, a large residential community in southern St. Andrew that developed as one of Kingston's major housing schemes in the mid-20th century. Duhaney Park is one of the more substantial planned residential developments in the Kingston metropolitan area, and the Church of the Resurrection was established to provide Anglican ministry to this sizeable community. The Easter dedication reflects the central Christian proclamation of Christ's rising from the dead.

## History
Duhaney Park was developed as a government housing scheme in the post-independence era, providing planned residential accommodation for Kingston's growing population. As new communities developed in the housing schemes of southern St. Andrew, the Anglican Diocese established churches to serve them. The Church of the Resurrection was planted in Duhaney Park as part of this urban expansion of the Diocese's ministry.

## Architecture
Church of the Resurrection is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural St. Andrew in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. Set in the rural interior of St. Andrew. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
Duhaney Park has been served within the southern St. Andrew deanery circuit, connected to the urban ministry network of the Kingston metropolitan area.

## Notable Facts
Duhaney Park is one of several large residential communities in southern St. Andrew developed from the 1950s onward as the Kingston metropolitan area expanded to accommodate population growth. The housing scheme communities of southern St. Andrew and Kingston represent a distinctive chapter in Jamaica's post-independence urban history, with the Anglican Diocese following population movements to establish churches in new communities as they developed.
