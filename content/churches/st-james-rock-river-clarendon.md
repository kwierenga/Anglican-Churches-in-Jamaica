# St. James', Rock River
**Clarendon** · Church · active

St. James' Anglican Church provides a second Anglican worship point in the **Rock River** area of Clarendon, alongside St. Stephen's also recorded at Rock River. The two Rock River entries are approximately 14 km apart, suggesting either a very large district sharing the Rock River name or a coordinate error in one entry. St. James' is administered as a mission of the **St. Paul's Chapelton** cure, alongside St. Mark's Beckford Kraal, All Saints' Mt. Providence, and St. Luke's Wood Hall. The cure rectory is at Chapelton P.O., Clarendon.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1664).** Clarendon parish was created in **1664**, nine years after the English seizure of Jamaica from Spain (**1655**), and named for **Sir Edward Hyde, 1st Earl of Clarendon** (1609–1674), Lord High Chancellor of England under Charles II. The original parish church was the **Church of the White Cross** at "The Cross" — Jamaica's third Anglican church. **St. Paul's Chapelton** was built as a chapel of ease in **1666** in the upland interior, and became the parish church when the Cross Church was destroyed in **1815**. **Vere** was carved out as a separate sugar-plain parish in **1671** and was reabsorbed into Clarendon in **1867**; **Law 20 of 1887** designated **May Pen** as chief town.

**Slavery, Sam Sharpe, and Emancipation.** Clarendon was one of Jamaica's great sugar parishes — the fertile Rio Minho plain held the great estates of the Suttons, Pennants, Ballards, and Dawkins, and the wealth of the parish was built on the labour of enslaved Africans. One of Jamaica's earliest slave rebellions erupted at **Suttons Plantation in 1690**: some 500 enslaved people escaped to form the Maroon communities eventually led by **Cudjoe** into the Cockpit Country. During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**, a vigilante alliance that sought to "wipe the non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of the Rock River mission.** The Rock River district of Clarendon supports two Anglican entries, suggesting that the Rock River name may apply to a broad valley or watershed district rather than a single compact community, with multiple settlements along the river's course each maintaining their own Anglican worship point. St. James' serves its particular community in the interior farming landscape of the parish, attached to the St. Paul's Chapelton cure. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**, and St. James' Rock River belongs to that broader expansion phase.

**Twentieth century: bauxite, May Pen, Independence.** Chapelton lost its status as parish capital in **1938**, with administrative functions transferring to **May Pen**. **Alcoa** began bauxite operations in Clarendon in **1959**; the **Jamalco** alumina refinery at Halse Hall opened in **1972**. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. James' a settled Chapelton-cure mission.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of St. James' Rock River after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. James' is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural Clarendon in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
St. James' Rock River is administered as a mission of the St. Paul's Chapelton cure (Clarendon Deanery), grouped with St. Mark's Beckford Kraal, All Saints' Mt. Providence, and St. Luke's Wood Hall. The cure rectory is at Chapelton P.O., Clarendon.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to **St. James the Greater** — apostle, brother of John the Evangelist, and patron saint of pilgrims (associated with the great pilgrimage route to Santiago de Compostela) — has a feast day on **25 July**.
