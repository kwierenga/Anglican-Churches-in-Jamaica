# St. Barnabas', Duncans
**Trelawny** · Church · active

St. Barnabas' Anglican Church serves the town of **Duncans** on Trelawny's north-coast road between Falmouth and the St. Ann border. Duncans is the second-largest town in Trelawny after Falmouth. The church is listed in the Diocese of Jamaica and the Cayman Islands directory within the Trelawny Deanery; it does not appear in the 1891 Handbook of Jamaica, indicating a post-1891 foundation. **Kettering** — a district of Duncans — was named after the English birthplace of the Baptist abolitionist **Rev. William Knibb** (1803–1845), giving Duncans a distinctive place in the geography of the emancipation movement.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1770).** Trelawny was created in **1770** out of St. James, in response to settler petitions dating from 1733. The new parish was named after Governor **Sir William Trelawny**; its first capital was **Martha Brae**, replaced around **1790** by **Falmouth** (renamed for the governor's birthplace, Falmouth in Cornwall, England).

**Slavery, sugar, and the Maroons.** Trelawny became the parish with **the most sugar estates in Jamaica** — at its peak there were as many as 100 estates with over 40 sugar factories. The interior was **Cockpit Country**, the Leeward Maroon heartland; the **1739 Peace Treaty** with Cudjoe ended the First Maroon War, and the **Second Maroon War of 1795–96** ended with the deportation of some 568 Trelawny Town Maroons to Nova Scotia and onward to Sierra Leone in 1800. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church.

**The Sam Sharpe Rebellion and William Knibb (1831–1838).** The Christmas Rebellion of 1831, led by the Baptist deacon **Samuel Sharpe** at the Kensington Estate in St. James on **27 December 1831**, spread directly into Trelawny. **Rev. William Knibb**, the Baptist abolitionist born in Kettering, Northamptonshire, who ministered at Falmouth, was outspoken against slavery and played a major role in the campaign that secured the accelerated end of the apprenticeship in 1838. Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832**. The **William Knibb Baptist Church** at Falmouth was built in 1844 (rebuilt 1948 after a 1944 fire); Knibb himself is buried in its churchyard.

**Emancipation, 1865, disestablishment.** The British Parliament passed the Slavery Abolition Act in 1833; Emancipation came in 1834, and full freedom followed at the end of the apprenticeship period on **1 August 1838**. After Emancipation, **Duncans** grew as a roadside service town along the north-coast corridor. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865, Crown Colony rule replaced the planter Assembly (1866); the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of St. Barnabas': Nuttall's expansion.** Bishop Enos Nuttall's tenure (1880–1916) saw the Diocese grow from 26 rural mission stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. The 1891 Handbook of Jamaica records the principal Trelawny Anglican churches as Falmouth, Swanswick (Clark's Town), Rio Bueno, Stewart Town, Gibraltar, St. Silas — with Albert Town and Wait-a-Bit as missions. Duncans is not recorded, placing its foundation after 1891 within Nuttall's expansion.

**Twentieth century, sugar decline, Independence.** By **1927** the Trelawny sugar estates had declined from a peak of 100 to just 16. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Barnabas' a settled town church.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. **Trelawny was among the five most affected parishes**, with particularly severe damage reported in Falmouth and the north-coast corridor. The specific status of St. Barnabas' Duncans after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. Barnabas' is a masonry church on the Duncans coastal plain. No detailed architectural record has been identified in the public heritage sources reviewed.

## Clergy
St. Barnabas' is served within the Trelawny Deanery under shared clergy arrangements.

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** **St. Barnabas** ("son of encouragement") was the early-Christian Cypriot Levite who introduced Saul (Paul) to the apostles after his conversion (Acts 9:27) and accompanied him on the first missionary journey. His feast day is **11 June**.

**Kettering and William Knibb.** Kettering in Duncans is named after the English birthplace of the Baptist abolitionist William Knibb — a reminder that Duncans, like much of Trelawny, was shaped by the nonconformist emancipation movement even though the parish church of the town itself is Anglican.
