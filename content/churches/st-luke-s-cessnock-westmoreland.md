# St. Luke's, Cessnock
**Westmoreland** · Church · active

St. Luke's Anglican Church at Cessnock is part of the Holy Trinity Cure, Grange Hill — one of four congregations under Rev. **Michael Solomon** alongside Holy Trinity Grange Hill (the mother church), St. James' Grange, and Transfiguration Mount Grace. The community name **Cessnock**, like Glen Islay and other toponyms in the northern Westmoreland hills, is of Scottish origin — Cessnock is a place name in Ayrshire, Scotland — reflecting the Scots planter presence in this part of the parish during the sugar era.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1703).** Westmoreland was officially formed in **1703** from the western portion of the older parish of St. Elizabeth, named for its position at the western part of the island. The parish capital, **Savanna-la-Mar**, was founded around 1730. In 1723 the new parish of Hanover was carved out of Westmoreland's northern section.

**Slavery and the sugar economy.** Under English rule, sugar and slavery accelerated in Westmoreland: an inventory in **1722** numbered the estates at **73**. The Beckford family — Richard, Peter, and William — were among the most prominent planters; modern Petersfield is named for Peter Beckford. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**The Sam Sharpe Rebellion (December 1831).** The Christmas Rebellion of 1831, led by the Baptist deacon Samuel Sharpe and ignited at the Kensington Estate in St. James on the night of 27 December 1831, spread rapidly through the western parishes — St. James, Trelawny, Hanover, and Westmoreland — with some 60,000 enslaved people involved. The Colonial Church Union's vigilante destruction of chapels included Savanna-la-Mar (Ellis, *Diocese of Jamaica*, 1913). Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on 23 May 1832 and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation and post-1838 settlement.** The British Parliament passed the Slavery Abolition Act in 1833; Emancipation came in 1834, and full freedom followed at the end of the apprenticeship period on **1 August 1838**. By 1854 the number of active sugar estates in Westmoreland had collapsed to 34, less than half the 1722 figure. Freed people settled the upland districts as small farmers; the Presbyterians established their first parish congregation at Grange Hill in **1837** — a community ministry that ran in parallel to the Anglican network.

**Foundation of the Grange Hill cure.** The rectory at Grange Hill is documented from **1869**, when Rev. **Henry Clarke** — a major figure in Westmoreland's nineteenth-century religious and economic history — served as rector. Clarke went on to found the **Westmoreland Building Society in 1874**, the first building society in Jamaica, in order to allow people across the socio-economic spectrum to access savings and loan services. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and the resulting Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica. Cessnock does not appear in the 1891 Handbook of Jamaica's Anglican returns, indicating that St. Luke's was established after 1891 — most likely during Bishop Enos Nuttall's rural mission expansion (Diocese grew from 26 rural stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**).

**1912 hurricane, 1938 Frome riots, Independence.** A November **1912** hurricane brought a tidal wave that nearly destroyed Savanna-la-Mar. The **1938 Frome labour riots** at the Frome sugar factory contributed directly to the formation of Jamaica's Trade Union Movement and the rise of Sir Alexander Bustamante. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Luke's an established mission within the Holy Trinity Grange Hill cure.

## Architecture
St. Luke's is a small rural chapel in the northern Westmoreland hills. No substantive architectural record has been identified in public heritage sources reviewed.

## Clergy
**1869** — Rev. **Henry Clarke** (rector at Grange Hill, founder of the 1874 Westmoreland Building Society). **Today** — Rev. **Michael Solomon** (Rector of the Holy Trinity Cure, Grange Hill), assisted by Rev. Ula Ruddock and Rev. Carol Haughton.

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** Luke the Evangelist — physician, traditional author of the third Gospel and the Book of Acts, with a feast day on **18 October** — falls in the calmer period after hurricane season. Luke is traditionally the patron of physicians and artists.

**Hurricane Melissa (28 October 2025)** made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The northern Westmoreland hills experienced destructive winds and heavy rain; Holy Trinity at Grange Hill (the cure's mother church) was damaged. The specific status of St. Luke's Cessnock has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.
