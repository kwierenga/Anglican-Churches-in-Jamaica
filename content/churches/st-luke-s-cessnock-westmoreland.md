# St. Luke's, Cessnock
**Westmoreland** · Church · active

St. Luke's Anglican Church at Cessnock is part of the Holy Trinity Cure, Grange Hill — one of four congregations under Rev. Michael Solomon alongside Holy Trinity Grange Hill (the mother church), St. James' Grange, and Transfiguration Mount Grace. The community name Cessnock, like Glen Islay and other toponyms in the northern Westmoreland hills, is of Scottish origin — reflecting the Scots planter presence in this part of the parish during the sugar era.

## History
Westmoreland was one of Jamaica's largest sugar parishes, its economy built on enslaved African labour until Emancipation in 1834 and full freedom on 1 August 1838. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church. The rectory at Grange Hill is documented from 1869, when Rev. Henry Clarke served as rector. Cessnock does not appear in the 1891 Handbook of Jamaica's Anglican returns — indicating that St. Luke's was established after 1891, most likely during Bishop Enos Nuttall's rural expansion of mission stations.

## Architecture
St. Luke's is a small rural chapel in the northern Westmoreland hills. No substantive architectural record has been identified in public heritage sources reviewed.

## Clergy
St. Luke's is served by the Rev. Michael Solomon as Rector of the Holy Trinity Cure, Grange Hill, assisted by Rev. Ula Ruddock and Rev. Carol Haughton.

## Hurricane Melissa (October 2025)
Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on 28 October 2025. The northern Westmoreland hills experienced destructive winds and heavy rain; Holy Trinity at Grange Hill (the cure's mother church) was damaged, and the specific status of St. Luke's Cessnock has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.
