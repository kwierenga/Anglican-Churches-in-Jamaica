# St. Margaret's, Middlesex
**St. Elizabeth** · Mission · active

St. Margaret's Anglican Church stands at **Middlesex**, a small farming district in southwest St. Elizabeth set between **Lacovia** and **Whitehall**, some three kilometres north of the **Holland Bamboo Avenue** stretch of the A2. Diocesan directories list it simply as "St. Margaret's, Middlesex"; it has often been associated instead with **Middle Quarters**, the better-known village some four kilometres to the southwest. The church is a mission of the **St. Thomas' Cure in Lacovia** within the Diocese of Jamaica's Montego Bay Region, where the directory groups it immediately with Holy Trinity, Whitehall and St. Jude's, Slipe — the two churches nearest to it.

## History
**Founding of the parish (c.1664).** St. Elizabeth was one of the original Jamaican parishes, established under Sir Thomas Modyford and named in honour of **Lady Elizabeth Modyford**, wife of the first Governor of Jamaica (1664–71). Prior to **1703**, the parish stretched across the territory of present-day Westmoreland, Hanover, and Manchester; in 1703 Westmoreland was created from the western portion, and in **1814** the final subdivision created Manchester from the southern section. **Lacovia** was the original parish capital before Black River became the sole capital. The parish church, **St. John the Evangelist** at Black River, dates structurally from around 1700.

**Slavery and the sugar economy.** Under English rule, sugar and slavery defined the parish economy: prominent estates included Holland, Vineyard, and Fullerswood. The **Holland estate**, which gave its name to the famous bamboo avenue running between Lacovia and Middle Quarters, was one of the most substantial sugar properties in this part of the parish. **Cudjoe**, leader of the Leeward Maroons, signed the **Peace Treaty of 1739** at the Peace Cave in St. Elizabeth's Cockpit Country, securing Maroon land rights — and the Maroon community of **Accompong Town** still exists in the parish today. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**The Sam Sharpe Rebellion (December 1831).** The Christmas Rebellion of 1831, led by the Baptist deacon **Samuel Sharpe** at the Kensington Estate in St. James on **27 December 1831**, spread through the western parishes; some 60,000 enslaved people were involved. Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation and post-1838 settlement.** The British Parliament passed the Slavery Abolition Act in 1833; Emancipation came in 1834, and full freedom followed at the end of the apprenticeship period on **1 August 1838**. Freed people from the Holland and surrounding estates took up land around the Black River basin as small farmers, and the inland districts between Lacovia and Whitehall — Middlesex among them — took much of their present shape in the decades that followed. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865, Crown Colony rule replaced the planter Assembly (1866); the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation: a date not yet established.** Bishop Enos Nuttall's tenure (1880–1916) saw the Diocese grow from 26 rural mission stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**, and a small chapel of this kind most plausibly belongs to that wave of rural mission-building. No founding date has been established for St. Margaret's, however, and it should not be assumed. The 1900 Handbook of Jamaica records the St. Elizabeth cure of "Lacovia and Siloah" under Rev. **B. A. S. MacCalla** but names no chapel at Middlesex, and the church does not appear in the Jamaican Ancestral Records index for the parish. In the sources consulted here it is documented only in the modern diocesan directory.

**Twentieth century: bauxite and Independence.** The bauxite industry developed in St. Elizabeth from **1943**, with **ALPART** later headquartered at Nain. To the south of Middlesex the A2 corridor, and with it the Holland Bamboo Avenue between Lacovia and Middle Quarters, became a cultural and tourism landmark; Middlesex itself lies off that road, among the farming settlements to its north. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Margaret's a settled mission within the Lacovia cure.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The storm caused severe regional disruption, including the prolonged blockage of Holland Bamboo Avenue and **catastrophic flooding in Black River**. The cure's mother church, **St. Thomas' Lacovia**, sustained major damage. No separate report of damage at St. Margaret's has been found, and aerial photographs taken in 2026 show the building standing and its roof sound.

## Architecture
St. Margaret's is a modest rural mission chapel of a type common across St. Elizabeth. Aerial photography made in 2026 shows a single rectangular hall under a broad corrugated-zinc gable roof, its rendered masonry walls painted pale cream, with plain rectangular window and door openings rather than the lancets of the larger parish churches. A lower cross-gabled annexe adjoins one end of the hall, and a small cross finial stands at the apex of the main gable. The building occupies its own fenced plot between the district road and a side lane, with a red-painted step at the principal entrance. The roof and structure appear sound in the 2026 photographs.

## Clergy
St. Margaret's operates within the well-established diocesan framework that links smaller mission churches to larger parish centres. Diocesan directories position St. Margaret's, Middlesex under the **St. Thomas' (Lacovia) Cure** within the Montego Bay Region, with all clergy assignments, service schedules, and administrative functions managed through the Lacovia rectory. The 1900 Handbook records B. A. S. MacCalla as rector of "Lacovia and Siloah", the cure from which Middlesex is served today — though the Handbook itself names no chapel here.

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** **St. Margaret of Antioch** (also venerated as Marina) was a virgin-martyr of the early Church (3rd or 4th century) traditionally associated with healing in childbirth; her feast day in the Anglican calendar falls on **20 July**. (A separate St. Margaret of Scotland has feast day **16 November**. Which of the two the Middlesex dedication honours is not recorded in the sources consulted; Margaret of Antioch is the commoner dedication in Jamaican Anglican usage.)

**Middlesex, not Middle Quarters.** The church is routinely filed under the name of the larger village some four kilometres to the southwest — this site did so itself until 2026, and placed it on Middle Quarters' coordinates. The diocese's own directory has always called it "St. Margaret's, Middlesex". Middlesex is a small enough district to be absent from the standard gazetteers: neither the GeoNames record for Jamaica nor OpenStreetMap names it, which is a large part of why the confusion went uncorrected. Its position here was fixed in 2026 from a visit to the church.

**The road to Black River.** The **Holland Bamboo Avenue**, the historic bamboo-canopied stretch of the A2 between Lacovia and Middle Quarters, runs a few kilometres south of the church and is the usual approach to it from Black River. Both the avenue and the wider Black River area suffered catastrophic damage during Hurricane Melissa in October 2025.
