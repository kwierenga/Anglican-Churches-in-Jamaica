# Holy Trinity, Whitehall
**St. Elizabeth** · Church · active

Holy Trinity Anglican Church serves Whitehall, a community in the interior of St. Elizabeth parish near the Black River area. Whitehall is a farming community in the flat landscape of the interior plain of St. Elizabeth, and Holy Trinity has served this community for several generations. The Trinity dedication — Father, Son, and Holy Spirit — is one of the most traditional Anglican church names.

## History
Whitehall sits in the interior of St. Elizabeth, in the flat farming landscape of the parish between the Black River and the hills. The name Whitehall evokes the famous London street and seat of British government — a common colonial naming gesture. The Anglican Diocese established Holy Trinity to serve this community.

## Architecture
Holy Trinity is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural St. Elizabeth in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. Set in the rural interior of St. Elizabeth. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
Whitehall has been served within the St. Elizabeth deanery under shared clergy arrangements.

## Notable Facts
The Black River Great Morass, the largest freshwater wetland in Jamaica, lies in the western interior of St. Elizabeth. Communities in this area, including those near Whitehall, live alongside this ecologically significant landscape, which supports significant bird life, American crocodiles, and the headwaters of the Black River. The morass has historically served as a natural flood buffer and resource area for surrounding communities.
