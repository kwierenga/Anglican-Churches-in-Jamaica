# St. Augustine's, Mountainside
**St. Elizabeth** · Church · active

St. Augustine Anglican Church serves the hillside community of Mountainside in the hills near Santa Cruz. It was established in the late 1800s to serve families who settled in the area after Emancipation in 1838. Despite facing storms and necessary repairs over the years, the church has remained a steady place of worship. Today, it continues to be an important spiritual home for the people of Mountainside and the surrounding districts.

## History
St. Augustine began as a small Anglican mission in the second half of the 19th century. Before Emancipation, most of the local population lived on estates in the lower Santa Cruz foothills. After 1838, freed families moved uphill to establish new villages and farms in areas like Mountainside. The Anglican Church sent clergy from the Black River Deanery to hold services for this growing community. By the 1880s, a permanent chapel was completed and dedicated to St. Augustine of Hippo. This church quickly became a community hub, supporting education, family life, and spiritual growth during Jamaica’s formative free-village movement.

## Architecture
St. Augustine's is a sturdy, simple structure built from local limestone, typical of rural Anglican churches. It features a rectangular nave, a small chancel, and a modest bell-cot above the western entrance. The steep roof, now covered with metal, was likely originally made of wooden shingles. Inside, the church has an open-timber roof, wooden pews, and narrow pointed windows with colored glass. The surrounding churchyard, with graves of early settlers, adds to the site's peaceful and historic atmosphere.

## Clergy
St. Augustine has always been part of the Black River Deanery, served by clergy who also ministered in nearby towns such as Leeds, Malvern, and Balaclava. Early priests, including Rev. H. C. Wharton and Canon W. R. Campbell, provided guidance to the congregation. The church supported local education, helping to provide basic schooling for children in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. After Jamaica’s Independence in 1962, activities like youth programs and Mothers’ Union meetings remained central to its mission. Lay readers were vital in maintaining regular services when ordained clergy were shared among several congregations.

## Notable Facts
St. Augustine has weathered many hurricanes, including Hurricane Charlie (1951) and Hurricane Gilbert (1988), which caused damage to the roof and windows. Each time, the community came together to repair and rebuild. More recent repairs have further strengthened the structure. There is no reported damage from Hurricane Melissa (2025). Today, St. Augustine Anglican Church remains active, holding weekly services, harvest celebrations, weddings, and funerals, continuing its role as a enduring symbol of faith and community in Mountainside.
