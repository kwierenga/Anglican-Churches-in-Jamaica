# St. Augustine's, Mountainside
**St. Elizabeth** · Church · active

St. Augustine Anglican Church serves the hillside community of Mountainside in the hills near Santa Cruz. It was established in the late 1800s to serve families who settled in the area after Emancipation in 1838 and is one of six congregations grouped under the **St. Peter's, Pedro Plains cure** (alongside All Saints' Newell, the Epiphany at Bigwoods, St. Matthew's Orange Grove, St. Paul's Barbary Hall, and the cure church St. Peter's). Despite facing storms and necessary repairs over the years, the church has remained a steady place of worship.

## History
**Founding of the parish (c.1664).** St. Elizabeth was one of the original Jamaican parishes, established under Sir Thomas Modyford and named in honour of **Lady Elizabeth Modyford**, wife of the first Governor of Jamaica (1664–71). Prior to **1703**, the parish stretched across the territory of present-day Westmoreland, Hanover, and Manchester; in 1703 Westmoreland was created from the western portion, and in **1814** the final subdivision created Manchester from the southern section. **Lacovia** was the original parish capital before Black River became the sole capital. The parish church, **St. John the Evangelist** at Black River, dates structurally from around 1700.

**Slavery and the sugar economy.** Under English rule, sugar and slavery defined the parish economy: prominent estates included Holland, Vineyard, and Fullerswood. **Cudjoe**, leader of the Leeward Maroons, signed the **Peace Treaty of 1739** at the Peace Cave in St. Elizabeth's Cockpit Country, securing Maroon land rights — and the Maroon community of **Accompong Town** still exists in the parish today. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**The Sam Sharpe Rebellion (December 1831).** The Christmas Rebellion of 1831, led by the Baptist deacon **Samuel Sharpe** at the Kensington Estate in St. James on **27 December 1831**, spread through the western parishes; some 60,000 enslaved people were involved. Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation and the 1880s foundation.** The British Parliament passed the Slavery Abolition Act in 1833; Emancipation came in 1834, and full freedom followed at the end of the apprenticeship period on **1 August 1838**. Before Emancipation, most of the local population lived on estates in the lower Santa Cruz foothills. After 1838, freed families moved uphill to establish new villages and farms in areas like Mountainside. The Anglican Church sent clergy from the **Black River Deanery** to hold services for this growing community. By the **1880s**, a permanent chapel was completed and dedicated to St. Augustine of Hippo. This church quickly became a community hub, supporting education, family life, and spiritual growth during Jamaica's formative free-village movement. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865, Crown Colony rule replaced the planter Assembly (1866); the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica. Bishop Enos Nuttall's tenure (1880–1916) saw the Diocese grow from 26 rural mission stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

**Twentieth century, Independence, hurricanes.** The 1893–94 logwood export boom shaped the parish economy. The bauxite industry developed in St. Elizabeth from **1943**, with **ALPART** later headquartered at Nain. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Augustine's a settled rural mission within the St. Peter's Pedro Plains cure. **Hurricane Charlie** (1951) and **Hurricane Gilbert** (1988) both caused damage to the roof and windows of the church; each time the community came together to repair and rebuild.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. St. Elizabeth was severely affected, with major Anglican losses including St. Luke's Balaclava and St. Thomas' Lacovia. There is **no reported damage** from Hurricane Melissa to St. Augustine's Mountainside in the public sources reviewed; the church remains active.

## Architecture
St. Augustine's is a sturdy, simple structure built from local limestone, typical of rural Anglican churches. It features a rectangular nave, a small chancel, and a modest bell-cot above the western entrance. The steep roof, now covered with metal, was likely originally covered with wooden shingles. Inside, the church has an open-timber roof, wooden pews, and narrow pointed windows with coloured glass. The surrounding churchyard, with graves of early settlers, adds to the site's peaceful and historic atmosphere.

## Clergy
St. Augustine has always been part of the **Black River Deanery**, served by clergy who also ministered in nearby towns such as Leeds, Malvern, and Balaclava. Early priests, including **Rev. H. C. Wharton** and **Canon W. R. Campbell**, provided guidance to the congregation. Lay readers have been vital in maintaining regular services when ordained clergy were shared among several congregations. As of recent diocesan listings the parent St. Peter's Pedro Plains cure has been led by the Rev. **Marjorie Downer**.

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** **St. Augustine of Hippo** (354–430) — the great fourth- and fifth-century theologian whose *Confessions* and *City of God* shaped Western Christianity, with a feast day on **28 August** — is one of the more theologically substantial Anglican dedications, distinct from St. Augustine of Canterbury (commemorated 26 May).

**Storm history.** St. Augustine has weathered many hurricanes, including Hurricane Charlie (1951) and Hurricane Gilbert (1988), which both caused damage to the roof and windows. Each time, the community came together to repair and rebuild. More recent repairs have further strengthened the structure. The church remains active, holding weekly services, harvest celebrations, weddings, and funerals.
