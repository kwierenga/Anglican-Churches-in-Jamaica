# Holy Trinity, Spanish Town
**St. Catherine** · Church · inactive

Holy Trinity Anglican Church served the **Spanish Town** community of St. Catherine as a second Anglican worship point alongside the **Cathedral of St. Jago de la Vega** — the parish church of St. Catherine, named the Cathedral of the Diocese in 1843.

## History
**Spanish Villa de la Vega (1534) and English conquest (1655).** The Spanish founded **Villa de la Vega** in 1534 — Jamaica's capital from 1538 until **1872**. Penn and Venables's English expedition captured Jamaica in May **1655**. The Cathedral itself was built on the site of the Spanish Catholic church the British destroyed after the 1655 conquest; it was rebuilt in **1714** after hurricane damage in 1712, named the Cathedral of the Anglican Diocese of Jamaica in **1843**, and saw its tower added in 1817.

**Founding parishes.** Under English rule from 1655, **St. Catherine** was one of Jamaica's original parishes. The neighbouring early parishes of **St. John, St. Dorothy, and St. Thomas-in-the-Vale** were merged into expanded St. Catherine under **Law 20 of 1867**.

**Slavery and the planter church.** Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds. **The Diocese of Jamaica** was established by Letters Patent on **24 July 1824**; **Christopher Lipscomb** was consecrated first Bishop and enthroned at the Cathedral on 15 February 1825.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838), Sligoville.** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Sligoville** in St. Catherine became Jamaica's **first free village** in 1838, founded with land provided by **Howe Peter Browne, 2nd Marquess of Sligo**, then Governor of Jamaica. Bishop Lipscomb (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

**Foundation of Holy Trinity.** Spanish Town's long history as Jamaica's capital — from the Spanish foundation as Villa de la Vega in 1534 through nearly three centuries of British colonial administration until 1872 — created a substantial Anglican community that the cathedral alone did not always fully serve. Holy Trinity grew up to provide additional Anglican ministry for residents elsewhere in the city. The dedication to the Holy Trinity is the same as that of the diocese's later cathedral on North Street, Kingston.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** Capital moved from Spanish Town to **Kingston in 1872**. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found Spanish Town a quieter town anchored by the cathedral.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Spanish Town's inland position — some distance from the coast — gives it a different storm-risk profile from coastal parishes; flooding from the **Rio Cobre** during hurricane rainfall is the primary natural hazard rather than direct storm surge.

## Architecture
Holy Trinity is a masonry church set in Spanish Town's historic core. The inland position of Spanish Town gives it a different storm-risk profile from coastal parishes — flooding from the Rio Cobre during hurricane rainfall events is the primary natural hazard rather than direct storm surge.

## Clergy
Holy Trinity has been served within the **St. Catherine portion of the Mandeville Region**. Spanish Town's Anglican community is anchored by the cathedral, with Holy Trinity providing supplementary ministry.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to the **Holy Trinity** — Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, celebrated on Trinity Sunday — is shared with the diocese's later **Holy Trinity Cathedral on North Street, Kingston (1911)** and with Holy Trinity at Linstead, Old Harbour, Westgate (Montego Bay), and Trinityville (St. Thomas).
