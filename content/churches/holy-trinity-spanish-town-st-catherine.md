# Holy Trinity, Spanish Town
**St. Catherine** · Church · inactive

Holy Trinity Anglican Church serves the Spanish Town community of St. Catherine, providing a second Anglican worship point in the former capital distinct from the Cathedral of St. Jago de la Vega. Spanish Town is a substantial city with a population large enough to support multiple Anglican churches, and Holy Trinity serves the congregation in its part of the town.

## History
Spanish Town's long history as Jamaica's capital created a substantial Anglican community that the cathedral alone could not fully serve. Holy Trinity was established to provide additional Anglican ministry within the city, serving residents in its part of Spanish Town. The church has continued to serve the community through the transformation of Spanish Town from colonial capital to a regional city.

## Architecture
Holy Trinity is a masonry church in Spanish Town. The inland position of Spanish Town, some distance from the coast, gives it a different storm risk profile from coastal parishes — flooding from the Rio Cobre during hurricane rainfall events is the primary natural hazard.

## Clergy
Holy Trinity has been served within the St. Catherine deanery under shared clergy arrangements. Spanish Town's Anglican community is anchored by the Cathedral, with Holy Trinity providing supplementary ministry for a different part of the city.

## Notable Facts
Spanish Town was Jamaica's capital for over 300 years, from the Spanish period through British colonial rule until 1872. The city retains a historic core including the Georgian Square, which is one of the finest examples of 18th-century colonial architecture in the Caribbean. The Cathedral dominates the ecclesiastical landscape of Spanish Town, but Holy Trinity reflects the city's continued population and the Diocese's commitment to multiple worship points in larger communities.
