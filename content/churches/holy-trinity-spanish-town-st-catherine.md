# Holy Trinity, Spanish Town
**St. Catherine** · Church · inactive

Holy Trinity Anglican Church served the Spanish Town community of St. Catherine as a second Anglican worship point alongside the Cathedral of St. Jago de la Vega — the parish church of St. Catherine, named the Cathedral of the Diocese in 1843. The principal Anglican presence in Spanish Town is the cathedral; Holy Trinity has historically supplemented that ministry for residents in another part of the city.

## History
Spanish Town's long history as Jamaica's capital — from the Spanish foundation as Villa de la Vega in 1534 through nearly three centuries of British colonial administration until 1872 — created a substantial Anglican community that the cathedral alone did not always fully serve. The Cathedral itself was built on the site of the Spanish Catholic church the British destroyed after the 1655 conquest; it was rebuilt in 1714 after hurricane damage in 1712, named the Cathedral of the Anglican Diocese of Jamaica in 1843, and saw its tower added in 1817. Holy Trinity grew up to provide additional Anglican ministry for residents elsewhere in the city. The dedication to the Holy Trinity is the same as that of the diocese's later cathedral on North Street, Kingston.

## Architecture
Holy Trinity is a masonry church set in Spanish Town's historic core. The inland position of Spanish Town, some distance from the coast, gives it a different storm-risk profile from coastal parishes — flooding from the Rio Cobre during hurricane rainfall events is the primary natural hazard rather than direct storm surge.

## Clergy
Holy Trinity has been served within the St. Catherine portion of the Mandeville Region. Spanish Town's Anglican community is anchored by the cathedral, with Holy Trinity providing supplementary ministry.
