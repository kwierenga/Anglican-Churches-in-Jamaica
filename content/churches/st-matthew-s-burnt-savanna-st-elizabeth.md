# St. Matthew's, Burnt Savanna
**St. Elizabeth** · Church · active

St. Matthew's Anglican Church serves Burnt Savanna in St. Elizabeth, in the dry savannah country of the parish's southern plain. The chapel is one of a network of small mission stations the Diocese of Jamaica established across the southern districts of the parish during Bishop Enos Nuttall's late-nineteenth-century rural church-planting expansion.

## History
**Founding of the parish (c.1664).** St. Elizabeth was one of the original Jamaican parishes, established under Sir Thomas Modyford and named in honour of **Lady Elizabeth Modyford**, wife of the first Governor of Jamaica (1664–71). Prior to **1703**, the parish stretched across the territory of present-day Westmoreland, Hanover, and Manchester; in 1703 Westmoreland was created from the western portion, and in **1814** the final subdivision created Manchester from the southern section. The original parish capital was **Lacovia** before Black River became the sole capital. The parish church, **St. John the Evangelist** at Black River, dates structurally from around 1700, with the tower foundation laid in 1837.

**Slavery and the sugar economy.** Under English rule, sugar and slavery defined the parish economy: prominent estates included Holland, Vineyard, and Fullerswood. **Cudjoe**, leader of the Leeward Maroons, signed the **Peace Treaty of 1739** at the Peace Cave in St. Elizabeth's Cockpit Country, securing Maroon land rights — and the Maroon community of **Accompong Town** still exists in the parish. The "Savanna" districts of southern St. Elizabeth (Burnt Savanna, Bull Savannah, Lacovia Savannah) record the cattle-grazing landscape of these dry coastal flats, an inheritance from Spanish-era stockmen. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**The Sam Sharpe Rebellion (December 1831).** The Christmas Rebellion of 1831, led by the Baptist deacon **Samuel Sharpe** at the Kensington Estate in St. James on **27 December 1831**, spread through the western parishes; some 60,000 enslaved people were involved. Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation, 1865, disestablishment.** The British Parliament passed the Slavery Abolition Act in 1833; Emancipation came in 1834, and full freedom followed at the end of the apprenticeship period on **1 August 1838**. After 1838, freed people settled the upland districts of southern St. Elizabeth as small farmers — Burnt Savanna belongs to that wave of post-Emancipation free settlement. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865, Crown Colony rule replaced the planter Assembly (1866); the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of St. Matthew's: Nuttall's expansion.** Bishop Enos Nuttall's tenure as Bishop of Jamaica (1880–1916) saw the Diocese grow from 26 rural mission stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. The 1891 Handbook of Jamaica lists the major St. Elizabeth congregations as Black River, Lacovia, Gilnock, Mount Hermon, Nain, St. Alban's, Plains, St. Mary, Siloah, and Tabernacle — Burnt Savanna is not separately recorded, placing its foundation in the late phase of Nuttall's expansion. The 1900 Handbook records the cures as Black River (C. Melville), Lacovia & Siloah (B. A. S. MacCalla), Gilnock & St. Alban's (A. P. Kennedy), Mount Hermon & Nain (A. E. Lewis), Plains/Mayfield & 8 Mission Stations (C. T. Rickard), St. Mary (J. D. Ellis), Whitehall (E. D. Tinling) — Burnt Savanna was likely one of the unnamed mission stations of the Plains cure.

**Twentieth century: logwood, bauxite, Independence.** The 1893–94 logwood export boom (when its value briefly surpassed sugar and coffee) shaped the parish economy. The bauxite industry developed in St. Elizabeth from **1943**, with **ALPART** later headquartered at Nain. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Matthew's a settled rural mission. The 1979 Newmarket flood (41 casualties) and Hurricane Gilbert (1988) marked the late twentieth century.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. St. Elizabeth was severely affected, with major Anglican losses including St. Luke's Balaclava and St. Thomas' Lacovia. The specific status of St. Matthew's Burnt Savanna after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. Matthew's is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for mission stations across the St. Elizabeth savannah country. The flat plain offers no shelter from storm surge during major hurricanes.

## Clergy
St. Matthew's Burnt Savanna has been served within the Plains/Mayfield cure of the Montego Bay Region's St. Elizabeth deanery network.

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** **Matthew the Evangelist** — apostle, former tax collector ("publican"), traditional author of the first Gospel, with a feast day on **21 September** — is a common Anglican choice for late-nineteenth-century mission foundations.

**The Savanna districts.** Burnt Savanna belongs to the cluster of "Savanna" community names of southern St. Elizabeth that record the cattle-grazing landscape of the dry coastal plains — an inheritance from the Spanish-era stockmen who reared cattle on the Pedro Plains before the English conquest of 1655.
