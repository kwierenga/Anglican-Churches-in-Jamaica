# Church of the Holy Trinity, Trinityville
**St. Thomas** · Church · active

The Church of the Holy Trinity serves **Trinityville** in St. Thomas parish — a community whose name derives directly from the church itself, in the wider Jamaican tradition where the Anglican mission station gave its name to the surrounding settlement.

## History
**English rule (1655) and the founding parish.** Under English rule from **1655**, St. Thomas was one of Jamaica's original parishes — originally **"St. Thomas-in-the-East"** with the first rector appointed in **1666**.

**Sugar economy and slavery.** St. Thomas was one of Jamaica's great sugar parishes. Large estates — Stokes Hall, Golden Grove, Albion, Holland — dominated the landscape, worked by enslaved Africans throughout the colonial period. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union** to "wipe non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb** (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**The Morant Bay Rebellion (11 October 1865).** On **11 October 1865**, **Paul Bogle** — a small farmer and Native Baptist deacon of **Stony Gut** — led a protest march to the Morant Bay courthouse. Governor **Edward John Eyre's** brutal response: **439 executed**, **600+ flogged**, **1,000+ homes burned**. Bogle was hanged on **24 October 1865**; **George William Gordon** was hanged on **23 October 1865**. **Both were declared National Heroes in 1965**. The 1865 Morant Bay Rebellion made the parish a focal point in Jamaica's nineteenth-century social and political history; its aftermath shaped the constitutional re-organisation of Jamaican governance and the redrawing of the island's parishes (1867, **fourteen parishes from twenty-two**). St. Thomas Parish Church at Morant Bay was a brick building erected in **1865** after the original eighteenth-century parish church at Church Corner had fallen into dilapidation.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall, and the founding of Holy Trinity.** Jamaica became a **Crown Colony in 1866**. Law 20 of 1867 absorbed the **old parish of St. David** into St. Thomas. The Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. Trinityville is one of the upland communities of St. Thomas, in the rural farming country of the parish. The community's name preserves the practice — common in post-Emancipation Jamaica — by which a new free village or settlement took its name from the chapel that became the social and spiritual centre of community life. (Sligoville, Freetown, Faith's, and others fit the same pattern.) The Anglican Diocese established the Holy Trinity here as a mission chapel; the dedication then defined the name of the surrounding district, reflecting the church's foundational role.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** Internal self-government came in **1944**. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** fulfilled the aspirations for which Bogle and Gordon died. **Hurricane Ivan (2004)** caused widespread damage across St. Thomas's interior.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Hurricane Melissa brought heavy rain to St. Thomas's interior; Trinityville was in the affected zone.

## Architecture
The Church of the Holy Trinity at Trinityville is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for mission stations across the St. Thomas interior. The hill-country position creates exposure to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
Holy Trinity Trinityville is served within the Diocese's **Eastern Jamaica Region**.

## Notable Facts
**Trinityville** belongs to a small but significant Jamaican naming tradition where the community took its name from the Anglican chapel — a tradition shared with **Sligoville** (St. Catherine), **Freetown** (Clarendon), and others. The dedication to the **Holy Trinity** — Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, celebrated on Trinity Sunday after Pentecost — is shared with the Diocese's Cathedral on North Street, Kingston (Holy Trinity Cathedral, 1911), and with the Holy Trinity at Old Harbour and Holy Trinity at Westgate, Montego Bay.
