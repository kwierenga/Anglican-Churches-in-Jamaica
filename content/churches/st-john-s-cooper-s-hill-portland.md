# St. John's, Cooper's Hill
**Portland** · Church · active

St. John's Anglican Church serves the small community of **Cooper's Hill** in Portland's upper Rio Grande valley, situated among the hillside settlements near Unity Valley and Berridale. The Diocese lists it among Portland churches in the **Eastern Jamaica Region**, grouping it with neighbouring missions including **St. Paul's Moore Town** and **St. Luke's Comfort Castle**.

## History
**English rule (1655) and the founding of Portland (1723).** Under English rule from **1655**, the future Portland coast remained largely Maroon territory. **Portland parish was created in 1723** by order of the then Governor, **Henry Bentinck, 1st Duke of Portland** (Governor 1722–1726), and named in his honour. The British built **Fort George** at Port Antonio in 1729.

**The First Maroon War (1728–1739) and Nanny — and the Rio Grande valley.** The **Windward Maroons** under **Quao** and **Nanny** controlled the Blue Mountains and John Crow Mountains — and the upper **Rio Grande valley** in particular was the heartland of Nanny's territory. The **1740 treaty** granted **Nanny a 500-acre land grant** that forms the present site of **Moore Town**, just up the Rio Grande from Cooper's Hill. **Nanny of the Maroons** became Jamaica's only female National Hero in October 1975, of Ashanti origin.

**Slavery and the planter church.** Sugar estates dominated the fertile coastal lowlands; coffee thrived in the hills. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union** to "wipe non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Christ Church Port Antonio** was completed around **1840** under Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb**.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall, and the banana revolution.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. In **1870**, **Captain Lorenzo Dow Baker** purchased bananas in Port Antonio market; Baker's Boston Fruit Company became **United Fruit Company in 1899**, and the Rio Grande valley became one of the most important banana-growing zones in the British Caribbean.

**Foundation of St. John's.** Cooper's Hill developed as one of many settlements along Portland's interior river valleys, appearing in parish records and local histories as part of the Rio Grande corridor's community network. Diocesan records consistently place St. John's, Cooper's Hill among Portland churches within the Eastern Jamaica Region, demonstrating a long-standing Anglican presence alongside other historic missions in Moore Town and Comfort Castle. This placement reflects the Church's strategic establishment of missions throughout Portland's interior to serve remote communities during the post-Emancipation period and beyond.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** The **1903 hurricane** damaged Christ Church Port Antonio and devastated the banana industry. Tourism grew at Port Antonio. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. John's a settled valley mission.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The entire Rio Grande valley, including the Comfort Castle and Mill Bank areas, experienced disruption during Melissa, though Portland was well east of the storm's western landfall point and no specific damage to this chapel has been reported.

## Architecture
While specific architectural documentation for St. John's remains unavailable in public databases, the church likely follows the pattern of modest mission chapels typical throughout Portland's rural valleys. Based on comparable structures in the region, it probably features masonry construction, timber roofing, simple lancet windows, and an adjacent burial ground serving local families.

## Clergy
St. John's operates within the **Eastern Jamaica Region's Portland deanery**, where it is administratively grouped with St. Paul's Moore Town and St. Luke's Comfort Castle. All clerical assignments, service schedules, and pastoral oversight for these missions are coordinated through the regional deanery network.

## Notable Facts
The church serves a community often confused with its St. Andrew namesake, though gazetteers precisely locate **Cooper's Hill, Portland** near Unity Valley and Berridale in the upper Rio Grande corridor. The dedication to **St. John** — most commonly the Evangelist (feast 27 December), occasionally the Baptist (24 June) — is one of the most common Anglican dedications.
