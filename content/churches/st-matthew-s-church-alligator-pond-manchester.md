# St. Matthew's Church, Alligator Pond
**Manchester** · Church · active

St. Matthew's Anglican Church serves Alligator Pond, a fishing village on the south coast of Manchester parish at the edge of the Caribbean Sea. Alligator Pond is a small but distinctive coastal community known for its fishing tradition and its proximity to the Great Pedro Bluff, one of the more dramatic coastal features of Jamaica's south coast. The church serves a community shaped entirely by its relationship with the sea.

## History
Alligator Pond developed as a fishing village on the south coast of Manchester, in a sheltered bay below the limestone cliffs of the interior. The community has maintained a fishing tradition for centuries, and the catch from Alligator Pond supplies fish markets in Mandeville and across the parish. The Anglican Diocese established St. Matthew's to serve this coastal fishing community. The south coast position has meant the church has experienced the full force of multiple hurricanes across its history.

## Architecture
St. Matthew's Church is a masonry church of modest proportions, representative of Anglican rural church building in Manchester. The structure balances practicality with the dignity appropriate to a place of worship, featuring a straightforward nave with local stone or concrete block construction typical of Jamaica's interior church-building tradition. Set in the rural interior of Manchester. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
Alligator Pond has been served within the Manchester deanery under shared clergy arrangements. The small fishing community creates a tight pastoral relationship between the church and the village.

## Notable Facts
Alligator Pond is one of the more evocatively named communities on Jamaica's south coast, its name reflecting the historical presence of American crocodiles (locally called alligators) in the coastal wetlands and mangroves of the south coast. The Great Pedro Bluff nearby is a dramatic limestone headland that marks one of the southern extremities of the island. Alligator Pond's fishing tradition has been documented in several studies of Jamaican coastal communities.
