# St. Andrew's, Rocky Point
**Clarendon** · Church · active

St. Andrew's Anglican Church serves Rocky Point, a coastal community on the Portland Point peninsula at the southern tip of Clarendon. The church is administered as a mission of the St. Peter's, Alley cure (Lionel Town circuit), alongside St. Luke's Mitchell Town, St. John's Portland Cottage, St. Saviour's Milk River, and St. Thomas' Race Course. The cure rectory is at P.O. Box 16, Lionel Town P.O., Clarendon.

## History
Rocky Point is a residential community on the western side of the Portland Point peninsula, the dry, low-lying spit of land that extends south from the Clarendon mainland into the Caribbean Sea. The peninsula has long supported fishing villages and, on its eastern side, a port used for the export of alumina from the bauxite operations of central Jamaica. The Anglican community at Rocky Point developed as part of the wider lowland Vere/Clarendon coastal economy and has been served from the ancient parish church of Vere — St. Peter's at the Alley, the third-oldest Anglican church in Jamaica (1671, rebuilt 1715).

The dedication to St. Andrew the apostle — patron saint of Scotland and brother of Simon Peter, whose feast day is 30 November — is found in several Jamaican Anglican churches and reflects the significant Scottish presence in the colonial-era plantation economy.

## Architecture
St. Andrew's is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for the lowland mission stations of southern Clarendon. The peninsula's exposed coastal position creates direct vulnerability to storm surge during major hurricanes — Rocky Point and the surrounding Portland Point peninsula sit in the path of storm tracks coming from the Caribbean Sea.

## Clergy
St. Andrew's Rocky Point is served as a mission of the St. Peter's, Alley cure within the Mandeville Region's Clarendon Deanery.
