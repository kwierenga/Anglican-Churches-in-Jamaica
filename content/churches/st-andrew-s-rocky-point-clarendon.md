# St. Andrew's, Rocky Point
**Clarendon** · Church · active

St. Andrew's Anglican Church serves **Rocky Point**, a coastal community on the **Portland Point peninsula** at the southern tip of Clarendon. The church is administered as a mission of the **St. Peter's, Alley** cure (Lionel Town circuit), alongside St. Luke's Mitchell Town, St. John's Portland Cottage, St. Saviour's Milk River, and St. Thomas' Race Course. The cure rectory is at P.O. Box 16, Lionel Town P.O., Clarendon.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1664).** Clarendon parish was created in **1664**, nine years after the English seizure of Jamaica from Spain (**1655**), and named for **Sir Edward Hyde, 1st Earl of Clarendon** (1609–1674), Lord High Chancellor of England under Charles II. The original parish church was the **Church of the White Cross** at "The Cross" — Jamaica's third Anglican church and the site of Jamaica's first House of Assembly elections. **St. Paul's Chapelton** was built as a chapel of ease in **1666** in the upland interior, and became the parish church when the Cross Church was destroyed in **1815**.

**Vere parish and St. Peter's Alley (1671).** **Vere** was carved out as a separate sugar-plain parish in **1673**, taking in the southern coastal plain south of the Rio Minho — including the Portland Point peninsula and Rocky Point. **St. Peter's Alley** (founded 1671 at Withywood, relocated to Alley after the 1692 earthquake, present building 1715/1735) was Vere's parish church and remains the third-oldest Anglican church in Jamaica. Vere was reabsorbed into Clarendon in **1867**, and **Law 20 of 1867** designated **May Pen** as chief town of the merged parish.

**Slavery and the Vere sugar plain.** Vere's economy was built on slavery. The sugar estates of the Vere plain — extending across the Portland Point peninsula and the Rocky Point hinterland — depended on the labour of thousands of enslaved Africans. The Anglican Church served as the established church of this system. One of Jamaica's earliest slave rebellions erupted at **Suttons Plantation in 1690**: some 500 enslaved people escaped to form the Maroon communities eventually led by **Cudjoe** into the Cockpit Country.

**Sam Sharpe, Emancipation, disestablishment.** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**, a vigilante alliance that sought to "wipe the non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of the Rocky Point mission.** The Anglican community at Rocky Point developed as part of the wider lowland Vere/Clarendon coastal economy and has been served from the ancient parish church of Vere — St. Peter's at Alley. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

**Twentieth century: bauxite, May Pen, Independence.** The **bauxite industry** transformed Clarendon from 1957 onward, with **Alcoa** beginning operations in **1959**, the first shipment in 1963, and the **Jamalco** alumina refinery at Halse Hall opening in **1972**; alumina is exported via the **Port Esquivel and Rocky Point** ports on the south coast, placing this community directly in the industrial transport corridor of central-Jamaica bauxite. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Andrew's a settled mission of the Lionel Town circuit.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The peninsula's exposed coastal position creates direct vulnerability to storm surge during major hurricanes. The specific status of St. Andrew's Rocky Point after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. Andrew's is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for the lowland mission stations of southern Clarendon. Rocky Point and the surrounding Portland Point peninsula sit in the path of storm tracks coming from the Caribbean Sea, creating direct vulnerability to storm surge during major hurricanes.

## Clergy
St. Andrew's Rocky Point is served as a mission of the St. Peter's, Alley cure within the Mandeville Region's Clarendon Deanery.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to **St. Andrew the apostle** — patron saint of Scotland and brother of Simon Peter, whose feast day is **30 November** — is found in several Jamaican Anglican churches and reflects the significant Scottish presence in the colonial-era plantation economy.
