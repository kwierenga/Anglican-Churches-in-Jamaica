# St. Stephen's, Cornwall Mountain
**St. James** · Church · active

St. Stephen's Anglican Church serves Cornwall Mountain, a community in the hilly southern interior of St. James, in the upland terrain that rises toward the boundary with Westmoreland and St. Elizabeth. The placename carries a colonial echo: **Cornwall** was the historic county of western Jamaica (alongside Middlesex and Surrey, the three counties created in 1758), and the mountain district takes its name from that imposed administrative geography. (Note: a separate St. Stephen's at Cornwall Mountain exists in **Westmoreland** — a different church in a different parish.)

## History
**Founding of the parish (1664).** St. James was one of the second batch of Jamaican parishes formed after the English conquest, established around **1664** by Sir Thomas Modyford and named after **James, Duke of York** (later King James II). At its formation it was much larger than today, including the territory of present-day Trelawny and Hanover. For many years the north side of the island was sparsely settled: in **1673** only **146 persons** resided in the entire parish.

**Eighteenth-century development and the Maroons.** Montego Bay was made a free port in **1738**; Charles Square was laid out in **1755**. Cudjoe, Maroon leader of western Jamaica, signed a peace treaty with the British in **1739**, after which the Maroons were given almost 1,500 acres of land at **Trelawny Town** in St. James. The Second Maroon War of **1789** saw 300 Maroons fight 1,500 English soldiers. By the 1780s Montego Bay was, next to Kingston, "the most flourishing town in the island" (NLJ).

**Slavery and the Sam Sharpe Rebellion (December 1831).** Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds. The **Christmas Rebellion** of 1831, led by the Baptist deacon **Samuel Sharpe** at the Kensington Estate in St. James on the night of **27 December 1831**, was one of the largest slave rebellions in the British Caribbean — some 60,000 enslaved people across St. James, Trelawny, Hanover, and Westmoreland involved. Sharpe was hanged in **Montego Bay marketplace** (now **Sam Sharpe Square**) on **23 May 1832**, and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation and post-1838 hill settlement.** Within two years the British Parliament passed the Slavery Abolition Act (1833); Emancipation came in 1834, and full freedom followed at the end of the apprenticeship period on **1 August 1838**. The estate that gave Cornwall Mountain its name was part of the plantation landscape of St. James, one of Jamaica's most heavily enslaved parishes. After Emancipation, freed families settled the interior hills, and the Anglican Diocese extended its mission network into upland communities like Cornwall Mountain to provide pastoral care to the post-Emancipation farming population. St. Stephen's — dedicated to the first Christian martyr — was established as part of this outreach.

**1865 to 1900: post-disestablishment Diocese.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865, Crown Colony rule replaced the planter Assembly (1866); the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica. Bishop Enos Nuttall's tenure (1880–1916) saw the Diocese grow from 26 rural mission stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. The 1900 Handbook records the St. James cures as Montego Bay (J. W. Austin), Holy Trinity (G. B. Hall), Marley (H. F. R. Sharpe), and Montpelier & 5 Mission Stations (C. G. McGregory) — Cornwall Mountain most likely served as one of the unnamed mission stations of one of these cures.

**Twentieth century: hurricanes, Independence.** Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Stephen's a settled rural mission. Hurricane Gilbert (1988) struck western Jamaica with enormous force, leaving interior hill communities isolated for days. Hurricane Ivan (2004) again caused serious damage to the interior hills. Hurricane Melissa (October 2025) made landfall in Westmoreland and its effects extended across the St. James interior highlands.

## Architecture
St. Stephen's is a small masonry chapel characteristic of Anglican mission buildings in the St. James interior highlands. Set in the hilly terrain of Cornwall Mountain, the church occupies an elevated position with the surrounding landscape of farms, woodland, and steep valleys. The building's construction is modest and functional, suited to a small rural congregation. The steep terrain creates significant landslide and road-closure risk during major rainfall events.

## Clergy
Cornwall Mountain has been served within the southern St. James clergy cluster of the Montego Bay Region, grouped with other interior hill congregations under shared clergy arrangements. Lay leaders — wardens, Mothers' Union members, and lay readers — have been essential to sustaining regular worship and pastoral care in a remote hill community.

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** Stephen the protomartyr was the first Christian martyr, deacon of the early Jerusalem church, stoned to death for his preaching as recorded in **Acts 7**. His feast day on **26 December** (the day after Christmas) makes "Boxing Day" a particularly significant date for Stephen-dedicated congregations.

**Two Cornwall Mountains.** Two entries for **St. Stephen's at Cornwall Mountain** appear in our dataset — one in St. James (this entry) and one in Westmoreland — at slightly different coordinates. They are recorded by the diocese as distinct congregations in two parishes whose mountain district shares the historic "Cornwall" county name.

**Hurricane Melissa (28 October 2025)** made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history — with effects extending across the St. James interior highlands. Cornwall Mountain and the surrounding hill communities experienced damaging winds, heavy rain, and potential road disruption. Across the Diocese, some **50 church buildings were destroyed or significantly damaged**.
