# St. Matthew's, Santa Cruz

St. Matthew's Anglican Church is the principal Anglican church of Santa Cruz, the commercial capital of St. Elizabeth parish. ' The church has an excellent hall used by different groups across the parish.

## History
Santa Cruz is the commercial capital and largest town of St. Elizabeth, serving as the hub for commerce, services, and transport across the parish. The Anglican Diocese established St. Matthew's as the principal Anglican church of the town. The church has served the Santa Cruz community through the development of the town from a colonial market settlement to the main urban centre of a major parish.

## Architecture
St. Matthew's is a masonry church of modest proportions, representative of Anglican rural church building in St. Elizabeth. The structure balances practicality with the dignity appropriate to a place of worship, featuring a straightforward nave with local stone or concrete block construction typical of Jamaica's interior church-building tradition. Set in the rural interior of St. Elizabeth. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
Santa Cruz has been served as the principal town of the St. Elizabeth deanery circuit. St. Matthew's has hosted weddings and special services for the parish and holds regular weekday office hours Monday through Friday.

## Notable Facts
Santa Cruz is named after the Spanish 'Holy Cross,' reflecting the area's Spanish colonial heritage — St. Elizabeth was one of the last areas of Jamaica to develop under British rule, and Spanish place names survived in the landscape. Santa Cruz is also the location of the yam market that supplies much of Jamaica, and the annual Yam Festival celebrates the crop that makes St. Elizabeth the 'breadbasket of Jamaica.' St. Matthew's position at the heart of Santa Cruz gives it a central role in the commercial and community life of the parish.
