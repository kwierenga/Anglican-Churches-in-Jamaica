# St. Matthew's, Santa Cruz
**St. Elizabeth** · Church · active

St. Matthew's Anglican Church serves Santa Cruz, the commercial capital and largest town of St. Elizabeth — Jamaica's biggest parish by area and historically known as the "breadbasket of Jamaica." The town's Spanish name, meaning "Holy Cross," is one of the clearest surviving markers of the Spanish colonial period (1509–1655) in this part of the island. The dedication to St. Matthew — the tax collector turned apostle, a man who understood commerce and the marketplace before Christ called him — is remarkably apt for a church planted in the heart of Jamaica's premier market town. St. Matthew's is part of a five-church cure served by Rev. Hilda Vaughan.

## History
Santa Cruz sits on a broad, semi-arid plain in the interior of St. Elizabeth, at the crossroads of routes connecting Black River (the parish capital and port) to Mandeville and the northern parishes. The name dates to the Spanish period, when the interior plains were used for cattle ranching (*hatos*). When the English captured Jamaica in 1655, they converted many of these ranching areas into sugar and provision estates worked by enslaved Africans. The estates surrounding Santa Cruz — alongside the major slaveholdings at Black River, Lacovia, and Appleton — were part of the plantation system that defined St. Elizabeth's economy for two centuries.

St. Elizabeth was central to the struggle against slavery. The Accompong Maroons, in the Cockpit Country just north of Santa Cruz, had won their freedom from the British through the 1739 treaty — one of the earliest successful freedom struggles in the Americas. The 1831 Christmas Rebellion led by National Hero Samuel Sharpe, the largest slave uprising in the British Caribbean, engulfed western Jamaica including St. Elizabeth and hastened Parliament's passage of the Emancipation Act.

After Emancipation in 1834 and full freedom on 1 August 1838, Santa Cruz grew as formerly enslaved people settled in the area, establishing small farms and trading at the market. The Anglican Church, which had been the established church during slavery and was often aligned with the planter class, reinvented its mission in the post-emancipation era. Building new churches in market towns like Santa Cruz was part of this effort — extending the church's reach to the free Black population. St. Matthew's was likely established as a chapel-of-ease in the mid-to-late nineteenth century, during the wave of Anglican expansion that followed Emancipation and especially after disestablishment of the Anglican Church in 1870.

Jamaica's independence on 6 August 1962 was celebrated across St. Elizabeth. In the decades that followed, bauxite mining transformed the parish. The discovery and exploitation of aluminium ore deposits in the red-earth highlands brought significant economic change from the 1950s onward, and Santa Cruz benefited enormously as the commercial hub serving the mining communities. The town has continued to grow; its weekly market and annual Yam Festival celebrate the crop that makes St. Elizabeth the breadbasket of Jamaica.

## Architecture
St. Matthew's is a masonry church in the heart of Santa Cruz, with a church hall used by different groups across the parish. The building follows the functional style typical of Anglican interior churches in St. Elizabeth — designed for ventilation in the heat of the semi-arid plain, with solid construction to withstand hurricanes.

## Education
The Anglican mission to educate was central to the church's purpose in a market town serving freed families. Anglican elementary schools were attached to many churches across St. Elizabeth, providing literacy and numeracy to children who would otherwise have had no access to education. The St. Elizabeth Technical High School (STETHS), one of Jamaica's prominent secondary schools, is located in Santa Cruz (founded 1960). Munro College (boys, 1856) and Hampton School (girls, 1874), both Anglican-founded institutions in the parish, are among the most important educational legacies of the Church in St. Elizabeth.

## Clergy
St. Matthew's is part of a five-church cure served by Rev. Hilda Vaughan, who also oversees St. Andrew's Gilnock, St. Stephen's Nain, Church of the Holy Spirit Pepper, and The Transfiguration Leeds — a circuit spanning the interior of St. Elizabeth that links the commercial heart of Santa Cruz with surrounding agricultural and mining communities.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to St. Matthew — born Levi, a tax collector working for the Roman occupation before Jesus called him with the words "Follow me" (Matthew 9:9) — creates a resonant connection between sacred and commercial life in a market town. His Gospel, the most structured and didactic of the four, emphasises Jesus's teachings on justice and mercy, including the Sermon on the Mount — themes that spoke directly to post-emancipation congregations building a just society. His feast day is 21 September. Santa Cruz's Spanish name is shared with towns across the former Spanish Empire, linking this Jamaican market town to a global colonial history that predates the English presence by 150 years.
