# St. Agnes', Priory
**St. Ann** · Church · active

St. Agnes Anglican Church is a mission church in the seaside village of **Priory**, St. Ann, situated near the historic sites of **Seville Heritage Park** and **Drax Hall**. The church is part of the St. Ann's Bay Cure, serving a community that originated as a post-Emancipation free village for formerly enslaved people on land purchased from the Seville estate.

## History
**Pre-English settlement: Sevilla la Nueva (1509–1534).** Priory sits beside the historic Seville sugar estate — the very ground of **Sevilla la Nueva**, the first permanent European settlement in Jamaica and the colony's first Spanish capital, founded in **1509** by Juan de Esquivel and abandoned in 1534 when the Spanish capital was moved to Villa de la Vega (Spanish Town). **Christopher Columbus** had been marooned at St. Ann's Bay for over a year (1503–1504) on his fourth voyage.

**English rule (1655) and the Seville sugar estate.** Under English rule from **1655**, the Spanish settlement gave way to the **Seville sugar estate**, one of the most important sugar properties on the north coast. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds; its vestry was composed of the planter class. The enslaved population of Seville and the neighbouring Drax Hall estate produced the wealth that defined St. Ann's coastal economy.

**Sam Sharpe Rebellion and the Colonial Church Union (1831–32).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, **Hamilton Brown** of Brown's Town led a Colonial Church Union mob that destroyed the Brown's Town Baptist chapel in 1832 — the **Colonial Church Union** sought to "wipe the non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth." **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation, free villages, and the founding of Priory.** Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. The village of **Priory** was established on land purchased from the Seville estate by Protestant reformers to create a free settlement for formerly enslaved individuals — part of the wider post-Emancipation free-village movement that also produced **Sturge Town** (1839, founded by the English Quaker abolitionist **Joseph Sturge**) and **Clarksonville** (1835, founded by Baptist missionary Rev. John Clark and named for the abolitionist Thomas Clarkson) elsewhere in St. Ann. As Priory grew into a permanent village, St. Agnes was founded as a mission of the **St. Ann Parish Church** in St. Ann's Bay.

**Disestablishment (1870) and Nuttall expansion.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. Historical records note that the St. Agnes Mission was restored during the 20th century, linking it directly to the long-term development of the Anglican Church in St. Ann.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** Bauxite mining began in St. Ann around **1952** (Reynolds, Kaiser); tourism grew at nearby Ocho Rios. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Agnes a settled mission of the parish church.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of St. Agnes Priory after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
While no formal architectural survey is available, St. Agnes is consistently listed in directories as a mission church on the Priory to Bamboo Road. Visual evidence depicts a modest, rectangular chapel built from masonry or blockwork. Its design — a pitched roof, plain windows, and a low tower or porch — is characteristic of late 19th and early 20th-century Anglican mission churches in rural Jamaica. The church is set just inland from the coast, near Priory Primary and Infant School.

## Clergy
As a mission within the **St. Ann's Bay Cure**, St. Agnes is served by the same rector and team that minister to St. Ann Parish Church and other outstations. The week-to-week liturgical and community life of the church is sustained by lay leaders, including wardens, readers, and Mothers' Union members.

## Notable Facts
St. Agnes occupies a uniquely historic landscape. It is located near the **Seville Heritage Park**, which encompasses the ruins of the first Spanish settlement and later British sugar works. The church itself serves the community established on part of this estate after Emancipation. This places St. Agnes at the confluence of Jamaica's layered history, connecting the era of enslavement at Drax Hall and Seville with the post-Emancipation free villages and modern residential developments. The dedication to **St. Agnes** — the early-fourth-century Roman martyr (feast day **21 January**) — is one of the more distinctive Anglican dedications in Jamaica.
