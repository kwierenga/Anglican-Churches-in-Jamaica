# St. Barnabas', George's Plain
**Westmoreland** · Church · active

St. Barnabas' Anglican Church serves the community of George's Plain, a district on the Westmoreland coastal plain north of Savanna-la-Mar. The plain is part of Jamaica's most extensive lowland sugar belt, and the community that grew here after Emancipation was composed largely of descendants of people enslaved on the surrounding estates. St. Barnabas' was established to provide Anglican worship and pastoral care in this rural flat-land setting, and it continues to serve a farming and small-trade community.

## History
George's Plain lies on the broad agricultural lowland that stretches north of Savanna-la-Mar, historically one of the most intensively cultivated sugar-producing areas in all of Jamaica. After Emancipation, freed families settled across this plain in small villages, working as wage labourers on the remaining estates or cultivating their own provisions. The Anglican Diocese extended its reach across this landscape by establishing mission chapels like St. Barnabas', connecting isolated rural communities to the wider church. The church's dedication to Barnabas — the apostle known as the 'Son of Encouragement' — was a common choice for mission churches serving post-Emancipation communities.

## Architecture
St. Barnabas' is a small masonry chapel suited to the flat, low-lying terrain of the Westmoreland plain. Such buildings in this area were typically simple rectangular structures with pitched zinc roofs and louvred windows, reflecting the need for ventilation in the hot coastal interior and some degree of storm resistance. The flat plain geography offers no natural elevation, making buildings on the George's Plain particularly vulnerable to storm surge and flooding during major hurricane events.

## Clergy
The church has been served within the Savanna-la-Mar deanery, grouped with St. George's Parish Church and other Westmoreland congregations under shared clergy arrangements. The congregation's ongoing ministry has depended on wardens and lay leaders who sustain worship and community life. Funeral notices and diocesan records confirm the church remains active as a venue for the pastoral marking of life's milestones in the George's Plain community.

## Notable Facts
The flat coastal plain on which St. Barnabas' sits is one of Jamaica's most storm-surge-vulnerable landscapes. Without the protection of hills or significant elevation, the George's Plain is exposed to the full force of surge from the Caribbean Sea. Hurricane Charlie (1951) sent surge inland across the Westmoreland plain, flooding communities far from the coast. Hurricane Gilbert (1988) again brought damaging conditions. Hurricane Ivan (2004) caused serious flooding across the southern plain. Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on 28 October 2025, subjecting the George's Plain to surge and high winds; the low-lying community around St. Barnabas' was in the direct impact zone.
