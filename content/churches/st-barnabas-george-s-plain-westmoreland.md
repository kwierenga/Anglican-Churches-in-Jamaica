# St. Barnabas', George's Plain
**Westmoreland** · Church · active

St. Barnabas' Anglican Church serves the community of George's Plain, a district on the Westmoreland coastal plain north of Savanna-la-Mar. The plain is part of Jamaica's most extensive lowland sugar belt, and the community that grew here after Emancipation was composed largely of descendants of people enslaved on the surrounding estates. Jamaican Ancestral Records maintains a tombstone index for St. Barnabas' Anglican, George's Plain.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1703).** Westmoreland was officially formed in **1703** from the western portion of the older parish of St. Elizabeth, named for its position at the western part of the island. The parish capital, **Savanna-la-Mar**, was founded around 1730. In 1723 the new parish of Hanover was carved out of Westmoreland's northern section.

**Slavery and the sugar economy on the Westmoreland plain.** Under English rule, sugar and slavery accelerated in Westmoreland: an inventory in **1722** numbered the estates at **73**, making the parish one of the leading sugar producers of the British Caribbean. The Beckford family — Richard, Peter, and William — were among the most prominent planters; modern Petersfield is named for Peter Beckford. George's Plain itself lies on the broad agricultural lowland that stretches north of Savanna-la-Mar, historically one of the most intensively cultivated sugar-producing areas in all of Jamaica. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds; its parish vestry was composed of the planter class, and its rectors held glebe lands worked by enslaved people.

**The Sam Sharpe Rebellion (December 1831).** The Christmas Rebellion of 1831, led by the Baptist deacon Samuel Sharpe and ignited at the Kensington Estate in St. James on 27 December 1831, spread rapidly through the western parishes — St. James, Trelawny, Hanover, and Westmoreland — with some 60,000 enslaved people involved. The Colonial Church Union's vigilante destruction of chapels included Sav.-la-Mar (Ellis, *Diocese of Jamaica*, 1913). Sharpe was hanged on 23 May 1832 and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation and post-1838 settlement.** The British Parliament passed the Slavery Abolition Act in 1833; Emancipation came in 1834, and full freedom followed on **1 August 1838**. By 1854 the number of active sugar estates in Westmoreland had collapsed to 34, less than half the 1722 figure. After Emancipation, freed families settled across the plain in small villages, working as wage labourers on the remaining estates or cultivating their own provisions. The Anglican Diocese extended its reach across this landscape by establishing mission chapels like St. Barnabas', connecting isolated rural communities to the wider church. Germans were settled at Seaford Town in **1839**; East Indian indentured labourers arrived from the 1840s onward.

**1865 to 1900: the post-disestablishment Diocese.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865, Crown Colony rule replaced the planter Assembly (1866); the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica. Bishop Enos Nuttall's tenure (1880–1916) saw the Diocese grow from 26 rural mission stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. St. Barnabas' is listed in the Jamaican Ancestral Records cemetery index as an established Anglican station of the Westmoreland circuit.

**1912 hurricane, 1938 Frome riots, Independence.** A November **1912** hurricane brought a tidal wave that nearly destroyed Savanna-la-Mar. The **1938 Frome labour riots** at the Frome sugar factory contributed directly to the formation of Jamaica's Trade Union Movement and the rise of Sir Alexander Bustamante. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Barnabas' a settled mission within the Westmoreland Anglican network.

## Architecture
St. Barnabas' is a small masonry chapel suited to the flat, low-lying terrain of the Westmoreland plain — typically a simple rectangular structure with a pitched zinc roof and louvred windows, reflecting the need for ventilation in the hot coastal interior and some degree of storm resistance. The flat-plain geography offers no natural elevation, making buildings on George's Plain particularly vulnerable to storm surge and flooding during major hurricane events.

## Clergy
The church has been served within the Savanna-la-Mar deanery, grouped with St. George's Parish Church and other Westmoreland congregations under shared clergy arrangements. The congregation's ongoing ministry has depended on wardens and lay leaders who sustain worship and community life. Funeral notices and diocesan records confirm the church remains active as a venue for the pastoral marking of life's milestones in the George's Plain community.

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** **St. Barnabas** ("son of encouragement") was the early-Christian Cypriot Levite who introduced Saul (Paul) to the apostles after his conversion (Acts 9:27) and accompanied him on the first missionary journey. His feast day is **11 June**. The dedication was a common choice for mission churches serving post-Emancipation communities, and St. Barnabas serves as the dedication of three Westmoreland Anglican churches (George's Plain, Beeston Spring, and the deanery cure church).

**Major hurricane history.** The flat coastal plain on which St. Barnabas' sits is one of Jamaica's most storm-surge-vulnerable landscapes. Hurricane Charlie (1951) sent surge inland across the Westmoreland plain, flooding communities far from the coast. Hurricane Gilbert (1988) again brought damaging conditions. Hurricane Ivan (2004) caused serious flooding across the southern plain. **Hurricane Melissa** made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history — subjecting the George's Plain to surge and high winds; the low-lying community around St. Barnabas' was in the direct impact zone. Across the Diocese, some **50 church buildings were destroyed or significantly damaged**.
