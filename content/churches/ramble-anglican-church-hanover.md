# Ramble Anglican Church
**Hanover** · Church · ruin

Ramble Anglican Church was a rural parish church in the community of Ramble, in the interior uplands of eastern Hanover near the St. James border. Dated in contemporary reporting to around 1925, it stood for a century as a place of worship and an informal tourist landmark before being demolished by Hurricane Melissa on 28 October 2025. No dedication patron is recorded in the public sources reviewed; the Jamaica Gleaner refers to the building simply as the "100-year-old Ramble Anglican Church."

## History
Hanover was established on 12 November 1723 from parts of Westmoreland and St. James, named for the ruling House of Hanover. Ramble is one of the parish's named towns (alongside Green Island, Sandy Bay, Dias, Cascade, and Lucea), and the community sits in the hills at about 200 metres elevation, close to Montpelier and the old sugar properties of the interior. The community does not appear in the nineteenth-century Jamaica Almanacs or the 1891 Handbook of Jamaica's Anglican returns, which indicates that the church was established later than 1891 — consistent with the Jamaica Gleaner's description of it as a century old in 2025. It most likely emerged during Bishop Enos Nuttall's expansion of rural mission stations: the Diocese grew from 26 such stations in 1880 to 122 by 1911, and the pattern of small church foundations in the hills of western Jamaica was a direct consequence of that programme.

Over its century of life, Ramble served as a place of worship for the surrounding community and — according to the Jamaica Gleaner — also as a landmark that tourists regularly used as a backdrop for keepsake photographs.

## Architecture
The building was described in contemporary reporting as "magnificent." No detailed architectural description has been identified; the church was one of several small rural Anglican churches in western Jamaica that combined community ministry with heritage-landmark status for passing visitors.

## Hurricane Melissa (October 2025)
Hurricane Melissa made landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on 28 October 2025 as a Category 5 storm. The Jamaica Gleaner reported that the 100-year-old Ramble Anglican Church was "all-but-completely damaged" by the hurricane's winds. A motorist quoted in the article said: "It is going to be hard to rebuild, but we would love to have back our church." Ramble was the most prominent Anglican loss confirmed in Hanover parish in the immediate aftermath of Melissa, and was among some 50 church buildings destroyed or significantly damaged across the Diocese of Jamaica and the Cayman Islands.

## Recent coverage

A century after its construction, Ramble Anglican Church — already a popular roadside photo-stop for visitors to west Hanover — was reduced to ruin by Hurricane Melissa in October 2025, and is listed among the heritage landmarks "all-but-completely damaged" by the storm ([Jamaica Gleaner, 7 Nov 2025](https://jamaica-gleaner.com/article/lead-stories/20251107/historic-landmarks-out-west-among-melissas-many-victims)).
