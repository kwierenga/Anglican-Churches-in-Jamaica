# St. John's, Portland Cottage
**Clarendon** · Church · inactive

St. John's Anglican Church serves **Portland Cottage** on the south coast of Clarendon, in the same cure circuit as St. Peter's Alley, St. Saviour's Milk River, and St. Thomas' Race Course. Portland Cottage is a fishing village on the south coast plain at the western edge of Clarendon's coastal zone, near the border with St. Elizabeth. The church is served from the **Lionel Town** rectory.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1664).** Clarendon parish was created in **1664**, nine years after the English seizure of Jamaica from Spain (**1655**), and named for **Sir Edward Hyde, 1st Earl of Clarendon** (1609–1674), Lord High Chancellor of England under Charles II. The original parish church was the **Church of the White Cross** at "The Cross" — Jamaica's third Anglican church. **St. Paul's Chapelton** was built as a chapel of ease in **1666** and became the parish church when the Cross Church was destroyed in **1815**.

**Vere parish and the southern plain (1673–1867).** **Vere** was carved out as a separate sugar-plain parish in **1673**, taking in the southern coastal plain — including Portland Cottage. **St. Peter's Alley** (founded 1671, in the district then known as Withywood; the present building was erected around 1715 on the foundations of the original) was Vere's parish church and remains the third-oldest Anglican church in Jamaica. Vere was reabsorbed into Clarendon in **1867**, and **Law 20 of 1867** designated **May Pen** as chief town of the merged parish. Lionel Town developed as an estate-village settlement during this same period and became the modern centre of the Lionel Town circuit serving the south Clarendon coast.

**Slavery, Sam Sharpe, and Emancipation.** The sugar estates of the Vere plain depended on the labour of thousands of enslaved Africans. The Anglican Church served as the established church of this system. One of Jamaica's earliest slave rebellions erupted at **Suttons Plantation in 1690**: some 500 enslaved people rose in revolt, and many of those who escaped joined the Maroon communities eventually led by **Cudjoe** into the Cockpit Country. During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**, a vigilante alliance that sought to "wipe the non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of the Portland Cottage mission.** Portland Cottage is a coastal community on the south Clarendon shoreline, directly exposed to the Caribbean Sea. It sits at the extreme western end of the Clarendon coast, near the St. Elizabeth border. The community is primarily a fishing settlement. St. John's serves this village as part of the larger Alley/Milk River/Race Course cure based at Lionel Town (telephone 987-5874). Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

**Twentieth century: bauxite, Independence.** **Alcoa** began bauxite operations in Clarendon in **1959**; the **Jamalco** alumina refinery at Halse Hall opened in **1972**. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. John's a settled mission of the Lionel Town circuit.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Hurricane Melissa would have posed a direct threat to this south-coast community and the other coastal cures in this circuit; the specific status of St. John's Portland Cottage after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. John's is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural Clarendon in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. Its coastal setting brings exposure to salt air and periodic storm surge, demanding robust masonry construction.

## Clergy
The Alley/Milk River/Race Course/Portland Cottage cure is served by **Ulric Wilson** from the Lionel Town rectory. This cure covers the entire south-western coastal strip of Clarendon.

## Notable Facts
Portland Cottage is one of the most remote coastal communities in Clarendon, sitting on the flat, mangrove-fringed shoreline west of the main coastal road. The area is directly vulnerable to storm surge from the Caribbean Sea.
