# St. Martin's Mission, Bull Bay
**St. Thomas** · Church · active

St. Martin's Anglican Mission serves the **Bull Bay** community on the south coast of Jamaica, ten miles east of Kingston on the boundary between St. Andrew and St. Thomas. The mission has been ministering in the Bull Bay community since the **1800s**.

## History
**English rule (1655) and the founding parish.** Under English rule from **1655**, St. Thomas was one of Jamaica's original parishes — originally **"St. Thomas-in-the-East"** with the first rector appointed in **1666**.

**Sugar economy and slavery.** St. Thomas was one of Jamaica's great sugar parishes. Large estates — Stokes Hall, Golden Grove, Albion, Holland — dominated the landscape, worked by enslaved Africans throughout the colonial period. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union** to "wipe non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb** (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**The Morant Bay Rebellion (11 October 1865).** On **11 October 1865**, **Paul Bogle** — a small farmer and Native Baptist deacon of **Stony Gut** — led a protest march to the Morant Bay courthouse. Governor **Edward John Eyre's** brutal response: **439 executed**, **600+ flogged**, **1,000+ homes burned**. Bogle was hanged on **24 October 1865**; **George William Gordon** was hanged on **23 October 1865**. **Both were declared National Heroes in 1965**.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall, and the founding of St. Martin's Mission.** Jamaica became a **Crown Colony in 1866**. Law 20 of 1867 absorbed **St. David** into St. Thomas. The Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. The Anglican Diocese established St. Martin's as a mission chapel during the nineteenth century to serve the Bull Bay coastal community. The mission's own public communications confirm the nineteenth-century founding ("ministering in the Bull Bay community since the 1800s"). St. Thomas Parish Church at Morant Bay was rebuilt as a brick structure in **1865** after the original eighteenth-century parish church at Church Corner had become dilapidated.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** Internal self-government came in **1944**. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** fulfilled the aspirations for which Bogle and Gordon died.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. **Hurricane Ivan (2004)** had earlier caused widespread damage on the south coast. Melissa brought direct coastal impacts to Bull Bay.

## Architecture
St. Martin's is a coastal mission building situated in the Bull Bay community. The exposed coastal position on the south coast creates direct vulnerability to storm surge during major hurricanes.

## Clergy
St. Martin's Mission, Bull Bay is served within the Diocese's **Eastern Jamaica Region**. The community sits administratively on the boundary line between St. Andrew (Kingston Region) and St. Thomas; St. Martin's belongs in the diocese's Eastern Jamaica Region directory under St. Thomas.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to **St. Martin of Tours** — fourth-century soldier-turned-bishop of Tours in Gaul, famous for the legend of cutting his cloak in half to share with a beggar (feast day **11 November**) — is one of the more theologically distinctive Anglican dedications in the Jamaican network. Martin's association with charity and care for the poor gives the mission an apt patron. Bull Bay's name derives from a colonial-era tradition; the village is on the highway east from Kingston toward Yallahs and Morant Bay, in the narrow coastal strip between the southern slopes of the Blue Mountains and the Caribbean Sea.
