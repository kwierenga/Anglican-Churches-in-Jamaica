# St. John's, Woburn Lawn
**St. Thomas** · Church · active

St. John's Anglican Church serves **Woburn Lawn**, a small farming district in the interior of St. Thomas parish.

## History
**English rule (1655) and the founding parish.** Under English rule from **1655**, St. Thomas was one of Jamaica's original parishes — originally **"St. Thomas-in-the-East"** with the first rector appointed in **1666**.

**Sugar economy and slavery.** St. Thomas was one of Jamaica's great sugar parishes. Large estates — Stokes Hall, Golden Grove, Albion, Holland — dominated the landscape, worked by enslaved Africans throughout the colonial period. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union** to "wipe non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb** (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**The Morant Bay Rebellion (11 October 1865).** On **11 October 1865**, **Paul Bogle** — a small farmer and Native Baptist deacon of **Stony Gut** — led a protest march to the Morant Bay courthouse. Governor **Edward John Eyre's** brutal response: **439 executed**, **600+ flogged**, **1,000+ homes burned**. Bogle was hanged on **24 October 1865**; **George William Gordon** was hanged on **23 October 1865**. **Both were declared National Heroes in 1965**.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall.** Jamaica became a **Crown Colony in 1866**. Law 20 of 1867 absorbed **St. David** into St. Thomas. The Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

**Foundation of St. John's.** Woburn Lawn sits in the interior of St. Thomas parish, away from the coastal communities that characterise much of the parish's settled landscape. The Anglican Diocese established St. John's as a mission chapel to serve this interior community in the post-Emancipation period.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** Internal self-government came in **1944**. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** fulfilled the aspirations for which Bogle and Gordon died. **Hurricane Ivan (2004)** caused widespread damage across St. Thomas, affecting both coastal and interior communities.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Hurricane Melissa brought wind and rain impacts to interior St. Thomas; the Woburn Lawn community was in the affected zone.

## Architecture
St. John's is a small masonry chapel in the interior of St. Thomas. The building sits in a farming landscape away from the direct coastal exposure of the south-coast parishes, though St. Thomas's interior is subject to the heavy rainfall associated with the Blue Mountains to the north.

## Clergy
Woburn Lawn has been served within the **St. Thomas deanery** under shared clergy arrangements. Lay leaders sustain the church's pastoral life between clerical visits.

## Notable Facts
St. Thomas parish stretches from the south coast north to the Blue Mountains, creating a diverse landscape from coastal sugar estates to mountain farming communities. **Woburn Lawn** represents the interior agricultural landscape of the parish. The dedication to **St. John** — most commonly the Evangelist (feast 27 December), occasionally the Baptist (24 June) — is one of the most common Anglican dedications.
