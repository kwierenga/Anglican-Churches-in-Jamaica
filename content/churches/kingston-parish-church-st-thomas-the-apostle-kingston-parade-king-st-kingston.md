# Kingston Parish Church (St. Thomas the Apostle), Kingston (Parade/King St)
**Kingston** · Church · active

Kingston Parish Church — formally dedicated to St. Thomas the Apostle — is the historic parish church of Kingston, situated at 70B King Street near the Parade in the heart of the city. It is one of the most historically significant churches in Jamaica, described by reviewers as 'a historical grandeur that sits in the heart of Kingston' and notable for containing the remains of Admiral John Benbow, the British naval hero who died in Jamaica in 1702, lying seven feet below the church floor. The church has stood at the centre of Jamaican national life for more than three centuries.

## History
Kingston Parish Church was established following the destruction of Port Royal by the 1692 earthquake and tsunami, as the survivors relocated to the north shore of the harbour and founded the new city of Kingston. The church became the principal place of Anglican worship for the colonial capital, serving the merchant class, government officials, and citizens of Jamaica's most important city. Among its most celebrated historical associations is the burial of Admiral John Benbow, who died at Port Royal in 1702 from wounds received in battle against a French fleet off Cartagena — his remains were interred in the church, where they rest to this day. The church has witnessed the full sweep of Jamaican history: the plantation era, the Abolition of the Slave Trade (1807), Emancipation (1838), the 1907 Kingston earthquake, and independence in 1962.

## Architecture
Kingston Parish Church is a substantial stone building on King Street, with stained glass windows and a historic interior that carries the memorial plaques and architectural details of three centuries of Kingston life. The Parade area of downtown Kingston, where the church stands, was historically the public heart of the colonial capital. The church has been restored and maintained through multiple disaster events including the 1907 earthquake, which devastated much of downtown Kingston. The low-lying coastal position of downtown Kingston creates flooding and storm surge vulnerability during major hurricane events.

## Clergy
As the parish church of Kingston, this church has been the principal see of the Anglican presence in the capital and a key institution of the Diocese of Jamaica and the Cayman Islands. Its rectors have included some of the most prominent clergy in Jamaican history, serving a congregation drawn from the commercial and professional life of the capital. The church has been a venue for state occasions, memorial services, and civic events of national significance.

## Notable Facts
The burial of Admiral John Benbow beneath the church floor is one of the most singular historical distinctions of any Jamaican church. Benbow was a celebrated naval commander who pursued a French fleet under Admiral du Casse in the Caribbean in 1702 despite being wounded and abandoned by several of his captains; his courage under adversity made him a popular hero in England. Two of his captains were later court-martialled and shot for cowardice. Benbow died at Port Royal from his wounds and was buried with full honours at Kingston Parish Church, where a reviewer notes his remains lie seven feet below the floor — 'a must see for visitors to the island capital.' The church also hosted a service for the 290th anniversary of Wolmers Trust School, one of Jamaica's oldest educational institutions.
