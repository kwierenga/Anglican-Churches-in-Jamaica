# St. George's, Mile Gully (Duppy Church)

**Manchester** · Church · ruin

St. George's Anglican Church in Mile Gully is a roofless ruin located in the district of Weir on the outskirts of Mile Gully, on the B6. Completed around 1835, it served the newly formed parish of Manchester and was one of the earliest Anglican churches in the area. The building has been abandoned for generations and is known throughout Jamaica as the "Duppy Church" — a landmark of local folklore and one of the most atmospheric ruins on the island.

## History

St. George's was built shortly after the formation of Manchester parish in 1814, serving the Anglican community of Mile Gully and surrounding districts. The church was eventually abandoned under circumstances that gave rise to enduring local legend. The congregation relocated, and the building fell into ruin. The active Anglican congregation in Mile Gully is now served by St. Paul's and St. Barnabas' churches in the town.

## Architecture

The building is a simple rectangular stone nave, now roofless and open to the sky. The thick walls remain largely intact, and a few memorial monuments survive on the interior walls. The surrounding churchyard contains old graves. The structure sits on the B6 road in the district of Weir, several kilometres northwest of Mile Gully town.

## Notable Facts

The ruin is known locally as "Duppy Church" — duppy being the Jamaican word for ghost. Local tradition holds that mournful music and congregational sounds have been heard emanating from the building at night. According to one account, a parson hanged himself in the doorway after a dispute with his congregation, after which the organ was removed to another location. The crossroads near the church takes its name from the ruin. Lonely Planet describes it as a genuinely creepy place and a notable landmark of southern Jamaica.
