# St. Aidan's, Bull Savannah
**St. Elizabeth** · Church · active

St. Aidan's Anglican Church serves Bull Savannah, a community on the south coast of St. Elizabeth parish in the dry plains country between the Black River Morass to the west and the Santa Cruz Mountains to the east. The community is one of several "Savannah" districts of southern Jamaica — open grassland zones whose names ("Bull Savannah," "Burnt Savannah," "Lacovia Savannah") record the cattle-grazing economy that characterised these dry coastal flats during and after the plantation era. The church maintains a public Facebook presence under the page "St. Aidan Anglican Church, Bull Savannah, St. Elizabeth."

## History
**Founding of the parish (c.1664).** St. Elizabeth was one of the original Jamaican parishes, established under Sir Thomas Modyford and named in honour of **Lady Elizabeth Modyford**, wife of the first Governor of Jamaica (1664–71). Prior to **1703**, the parish stretched across the territory of present-day Westmoreland, Hanover, and Manchester; in 1703 Westmoreland was created from the western portion, and in **1814** the final subdivision created Manchester from the southern section. **Lacovia** was the original parish capital before Black River became the sole capital. The parish church, **St. John the Evangelist** at Black River, dates structurally from around 1700.

**Slavery and the cattle-grazing economy.** Under English rule, sugar and slavery defined the parish economy: prominent estates included Holland, Vineyard, and Fullerswood. The Spanish-era cattle-grazing tradition on the **Pedro Plains** — the plains east and south of Bull Savannah — was preserved into the British period as a complement to sugar; the "Savanna" districts of southern St. Elizabeth (Bull Savannah, Burnt Savannah) record this open-grassland landscape. **Cudjoe**, leader of the Leeward Maroons, signed the **Peace Treaty of 1739** at the Peace Cave in St. Elizabeth's Cockpit Country, securing Maroon land rights — and the Maroon community of **Accompong Town** still exists in the parish today. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**The Sam Sharpe Rebellion (December 1831).** The Christmas Rebellion of 1831, led by the Baptist deacon **Samuel Sharpe** at the Kensington Estate in St. James on **27 December 1831**, spread through the western parishes; some 60,000 enslaved people were involved. Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation and post-1838 settlement.** The British Parliament passed the Slavery Abolition Act in 1833; Emancipation came in 1834, and full freedom followed at the end of the apprenticeship period on **1 August 1838**. Freed people settled the upland and savannah districts of southern St. Elizabeth as small farmers — Bull Savannah belongs to that wave of post-Emancipation free settlement. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865, Crown Colony rule replaced the planter Assembly (1866); the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of St. Aidan's: Nuttall's expansion.** Bishop Enos Nuttall's tenure as Bishop of Jamaica (1880–1916) saw the Diocese grow from 26 rural mission stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. The church does not appear in the 1891 Handbook of Jamaica's Anglican clergy returns, consistent with a foundation date in the late phase of Nuttall's expansion.

**Twentieth century, Independence, hurricanes.** The 1893–94 logwood export boom shaped the parish economy. The bauxite industry developed in St. Elizabeth from **1943**, with **ALPART** later headquartered at Nain. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Aidan's a settled rural mission.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The flat coastal position offers no shelter from storm surge; the community shared in the broader hurricane vulnerability of the south-coast plains, including the destruction of nearby parishes. The specific status of St. Aidan's after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. Aidan's is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for mission stations across the St. Elizabeth south coast. The flat coastal position offers no shelter from storm surge during major hurricanes.

## Clergy
St. Aidan's Bull Savannah is served within the Montego Bay Region's St. Elizabeth deanery network.

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** **St. Aidan of Lindisfarne** (d. 651) — the seventh-century Irish monk who founded the great monastery of Lindisfarne off the Northumbrian coast at the invitation of King Oswald, and is regarded as the apostle of northern England — is one of the more theologically distinctive Anglican dedications in Jamaica. Aidan's choice for a small Caribbean farming community evokes the parallel of monastic mission in difficult terrain. His feast day is **31 August**.
