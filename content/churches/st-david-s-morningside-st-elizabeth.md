# St. David's, Morningside
**St. Elizabeth** · Church · unknown

St. David's Anglican Church serves Morningside in St. Elizabeth, listed as a cure in the Southfield circuit. Morningside is an aspirationally named community — 'morning side' evoking the east-facing slope that catches the first light of day, a common optimistic naming pattern in post-Emancipation Jamaica. St. David's dedication to the patron saint of Wales is a modest but meaningful presence of Welsh heritage in the southern St. Elizabeth hills.

## History
Morningside sits in the interior hills of southern St. Elizabeth, in the Southfield cure circuit served by Olando Gayle. The aspirational 'morningside' name follows a pattern of post-Emancipation community naming that expressed hope, spiritual identity, and a new relationship with the land.

## Architecture
St. David's is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural St. Elizabeth in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. Set in the rural interior of St. Elizabeth. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
The Southfield cure covering Morningside is served by Olando Gayle from the Southfield rectory.

## Notable Facts
St. David, patron saint of Wales, is one of the less common dedications in Jamaica's Anglican network, where Scottish (St. Andrew) and English (St. George) patron saints appear far more frequently. This reflects the relatively smaller Welsh component in Jamaican colonial history compared to English, Scottish, and Irish influences. David's feast day (1 March) falls at the end of Jamaica's dry season.
