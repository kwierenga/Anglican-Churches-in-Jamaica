# St. David's, Morningside
**St. Elizabeth** · Church · inactive

St. David's Anglican Church stands in Morningside, a small farming community in the southern interior of St. Elizabeth between Nain and Southfield. Listed as inactive, the church is still carried on the diocesan rolls under the Southfield Cure served by Rev. Olando Gayle. The dedication to St. David — patron saint of Wales — is one of the rarer Anglican dedications in Jamaica, a modest marker of Welsh heritage in the south St. Elizabeth hills.

## History
Morningside sits in the hilly terrain of the south coast interior — drier, more rugged country that was less suited to large-scale sugar but characteristic of the post-emancipation smallholding pattern where freed families established independent farming communities after 1838. The name "Morningside" follows a documented tradition of aspirational place-naming by freed people: the "morning side" evoking the east-facing slope that catches the first light of day. Similar optimistic names — Content, Comfort, Friendship, Good Hope, Top Hill — are found across Jamaica and reflect the hope that formerly enslaved people invested in their new settlements.

The south coast of St. Elizabeth was plantation country throughout the colonial period. Southfield Pen, nearby, was founded around 1740 with 80 enslaved people under Andrew Bromfield by 1796. Yardley Chase, a 1,500-acre coffee and cotton plantation, is remembered for the Lovers' Leap legend — where enslaved lovers Mizzy and Tunkey chose death over separation. After Emancipation in 1834 and full freedom on 1 August 1838, freed people settled on subdivided lands across this terrain, building the farming communities that churches like St. David's were established to serve.

St. David's was likely established in the late nineteenth century as part of the wave of Anglican mission expansion that followed disestablishment in 1870. Under Bishop Enos Nuttall (1880–1916), mission stations grew from 26 to 122 across Jamaica. By 1900, Rev. C. T. Rickard served "Plains, Mayfield, and 8 mission stations" in the south coast area — St. David's may have been among them. The Anglican Church was reinventing itself during this period, transforming from the established church of the planter class — widely known as the "church of slave-owners" — into an institution that served the freed Black majority through worship and education.

Jamaica's independence on 6 August 1962 accelerated changes that challenged small rural churches. Younger generations migrated to Kingston, Montego Bay, and overseas. Bauxite mining disrupted traditional farming communities in the Nain area from the 1960s onward. Recurring hurricanes damaged buildings that small congregations lacked the resources to repair. At some point, regular worship at St. David's ceased, though the Diocese continues to list it under the Southfield Cure — a six-church circuit spanning a wide area of southern St. Elizabeth.

## Architecture
St. David's is a modest masonry chapel typical of the rural Anglican mission stations built across St. Elizabeth in the late nineteenth century — simple, functional, and suited to a small farming congregation.

## Education
No school specifically associated with St. David's was identified. The Anglican mission to educate was central to the church's broader purpose in the region: Bishop Lipscomb established 142 elementary schools across Jamaica in the 1830s, and rural churches routinely provided basic instruction. Munro College (1856) and Hampton School (1874), both Anglican-founded, are the premier educational institutions in St. Elizabeth.

## Clergy
St. David's is listed under the Southfield Cure served by Rev. Olando Gayle, alongside St. Mary's Southfield, St. Mark's Mayfield, St. Alban's Stanmore, St. Paul's Tryall, and St. Aidan's Bull Savannah. The scale of the cure — six churches across a wide area — reflects the challenge of maintaining an Anglican presence in Jamaica's rural interior with limited clergy.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to St. David (Dewi Sant), the sixth-century Welsh bishop canonised in 1120, is uncommon in Jamaica's Anglican network. While St. Andrew (Scotland), St. George (England), and St. Patrick (Ireland) are frequent church dedications, St. David represents the smaller Welsh presence in Jamaica. His feast day is 1 March. His famous last words to his monks — "Be joyful, keep the faith, and do the little things" — carry resonance for a small rural congregation that did the quiet, steady work of faith in a hillside community.
