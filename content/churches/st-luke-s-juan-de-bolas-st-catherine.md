# St. Luke's, Juan-de-Bolas
**St. Catherine** · Church · active

St. Luke's Anglican Church serves **Juan-de-Bolas**, a community in the upland **Lluidas Vale** area of central St. Catherine. The community takes its name from one of the most important early figures in Jamaican history, the seventeenth-century Black guerrilla leader **Juan Lubolo / Juan de Bolas**.

## History
**Spanish Villa de la Vega (1534), Juan de Bolas, and English conquest (1655).** The Spanish founded **Villa de la Vega** in 1534 — Jamaica's capital until **1872**. **Juan Lubolo** (known to the Spanish as Juan Lubolo and to the English as **Juan de Bolas**) was a Black guerrilla leader and freedom fighter — an enslaved African during the Spanish occupation of Jamaica that ended in 1655. In **1655**, Penn and Venables's English expedition captured Jamaica from Spain; Juan de Bolas led a group of formerly enslaved Africans into the hills of **Lluidas Vale, St. Catherine**, to fight against the new English regime. He later switched allegiance to the English, and the Lluidas Vale uplands where he established his stronghold became known as "Juan de Bolas country" — a name still attached to a hill, a district, and now a church in the area. The Jamaica National Heritage Trust maintains the **Juan de Bolas Marker** in the centre of Lluidas Vale town in commemoration.

**Founding parishes.** Under English rule from 1655, **St. Catherine** was one of Jamaica's original parishes. The neighbouring early parishes of **St. John, St. Dorothy, and St. Thomas-in-the-Vale** were merged into expanded St. Catherine under **Law 20 of 1867**.

**Worthy Park and the Lluidas Vale sugar economy.** The **Worthy Park Estate** in Lluidas Vale — one of the oldest continuously operating sugar estates in the Caribbean (17th-century origin) — has long been the dominant economic presence in this upland valley. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds. **The Diocese of Jamaica** was established by Letters Patent on **24 July 1824**; **Christopher Lipscomb** was consecrated first Bishop in 1824.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838), Sligoville.** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Sligoville** in St. Catherine became Jamaica's **first free village** in 1838, founded with land provided by **Howe Peter Browne, 2nd Marquess of Sligo**, then Governor of Jamaica. Bishop Lipscomb (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. Capital moved from Spanish Town to **Kingston in 1872**.

**Foundation of St. Luke's.** The Anglican Diocese established St. Luke's as a mission chapel to serve the Juan-de-Bolas community within the wider Lluidas Vale upland landscape. The post-Emancipation settlement of the Lluidas Vale uplands by freed estate workers created the kind of small-farmer congregation that the diocese reached through chapels like this.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Luke's a settled rural mission. **Worthy Park** continued operations as one of the few sugar estates to survive into the modern era.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Hurricane Melissa brought wind and rain to upland St. Catherine.

## Architecture
St. Luke's is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for mission stations across the St. Catherine uplands. The high valley position creates wind exposure during storm passages and brings cooler conditions than the parish's coastal districts.

## Clergy
St. Luke's Juan-de-Bolas is served within the **Kingston Region's St. Catherine Deanery**.

## Notable Facts
**Juan de Bolas** stands as one of Jamaica's most consequential early-modern figures: a Black freedom fighter who first resisted the English conquest and later allied with them, leaving his name on the upland landscape of central Jamaica three and a half centuries later. The dedication to **St. Luke** — physician and evangelist (feast day **18 October**) — falls in the calmer period after hurricane season.
