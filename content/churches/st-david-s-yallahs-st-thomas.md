# St. David's, Yallahs
**St. Thomas** · Church · active

St. David's Anglican Church serves **Yallahs**, one of the larger communities on the south coast of St. Thomas parish, at the mouth of the **Yallahs River** at the western end of the parish. The **Yallahs Ponds** — a series of salt ponds separated from the sea by a narrow spit — lie nearby. The dedication is to **St. David**, patron saint of Wales (feast day **1 March**).

## History
**English rule (1655) and the founding parish.** Under English rule from **1655**, St. Thomas was one of Jamaica's original parishes — originally **"St. Thomas-in-the-East"** with the first rector appointed in **1666**. The 1867 reorganisation absorbed the **old parish of St. David** into St. Thomas — a fitting historical resonance for this Yallahs church which retains the St. David dedication.

**Sugar economy and slavery.** Yallahs lies at the westernmost end of St. Thomas parish, close to the St. Andrew boundary. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union** to "wipe non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb** (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**The Morant Bay Rebellion (11 October 1865).** On **11 October 1865**, **Paul Bogle** — a small farmer and Native Baptist deacon of **Stony Gut** — led a protest march to the Morant Bay courthouse. Governor **Edward John Eyre's** brutal response: **439 executed**, **600+ flogged**, **1,000+ homes burned**. Bogle was hanged on **24 October 1865**; **George William Gordon** was hanged on **23 October 1865**. **Both were declared National Heroes in 1965**.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall.** Jamaica became a **Crown Colony in 1866**. Law 20 of 1867 absorbed the **old parish of St. David** into St. Thomas. The Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

**Foundation of St. David's.** Yallahs developed at the mouth of the **Yallahs River**, one of the larger rivers draining the eastern Blue Mountains. The community lies at the westernmost end of St. Thomas parish, close to the St. Andrew boundary, and its coastal position made it significant in the banana and sugar economy of the south coast. The Anglican Diocese established St. David's at Yallahs as part of its south-coast mission network. The St. David dedication is particularly fitting given that the 1867 parish reorganisation absorbed the old parish of **St. David** into St. Thomas — preserving the Welsh patron's name in this Yallahs church on the parish boundary.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** Internal self-government came in **1944**. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** fulfilled the aspirations for which Bogle and Gordon died. The Yallahs River is subject to flash flooding during heavy rainfall events, and the community has experienced repeated flood damage across its history. **Hurricane Ivan (2004)** caused severe flooding across the Yallahs area.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Hurricane Melissa brought heavy rain to the south coast of St. Thomas; the Yallahs community and congregation were in the affected zone.

## Architecture
St. David's is a coastal church at the mouth of the Yallahs River, directly exposed to the Caribbean Sea. The compound risk of storm surge from the sea and flooding from the river makes Yallahs one of the more flood-vulnerable communities on the St. Thomas south coast. The church's construction reflects the requirements of a building in a coastal, riverside environment.

## Clergy
Yallahs has been served within the **St. Thomas deanery** under shared clergy arrangements. The western position of Yallahs near the St. Andrew boundary has sometimes connected it to the eastern St. Andrew ministry circuit.

## Notable Facts
The **Yallahs Ponds** are a distinctive natural feature of the Yallahs area — salt ponds separated from the sea by a narrow coastal spit that has been used for salt production historically. The Yallahs River catchment drains a significant portion of the eastern Blue Mountains, making it subject to periodic catastrophic flooding when heavy rain falls on the upper catchment. The dedication to **St. David** carries an extra historical resonance: the **old parish of St. David** was absorbed into St. Thomas under the 1867 Law 20 reorganisation, and this Yallahs church preserves the dedication.
