# St. David's, Yallahs
**St. Thomas** · Church · active

St. David's Anglican Church serves Yallahs, one of the larger communities on the south coast of St. Thomas parish at the western end of the parish, at the mouth of the Yallahs River. Yallahs is a coastal and riverside community with a distinctive landscape — the Yallahs Ponds, a series of salt ponds separated from the sea by a narrow spit, lie nearby. The church has served this coastal community for well over a century, providing Anglican ministry to the fishing and farming families of the Yallahs area.

## History
Yallahs developed at the mouth of the Yallahs River, one of the larger rivers draining the eastern Blue Mountains. The community lies at the westernmost end of St. Thomas parish, close to the St. Andrew boundary, and its coastal position made it significant in the banana and sugar economy of the south coast. The Anglican Diocese established St. David's at Yallahs as part of its south-coast mission network. The Yallahs River is subject to flash flooding during heavy rainfall events, and the community has experienced repeated flood damage across its history.

## Architecture
St. David's is a coastal church at the mouth of the Yallahs River, directly exposed to the Caribbean Sea. The compound risk of storm surge from the sea and flooding from the river makes Yallahs one of the more flood-vulnerable communities on the St. Thomas south coast. The church's construction reflects the requirements of a building in a coastal, riverside environment.

## Clergy
Yallahs has been served within the St. Thomas deanery under shared clergy arrangements. The western position of Yallahs near the St. Andrew boundary has sometimes connected it to the eastern St. Andrew ministry circuit.

## Notable Facts
The Yallahs Ponds are a distinctive natural feature of the Yallahs area — salt ponds separated from the sea by a narrow coastal spit that has been used for salt production historically. The Yallahs River catchment drains a significant portion of the eastern Blue Mountains, making it subject to periodic catastrophic flooding when heavy rain falls on the upper catchment. Hurricane Ivan (2004) caused severe flooding across the Yallahs area. Hurricane Melissa (October 2025) brought heavy rain to the south coast of St. Thomas; the Yallahs community and congregation were in the affected zone.
