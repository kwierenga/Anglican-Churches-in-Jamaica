# St. David's, Snowdon
**Manchester** · Church · active

St. David's Anglican Church serves Snowdon, a community in Manchester parish. Snowdon is a farming community in the interior of the parish, and the church has served this community for several generations. The dedication to St. David, patron of Wales, is one of only a small number of Welsh saint dedications in Jamaica's Anglican network.

## History
Snowdon sits in the interior of Manchester, its name evoking the highest peak in Wales — following the pattern of colonial estate names drawn from British landscape features. The Anglican Diocese established St. David's to serve this community, a dedication to the patron saint of Wales that may reflect the Welsh origins of a colonial proprietor.

## Architecture
St. David's is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural Manchester in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. Set in the rural interior of Manchester. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
Snowdon has been served within the Manchester deanery under shared clergy arrangements.

## Notable Facts
Snowdon is Wales' highest mountain and one of the most iconic landscape names in the British Isles. The Jamaican Snowdon's naming connects this interior Manchester community to the Welsh highlands. St. David (1 March) is the only one of the four patron saints of the British Isles whose feast day falls in the first quarter of the year. Welsh connections to Jamaica include several colonial-era families, and Welsh surnames appear in Jamaican records.
