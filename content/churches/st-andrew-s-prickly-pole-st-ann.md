# St. Andrew's, Prickly Pole
**St. Ann** · Church · active

St. Andrew's Anglican Church is a small mission church serving the rural community of **Prickly Pole** in the hills of St. Ann. As part of the St. Ann Deanery, it provides a local spiritual centre for a district that allows residents to worship without travelling to larger towns like Brown's Town or Claremont. The church, alongside the well-known Prickly Pole All-Age/Primary School, forms a crucial institutional pillar for the village. The community's name originates from the **prickly pole palm** that was once abundant in the area.

## History
**Pre-English settlement: Sevilla la Nueva (1509–1534).** St. Ann has a deeper colonial past than almost any other Jamaican parish. **Sevilla la Nueva**, the first permanent European settlement in Jamaica and the colony's first Spanish capital, was founded in **1509** by Juan de Esquivel near present St. Ann's Bay and served as Spanish capital until **1534**. **Christopher Columbus** had been marooned at St. Ann's Bay for over a year (1503–1504).

**English rule (1655) and the planter church.** Under English rule from **1655**, St. Ann became a major **sugar, pimento, and livestock** region. Like many districts in interior St. Ann, Prickly Pole developed on lands that were once sugar, pimento, and livestock estates worked by enslaved Africans, which later fragmented into small freehold farms after Emancipation. Until **1870**, the Church of England was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**Sam Sharpe Rebellion and the Colonial Church Union (1831–32).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, **Hamilton Brown** of Brown's Town led a Colonial Church Union mob that destroyed the Brown's Town Baptist chapel in 1832 — the **Colonial Church Union** sought to "wipe the non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth." **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation (1838) and free villages.** Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Sturge Town** (1839, founded by the English Quaker abolitionist **Joseph Sturge**) and **Clarksonville** (1835, founded by Baptist missionary Rev. John Clark, named for the abolitionist Thomas Clarkson) were among Jamaica's earliest free villages — both in St. Ann (NLJ, *History of St. Ann*). Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb** (1824–1843) had begun extending Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**Disestablishment (1870) and the Prickly Pole mission.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. By the mid-20th century, the community had established its own all-age school, and an Anglican congregation formed. St. Andrew's continues the story of post-slavery Anglican mission work, providing a local parish centre for a remote farming community.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** Bauxite mining began in St. Ann around **1952** (Reynolds, Kaiser); tourism grew at Ocho Rios. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Andrew's a settled mission of the Claremont cure.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of St. Andrew's Prickly Pole after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
No detailed architectural survey of St. Andrew's exists in public records. Based on the typical design of similar rural Anglican churches in St. Ann from the late 19th or early 20th century, it is likely a modest, rectangular chapel constructed from stone or concrete block. Its features probably include a simple porch or small bell-cote, a pitched zinc or tile roof, and plain windows, set within a fenced yard. The church is situated in a hilly landscape south of the main coastal highway, a region characterised by narrow, winding roads and steep slopes — making the area prone to landslides and placing churches and schools like St. Andrew's in the role of potential community shelters during natural disasters.

## Clergy
Within the diocesan structure, St. Andrew's is part of the **St. Ann Deanery** in the Montego Bay Region. It is grouped with other inland congregations such as St. Mark's in Brown's Town under a shared clergy team based in Claremont. The week-to-week life of this small mission church relies heavily on lay leadership — churchwardens, Mothers' Union members, and musicians.

## Notable Facts
**Prickly Pole** is named for the local prickly pole palm, linking the community's identity to a distinctive native plant. The village has been described in media reports as one of the poorer districts in St. Ann, with St. Andrew's and the local school serving as vital institutions for residents. In **September 2024**, a fatal roadway collapse highlighted the fragility of the area's infrastructure, underscoring the vulnerability of this hill community and the critical role its local institutions play in times of crisis. The dedication to **St. Andrew the apostle** (feast day **30 November**) — patron of Scotland — reflects the significant Scottish presence in the colonial-era plantation economy.
