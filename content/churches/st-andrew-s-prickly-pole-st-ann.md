# St. Andrew's, Prickly Pole
**St. Ann** · Church · active

St. Andrew’s Anglican Church is a small mission church serving the rural community of Prickly Pole in the hills of St. Ann. As part of the St. Ann Deanery, it provides a local spiritual centre for a district that faces socioeconomic challenges, allowing residents to worship without traveling to larger towns like Brown's Town or Claremont. The church, alongside the well-known Prickly Pole All-Age/Primary School, forms a crucial institutional pillar for the village, offering worship, community support, and a sense of stability in a remote and often vulnerable landscape.

## History
St. Andrew’s Anglican Church serves the small farming village of Prickly Pole, located in the interior hills of St. Ann near Claremont and Brown’s Town. The village's name originates from the prickly pole palm that was once abundant in the area. Like many districts in interior St. Ann, Prickly Pole developed on lands that were once sugar, pimento, and livestock estates worked by enslaved Africans, which later fragmented into small freehold farms after Emancipation. By the mid-20th century, the community had established its own all-age school, and subsequently, an Anglican congregation formed. Now listed in diocesan records, St. Andrew’s continues the story of post-slavery Anglican mission work, providing a local parish centre for a remote farming community.

## Architecture
No detailed architectural survey of St. Andrew’s exists in public records. Based on the typical design of similar rural Anglican churches in St. Ann from the late 19th or early 20th century, it is likely a modest, rectangular chapel constructed from stone or concrete block. Its features probably include a simple porch or small bell-cote, a pitched zinc or tile roof, and plain windows, set within a fenced yard. The church is situated in a hilly landscape south of the main coastal highway, a region characterized by narrow, winding roads and steep slopes. This location makes the area prone to landslides, placing churches and schools like St. Andrew’s in the role of potential community shelters during natural disasters.

## Clergy
Within the diocesan structure, St. Andrew’s is part of the St. Ann Deanery in the Montego Bay Region. It is grouped with other inland congregations such as St. Mark’s in Brown’s Town under a shared clergy team based in Claremont. The week-to-week life of this small mission church relies heavily on lay leadership—churchwardens, Mothers’ Union members, and musicians—who sustain Sunday services, funerals, and celebrations when the priest is rotating between churches. St. Andrew’s works alongside the Prickly Pole Primary and Infant School, together forming a church-and-school hub that is central to the community's family and spiritual life.

## Notable Facts
Prickly Pole is named for the local prickly pole palm, linking the community's identity to a distinctive native plant. The village has been described in media reports as one of the poorer districts in St. Ann, with St. Andrew’s and the local school serving as vital institutions for residents. The community has also faced national scrutiny, such as in 2015 when a tragic incident involving a school pupil sparked a national debate about child safety. More recently, in September 2024, a fatal roadway collapse highlighted the fragility of the area's infrastructure, underscoring the vulnerability of this hill community and the critical role its local institutions play in times of crisis.
