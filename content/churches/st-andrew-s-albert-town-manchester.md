# St. Andrew's, Albert Town
**Manchester** · Church · unknown

St. Andrew's Anglican Church at Albert Town is the principal church of a cure covering the far northern part of Manchester, close to the Trelawny border. The Albert Town cure also includes St. James' Craighead, St. Silas' Troy, St. Peter's Wait-a-Bit, and St. Barnabas' Warsop. Albert Town is a market town in the upper reaches of the Martha Brae watershed, at the northern edge of the Cockpit Country.

## History
Albert Town sits in the hills of northern Manchester near the Trelawny border, in the agricultural landscape on the edge of the Cockpit Country. The town serves as a market centre for the surrounding communities. The cure is based at the Albert Town rectory (tel: 610-1291) and covers five churches across a wide rural area.

## Architecture
St. Andrew's is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural Manchester in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. Set in the rural interior of Manchester. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
The Albert Town cure covers one of the most rural and geographically spread circuits in Manchester. The rectory address is Albert Town P.O., tel: 610-1291.

## Notable Facts
Albert Town was named for Prince Albert, Queen Victoria's consort, following the fashion for commemorating the royal family in Jamaican place names during the Victorian era. The community sits on the edge of the Cockpit Country, one of Jamaica's most rugged and ecologically significant landscapes. The Anglican presence in Albert Town connects the church to the remote northern interior of Manchester, far from the commercial capital at Santa Cruz or the parish capital at Mandeville.
