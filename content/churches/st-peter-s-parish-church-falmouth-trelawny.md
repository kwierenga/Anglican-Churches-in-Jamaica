# St. Peter's (Parish Church), Falmouth
**Trelawny** · Parish Church · active

St. Peter’s Anglican Church is a landmark in the heart of Falmouth, one of Jamaica’s best-preserved historic towns. It was constructed in the late 1700s, at the peak of the sugar and slavery era, and later became a spiritual center for freed people after Emancipation in 1838. Despite facing hurricanes and other challenges over two centuries, the church has been continually rebuilt and renewed. Today, it remains both an active place of worship and a major historical site in Falmouth.

## History
St. Peter’s Anglican Church was built between 1790 and 1795, when Falmouth was a wealthy port for sugar, rum, and the slave trade. During slavery, the church mainly served the white planter class and free people of color, who had limited membership. After Emancipation in 1838, the church's role transformed as it became a vital institution for the newly freed Black community, hosting baptisms, marriages, and gatherings. Throughout the 1800s, St. Peter’s provided education and guidance as Jamaican society shifted from slavery to freedom. By the late 19th century, it had become one of the most important Anglican churches on the island’s north coast.

## Architecture
St. Peter’s is a premier example of Georgian architecture in the Caribbean. Built from cut limestone, it features thick walls, tall rounded-arch windows, and a large rectangular nave with interior balconies (galleries). A soaring square tower above the main entrance makes it a commanding presence in Falmouth’s skyline. Inside, the church has a high wooden ceiling, carved pews, and large windows that provide light and air. The churchyard contains historic graves from the 1700s and 1800s, including those of merchants, clergy, and town founders. Its grand scale and detailed craftsmanship make it one of Jamaica’s most significant architectural treasures.

## Clergy
St. Peter’s has always been a leading church in Trelawny, with its clergy often overseeing smaller missions in the surrounding countryside. In the 1800s, the rectors were influential community figures who focused on education and social support, especially after Emancipation. During the 1900s, the church expanded its programs to include Sunday schools, youth groups, and the Mothers’ Union. After Jamaica’s Independence in 1962, St. Peter’s continued as a central parish, partnering with local schools and community organizations. Lay readers have always played a key role in leading services, ensuring the church remained active.

## Notable Facts
Throughout its 200-year history, St. Peter’s has withstood major hurricanes, including Hurricane Charlie (1951) and Hurricane Gilbert (1988), which both caused significant damage. Each time, the community and the Diocese funded repairs. The church was also carefully restored in the 2000s and 2010s as part of Falmouth’s heritage preservation project. Today, St. Peter’s is a vibrant parish hosting regular services, weddings, funerals, and heritage tours, standing as a powerful symbol of faith, history, and resilience in Jamaica. Hurricane Melissa (October 28, 2025) severely damaged St Peter's Parish Church — the oldest public building in Falmouth, completed in 1795. The Jamaica Gleaner reported that key buildings in Falmouth's historic square, including the St Peter's Anglican Parish Church, were severely damaged. Falmouth is one of Jamaica's five designated historic districts and was described as among the worst-hit by Melissa. The church's 230-year-old fabric, including the historic Georgian fabric and fittings, sustained significant damage from winds and surge. As of late 2025, the building was undergoing emergency assessment as part of the broader recovery of the Falmouth heritage district, which Prime Minister Holness described as requiring a 'massive task to rebuild.'
