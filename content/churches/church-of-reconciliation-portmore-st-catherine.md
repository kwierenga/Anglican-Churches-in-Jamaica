# Church of Reconciliation, Portmore
**St. Catherine** · Church · active

The Church of Reconciliation in Portmore is one of the most distinctive churches in Jamaica: a building **jointly owned and shared by the Roman Catholic Church and the Anglican Diocese of Jamaica**, with separate Catholic and Anglican congregations worshipping in the same physical sanctuary. It is, by the church's own claim, "possibly the only such shared church" of its kind. Located at 307 Goodman Avenue, Kenton Road, Portmore 14, St. Catherine.

## History
The Church of Reconciliation was completed in the early months of 1977 and officially opened on Sunday, 4 September 1977 in a joint dedication by the Most Reverend Samuel E. Carter (Roman Catholic Archbishop of Kingston) and the Right Reverend Herbert Edmondson (Anglican Lord Bishop of Jamaica). The shared foundation was a remarkable expression of post-Vatican II ecumenical practice: where the two largest historic churches of Jamaica — the Anglican (the Church of England in Jamaica until 1870) and the Roman Catholic — formed a single, undivided physical congregation in a then-new town.

Portmore itself is one of Jamaica's most rapidly developed dormitory communities of the 1970s and 1980s, a planned settlement on the Hellshire plain west of Kingston Harbour. As the new community grew, both denominations chose to plant a single shared building rather than two parallel ones. From the outset the staffing reflected the joint character: the Roman Catholic priests at the founding were two Friars of the Atonement — Fr. Martin Carter (pastor) and Fr. Richard Albert (associate) — while the Anglican pastor was Fr. Edmund Davis, then also serving as General Secretary of the Jamaica Council of Churches. Two houses beside the Church were built — one for the Roman Catholic clergy, one for the Anglican priest and his family.

The "Reconciliation" dedication is a deliberate theological choice: the building is not named for a saint but for the Christian doctrine of reconciliation between God and humanity, and (by extension) between separated Christian traditions. The choice anchors the church in the long, historical Anglican-Roman conversation that began in the post-Reformation period and that the post-1965 ecumenical movement made unusually concrete.

## Architecture
The Church of Reconciliation is a modern post-1970 building of functional design, suited to dual-tradition worship. The flat coastal plain of Portmore creates direct vulnerability to storm surge during major hurricanes, a recurring concern for the entire Hellshire / Portmore zone.

## Clergy
The Church of Reconciliation has a continuous joint pastoral structure: an Anglican priest and a Roman Catholic priest serve concurrently, with each tradition's congregation maintaining its own liturgy and sacraments while sharing the building, the campus, and certain joint events.
