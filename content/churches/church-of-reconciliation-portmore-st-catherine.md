# Church of Reconciliation, Portmore
**St. Catherine** · Church · active

The Church of Reconciliation in **Portmore** is one of the most distinctive churches in Jamaica: a building **jointly owned and shared by the Roman Catholic Church and the Anglican Diocese of Jamaica**, with separate Catholic and Anglican congregations worshipping in the same physical sanctuary. It is, by the church's own claim, "possibly the only such shared church" of its kind. Located at 307 Goodman Avenue, Kenton Road, Portmore 14, St. Catherine.

## History
**Spanish Villa de la Vega (1534) and English conquest (1655).** The Spanish founded **Villa de la Vega** in 1534 — Jamaica's capital until **1872**. Penn and Venables's English expedition captured Jamaica in May **1655**.

**Founding parishes.** Under English rule from 1655, **St. Catherine** was one of Jamaica's original parishes. The neighbouring early parishes of **St. John, St. Dorothy, and St. Thomas-in-the-Vale** were merged into expanded St. Catherine under **Law 20 of 1867**.

**Slavery and the planter church.** Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds. **The Diocese of Jamaica** was established by Letters Patent on **24 July 1824** by King George IV; **Christopher Lipscomb** was consecrated first Bishop in 1824. During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation (1838) and Sligoville.** Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Sligoville** in St. Catherine became Jamaica's **first free village** in 1838, founded with land provided by **Howe Peter Browne, 2nd Marquess of Sligo**, then Governor of Jamaica. Bishop Lipscomb (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. Capital moved from Spanish Town to **Kingston in 1872**.

**Foundation of the Church of Reconciliation (1977).** Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found Portmore a small coastal area on the Hellshire plain. Portmore itself became one of Jamaica's most rapidly developed dormitory communities of the 1970s and 1980s, a planned settlement on the Hellshire plain west of Kingston Harbour. As the new community grew, both the Anglican and Roman Catholic denominations chose to plant a single shared building rather than two parallel ones. The Church of Reconciliation was completed in the early months of **1977** and officially opened on **Sunday, 4 September 1977** in a joint dedication by the **Most Reverend Samuel E. Carter** (Roman Catholic Archbishop of Kingston) and the **Right Reverend Herbert Edmondson** (Anglican Lord Bishop of Jamaica). The shared foundation was a remarkable expression of post-Vatican II ecumenical practice: where the two largest historic churches of Jamaica — the Anglican (the Church of England in Jamaica until 1870) and the Roman Catholic — formed a single, undivided physical congregation in a then-new town. From the outset the staffing reflected the joint character: the Roman Catholic priests at the founding were two **Friars of the Atonement** — **Fr. Martin Carter** (pastor) and **Fr. Richard Albert** (associate) — while the Anglican pastor was **Fr. Edmund Davis**, then also serving as General Secretary of the Jamaica Council of Churches. Two houses beside the Church were built — one for the Roman Catholic clergy, one for the Anglican priest and his family.

**The "Reconciliation" dedication** is a deliberate theological choice: the building is not named for a saint but for the Christian doctrine of reconciliation between God and humanity, and (by extension) between separated Christian traditions. The choice anchors the church in the long, historical Anglican-Roman conversation that began in the post-Reformation period and that the post-1965 ecumenical movement made unusually concrete.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The flat coastal plain of Portmore creates direct vulnerability to storm surge during major hurricanes, a recurring concern for the entire Hellshire / Portmore zone.

## Architecture
The Church of Reconciliation is a modern post-1970 building of functional design, suited to dual-tradition worship. The flat coastal plain of Portmore creates direct vulnerability to storm surge during major hurricanes.

## Clergy
The Church of Reconciliation has a continuous joint pastoral structure: an Anglican priest and a Roman Catholic priest serve concurrently, with each tradition's congregation maintaining its own liturgy and sacraments while sharing the building, the campus, and certain joint events.

## Notable Facts
The Church of Reconciliation is one of the most distinctive ecumenical foundations in the Anglican Communion — a **shared Anglican–Roman Catholic building** in continuous joint use since 1977, its dedication recalling the post-Vatican II ecumenical commitment to "reconciliation."
