# Mary Magdalene, Granville
**St. James** · Church · active

The Church of Mary Magdalene at Granville serves one of the larger residential communities on the outskirts of Montego Bay, in the expanding western suburbs of St. James. Granville grew rapidly in the second half of the twentieth century as Montego Bay's urban area spread westward, drawing in families from the rural interior and creating a large peri-urban community. The dedication to **Mary Magdalene** — *the apostle to the apostles* — is a notably distinctive choice, one of the few Anglican churches in Jamaica carrying this dedication.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1664).** St. James was one of the second batch of Jamaican parishes formed after the English conquest, established around **1664** by Sir Thomas Modyford and named after **James, Duke of York** (later King James II). At its formation it was much larger than today, including the territory of present-day Trelawny and Hanover. For many years the north side of the island was sparsely settled: in **1673** only **146 persons** resided in the entire parish.

**Eighteenth-century development.** Montego Bay was made a free port in **1738**; Charles Square was laid out in **1755**. Cudjoe, Maroon leader of western Jamaica, signed a peace treaty with the British in **1739**. The Second Maroon War of **1789** saw 300 Maroons fight 1,500 English soldiers. By the 1780s Montego Bay was, next to Kingston, "the most flourishing town in the island" (NLJ).

**Slavery and the Sam Sharpe Rebellion (December 1831).** Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds. The **Christmas Rebellion** of 1831, led by the Baptist deacon **Samuel Sharpe** at the Kensington Estate in St. James on the night of **27 December 1831**, was one of the largest slave rebellions in the British Caribbean — some 60,000 enslaved people across St. James, Trelawny, Hanover, and Westmoreland involved. Sharpe was hanged in **Montego Bay marketplace** (now **Sam Sharpe Square**) on **23 May 1832**, and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation, 1865, disestablishment.** Within two years the British Parliament passed the Slavery Abolition Act (1833); Emancipation came in 1834, and full freedom followed on **1 August 1838**. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865, Crown Colony rule replaced the planter Assembly (1866); the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of Mary Magdalene: a twentieth-century urban mission.** Granville's development as a residential community accelerated from the mid-twentieth century onwards, as Montego Bay's growth drew workers and families westward along the coast. The Anglican Diocese established Mary Magdalene to serve this expanding community, following the pattern of mission planting in new urban and peri-urban settlements that characterised the Diocese's response to Jamaica's rapid post-war urbanisation. Mary Magdalene belongs to the same generation of Montego Bay urban missions as Holy Trinity Westgate, Holy Cross Mount Salem, St. Augustine's Coral Gardens, and St. Francis Glendevon. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** marked the transition from colonial to national church.

**Modern era and tourism.** Montego Bay has been Jamaica's tourism capital since the latter half of the twentieth century; it received city status by act of Parliament on **9 October 1980** and officially became Jamaica's second city in 1982. The St. James Parish Church in central Montego Bay had been wrecked by the **1957 earthquake** but restored. Major hurricanes have repeatedly struck the parish — Gilbert (1988), Ivan (2004), Melissa (October 2025).

## Architecture
Mary Magdalene is a functional church building suited to an urban and peri-urban congregation in a western suburb of Montego Bay. The building is set within the Granville community close to the main road corridor, giving it visibility and accessibility to the surrounding residential area. The church property likely includes a hall or meeting space that supports community activities beyond Sunday worship, consistent with Anglican urban mission practice across Jamaica's larger towns.

## Clergy
Mary Magdalene is part of the Montego Bay Region of the Anglican Diocese and has been served within the western Montego Bay clergy cluster. The church's ministry in a peri-urban community with significant social needs has shaped its pastoral character, combining traditional Anglican liturgical worship with active community engagement. Lay leaders — wardens, Mothers' Union members, and readers — have been central to sustaining the church's life.

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** **Mary Magdalene** is the disciple who, in all four Gospels, was the first witness to the Risen Christ on Easter morning — a status that has earned her the patristic title *apostola apostolorum* ("apostle to the apostles"). Her feast day is **22 July**. Mary Magdalene was traditionally identified in the Western Church (following Pope Gregory the Great in the sixth century) with the unnamed sinful woman of Luke 7:36–50; this conflation has been corrected in modern scholarly and liturgical practice. Her dedication for a Jamaican peri-urban mission church carries a notably distinctive theological character — naming the church for the woman whom the resurrected Christ called by name, the figure of redemption and faithful witness.

**Coastal vulnerability.** Granville's proximity to the coast and to Montego Bay's urban infrastructure means the community sits in a complex risk environment: neither as exposed to direct storm surge as the beachfront properties, nor as sheltered as the deep interior. The western corridor of Montego Bay has been affected by multiple major storms — Gilbert (1988), Ivan (2004), and **Hurricane Melissa (28 October 2025)**, which made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Granville, as a western suburb close to the storm's landfall zone, was among the communities experiencing damaging wind conditions, and Mary Magdalene's congregation was directly affected. Across the Diocese, some **50 church buildings were destroyed or significantly damaged**.
