# St. Mark's, Boston
**Portland** · Church · active

St. Mark's Anglican Church serves Boston, a community on the north coast of Portland known internationally for its jerk pork tradition. Boston Bay is a small fishing and agricultural community east of Port Antonio that has become synonymous with Jamaican jerk cooking, and St. Mark's has served its congregation here for well over a century. The church is confirmed at Boston Bay on the coastal road.

## History
Boston and Boston Bay are situated on the north coast of Portland east of Port Antonio, in the banana-growing district that made Portland prosperous in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The community developed its own culinary tradition — jerk pork pit-smoked and sold roadside — that eventually brought Boston Bay international recognition. The Anglican Diocese established St. Mark's as part of its coastal mission network, serving the fishing and farming families of the Boston community.

## Architecture
St. Mark's is a coastal church on the Portland north coast, confirmed by Google Places at Boston Bay on the coastal road. The beach and coastal position creates storm surge exposure during hurricane passages, and the steep hills immediately behind the coastal strip create compound risk from landslides during heavy rainfall.

## Clergy
Boston has been served within the Portland deanery circuit. The coastal community maintains an active congregation through the local fishing and farming families.

## Notable Facts
Boston Bay's claim to be the home of Jamaican jerk cooking gives this small community an outsized international reputation. The Boston Bay jerk pits are a major attraction for visitors driving the coastal road through Portland. Hurricane Ivan (2004) struck the Portland coast severely. Hurricane Melissa (October 2025) tracked along the north coast; Boston Bay was in the storm's direct path and St. Mark's coastal position made it vulnerable to surge damage.
