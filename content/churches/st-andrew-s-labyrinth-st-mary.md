# St. Andrew's, Labyrinth
**St. Mary** · Church · active

St. Andrew's Anglican Church in **Labyrinth**, western St. Mary, serves as a daughter church of **Holy Trinity Retreat**. This countryside church ministers to farming communities near the historic **White River bridge** ("Old Spanish Bridge") on the boundary with St. Ann.

## History
**Pre-English era and the Battle of Rio Nuevo (1658).** **Christopher Columbus** sighted Jamaica in 1494 on his second voyage; the western St. Mary coast where Labyrinth sits lay on the path of his exploration. The **Battle of Rio Nuevo (June 1658)**, fought near the mouth of the Rio Nuevo on the St. Mary/St. Ann boundary just east of Labyrinth, saw British forces under **Sir Edward D'Oyley** decisively defeat a Spanish expedition attempting to recapture Jamaica — effectively ending Spanish resistance to British rule.

**English rule (1655) and the planter church.** Under English rule from **1655**, St. Mary became a major sugar-producing parish. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**Tacky's Revolt (1760).** On Easter Monday, **7 April 1760**, **Tacky's Revolt** broke out on **Frontier and Trinity estates** near Port Maria. Tacky — a **Fante royal** from the Gold Coast of West Africa — led a rising of self-emancipated **Coromantee** people intended to overthrow British colonial rule. Over **500 Black men and women** were killed in battle, executed, or took their own lives — the most significant slave rebellion in the British West Indies between the 1733 St. John insurrection and the 1791 Haitian Revolution.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union** to "wipe non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb** (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**Foundation: Holy Trinity Retreat and its daughter chapels.** **Holy Trinity at Retreat** — built around **1835** on land donated in 1828 by the planter **Henry Rigg** (1791–1869) — is the oldest-standing Anglican church in St. Mary, and St. Andrew's Labyrinth was established as one of its **four daughter chapels** (with St. Matthew's Boscobel, St. John's Gayle, and St. Margaret's Mission at Clifton Lodge). St. Andrew's likely dates to the late 19th century, serving post-Emancipation communities in the **White River valley**.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** The **banana trade** transformed the St. Mary economy in the late 19th and 20th centuries. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Andrew's a settled daughter chapel of Holy Trinity Retreat.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Western St. Mary suffered severe infrastructure damage, leaving the congregation facing prolonged outages and access challenges. (Labyrinth Primary had previously lost its roof in **Hurricane Gilbert** in 1988.)

## Architecture
Photographs depict St. Andrew's as a traditional Jamaican country church with masonry walls and zinc roof, typical of late 19th-century rural Anglican architecture. The active churchyard cemetery and hillside location above the White River make it vulnerable to erosion and landslide risks during severe weather events.

## Clergy
St. Andrew's shares clergy with Holy Trinity Retreat and other daughter churches, supported by local lay leadership. The church remains active in hosting funerals and community events, maintaining strong ties with local schools and farms.

## Notable Facts
As one of Holy Trinity's four daughter churches, St. Andrew's ministers in a district famous for the **Old Spanish Bridge** swimming spot at the White River. The dedication to **St. Andrew the apostle** (feast day **30 November**) — patron of Scotland — reflects the significant Scottish presence in the colonial-era plantation economy.
