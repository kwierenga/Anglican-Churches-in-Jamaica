# St. Matthew's, Claremont
**St. Ann** · Church · active

St. Matthew's Anglican Church serves the hillside town of **Claremont** in St. Ann. It was established in the 19th century to serve the communities formed by freed families after Emancipation in 1838. Today, St. Matthew's continues to be a cornerstone for worship and community life in Claremont and the surrounding districts.

## History
**Pre-English settlement: Sevilla la Nueva (1509–1534).** St. Ann has a deeper colonial past than almost any other Jamaican parish. **Sevilla la Nueva**, the first permanent European settlement in Jamaica and the colony's first Spanish capital, was founded in **1509** by Juan de Esquivel near present St. Ann's Bay and served as Spanish capital until **1534**. **Christopher Columbus** had been marooned at St. Ann's Bay for over a year (1503–1504).

**English rule (1655) and the planter church.** Under English rule from **1655**, St. Ann became a major **sugar and livestock** region. The Claremont area was previously dominated by plantations where the population was enslaved. Until **1870**, the Church of England was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**Sam Sharpe Rebellion and the Colonial Church Union (1831–32).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, **Hamilton Brown** of Brown's Town led a Colonial Church Union mob that destroyed the Brown's Town Baptist chapel in 1832 — the **Colonial Church Union** sought to "wipe the non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth." **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation (1838) and free villages.** Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. After 1838, freed people moved into the cooler hills to establish independent farms and villages. **Sturge Town** (1839, founded by the English Quaker abolitionist **Joseph Sturge**) and **Clarksonville** (1835, founded by Baptist missionary Rev. **John Clark**, named for the abolitionist **Thomas Clarkson**) were among Jamaica's earliest free villages — both in St. Ann (NLJ, *History of St. Ann*) — Clarksonville lying just to the south of Brown's Town. Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb** (1824–1843) had begun extending Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**Disestablishment (1870) and the founding of St. Matthew's.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. As Claremont grew with families building new lives after Emancipation, the Anglican Diocese sent clergy to serve them, and by the late 1800s a permanent church dedicated to **St. Matthew** was completed. It quickly became a vital centre for baptisms, education, and community gatherings.

**Twentieth century: bauxite, Independence.** Bauxite mining began in St. Ann around **1952** (Reynolds, Kaiser). **Hurricane Charlie (1951)** and **Hurricane Gilbert (1988)** damaged the roof and windows; each time, the congregation, with support from the Diocese, carried out the necessary repairs. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Matthew's a settled hillside town parish.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of St. Matthew's Claremont after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. Matthew's is a classic example of a rural Jamaican Anglican church, built from hand-cut limestone in a simple Gothic Revival style. It features a rectangular nave, a small chancel, and pointed-arch windows with coloured glass. The steep roof, now covered with metal, was designed to shed the heavy rainfall common in the hills. Inside, the church has an open-timber roof, wooden pews, and a modest pulpit. The surrounding churchyard, with graves of the community's founding families, adds to the site's peaceful and historic atmosphere.

## Clergy
St. Matthew's has always been part of the **St. Ann Deanery**, sharing clergy with larger parishes in Ocho Rios, St. Ann's Bay, and Brown's Town. Early priests travelled difficult routes to hold services and support education and family life. Lay readers were essential in maintaining regular worship when ordained clergy were serving multiple congregations.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to **St. Matthew** — the tax collector at Capernaum whom Jesus called with the words "Follow me" (Matthew 9:9), feast day **21 September** — is a story of transformation: from despised collaborator with the Roman authorities to apostle and evangelist. For a community of freed people building new lives after slavery, a patron saint whose story centres on being called out of one life into a wholly different one would have carried particular meaning.
