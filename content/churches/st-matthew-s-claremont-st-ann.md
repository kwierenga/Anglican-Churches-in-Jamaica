# St. Matthew's, Claremont
**St. Ann** · Church · active

St. Matthew’s Anglican Church serves the hillside town of Claremont in St. Ann. It was established in the 1800s to serve the communities formed by freed families after Emancipation in 1838. Despite facing storms and other challenges over the years, the church has remained a constant presence in the town. Today, St. Matthew’s continues to be a cornerstone for worship and community life in Claremont and the surrounding districts.

## History
St. Matthew's Anglican Church was founded in the mid-19th century as the community of Claremont grew with families building new lives after Emancipation. The area was previously dominated by plantations where the population was enslaved. After 1838, freed people moved into the cooler hills to establish independent farms and villages. In response, the Anglican Diocese sent clergy to serve them, and by the late 1800s, a permanent church dedicated to St. Matthew was completed. It quickly became a vital center for baptisms, education, and community gatherings, supporting the transition to a free society.

## Architecture
St. Matthew’s is a classic example of a rural Jamaican Anglican church, built from hand-cut limestone in a simple Gothic Revival style. It features a rectangular nave, a small chancel, and pointed-arch windows with colored glass. The steep roof, now covered with metal, was designed to shed the heavy rainfall common in the hills. Inside, the church has an open-timber roof, wooden pews, and a modest pulpit. The surrounding churchyard, with graves of the community's founding families, adds to the site's peaceful and historic atmosphere.

## Clergy
St. Matthew’s has always been part of the St. Ann Deanery, sharing clergy with larger parishes in Ocho Rios, St. Ann’s Bay, and Brown’s Town. Early priests traveled difficult routes to hold services and support education and family life. The church was instrumental in providing basic schooling and literacy, which were especially crucial in the decades after Emancipation. After Jamaica’s Independence in 1962, the congregation remained active through the Mothers’ Union, youth groups, and community outreach. Lay readers were essential in maintaining regular worship when ordained clergy were serving multiple congregations.

## Notable Facts
St. Matthew’s has endured several major hurricanes, including Charlie (1951) and Gilbert (1988), which damaged the roof and windows. Each time, the congregation, with support from the Diocese, carried out the necessary repairs. Ongoing maintenance in recent years has preserved the building's structure. Today, St. Matthew’s Anglican Church remains an active parish, holding regular services, weddings, and community events, serving as a living link between Jamaica’s history of freedom and its present-day faith.
