# St. Peter's, Lluidas Vale
**St. Catherine** · Church · inactive

St. Peter's Anglican Church serves **Lluidas Vale**, a community in the high-altitude interior valley of St. Catherine, surrounded by the hills of central Jamaica. Lluidas Vale is home to the **Worthy Park Estate**, one of the oldest continuously operating sugar estates in the Caribbean.

## History
**Spanish Villa de la Vega (1534), Juan de Bolas, and English conquest (1655).** The Spanish founded **Villa de la Vega** in 1534 — Jamaica's capital until **1872**. **Juan de Bolas** (Juan Lubolo) — a Black guerrilla leader — led formerly enslaved Africans into the Lluidas Vale hills to fight against the new English regime in **1655**. He later switched allegiance to the English, and "Juan de Bolas country" became the name of the surrounding upland.

**Founding parishes.** Under English rule from 1655, **St. Catherine** was one of Jamaica's original parishes. The neighbouring early parishes of **St. John, St. Dorothy, and St. Thomas-in-the-Vale** were merged into expanded St. Catherine under **Law 20 of 1867**.

**Slavery and Worthy Park Estate.** The **Worthy Park Estate** in Lluidas Vale, established in the 17th century, has supported sugar cultivation and cattle farming since the colonial period. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds. **The Diocese of Jamaica** was established by Letters Patent on **24 July 1824**; **Christopher Lipscomb** was consecrated first Bishop in 1824.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838), Sligoville.** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Sligoville** in St. Catherine became Jamaica's **first free village** in 1838, founded with land provided by **Howe Peter Browne, 2nd Marquess of Sligo**, then Governor of Jamaica. Bishop Lipscomb (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. Capital moved from Spanish Town to **Kingston in 1872**.

**Foundation of St. Peter's.** The Anglican Diocese established St. Peter's to serve the Lluidas Vale community alongside St. Luke's at Juan-de-Bolas elsewhere in the upland valley. The Worthy Park sugar economy created a substantial estate workforce that the Diocese reached through chapels in the valley.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found Lluidas Vale a settled valley. **Worthy Park** continues to operate as one of the few sugar estates to survive into the modern era.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Hurricane Melissa brought wind and rain to upland St. Catherine.

## Architecture
St. Peter's Lluidas Vale is a masonry chapel in the interior of St. Catherine. The high valley position gives it some of the coolest conditions of any St. Catherine Anglican church, with elevation creating both pleasant climate and significant wind exposure during hurricane passages.

## Clergy
Lluidas Vale has been served within the **St. Catherine deanery** under shared clergy arrangements.

## Notable Facts
**Worthy Park Estate** in Lluidas Vale is one of the oldest continuously operating sugar estates in the Caribbean, with a documented history stretching back to the 17th century. The estate has survived changes of ownership, the abolition of slavery, the decline of the sugar industry, and the modernisation of Caribbean agriculture to remain a producing sugar and rum operation. The dedication to **St. Peter the apostle** (feast day **29 June** with St. Paul) — chief of the twelve, traditionally the first bishop of Rome — is one of the most common Anglican dedications.
