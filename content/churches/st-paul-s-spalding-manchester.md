# St. Paul's, Spalding
**Manchester** · Church · active

St. Paul's Anglican Church serves Spalding, a community in the northern interior of Manchester near the Christiana area. Spalding is a farming community in the highland hills of Manchester, and the church has served the local community for several generations. Manchester's northern interior — of which Spalding is part — is among the highest-elevation farming landscapes of central Jamaica, known for cool-climate crops including Irish potatoes.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1814).** Manchester is one of Jamaica's youngest parishes, created by **Act 55 George III. c. 23** of **1814** in response to a petition of 29 November 1814 by the residents of Mile Gully, May Day, and Carpenter's Mountain — who had complained that "the closest public building was 40 miles away." The Act came into effect on **13 December 1814**, taking the "hill country" forming the eastern portion of St. Elizabeth and the western portions of Vere and Clarendon, and constituting it as a separate parish. The new parish was named after the then Governor of colonial Jamaica, **William Montagu, 5th Duke of Manchester** (Governor 1808–1827); the parish capital, **Mandeville**, was named after the Duke's son, **Viscount Mandeville**, on 27 August 1816, on land bought from Robert Crawford's Caledonia Estate.

**Slavery, the parish church, and the Sam Sharpe Rebellion (1831).** Manchester's hill-country economy was built on coffee from the eighteenth century onward, supplemented by sugar in lower zones; both depended on enslaved African labour. The Mandeville **Court House** was built between 1817 and 1820 of limestone blocks "cut by slaves" (NLJ). **St. Mark's Anglican Church, Mandeville** — the parish church of Manchester — was completed and consecrated in **1820** and for many years was the only church in the parish; its organ loft was used as a jail during the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, in which a Moravian minister was held awaiting court martial. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**Emancipation, 1865, disestablishment.** The British Parliament passed the Slavery Abolition Act in 1833; Emancipation came in 1834, and full freedom followed at the end of the apprenticeship period on **1 August 1838**. **Maidstone in Manchester** (founded 1840 by the Nazareth Moravian Church on 341 acres of Thomas Frith's coffee estate) was among Jamaica's earliest free villages. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865, Crown Colony rule replaced the planter Assembly (1866); the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of St. Paul's: Nuttall's expansion.** Bishop Enos Nuttall (Bishop of Jamaica 1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. The 1900 Handbook of Jamaica records the Manchester cures including Christiana under **Rev. J. Cass** — Spalding lies in the upland country between Mandeville and Christiana.

**Twentieth century: Kendal, bauxite, Independence.** The **Kendal train tragedy** of **1 September 1957** claimed almost 200 lives at the Manchester community of Kendal — Jamaica's worst rail accident. The **bauxite industry** transformed Manchester from 1957 onward, with **Alcan** and Kaiser employing over 3,000 by the 1960s. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Paul's a settled rural mission. The **Pioneer Chocolate Factory** at nearby Williamsfield and the **High Mountain Coffee Factory** continue traditions of Manchester's specialty agriculture.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of St. Paul's Spalding after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. Paul's is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural Manchester in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries.

## Clergy
Spalding has been served within the Manchester deanery under shared clergy arrangements.

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** **St. Paul the Apostle** — author of much of the New Testament, missionary across the Mediterranean world, with feast days on **25 January** (his conversion) and **29 June** (with St. Peter) — is one of the most common Anglican dedications. Paul's missionary journeys make his dedication particularly fitting for a church serving a community that sees itself as an outpost of the faith in the wider landscape.

**Highland farming.** Spalding sits in the cool-climate zone of northern Manchester, suitable for Irish potatoes and other temperate crops. The area around Spalding and Christiana is one of the few parts of Jamaica where Irish potato cultivation thrives.
