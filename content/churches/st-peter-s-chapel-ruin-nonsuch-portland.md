# St. Peter's Chapel (Ruin), Nonsuch
**Portland** · Chapel · ruin

St. Peter's Chapel at Nonsuch is a ruined Anglican chapel in the interior hills of Portland parish, one of Jamaica's more evocative ecclesiastical ruins. Nonsuch is a small interior community in the hill country south-east of Port Antonio, and the chapel ruins here represent an early Anglican presence in this part of Portland that predates the more accessible coastal mission network. The ruin designation in the dataset indicates the building is no longer in active use, though the site retains its historical and heritage significance.

## History
The Portland interior was among the more challenging terrain for Anglican mission work in Jamaica, with the Blue Mountains and John Crow Mountains creating a landscape of steep valleys, dense forest, and difficult access. Nonsuch sits in the hill country inland from Port Antonio, and St. Peter's Chapel was established to serve the early post-Emancipation community here. The building eventually fell out of regular use as the congregation declined or consolidated with other worship points, and the chapel has since fallen into ruin. Its survival as a recognisable ruin places it in a tradition of abandoned Anglican mission buildings found across Jamaica's interior landscapes.

## Architecture
St. Peter's is recorded as a ruin, indicating the building fabric has deteriorated beyond regular use. The chapel would originally have been a small masonry structure consistent with early 19th-century Anglican mission buildings in Jamaica. The Nonsuch area sits in the limestone karst country east of Port Antonio, a distinctive geological landscape with sink holes, caves, and rough terrain. The ruins exist in this karst environment, at some distance from the main coastal road network.

## Clergy
As a ruined chapel, St. Peter's Nonsuch is no longer served by active clergy. In its operational years it would have been served within the Portland deanery circuit, likely linked to the Port Antonio parish church or a nearby mission hub.

## Notable Facts
The Nonsuch area of Portland is known for the Nonsuch Caves, a limestone cave system that has been developed as a heritage attraction. The chapel ruin and the caves exist in proximity in this karst landscape. Ruins of Anglican mission chapels in Jamaica's interior represent the Diocese's ambitious 19th-century expansion into every community across the island, many of which subsequently declined as population moved to coastal and urban centres.
