# St. Barnabas', Warsop
**Trelawny** · Church · active

St. Barnabas' Anglican Church serves the hamlet of **Warsop** in the interior hills of south Trelawny, at an elevation of about **696 metres (2,283 feet)** — higher than Albert Town and deep in the country along historical routes through Cockpit Country. The church is listed in the Diocese of Jamaica and the Cayman Islands directory within the Trelawny Deanery; it does not appear in the 1891 Handbook of Jamaica's Anglican returns, placing its foundation in the late phase of Bishop Enos Nuttall's expansion of rural mission stations (Diocese grew from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**). The Diocese maintains a directory image of the church (attachment_id 886).

## History
**Founding of the parish (1770).** Trelawny was created in **1770** out of St. James, in response to settler petitions dating from 1733. The new parish was named after Governor **Sir William Trelawny**; its first capital was **Martha Brae**, replaced around **1790** by **Falmouth** (renamed for the governor's birthplace, Falmouth in Cornwall, England).

**Slavery, sugar, and the Maroons.** Trelawny became the parish with **the most sugar estates in Jamaica** — at its peak there were as many as 100 estates with over 40 sugar factories. The interior of the parish was **Cockpit Country** — the Leeward Maroon heartland. **Cudjoe's Town** (also known as Trelawny Town) was the principal Maroon settlement, protected by the **1739 Peace Treaty** that ended the First Maroon War. The **Second Maroon War of 1795–96** ended with the deportation of some 568 Trelawny Town Maroons to Nova Scotia, and their onward transport to Sierra Leone in 1800. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**The Sam Sharpe Rebellion (December 1831).** The Christmas Rebellion of 1831, led by the Baptist deacon **Samuel Sharpe** at the Kensington Estate in St. James on **27 December 1831**, spread directly into Trelawny. **Rev. William Knibb**, the Baptist abolitionist who ministered at Falmouth, played a major role in the campaign that secured the accelerated end of the apprenticeship in 1838. Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation, 1865, disestablishment.** The British Parliament passed the Slavery Abolition Act in 1833; Emancipation came in 1834, and full freedom followed at the end of the apprenticeship period on **1 August 1838**. Warsop is one of several upland communities whose Anglican churches were founded after Emancipation to serve freed populations settling in the Cockpit Country foothills. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865, Crown Colony rule replaced the planter Assembly (1866); the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of St. Barnabas' and Nuttall's expansion.** Bishop Enos Nuttall's tenure (1880–1916) saw the Diocese grow from 26 rural stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. The 1891 Handbook of Jamaica does not list Warsop among the Trelawny Anglican stations — which then included Falmouth, Swanswick (Clark's Town), Rio Bueno, Stewart Town, Gibraltar, St. Silas, with Albert Town and Wait-a-Bit as missions. Warsop's foundation is therefore after 1891. By the **1900 Handbook**, the Albert Town cure had been formally constituted as **"Albert Town & 4 Mission Stations"** under **Rev. J. R. M. Cass**.

**Twentieth century, sugar decline, Independence.** By **1927** the Trelawny sugar estates had declined from a peak of 100 to just 16. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Barnabas' a settled upland mission within the Albert Town cure.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. **Trelawny was among the five most affected parishes**. The specific status of St. Barnabas' Warsop after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. Barnabas' is a small rural stone chapel of the kind built across Cockpit Country's upland communities in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries. The Diocese of Jamaica's online directory image collection includes a photograph of the church (attachment_id 886). No detailed architectural record beyond this has been identified in public heritage sources reviewed.

## Clergy
St. Barnabas' is served within the Trelawny Deanery under shared clergy arrangements; historically attached to the Albert Town cure, where Rev. J. R. M. Cass served as rector by 1900 over "Albert Town & 4 Mission Stations."

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** **St. Barnabas** ("son of encouragement") was the early-Christian Cypriot Levite who introduced Saul (Paul) to the apostles after his conversion (Acts 9:27) and accompanied him on the first missionary journey. His feast day is **11 June**.

**Cockpit Country setting.** Warsop's elevation of about 696 m makes it one of the highest Anglican sites in Trelawny. The community sits in the landscape of the Leeward Maroons — Cudjoe's Town lay a short distance away — and the modern Cockpit Country visitor routes pass through several historical upland villages including Warsop.
