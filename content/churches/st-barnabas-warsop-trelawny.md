# St. Barnabas', Warsop
**Trelawny** · Church · active

St. Barnabas' Anglican Church serves the hamlet of Warsop in the interior hills of south Trelawny, at an elevation of about 696 metres (2,283 feet) — higher than Albert Town and deep in the country along historical routes through Cockpit Country. The church is listed in the Diocese of Jamaica and the Cayman Islands directory within the Trelawny Deanery of the Montego Bay Region. It does not appear in the 1891 Handbook of Jamaica's Anglican returns — indicating a post-1891 foundation, most likely during Bishop Enos Nuttall's expansion of rural mission stations (the Diocese grew from 26 stations in 1880 to 122 by 1911).

## History
Trelawny was created as a parish in 1770 out of St. James. The parish's interior was Cockpit Country — the Leeward Maroon heartland. Cudjoe's Town (also known as Trelawny Town) was the principal Maroon settlement, protected by the 1739 treaty that ended the First Maroon War. The Second Maroon War of 1795–96 ended with the deportation of some 568 Trelawny Town Maroons to Nova Scotia and their onward transport to Sierra Leone in 1800.

Warsop is one of several upland communities whose Anglican churches were founded after Emancipation (1834) and full freedom (1 August 1838) to serve freed populations settling in the Cockpit Country foothills. The 1891 Handbook of Jamaica does not list Warsop among the Trelawny Anglican stations — which then included Falmouth, Swanswick (Clark's Town), Rio Bueno, Stewart Town, Gibraltar, St. Silas, Albert Town, and Wait-a-bit as missions. Warsop's foundation is therefore after 1891.

The dedication is to Barnabas the apostle, the companion of Paul recorded in the Acts of the Apostles, known in tradition as the "Son of Encouragement." His feast day is 11 June.

## Architecture
St. Barnabas' is a small rural stone chapel of the kind built across Cockpit Country's upland communities in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries. No substantive architectural record has been identified in public heritage sources reviewed.

## Clergy
St. Barnabas' is served within the Trelawny Deanery under shared clergy arrangements, with lay leaders and wardens sustaining regular worship.

## Hurricane Melissa (October 2025)
Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on 28 October 2025. Trelawny was among the five most affected parishes. The specific status of St. Barnabas' Warsop after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Notable Facts
Warsop's elevation of about 696 m makes it one of the highest Anglican sites in Trelawny. The community sits in the landscape of the Leeward Maroons — Cudjoe's Town lay a short distance away — and the modern Cockpit Country visitor routes pass through several historical upland villages including Warsop.
