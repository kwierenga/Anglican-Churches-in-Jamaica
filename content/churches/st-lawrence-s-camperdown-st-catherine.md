# St. Lawrence's, Camperdown
**St. Catherine** · Church · active

St. Lawrence's Anglican Church serves **Camperdown**, a community in the rural agricultural interior of central St. Catherine. The name **Camperdown** preserves the memory of the **1797 naval Battle of Camperdown** — a Royal Navy victory off the Dutch coast — and is one of several Jamaican estate names commemorating military victories of the period.

## History
**Spanish Villa de la Vega (1534) and English conquest (1655).** The Spanish founded **Villa de la Vega** in 1534 — Jamaica's capital until **1872**. Penn and Venables's English expedition captured Jamaica in May **1655**.

**Founding parishes.** Under English rule from 1655, **St. Catherine** was one of Jamaica's original parishes. The neighbouring early parishes of **St. John, St. Dorothy, and St. Thomas-in-the-Vale** were merged into expanded St. Catherine under **Law 20 of 1867**.

**Slavery and the planter church.** Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds. **The Diocese of Jamaica** was established by Letters Patent on **24 July 1824**; **Christopher Lipscomb** was consecrated first Bishop in 1824.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838), Sligoville.** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Sligoville** in St. Catherine became Jamaica's **first free village** in 1838, founded with land provided by **Howe Peter Browne, 2nd Marquess of Sligo**, then Governor of Jamaica. Bishop Lipscomb (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. Capital moved from Spanish Town to **Kingston in 1872**.

**Foundation of St. Lawrence's.** The Anglican Diocese established St. Lawrence's as part of its network of rural mission chapels across St. Catherine, extending Anglican ministry into the small farming districts away from the parish church and the cathedral at Spanish Town. No substantive foundational date or architectural history for St. Lawrence's Camperdown has been identified in the public heritage sources reviewed; the church does not appear in the **1891 Handbook of Jamaica** clergy listings, which is consistent with a foundation date in the early twentieth century, after the broad rural church-planting expansion of Bishop Nuttall's tenure.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Lawrence's a settled rural mission.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Hurricane Melissa brought wind and rain to interior St. Catherine.

## Architecture
St. Lawrence's is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for mission stations across the St. Catherine interior. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal storm surge while remaining exposed to wind and rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
St. Lawrence's Camperdown is served within the **Mandeville Region's St. Catherine portion** of the diocesan structure.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to **St. Lawrence** — a third-century Roman deacon and martyr who was, by tradition, executed by being roasted alive on a gridiron in the year **258 AD** — is one of the more striking dedications in Jamaica's Anglican network. Lawrence is patron of cooks and (because of the legend that he asked his executioners to "turn me over, I'm done on this side") of comedians; his feast day on **10 August** falls in the height of hurricane season. The community name **Camperdown** commemorates the **1797 Battle of Camperdown** off the Dutch coast — Admiral Adam Duncan's Royal Navy victory over the Dutch fleet.
