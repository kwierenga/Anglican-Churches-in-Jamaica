# St. Lawrence's, Camperdown
**St. Catherine** · Church · active

St. Lawrence's Anglican Church serves Camperdown, a community in the rural interior of St. Catherine parish.

## History
Camperdown sits in the agricultural country of central St. Catherine. The community's name preserves the memory of the 1797 naval Battle of Camperdown — a Royal Navy victory off the Dutch coast that became a celebrated event in late-eighteenth-century Britain — and is one of several Jamaican estate names commemorating military victories of the period. The Anglican Diocese established St. Lawrence's as part of its network of rural mission chapels across St. Catherine, extending Anglican ministry into the small farming districts away from the parish church and the cathedral at Spanish Town.

The dedication to St. Lawrence — a third-century Roman deacon and martyr who was, by tradition, executed by being roasted alive on a gridiron in the year 258 — is one of the more striking dedications in Jamaica's Anglican network. Lawrence is patron of cooks and (because of the legend that he asked his executioners to "turn me over, I'm done on this side") of comedians; his feast day on 10 August falls in the height of hurricane season.

No substantive foundational date or architectural history for St. Lawrence's Camperdown has been identified in the public heritage sources reviewed; the church does not appear in the 1891 Handbook of Jamaica clergy listings, which is consistent with a foundation date in the early twentieth century, after the broad rural church-planting expansion of Bishop Enos Nuttall's tenure.

## Architecture
St. Lawrence's is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for mission stations across the St. Catherine interior. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal storm surge while remaining exposed to wind and rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
St. Lawrence's Camperdown is served within the Mandeville Region's St. Catherine portion of the diocesan structure.
