# Church of the Transfiguration, Havendale
**St. Andrew** · Church · active

The Church of the Transfiguration in Havendale is a mid-twentieth-century Anglican parish serving the Meadowbrook and Havendale suburbs in north-west St Andrew. Founded as a mission in 1963, it first worshipped in a private home before building a hall and then the present modern concrete church in 1973–74. Today, it is part of the Eastern Jamaica Region and is known for its lively worship, steel orchestra, and active ministries that link Sunday services with community life and online outreach.

## History
The parish began in February 1963 with services held in the home of Mrs. Thomas at 34 Knightsdale Drive under diocesan sponsorship. Its first dedicated structure, the Percy Powell Church Hall, was constructed between November 1964 and March 1965, providing a permanent base. As the congregation grew, a purpose-built church was constructed on 1 Meadowbrook Main, with work beginning in June 1973 and completed in 1974. In February 2013, the parish celebrated fifty years of ministry, marking its journey from a small mission to an established suburban church.

## Architecture
The Church of the Transfiguration is a striking modernist concrete building, distinct from older stone parish churches. Its design features a low main worship block fronted by a dramatic pair of tall triangular pylons that frame a bell and create a bold vertical accent at the entrance. The sanctuary behind presents clean, angular planes and a modest roofline, with a small forecourt and boundary walls providing a sheltered feel. The original 1960s hall remains on the compound, forming a small campus used for meetings, Sunday school, and social events.

## Clergy
For many years, the parish was closely associated with The Reverend Franklyn Jackson, its rector known for outspoken commentary on crime and public life. Under his leadership and others, the church deepened its engagement with social issues. Current diocesan listings show the church as having no resident priest, indicating a period of transition where lay leaders and regional clergy maintain its work. The congregation belongs to the Eastern Jamaica Region, sharing in its network's training, retreats, and joint services.

## Notable Facts
The parish has a strong music and arts ministry, with a steel orchestra and dancers featuring prominently in concerts, Christmas services, and patriotic celebrations. Its compound also serves civic purposes, listed as a polling station for the St Andrew North Western constituency. The church is a frequent site for thanksgiving services and funerals, underlining its role in the community's significant life events. Additionally, it has built an online presence through livestreamed worship, allowing overseas members to stay connected.
