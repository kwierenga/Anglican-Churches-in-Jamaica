# Church of the Transfiguration, Havendale
**St. Andrew** · Church · active

The Church of the Transfiguration in **Havendale** is a mid-twentieth-century Anglican parish serving the Meadowbrook and Havendale suburbs in north-west St Andrew. **Founded as a mission in 1963**, it first worshipped in a private home before building a hall and then the present modern concrete church in 1973–74.

## History
**English conquest (1655) and St. Andrew parish (1664).** Penn and Venables's English expedition captured Jamaica from Spain in May **1655**. **St. Andrew parish was established in 1664** — one of Jamaica's original parishes — with first rector Rev. James Zellers and a 600-acre glebe granted in 1667 by Charles II.

**Port Royal, the 1692 earthquake, and the founding of Kingston (1693).** The **Great Earthquake of 7 June 1692** destroyed two-thirds of Port Royal; survivors relocated and the new city of **Kingston** was formally established on **22 July 1692**, with the Assembly constituting Kingston as a parish in **1693**.

**Slavery and the planter church.** Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church. The **Diocese of Jamaica** was established by Letters Patent on **24 July 1824**; **Christopher Lipscomb** was consecrated first Bishop in 1824. During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation (1838) and disestablishment (1870).** Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. Bishop Lipscomb (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

**1872 capital, 1907 earthquake, 1923 KSAC.** The capital moved from Spanish Town to **Kingston in 1872**. The **14 January 1907 earthquake** destroyed much of Kingston; under Nuttall's £50,000 reconstruction programme, ten churches were consecrated within a single month in **January 1911**. The **Kingston and St. Andrew Corporation (KSAC)** unified the two parishes for civic administration in **1923**.

**Foundation of the Church of the Transfiguration (1963).** Bishop **Percival Gibson** (1957–1967) was the first native-born Bishop of Jamaica, and his episcopate saw the creation of new parishes in the expanding suburbs of north-west St. Andrew. The parish began in **February 1963** with services held in the home of Mrs. Thomas at 34 Knightsdale Drive under diocesan sponsorship — coming **just before** Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** transformed the political context. (Independence had come the year before; the parish was founded in the post-independence year.) Its first dedicated structure, the **Percy Powell Church Hall**, was constructed between November 1964 and March 1965, providing a permanent base. As the congregation grew, a purpose-built church was constructed on 1 Meadowbrook Main, with work beginning in June **1973** and completed in **1974**. In **February 2013**, the parish celebrated **fifty years of ministry**.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Hurricane Melissa brought wind and rain to Kingston.

## Architecture
The Church of the Transfiguration is a striking modernist concrete building, distinct from older stone parish churches. Its design features a low main worship block fronted by a dramatic pair of tall triangular pylons that frame a bell and create a bold vertical accent at the entrance. The sanctuary behind presents clean, angular planes and a modest roofline, with a small forecourt and boundary walls providing a sheltered feel. The original 1960s **Percy Powell Church Hall** remains on the compound, forming a small campus used for meetings, Sunday school, and social events.

## Clergy
For many years, the parish was closely associated with **The Reverend Franklyn Jackson**, its rector known for outspoken commentary on crime and public life. Current diocesan listings show the church as having no resident priest, indicating a period of transition where lay leaders and regional clergy maintain its work. The congregation belongs to the **Eastern Jamaica Region**, sharing in its network's training, retreats, and joint services.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to the **Transfiguration** — the moment on Mount Tabor when Jesus appeared transfigured before Peter, James, and John (Matthew 17, Mark 9, Luke 9), with a feast day on **6 August** — coincides annually with Jamaica's Independence Day. The parish has a strong music and arts ministry, with a steel orchestra and dancers featuring prominently in concerts, Christmas services, and patriotic celebrations.
