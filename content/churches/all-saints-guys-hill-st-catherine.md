# All Saints', Guy's Hill
**St. Catherine** · Church · active

All Saints' Anglican Church serves **Guy's Hill**, a market town in the upland country of northern St. Catherine, on the parish boundary with St. Mary. The church is administered as a mission of the **St. George's, Blackstonedge** cure, alongside St. Paul's Clapham.

## History
**Spanish Villa de la Vega (1534) and English conquest (1655).** The Spanish founded **Villa de la Vega** in 1534 — Jamaica's capital until **1872**. Penn and Venables's English expedition captured Jamaica in May **1655**.

**Founding parishes.** Under English rule from 1655, **St. Catherine** was one of Jamaica's original parishes. The neighbouring early parishes of **St. John, St. Dorothy, and St. Thomas-in-the-Vale** were merged into expanded St. Catherine under **Law 20 of 1867**.

**Slavery and the planter church.** Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds. **The Diocese of Jamaica** was established by Letters Patent on **24 July 1824** by King George IV; **Christopher Lipscomb** was consecrated first Bishop and enthroned at the Cathedral in Spanish Town on 15 February 1825.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838), Sligoville (1838).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Sligoville** in St. Catherine became Jamaica's **first free village** in 1838, founded with land provided by **Howe Peter Browne, 2nd Marquess of Sligo**, then Governor of Jamaica. Bishop Lipscomb (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

**Foundation of All Saints' Guy's Hill.** Guy's Hill sits in the broken upland country of northern St. Catherine, a historic market town that grew up at a crossroads of the road network connecting the St. Catherine interior to the parishes of St. Mary, St. Ann, and the north coast. The town has been an important regional hub for the surrounding farming communities, with its weekly market drawing produce from across the upland districts. The Anglican Diocese established All Saints' as a mission of the **St. George's Blackstonedge** cure, extending its presence into Guy's Hill as part of the wider rural church-planting expansion that the diocese carried out from the late nineteenth century onward. After the disestablishment of the Anglican Church in 1870, the diocese was able to organise its mission network on its own canons and resources, founding small chapels in upland market communities like Guy's Hill that had previously had no direct Anglican presence.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** Capital moved from Spanish Town to **Kingston in 1872**. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found All Saints' a settled mission of the Blackstonedge cure.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Hurricane Melissa brought wind and rain to interior St. Catherine.

## Architecture
All Saints' is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for mission stations across the St. Catherine uplands. The hill-country position creates wind exposure during storm passages.

## Clergy
All Saints' Guy's Hill is served as a mission of the **St. George's, Blackstonedge** cure within the **Kingston Region's St. Catherine Deanery**.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to **All Saints** — commemorating on **1 November** the full company of recognised and unrecognised saints — is the most general dedication in the Anglican tradition and is a common choice for mission chapels intended to gather a scattered farming community.
