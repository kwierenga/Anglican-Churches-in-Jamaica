# St. Christopher's, Cavaliers
**St. Andrew** · Church · active

St. Christopher's Anglican Church at Cavaliers serves the Cavaliers community in northern St. Andrew, Cavaliers is a residential community in the hills above the city, and the church has served its congregation here for several generations. The dedication to St. Christopher, patron of travellers, is a fitting choice for a community in the hills above the city where travel on winding mountain roads is a daily reality.

## History
Cavaliers developed as a residential community in the northern hills of St. Andrew, part of the suburban expansion into the cooler hill country above Kingston. The Anglican Diocese established St. Christopher's to serve this hillside community. The name Cavaliers may derive from a historical estate name or reflect a colonial naming tradition in the parish.

## Architecture
St. Christopher's is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural St. Andrew in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. Set in the rural interior of St. Andrew. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
Cavaliers has been served within the northern St. Andrew deanery circuit under shared clergy arrangements with the Stony Hill circuit.

## Notable Facts
St. Christopher's Cavaliers sits in the northern hill country of St. Andrew, in the residential zone that attracts families seeking the cooler temperatures and larger properties of the hills above Kingston. The church has developed a strong community identity, with reviewers expressing a deep personal connection to the congregation. The dedication to Christopher — whose name means 'Christ-bearer' — connects the church to the tradition of the patron saint who carries others safely across difficult terrain, resonant for a hillside community.
