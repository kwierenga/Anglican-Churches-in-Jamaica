# St. Paul's, Clapham
**St. Catherine** · Church · active

St. Paul's Anglican Church serves Clapham, a community in the upland country of northern St. Catherine. The church is administered as a mission of the St. George's, Blackstonedge cure, alongside All Saints' Guy's Hill.

## History
Clapham takes its name from Clapham in south London — one of many Jamaican community names transferred from English places by colonial-era estate proprietors. The Clapham district sits in the broken hill country of northern St. Catherine, in the agricultural landscape that connects the parish's interior to the St. Mary border.

The Anglican Diocese established St. Paul's as a mission of the St. George's Blackstonedge cure during the late-nineteenth-century rural church-planting expansion that extended the diocese's network across Jamaica's interior parishes after the disestablishment of the Anglican Church in 1870. The wider Clapham name has a notable Anglican resonance: in early-nineteenth-century England the "Clapham Sect" — a group of Anglican evangelical reformers based at Clapham, including William Wilberforce — was the central force in the campaign that led to the abolition of the British slave trade (1807) and slavery itself (1834). For a Jamaican community sharing the name, this is a particularly resonant historical association.

The dedication to St. Paul the apostle — author of much of the New Testament, missionary across the Mediterranean world, with feast days on 25 January (his conversion) and 29 June (with St. Peter) — is one of the most common Anglican dedications.

## Architecture
St. Paul's is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for mission stations across the St. Catherine interior. The hill-country position creates wind exposure during storm passages.

## Clergy
St. Paul's Clapham is served as a mission of the St. George's, Blackstonedge cure within the Kingston Region's St. Catherine Deanery.
