# St. Paul's, Clapham
**St. Catherine** · Church · active

St. Paul's Anglican Church serves **Clapham**, a community in the upland country of northern St. Catherine. The church is administered as a mission of the **St. George's, Blackstonedge** cure, alongside All Saints' Guy's Hill. The community name carries an unusually resonant Anglican history: in early-nineteenth-century England the **"Clapham Sect"** of evangelical Anglicans, including **William Wilberforce**, led the campaign that ended the British slave trade and slavery itself.

## History
**Spanish Villa de la Vega (1534) and English conquest (1655).** The Spanish founded **Villa de la Vega** in 1534 — Jamaica's capital until **1872**. Penn and Venables's English expedition captured Jamaica in May **1655**.

**Founding parishes.** Under English rule from 1655, **St. Catherine** was one of Jamaica's original parishes. The neighbouring early parishes of **St. John, St. Dorothy, and St. Thomas-in-the-Vale** were merged into expanded St. Catherine under **Law 20 of 1867**.

**Slavery and the planter church.** Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds. **The Diocese of Jamaica** was established by Letters Patent on **24 July 1824**; **Christopher Lipscomb** was consecrated first Bishop in 1824.

**The Clapham Sect, Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838), Sligoville.** In early-nineteenth-century England, the **"Clapham Sect"** — a group of Anglican evangelical reformers based at Clapham, including **William Wilberforce** — was the central force in the campaign that led to the abolition of the British slave trade (**1807**) and slavery itself (**1834**). For a Jamaican community sharing the name, this is a particularly resonant historical association. During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Sligoville** in St. Catherine became Jamaica's **first free village** in 1838, founded with land provided by **Howe Peter Browne, 2nd Marquess of Sligo**, then Governor of Jamaica. Bishop Lipscomb (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. Capital moved from Spanish Town to **Kingston in 1872**.

**Foundation of St. Paul's.** Clapham takes its name from Clapham in south London — one of many Jamaican community names transferred from English places by colonial-era estate proprietors. The Clapham district sits in the broken hill country of northern St. Catherine, in the agricultural landscape that connects the parish's interior to the St. Mary border. The Anglican Diocese established St. Paul's as a mission of the **St. George's Blackstonedge** cure during the late-nineteenth-century rural church-planting expansion that extended the diocese's network across Jamaica's interior parishes after the disestablishment of the Anglican Church in 1870.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Paul's a settled mission.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Hurricane Melissa brought wind and rain to interior St. Catherine.

## Architecture
St. Paul's is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for mission stations across the St. Catherine interior. The hill-country position creates wind exposure during storm passages.

## Clergy
St. Paul's Clapham is served as a mission of the **St. George's, Blackstonedge** cure within the Kingston Region's St. Catherine Deanery.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to **St. Paul** the apostle — author of much of the New Testament, missionary across the Mediterranean world, with feast days on **25 January** (his conversion) and **29 June** (with St. Peter) — is one of the most common Anglican dedications. The wider Clapham name has a notable Anglican resonance: the **Clapham Sect** of evangelical Anglicans was the central force in the campaign that led to the abolition of slavery — making this Jamaican Clapham's name a quiet historical irony.
