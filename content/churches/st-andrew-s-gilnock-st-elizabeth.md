# St. Andrew's, Gilnock
**St. Elizabeth** · Church · active

St. Andrew's Anglican Church serves Gilnock, a community in St. Elizabeth parish. Gilnock is a small farming community in the interior of the parish, and the church has provided Anglican ministry here for several generations. The dedication to St. Andrew, patron of Scotland, is found in several Jamaican Anglican churches — a reflection of the significant Scottish presence in the colonial history of the island.

## History
Gilnock sits in the interior of St. Elizabeth, in the farming landscape of the parish. The name Gilnock may derive from Scottish place names — Gilnockie is a tower in Dumfriesshire associated with the outlaw Johnny Armstrong. The Anglican Diocese established St. Andrew's to serve this community. The Scottish patron saint dedication may reflect Scottish proprietors in this part of the parish.

## Architecture
St. Andrew's is a masonry church of modest proportions, representative of Anglican rural church building in St. Elizabeth. The structure balances practicality with the dignity appropriate to a place of worship, featuring a straightforward nave with local stone or concrete block construction typical of Jamaica's interior church-building tradition. Set in the rural interior of St. Elizabeth. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
Gilnock has been served within the St. Elizabeth deanery under shared clergy arrangements.

## Notable Facts
St. Andrew's Day (30 November) is celebrated as the feast of the patron saint of Scotland, and the saltire — the white diagonal cross on blue — is said to represent the form of Andrew's martyrdom, crucified on an X-shaped cross. Scotland's presence in Jamaica was disproportionately large relative to its population, and Scottish influence on Jamaican place names, surnames, and church dedications is evident across all parishes.
