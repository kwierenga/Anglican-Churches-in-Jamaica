# St. Mark's, Craighton
**St. Andrew** · Chapel · active

St. Mark's Anglican Chapel sits atop a hill in the Blue Mountain foothills near Irish Town, in the rural Newcastle / Craighton area of St. Andrew. The chapel is recorded in our inventory as a chapel of the wider St. Andrew network and is described in public sources as offering panoramic views from Mona to Portmore across rolling hills and winding mountain roads.

## History
Craighton was the site of one of the historic colonial-era mountain estates above Kingston — Craighton Great House, sometimes referred to as "the Governor's cottage" because of its long association with successive Governors of Jamaica who used the elevated property for cool-climate retreat. An 1888 engraving published in contemporary illustrated weeklies depicts a party of gentlemen mustering at Craighton Great House and starting along the road past St. Mark's Anglican Church near Irish Town — placing the church's existence at least in the late nineteenth century, in the era when the diocese was extending its rural network across the St. Andrew highlands.

St. Andrew Parish itself was created in 1664, and the parish church at Half Way Tree was, until the disestablishment of the Anglican Church in 1870, the only Anglican church in the parish. After disestablishment the Diocese expanded its mission network across the parish's rural and mountain districts, founding small chapels in the cool upland zones around Newcastle, Irish Town, Gordon Town, and the surrounding mountain villages.

The dedication to St. Mark the Evangelist — author of the second Gospel, traditionally associated with Alexandria, with a feast day on 25 April — is shared with the Manchester Parish Church (St. Mark's Mandeville, 1820) and other Jamaican Anglican churches.

## Architecture
St. Mark's Craighton is a small mountain chapel set on its hilltop above Irish Town. Reviewers and historic sources describe its panoramic views, from Mona on the eastern side of Kingston to Portmore on the western. The high-elevation Blue Mountain foothill position creates significant wind exposure during major storm events and brings frequent low-cloud and mist conditions. No detailed architectural survey has been published in the public heritage sources reviewed.

## Clergy
St. Mark's Craighton is served within the Kingston Region's St. Andrew Deanery.
