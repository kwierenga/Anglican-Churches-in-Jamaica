# St. Mark's, Craighton
**St. Andrew** · Chapel · active

St. Mark's Anglican Chapel sits atop a hill in the Blue Mountain foothills near **Irish Town**, in the rural Newcastle / Craighton area of St. Andrew, with panoramic views from Mona to Portmore.

## History
**English conquest (1655) and St. Andrew parish (1664).** Penn and Venables's English expedition captured Jamaica from Spain in May **1655**. **St. Andrew parish was established in 1664** with a 600-acre glebe granted in 1667 by Charles II. The parish church at Half Way Tree was, until the disestablishment of the Anglican Church in **1870**, the only Anglican church in the parish.

**Port Royal, the 1692 earthquake, and the founding of Kingston (1693).** The **Great Earthquake of 7 June 1692** destroyed two-thirds of Port Royal; the new city of **Kingston** was formally established on **22 July 1692**, with the Assembly constituting Kingston as a parish in **1693**.

**Slavery, Diocese, Sam Sharpe, Emancipation.** Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church. The **Diocese of Jamaica** was established by Letters Patent on **24 July 1824**; **Christopher Lipscomb** was consecrated first Bishop in 1824. During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. Bishop Lipscomb (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall, and rural expansion.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. After disestablishment the Diocese expanded its mission network across the parish's rural and mountain districts, founding small chapels in the cool upland zones around Newcastle, Irish Town, Gordon Town, and the surrounding mountain villages.

**Foundation of St. Mark's Craighton.** **Craighton** was the site of one of the historic colonial-era mountain estates above Kingston — **Craighton Great House**, sometimes referred to as "the Governor's cottage" because of its long association with successive Governors of Jamaica who used the elevated property for cool-climate retreat. **An 1888 engraving** published in contemporary illustrated weeklies depicts a party of gentlemen mustering at Craighton Great House and starting along the road past **St. Mark's Anglican Church** near Irish Town — placing the church's existence at least in the late nineteenth century.

**1907 earthquake, 1923 KSAC.** The **14 January 1907 earthquake** destroyed much of Kingston; under Nuttall's £50,000 reconstruction programme, ten churches were consecrated within a single month in **January 1911**. The capital had moved from Spanish Town to **Kingston in 1872**. The **Kingston and St. Andrew Corporation (KSAC)** unified the two parishes for civic administration in **1923**.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Mark's a settled mountain chapel. Coffee farming continued to define the Newcastle/Craighton economy.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The high-elevation Blue Mountain foothill position creates significant wind exposure during major storm events.

## Architecture
St. Mark's Craighton is a small mountain chapel set on its hilltop above Irish Town. Reviewers and historic sources describe its panoramic views, from Mona on the eastern side of Kingston to Portmore on the western. The high-elevation Blue Mountain foothill position creates significant wind exposure during major storm events and brings frequent low-cloud and mist conditions.

## Clergy
St. Mark's Craighton is served within the **Kingston Region's St. Andrew Deanery**.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to **St. Mark the Evangelist** — author of the second Gospel, traditionally associated with Alexandria, with a feast day on **25 April** — is shared with the Manchester Parish Church (**St. Mark's Mandeville, 1820**) and other Jamaican Anglican churches.
