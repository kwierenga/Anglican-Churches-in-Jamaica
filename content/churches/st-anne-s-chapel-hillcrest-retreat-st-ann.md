# St. Anne's Chapel, Hillcrest Retreat
**St. Ann** · Chapel · active

The **St. Anne Chapel** is the chapel of the **Hillcrest Diocesan Retreat Centre**, the spiritual retreat house of the Anglican Diocese of Jamaica and the Cayman Islands. Hillcrest sits on five acres of upland Brown's Town, **St. Ann**, on St. Christopher's Crescent, looking north over the parish's coastline; the chapel, prayer rooms, labyrinth, conference rooms and 44-bed accommodation make it the principal site for diocesan retreats, sacramental preparation, and quiet days drawn from across the Province of the West Indies.

## History
**English conquest (1655) and St. Ann parish (1675).** Penn and Venables's English expedition captured Jamaica from Spain in May **1655**. The parish of **St. Ann** was created by Act of the Assembly in **1675**, named for Anne Hyde, Duchess of York and mother of two future English queens. The parish takes in the long northern coast of central Jamaica between St. James and St. Mary, and its hilly interior — Browns Town among the principal upland settlements — became cattle-pen and provision country in the eighteenth century, alongside the smaller sugar estates of the coastal districts.

**Sugar, slavery, and the St. Ann uprisings.** The wealth of colonial St. Ann was built on the labour of enslaved Africans. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church and was deeply implicated in the system: clergy held glebe lands worked by enslaved people, and the Anglican rectors of St. Ann's Bay and Browns Town received the full apparatus of state support. After the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion** the Anglican clergy of Jamaica joined the **Colonial Church Union**, an alliance with the planters formed to suppress the non-conformist (Baptist and Methodist) preachers whom the planters held responsible for the rebellion. **Sam Sharpe** was hanged on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. **Emancipation came in 1834**, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. Browns Town, where freed people gathered after Emancipation, became one of the early **free villages** of post-slavery Jamaica.

**Disestablishment and the rural mission of St. Ann.** After the **Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865** and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. The redirected clergy stipends powered the diocese's elementary-school network — **263 schools** island-wide by 1900 — and the rural mission expansion under Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916), who increased the diocese's mission stations from 26 in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. St. Ann was a major theatre of this expansion: large parishes like **St. Mark's, Brown's Town**, became the bases from which dozens of small chapels in the surrounding hills were served.

**The founding of Hillcrest as a diocesan retreat.** Through the twentieth century the Diocese of Jamaica and the Cayman Islands consolidated its property in St. Ann around its parish churches and schools, while seeking a place set apart for spiritual formation. The hills above Brown's Town — cool, well-watered, and accessible by good road from both Kingston and the north-coast towns — proved an ideal site, and **Hillcrest Diocesan Retreat Centre** was established on its present five-acre property at St. Christopher's Crescent. The chapel dedicated to **St. Anne** — mother of the Virgin Mary in Christian tradition, and the namesake of the parish — became the centrepiece of the retreat alongside a separate prayer room, an "Upper Room", and an outdoor **labyrinth** for walking meditation.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found the Anglican Church transitioning from its colonial role into its present life as the principal church of the West Indian Province in Jamaica. Hillcrest emerged as the diocese's spiritual centre at this period, hosting youth retreats, women's retreats, couples' retreats, choir retreats, sacramental preparation, clergy quiet days, and inter-parish gatherings — a function it has served for the diocese ever since.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The St. Ann uplands experienced wind and rain during Melissa's passage, though the parish was spared the worst of the western damage.

## Architecture and Facilities
The St. Anne Chapel is one of several built structures on the Hillcrest property. The five-acre site comprises:
- **St. Anne Chapel** for the daily and Sunday office and the celebration of the Eucharist
- A separate **prayer room** and an **"Upper Room"** for smaller-group worship
- An outdoor **labyrinth** — a prayer-and-meditation path
- Two **dining rooms** and a kitchen
- Conference and meeting spaces
- A **reference library**
- Quiet **gardens** for outdoor reflection
- Accommodation for **44 guests** in double or triple rooms with en-suite bathrooms
- An **Administrative Block**

The chapel itself is a modest masonry building suited to a small worshipping community of retreatants, with a wooden interior, plain glass windows, and a simple altar arrangement.

## Clergy and Use
Hillcrest is administered by a **Director** appointed by the Diocese, contactable at the centre's listed phone (876 917 7893) and email (`hillcrestdirector@anglicandioceseja.org`). The chapel does not have a resident parish priest; clergy from across the diocese use the chapel during retreats and quiet days.

## Notable Facts
**St. Anne** — the mother of the Virgin Mary in Christian tradition — is the namesake of the **parish of St. Ann** itself, making the dedication of the Hillcrest chapel a deliberate echo of the parish's identity. Anne's feast day is **26 July** (with Joachim, her husband and the grandfather of Christ in tradition).

The five-acre Hillcrest property is the diocese's principal **retreat house** and the only diocesan facility of its kind in the Province of the West Indies that offers full residential accommodation for spiritual retreats.
