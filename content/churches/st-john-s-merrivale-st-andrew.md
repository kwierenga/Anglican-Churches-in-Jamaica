# St. John's, Merrivale
**St. Andrew** · Church · active

St. John's Anglican Church serves Merrivale, a community in the northern suburban area of St. Andrew, in the upland residential districts north of Half-Way-Tree. Merrivale is a residential neighbourhood in the hills above the city, and St. John's has served its community for several generations. The dedication to the apostle and evangelist John is one of the most common in the Anglican network.

## History
Merrivale developed as a residential suburb in the northern hills of St. Andrew, part of the gradual expansion of Kingston's suburban footprint into the cooler hill country above the city. The Anglican Diocese established St. John's to serve this growing community, providing a local place of worship for the families who settled in the suburban hills.

## Architecture
St. John's is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural St. Andrew in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. Set in the hilly interior of St. Andrew. The elevated terrain channels wind during storm passages and makes good drainage essential to the building's longevity.

## Clergy
Merrivale has been served within the St. Andrew deanery under shared clergy arrangements with the northern suburban circuit.

## Notable Facts
The northern suburban hills of St. Andrew — communities like Merrivale, Stony Hill, and their surroundings — represent the upwardly mobile residential expansion of Kingston through the 20th century. These hillside communities developed as families sought cooler conditions and larger properties away from the urban centre, creating a distinct suburban character in the northern parish.
