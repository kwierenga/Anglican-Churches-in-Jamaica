# St. John the Evangelist, Meadowbrook
**St. Andrew** · Church · active

St. John the Evangelist Anglican Church stands on Mannings Hill Road in **Constant Spring Grove / Meadowbrook**, serving the residential communities of Meadowbrook, Constant Spring, and the surrounding northern St. Andrew suburbs. The church is paired with St. Andrew's, Merrivale, in the **Meadowbrook–Merrivale Cure** within the Diocese of Jamaica and the Cayman Islands.

## History
**English conquest (1655) and St. Andrew parish (1664).** Penn and Venables's English expedition captured Jamaica from Spain in May **1655**. **St. Andrew parish was established in 1664** with a 600-acre glebe granted in 1667 by Charles II.

**Port Royal, the 1692 earthquake, and the founding of Kingston (1693).** The **Great Earthquake of 7 June 1692** destroyed two-thirds of Port Royal; the new city of **Kingston** was formally established on **22 July 1692**, with the Assembly constituting Kingston as a parish in **1693**.

**Slavery, Diocese, Sam Sharpe, Emancipation, disestablishment.** Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church. The **Diocese of Jamaica** was established by Letters Patent on **24 July 1824**; **Christopher Lipscomb** was consecrated first Bishop in 1824. During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

**1872 capital, 1907 earthquake, 1923 KSAC.** The capital moved from Spanish Town to **Kingston in 1872**. The **14 January 1907 earthquake** destroyed much of Kingston. The **Kingston and St. Andrew Corporation (KSAC)** unified the two parishes for civic administration in **1923**.

**Suburban St. Andrew and the founding of St. John the Evangelist.** Through the 20th century, Kingston expanded north into the cooler upland districts of St. Andrew. Constant Spring — long associated with the Constant Spring estate and its hotel — became a residential suburb, and the adjoining Meadowbrook subdivision grew up alongside Mannings Hill Road. St. John the Evangelist was established to serve this growing community, and continues to do so as part of the Meadowbrook–Merrivale Cure within the St. Andrew Deanery.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. John the Evangelist a settled suburban parish.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Hurricane Melissa brought wind and rain to St. Andrew.

## Architecture
St. John the Evangelist is a modest masonry parish church on Mannings Hill Road, of the kind built by the Anglican Diocese to serve the suburban subdivisions of northern St. Andrew. The interior accommodates regular Sunday worship and the parish's mid-week activities; the elevated terrain channels wind during storm passages and makes good drainage essential to the building's longevity.

## Clergy
The church is served within the **Meadowbrook–Merrivale Cure** in the St. Andrew Deanery, sharing clergy with St. Andrew's, Merrivale.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to **St. John the Evangelist** (feast 27 December) is one of the most common Anglican dedications. The parish maintains its own website at [stjohnevangelistja.com](https://stjohnevangelistja.com/), which includes the parish's own history and contact details.
