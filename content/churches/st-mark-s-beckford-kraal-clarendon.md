# St. Mark's, Beckford Kraal
**Clarendon** · Church · active

St. Mark's Anglican Church serves **Beckford Kraal**, a community in the interior of Clarendon parish. The "Kraal" in the community name is a word of Dutch/Afrikaans origin referring to an enclosure for cattle — its appearance in Jamaica reflects the Dutch and other European linguistic influences on Jamaican place naming, possibly through a proprietor with Dutch connections or through the livestock pen tradition. Beckford Kraal has been served by St. Mark's for several generations.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1664).** Clarendon parish was created in **1664**, nine years after the English seizure of Jamaica from Spain (**1655**), and named for **Sir Edward Hyde, 1st Earl of Clarendon** (1609–1674), Lord High Chancellor of England under Charles II. The original parish church was the **Church of the White Cross** at "The Cross" — Jamaica's third Anglican church. **St. Paul's Chapelton** was built as a chapel of ease in **1666** and became the parish church when the Cross Church was destroyed in **1815**. **Vere** was carved out as a separate sugar-plain parish in **1671** and was reabsorbed into Clarendon in **1867**; **Law 20 of 1887** designated **May Pen** as chief town.

**Slavery, the Beckford estates, and the Suttons rebellion.** Clarendon was one of Jamaica's great sugar parishes. The fertile Rio Minho plain held the great estates of the Suttons, Pennants, Ballards, and **Beckfords**. **William Beckford the Elder** was Lord Mayor of London twice and among the richest men in England, his wealth derived from Jamaican sugar estates. The Beckford name appears in several Jamaican place names reflecting the family's once-vast landholdings. The wealth of the parish was built on the labour of enslaved Africans. One of Jamaica's earliest slave rebellions erupted at **Suttons Plantation in 1690**: some 500 enslaved people escaped to form the Maroon communities eventually led by **Cudjoe** into the Cockpit Country.

**Sam Sharpe, Emancipation, disestablishment.** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**, a vigilante alliance that sought to "wipe the non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of St. Mark's.** Beckford Kraal sits in the interior of Clarendon, in the farming landscape of the parish; the "Beckford" part of the name likely refers to the Beckford family's once-extensive landholdings in this part of central Jamaica. The Anglican Diocese established St. Mark's to serve this community as part of the post-Emancipation interior mission network. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**, and St. Mark's Beckford Kraal belongs to that broader expansion phase.

**Twentieth century: bauxite, May Pen, Independence.** Chapelton lost its status as parish capital in **1938**, with administrative functions transferring to **May Pen**. **Alcoa** began bauxite operations in Clarendon in **1959**; the **Jamalco** alumina refinery at Halse Hall opened in **1972**. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Mark's a settled mission of the Chapelton cure.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of St. Mark's Beckford Kraal after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. Mark's is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural Clarendon in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
St. Mark's Beckford Kraal is administered as a mission of the **St. Paul's Chapelton** cure (Clarendon Deanery), grouped with St. James' Rock River, All Saints' Mt. Providence, and St. Luke's Wood Hall. The cure rectory is at Chapelton P.O., Clarendon (telephone 785-0782).

## Notable Facts
The Beckford family were one of the most prominent and wealthy plantation families in 18th-century Jamaica. The "Kraal" designation is one of the most unusual elements in Jamaican place naming — a Dutch livestock-enclosure term embedded in an interior Clarendon community name. The dedication to **St. Mark** — the Evangelist, author of the shortest of the four Gospels, whose feast day is **25 April** — is also the dedication of the Manchester parish church at Mandeville.
