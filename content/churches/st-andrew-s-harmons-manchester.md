# St. Andrew's, Harmons
**Manchester** · Church · active

St. Andrew's Anglican Church serves Harmons, a small upland farming community in the interior of Manchester parish. The church is administered as a mission of the **St. Augustine's, Porus** cure, alongside St. Stephen's Chantilly, St. James' Toll Gate, and Holy Trinity St. Toolies; the cure rectory is at Porus P.O., Manchester.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1814).** Manchester was created by **Act 55 George III. c. 23** of **1814** in response to a petition of 29 November 1814 by residents of Mile Gully, May Day, and Carpenter's Mountain. The Act came into effect on **13 December 1814**, taking the "hill country" of eastern St. Elizabeth and the western portions of Vere and Clarendon. The new parish was named after **William Montagu, 5th Duke of Manchester** (Governor 1808–1827); **Mandeville**, the capital, was named after his son, **Viscount Mandeville**.

**Slavery, the parish church, and the Sam Sharpe Rebellion (1831).** Manchester's hill-country economy was built on coffee, supplemented by sugar in lower zones, both worked by enslaved African labour. **St. Mark's Anglican Church, Mandeville** — the parish church — was completed and consecrated in **1820**; its organ loft was used as a jail during the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**. Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation, free villages, disestablishment.** Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Maidstone** in Manchester (1840) was among Jamaica's earliest Moravian-led free villages. **Porus**, in eastern Manchester, was founded as a free village for ex-slaves: missionaries purchased tracts of land and divided them into parcels for sale to ex-slaves, and many freed people migrated there to grow coffee and provision crops (Jamaica Observer, "The story of Manchester part III: Porus," 13 Jul 2014). After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**The Porus cure and Harmons.** Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. The 1900 Handbook of Jamaica records "Porus & Chantilly" as a cure under **Rev. W. Lund**, anchoring Anglican ministry in the post-Emancipation Porus landscape. The St. Augustine's cure's network of mission stations — Harmons, Toll Gate, Chantilly, St. Toolies — extends Anglican worship to the dispersed congregations of the Porus hinterland.

**Twentieth century: Kendal, bauxite, Independence.** The **Kendal train tragedy** of **1 September 1957** at Kendal Manchester claimed almost 200 lives — Jamaica's worst rail accident. The **bauxite industry** transformed Manchester from 1957 onward. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Andrew's a settled rural mission within the Porus cure.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of St. Andrew's Harmons after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. Andrew's is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for mission stations across the Manchester interior. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal storm surge while remaining exposed to wind and rainfall during major hurricane events.

## Clergy
St. Andrew's Harmons is served as a mission of the St. Augustine's, Porus cure within the Mandeville Region's Manchester Deanery. The 1900 Handbook recorded the Porus & Chantilly cure under **Rev. W. Lund**.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to **St. Andrew the apostle** — brother of Simon Peter, the first-called of the twelve, and patron saint of Scotland (feast day **30 November**) — is found in a number of Jamaican Anglican churches and reflects the strong Scottish presence in the colonial-era Caribbean plantation economy.
