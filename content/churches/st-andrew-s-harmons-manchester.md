# St. Andrew's, Harmons
**Manchester** · Church · active

St. Andrew's Anglican Church serves Harmons, a community in the interior of Manchester parish. The church is administered as a mission of the St. Augustine's, Porus cure, alongside St. Stephen's Chantilly, St. James' Toll Gate, and Holy Trinity St. Toolies. The cure rectory is at Porus P.O., Manchester (cellular 349-2271).

## History
Harmons is a small upland farming district in Manchester parish, near the broader belt of free-village and small-farmer settlements that grew up across the parish's interior after Emancipation in 1838. Manchester itself was a comparatively young parish, created by Act of the House of Assembly on 13 December 1814 by amalgamating portions of St. Elizabeth, Clarendon, and the entirety of the old parish of Vere, and named after William Montagu, 5th Duke of Manchester, then Governor of Jamaica.

The Anglican Diocese reached Harmons through the St. Augustine's Porus cure, which itself anchors Anglican ministry in the Porus area where, after Emancipation, missionaries purchased tracts of land and divided them into parcels for sale to ex-slaves who came to grow coffee and provision crops. The St. Augustine's cure's network of mission stations — Harmons, Toll Gate, Chantilly, St. Toolies — extends Anglican worship to the dispersed congregations of the Porus hinterland.

The dedication to St. Andrew the apostle — brother of Simon Peter, the first-called of the twelve, and patron saint of Scotland (feast day 30 November) — is found in a number of Jamaican Anglican churches and reflects the strong Scottish presence in the colonial-era Caribbean plantation economy.

## Architecture
St. Andrew's is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for mission stations across the Manchester interior. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal storm surge while remaining exposed to wind and rainfall during major hurricane events.

## Clergy
St. Andrew's Harmons is served as a mission of the St. Augustine's, Porus cure within the Mandeville Region's Manchester Deanery.
