# St. Saviour's, Lime Hall
**St. Ann** · Church · active

St. Saviour’s Anglican Church serves the historic hillside community of Lime Hall, one of the earliest free-village districts in St. Ann. It was established in the 1800s to support the strong, independent communities built by newly freed families after Emancipation in 1838. Despite facing storms and other challenges, the church has maintained its importance as a place of worship. Today, it continues to function as a vital center of faith and a guardian of community history in Lime Hall.

## History
St. Saviour's Anglican Church was founded in the late 19th century, emerging alongside the rapid growth of Lime Hall as a pioneering free village after Emancipation. This community was built by freed men and women who purchased small plots of land to establish a stable society independent of former plantations like Seville and Windsor. Recognizing this growth, the Anglican Church sent clergy from St. Ann’s Bay to provide spiritual guidance and support. By the 1880s, a permanent stone chapel was completed and dedicated as St. Saviour’s, becoming a permanent venue for worship, baptisms, and community gatherings. The church served as a crucial anchor for Lime Hall, reinforcing its identity and stability as a self-sustaining free village.

## Architecture
St. Saviour’s is a modest stone church built in the simple Gothic Revival style characteristic of rural Anglican missions. It features a rectangular nave, a small chancel, and tall pointed-arch windows with colored glass. The steep roof, now clad in metal, was likely originally covered with wooden shingles. Inside, the church is furnished with wooden pews, an open-timber roof, and a simple pulpit. The surrounding churchyard, final resting place of Lime Hall's founding families, contributes to the site's serene and historic atmosphere.

## Clergy
St. Saviour’s has always been part of the St. Ann Deanery, sharing clergy with other missions in St. Ann’s Bay and Windsor. Early priests traveled difficult, hilly routes to conduct services and provide pastoral care to the congregation. The church was a cornerstone of local education, championing literacy and moral instruction for children of freed families when formal schooling was scarce. After Jamaica’s Independence in 1962, it sustained its community role through the Mothers’ Union, youth programs, and outreach. Lay readers were indispensable in leading worship during periods when ordained clergy were shared across multiple parishes.

## Notable Facts
St. Saviour’s has endured several major hurricanes, including Charlie (1951) and Gilbert (1988), which necessitated repairs to the roof and windows. Each time, the congregation, supported by the Diocese, successfully restored the building. The structure has also required ongoing maintenance to withstand the heavy rains common in the hills of Lime Hall. Today, St. Saviour’s Anglican Church remains an active parish, hosting regular services, weddings, and community festivities, standing as a proud symbol of the strength and enduring legacy of the Lime Hall community.
