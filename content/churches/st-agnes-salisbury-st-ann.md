# St. Agnes, Salisbury
**St. Ann** · Church · active

St. Agnes Anglican Church is a historic rural church serving the hill communities of Salisbury and Hinds Town in St. Ann. Established by the early 20th century, it originated in a post-Emancipation landscape of small farms developed from former sugar estates. The church is a modest chapel with an adjacent cemetery, functioning as the primary Anglican worship and burial ground for the district. For nearly a century, it has provided a spiritual anchor for this agricultural community, marking life's milestones and supporting the area's transition into the modern era.

## History
St. Agnes Anglican Church serves the hill communities of Salisbury and Hinds Town in St. Ann, located approximately 11 kilometres south of St Ann’s Bay. The district developed on former sugar and livestock estates that relied on enslaved African labour, later fragmenting into small farms and freehold plots after Emancipation. The church's establishment by the early 20th century is confirmed by a 1933 Gleaner photograph featuring Bishop Hardie and local leaders at St. Agnes. For decades, the church has been a central institution for the area, with funeral notices regularly documenting thanksgiving services and burials in its churchyard. Through this long history, the congregation has supported the community's transition from a plantation-based economy to a modern landscape of small-scale farming and residential development.

## Architecture
While no formal architectural survey for St. Agnes, Salisbury exists in the public record, its presence is noted in diocesan documents as part of the St. Ann’s Bay Cure. The church’s traditional layout, featuring a chapel with an adjacent cemetery, is consistently referenced in funeral notices and online videos. By comparing it to similar mission churches in St. Ann, St. Agnes can be inferred as a modest, rectangular chapel constructed from stone or blockwork. Its design likely includes a simple porch or small tower, plain windows, and a zinc or tile roof, situated on gently sloping ground surrounded by graves. This description remains an informed comparison rather than a documented architectural record.

## Clergy
Within the current diocesan structure, St. Agnes, Salisbury is part of the St. Ann Deanery and is grouped with St. John’s Ocho Rios and St. Francis Hiattsfield under the care of the Very Rev. Richard Tucker. The clerical directory lists a Sunday service at 8:00 a.m., with clergy and lay ministers rotating among the cure's churches. The church's active role is evidenced by funeral notices from the 1990s and 2000s, which document its use for thanksgiving services for residents of Salisbury, Hinds Town, and nearby districts, followed by interment in the churchyard. Week-to-week church life is sustained by lay leaders—wardens, readers, and Mothers’ Union members—fostering a strong sense of shared responsibility for worship and the upkeep of the building and cemetery.

## Notable Facts
Hinds Town and Salisbury are classic St. Ann hill communities, known for Irish potato fields and active farmers' groups; St. Agnes is integral to this agricultural landscape, sharing its concerns about climate and rural livelihoods. The church is also part of a network of local institutions, including the Hinds Town Basic School, shaping early childhood experiences of community and faith. For nearly a century, St. Agnes has marked the key milestones of its congregation—baptisms, weddings, and funerals—offering a window into how a small rural Anglican church has accompanied its community from the colonial era, through Emancipation and Independence, into the present-day economy of the Ocho Rios uplands.
