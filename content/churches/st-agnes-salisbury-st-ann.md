# St. Agnes, Salisbury
**St. Ann** · Church · active

St. Agnes Anglican Church is a historic rural church serving the hill communities of **Salisbury** and **Hinds Town** in St. Ann, approximately 11 km south of St. Ann's Bay. Established by the early 20th century, it originated in a post-Emancipation landscape of small farms developed from former sugar estates. The church is a modest chapel with an adjacent cemetery, functioning as the primary Anglican worship and burial ground for the district.

## History
**Pre-English settlement: Sevilla la Nueva (1509–1534).** St. Ann has a deeper colonial past than almost any other Jamaican parish. **Sevilla la Nueva**, the first permanent European settlement in Jamaica and the colony's first Spanish capital, was founded in **1509** by Juan de Esquivel near present St. Ann's Bay. It served as Spanish capital until **1534** when relocated to Villa de la Vega (Spanish Town). **Christopher Columbus** had been marooned at St. Ann's Bay for over a year (1503–1504) on his fourth voyage.

**English rule (1655) and the planter church.** Under English rule from **1655**, St. Ann became a major **sugar and livestock** region — the coastal plain to sugar estates and the interior plateau to "**pens**" (livestock properties). The Salisbury and Hinds Town district developed on former sugar and livestock estates that relied on enslaved African labour. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds; its vestry was composed of the planter class.

**Sam Sharpe Rebellion and the Colonial Church Union (1831–32).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, **Hamilton Brown** of Brown's Town led a Colonial Church Union mob that destroyed the Brown's Town Baptist chapel in 1832 — the **Colonial Church Union** sought to "wipe the non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth." **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation (1838), disestablishment (1870), Nuttall.** Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. The freed population settled the hills, fragmenting the old sugar estates into small farms and freehold plots. Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb** (1824–1843) had begun extending Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911** — the period in which St. Agnes Salisbury was established.

**Foundation in the early 20th century.** The church's establishment is confirmed by a **1933 Gleaner photograph** featuring Bishop Hardie and local leaders at St. Agnes. For decades, the church has been a central institution for the area, with funeral notices regularly documenting thanksgiving services and burials in its churchyard.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** Bauxite mining began in St. Ann around **1952** (Reynolds, Kaiser); tourism grew at Ocho Rios. Through this long history, the congregation has supported the community's transition from a plantation-based economy to a modern landscape of small-scale farming and residential development. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Agnes a settled rural mission.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of St. Agnes Salisbury after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
While no formal architectural survey for St. Agnes, Salisbury exists in the public record, its presence is noted in diocesan documents as part of the **St. Ann's Bay Cure**. The church's traditional layout — a chapel with an adjacent cemetery — is consistently referenced in funeral notices and online videos. Comparing it to similar mission churches in St. Ann, St. Agnes can be inferred as a modest, rectangular chapel constructed from stone or blockwork, likely with a simple porch or small tower, plain windows, and a zinc or tile roof, situated on gently sloping ground surrounded by graves.

## Clergy
Within the current diocesan structure, St. Agnes, Salisbury is part of the **St. Ann Deanery** and is grouped with **St. John's Ocho Rios** and **St. Francis Hiattsfield** under the care of the **Very Rev. Richard Tucker**. The clerical directory lists a Sunday service at 8:00 a.m., with clergy and lay ministers rotating among the cure's churches. Week-to-week church life is sustained by lay leaders — wardens, readers, and Mothers' Union members.

## Notable Facts
Hinds Town and Salisbury are classic St. Ann hill communities, known for **Irish potato fields** and active farmers' groups; St. Agnes is integral to this agricultural landscape, sharing its concerns about climate and rural livelihoods. The church is also part of a network of local institutions, including the Hinds Town Basic School. The dedication to **St. Agnes** — the early-fourth-century Roman martyr (feast day **21 January**) — is one of the more distinctive Anglican dedications in Jamaica, shared with the parish's other St. Agnes at Priory.
