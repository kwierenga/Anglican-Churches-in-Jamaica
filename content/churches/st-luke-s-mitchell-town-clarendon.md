# St. Luke's, Mitchell Town
**Clarendon** · Church · active

St. Luke's Anglican Church serves **Mitchell Town**, a community in the southern coastal plain of Clarendon. The church is administered as a mission of the **St. Peter's, Alley** cure (Lionel Town circuit), alongside St. Andrew's Rocky Point, St. John's Portland Cottage, St. Saviour's Milk River, and St. Thomas' Race Course. The cure rectory is at P.O. Box 16, Lionel Town P.O., Clarendon.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1664).** Clarendon parish was created in **1664**, nine years after the English seizure of Jamaica from Spain (**1655**), and named for **Sir Edward Hyde, 1st Earl of Clarendon** (1609–1674), Lord High Chancellor of England under Charles II. The original parish church was the **Church of the White Cross** at "The Cross." **St. Paul's Chapelton** was built as a chapel of ease in **1666** and became the parish church when the Cross Church was destroyed in **1815**.

**Vere parish and St. Peter's Alley (1671).** **Vere** was carved out as a separate sugar-plain parish in **1671**, taking in the southern coastal plain — including Mitchell Town. **St. Peter's Alley** (founded 1671 at Withywood, relocated to Alley after the 1692 earthquake, present building 1715/1735) was Vere's parish church and remains the **third-oldest Anglican church in Jamaica**. The Vere region was one of the great Caribbean sugar landscapes of the eighteenth and early nineteenth centuries. Vere was reabsorbed into Clarendon under the **1867 reorganisation** that reduced Jamaica's twenty-two parishes to fourteen — and the Alley cure absorbed responsibility for the surrounding lowland communities, with Mitchell Town becoming one of the mission stations attached to it. **Law 20 of 1887** then designated **May Pen** as chief town of the merged parish.

**Slavery, Sam Sharpe, and Emancipation.** The sugar estates of the Vere plain depended on the labour of thousands of enslaved Africans. One of Jamaica's earliest slave rebellions erupted at **Suttons Plantation in 1690**: some 500 enslaved people escaped to form the Maroon communities eventually led by **Cudjoe** into the Cockpit Country. During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**, a vigilante alliance that sought to "wipe the non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of St. Luke's Mitchell Town.** Mitchell Town lies in the southern lowlands of Clarendon, in the flat agricultural country that stretches from May Pen toward the Caribbean coast. Its 1867-onward attachment to the Alley/Lionel Town cure made it part of the diocesan effort to maintain Anglican ministry across the lowland sugar belt as the sugar economy declined and the Vere plain reverted to mixed farming. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

**Twentieth century: bauxite, May Pen, Independence.** Chapelton lost its status as parish capital in **1938**, with administrative functions transferring to **May Pen**. **Alcoa** began bauxite operations in Clarendon in **1959**; the **Jamalco** alumina refinery at Halse Hall opened in **1972**. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Luke's a settled mission of the Lionel Town circuit.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of St. Luke's Mitchell Town after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. Luke's is a small masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for the lowland mission stations of southern Clarendon. The exposed coastal plain position offers no shelter from the storm surge and high winds that pass over this part of the parish during major hurricanes.

## Clergy
St. Luke's Mitchell Town is served as a mission of the St. Peter's, Alley cure within the Mandeville Region's Clarendon Deanery.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to **St. Luke** — physician and evangelist, whose feast day is **18 October** — falls in the calmer period after hurricane season and is a common Anglican choice for communities that have endured storm damage and recovery.
