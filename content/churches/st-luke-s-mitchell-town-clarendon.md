# St. Luke's, Mitchell Town
**Clarendon** · Church · active

St. Luke's Anglican Church serves Mitchell Town, a community in the southern coastal plain of Clarendon. The church is administered as a mission of the St. Peter's, Alley cure (Lionel Town circuit), alongside St. Andrew's Rocky Point, St. John's Portland Cottage, St. Saviour's Milk River, and St. Thomas' Race Course. The cure rectory is at P.O. Box 16, Lionel Town P.O., Clarendon.

## History
Mitchell Town lies in the southern lowlands of Clarendon, in the flat agricultural country that stretches from May Pen toward the Caribbean coast and the historic sugar parish of Vere. The Vere region was one of the great Caribbean sugar landscapes of the eighteenth and early nineteenth centuries, and the parish church of Vere — St. Peter's at the Alley, founded in 1671 and rebuilt in 1715 after the 1692 earthquake — is the third-oldest Anglican church in Jamaica. When Vere was merged into Clarendon under the 1867 reorganisation that reduced Jamaica's twenty-two parishes to fourteen, the Alley cure absorbed responsibility for the surrounding lowland communities, with Mitchell Town becoming one of the mission stations attached to it.

The dedication to St. Luke — physician and evangelist, whose feast day is 18 October — falls in the calmer period after hurricane season and is a common Anglican choice for communities that have endured storm damage and recovery.

## Architecture
St. Luke's is a small masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for the lowland mission stations of southern Clarendon. The exposed coastal plain position offers no shelter from the storm surge and high winds that pass over this part of the parish during major hurricanes.

## Clergy
St. Luke's Mitchell Town is served as a mission of the St. Peter's, Alley cure within the Mandeville Region's Clarendon Deanery.
