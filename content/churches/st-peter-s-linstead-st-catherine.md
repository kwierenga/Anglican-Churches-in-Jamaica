# St. Peter's, Linstead
**St. Catherine** · Church · unknown

St. Peter's Anglican Church provides a second worship point in Linstead, St. Catherine's principal interior market town, alongside St. John's Church also recorded here. Linstead is large enough to sustain two Anglican congregations, and St. Peter's serves its part of the town community. The dedication to the prince of the apostles is one of the most common in the Anglican network.

## History
Linstead's status as the main market town of the St. Catherine interior has historically supported a denser church presence than smaller rural communities. St. Peter's was established to supplement the ministry of St. John's, providing Anglican worship for a different part of the town.

## Architecture
St. Peter's is a masonry church of modest proportions, representative of Anglican rural church building in St. Catherine. The structure balances practicality with the dignity appropriate to a place of worship, featuring a straightforward nave with local stone or concrete block construction typical of Jamaica's interior church-building tradition. Set in the rural interior of St. Catherine. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
Linstead's Anglican churches have been served within the St. Catherine deanery, sharing circuit clergy arrangements across the town's worship points.

## Notable Facts
Linstead is most widely known across Jamaica through the folk song 'Linstead Market' — 'Carry me ackee go a Linstead Market, not a quattie worth sell' — celebrating the ackee trade at the town's weekly market. The song has become one of the most recognised Jamaican folk traditions worldwide, making Linstead a name known far beyond its size as an interior market town.
