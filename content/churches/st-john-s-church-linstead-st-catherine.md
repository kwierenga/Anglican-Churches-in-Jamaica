# St. John's Church, Linstead
**St. Catherine** · Church · active

St. John's Anglican Church serves **Linstead**, the largest town in the interior of St. Catherine parish on the main road between Spanish Town and the north coast. Linstead is a market town famous across Jamaica as the setting of the traditional folk song **"Linstead Market"**, and the church has served its community for well over a century. A second Anglican church, the **Church of the Holy Trinity**, is also recorded at Linstead, reflecting the town's size.

## History
**Spanish Villa de la Vega (1534) and English conquest (1655).** The Spanish founded **Villa de la Vega** in 1534 — Jamaica's capital until **1872**. Penn and Venables's English expedition captured Jamaica in May **1655**.

**Founding parishes — including the old St. Thomas-in-the-Vale.** Under English rule from 1655, **St. Catherine** was one of Jamaica's original parishes. The neighbouring early parishes of **St. John, St. Dorothy, and St. Thomas-in-the-Vale** were merged into expanded St. Catherine under **Law 20 of 1867**. **Linstead was the principal town of the old St. Thomas-in-the-Vale parish** before the 1867 merger.

**Slavery and the planter church.** Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds. **The Diocese of Jamaica** was established by Letters Patent on **24 July 1824**; **Christopher Lipscomb** was consecrated first Bishop in 1824.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838), Sligoville.** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Sligoville** in St. Catherine became Jamaica's **first free village** in 1838, founded with land provided by **Howe Peter Browne, 2nd Marquess of Sligo**, then Governor of Jamaica. Bishop Lipscomb (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. Capital moved from Spanish Town to **Kingston in 1872**.

**Foundation of St. John's.** Linstead developed as a market town in the interior of St. Catherine, at an elevation that made it a natural stopping point on the road between Spanish Town and the north coast. The **Linstead Market** was a significant commercial institution for the surrounding farming communities, and the folk song "Linstead Market" — celebrating the ackee trade — has made the town's name known across Jamaica and the Jamaican diaspora worldwide. The Anglican Diocese established St. John's to serve Linstead's community, providing the town's principal Anglican church.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. John's a settled town parish.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Hurricane Melissa brought wind and rain to interior St. Catherine.

## Architecture
St. John's is a masonry church in Linstead town at significant elevation above the coastal plain. The inland hill position gives some protection from coastal surge while exposing the church to the heavy rainfall associated with the interior of the island during hurricane passages.

## Clergy
Linstead has been served within the **St. Catherine deanery**. As the principal town of the interior, Linstead's clergy have traditionally served a circuit of smaller mission chapels across the surrounding hill communities.

## Notable Facts
The song **"Linstead Market"** — *"Carry me ackee go a Linstead Market, not a quattie worth sell"* — is one of the most widely recognised Jamaican folk songs, celebrating the ackee trade at the town's weekly market. **Ackee**, introduced to Jamaica from West Africa, is Jamaica's national fruit and the centrepiece of the national dish.
