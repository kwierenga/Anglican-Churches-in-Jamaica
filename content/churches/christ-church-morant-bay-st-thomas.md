# Christ Church, Morant Bay
**St. Thomas** · Parish Church · active

Christ Church is the principal Anglican church of Morant Bay, the capital of St. Thomas parish, situated on elevated ground with a panoramic view over the Caribbean Sea. It is one of the most historically significant Anglican churches in Jamaica, standing at the centre of a town whose name is inseparable from the 1865 Morant Bay Rebellion, the most consequential civil uprising in 19th-century Jamaican history. The church's commanding position overlooking the harbour gives it a presence that matches its historical weight within the story of the parish.

## History
Morant Bay was the site of the Morant Bay Rebellion of October 1865, led by Paul Bogle, a deacon of the Native Baptist Church, alongside George William Gordon, a mixed-race politician and justice of the peace. The uprising began when a crowd of protesters marched on the Morant Bay courthouse, leading to a violent confrontation that left 25 people dead. The British colonial government's suppression was brutal: over 430 people were executed, including Paul Bogle and George William Gordon, and more than 1,000 homes were burned. Both Bogle and Gordon are now National Heroes of Jamaica. Christ Church stands near this courthouse, and the history of Anglican clergy in Morant Bay is complexly entangled with the colonial response to the rebellion. The present brick-built church dates from 1865, constructed when the earlier parish church at Church Corner was abandoned as dilapidated.

## Architecture
Christ Church is a stone and brick structure with a panoramic view over the sea, set on elevated ground near the courthouse and town centre. The coastal position on Morant Bay creates storm surge vulnerability during hurricane passages — the bay is directly open to the Caribbean Sea on the south coast of Jamaica.

## Clergy
As the parish church of St. Thomas, Morant Bay has been the seat of the senior clergy of the St. Thomas deanery within the Anglican Diocese. The church has had a long succession of rectors dating back to the first appointment in 1666, including clergy present during the traumatic events of 1865. The relationship between the Anglican church establishment and the Morant Bay Rebellion is a complex chapter in Jamaican ecclesiastical history — Anglican clergy were associated with the colonial establishment at the time of the uprising, while the rebellion's leaders came from the Native Baptist tradition.

## Notable Facts
Paul Bogle and George William Gordon, the leaders of the 1865 Morant Bay Rebellion, are among Jamaica's seven National Heroes. Their execution by the colonial government and the brutal suppression of the uprising eventually led to the abolition of the Jamaican House of Assembly and the transformation of Jamaica into a Crown Colony. The events of 1865 directly shaped the trajectory of Jamaican political history toward eventual independence. The courthouse where the rebellion began — and which was burned — stands near the parish church, and cannons from the period are still visible nearby. Hurricane Ivan (2004) struck this coast severely.
