# St. Luke's, Bath
**St. Thomas** · Church · active

St. Luke's Anglican Church provides a second Anglican worship point in the **Bath** area of St. Thomas, alongside **St. Thomas' Anglican Church** which is the historic principal church of the spa town. The dedication to **St. Luke** — physician and evangelist (feast day **18 October**) — is a fitting choice for a town associated with healing waters.

## History
**English rule (1655) and the founding parish.** Under English rule from **1655**, St. Thomas was one of Jamaica's original parishes — originally **"St. Thomas-in-the-East"** with the first rector appointed in **1666**.

**Bath spa town and Bath Botanical Garden (1779).** **Bath was established as a spa town** in the late 17th century when the mineral hot springs of the Rio Grande Valley were developed as a therapeutic resort. **Bath Botanical Garden, founded in 1779**, is one of the oldest botanical gardens in the Western Hemisphere. The garden received plants from across the Caribbean and tropical world, including the **breadfruit trees brought from Tahiti by Captain Bligh in 1793** — one of the most famous episodes in Jamaican botanical history. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**Sugar economy and slavery.** Bath sat in the inland part of one of Jamaica's great sugar parishes. Large estates — Stokes Hall, Golden Grove, Albion, Holland — dominated the landscape, worked by enslaved Africans throughout the colonial period.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union** to "wipe non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb** (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**The Morant Bay Rebellion (11 October 1865).** On **11 October 1865**, **Paul Bogle** — a small farmer and Native Baptist deacon of **Stony Gut** — led a protest march to the Morant Bay courthouse. Governor **Edward John Eyre's** brutal response: **439 executed**, **600+ flogged**, **1,000+ homes burned**. Bogle was hanged on **24 October 1865**; **George William Gordon** was hanged on **23 October 1865**. **Both were declared National Heroes in 1965**.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall.** Jamaica became a **Crown Colony in 1866**. Law 20 of 1867 absorbed **St. David** into St. Thomas. The Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

**Foundation of St. Luke's.** Bath's status as a spa town and its long history as a place of resort and healing created a more substantial population than many interior communities, sustaining more than one Anglican worship point. St. Luke's serves a part of this Bath-area community, dedicated to the physician apostle and evangelist.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** Internal self-government came in **1944**. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** fulfilled the aspirations for which Bogle and Gordon died. **Hurricane Ivan (2004)** brought severe flooding to the Rio Grande Valley around Bath.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Hurricane Melissa brought heavy rain to the Bath area.

## Architecture
St. Luke's is a masonry chapel in the Bath area of St. Thomas. Bath sits in the valley of the Rio Grande in eastern St. Thomas, in a landscape subject to valley flooding during heavy rainfall events and to the historical impacts of hurricanes on the eastern interior of the parish.

## Clergy
The Bath area's Anglican worship points have been served within the **St. Thomas deanery** under shared clergy arrangements, with the two churches in Bath likely sharing the same circuit clergy.

## Notable Facts
The proximity of St. Luke's and St. Thomas' in **Bath** reflects the town's historical importance as one of the more substantial interior communities of St. Thomas. **Bath's healing waters** attracted visitors across the colonial period, and the town's genteel character supported a more complex social and religious life than typical agricultural communities. The **Bath Botanical Garden (1779)** is one of the oldest in the Western Hemisphere and famously received Captain Bligh's Tahitian breadfruit in 1793.
