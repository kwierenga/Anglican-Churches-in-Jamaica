# St. Matthew's, Wilmington
**St. Thomas** · Church · active

St. Matthew's Anglican Church serves **Wilmington**, a small community in St. Thomas parish.

## History
**English rule (1655) and the founding parish.** Under English rule from **1655**, St. Thomas was one of Jamaica's original parishes — originally **"St. Thomas-in-the-East"** with the first rector appointed in **1666**.

**Sugar economy and slavery.** Wilmington is one of the small communities of St. Thomas, in the parish whose colonial-era plantation landscape stretched from the south coast at Morant Bay up into the Blue Mountain foothills and the John Crow Mountain slopes. Great estates — Stokes Hall, Golden Grove, Albion, Holland — dominated the landscape, worked by enslaved Africans throughout the colonial period. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union** to "wipe non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb** (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**The Morant Bay Rebellion (11 October 1865).** On **11 October 1865**, **Paul Bogle** — a small farmer and Native Baptist deacon of **Stony Gut** — led a protest march to the Morant Bay courthouse. Governor **Edward John Eyre's** brutal response: **439 executed**, **600+ flogged**, **1,000+ homes burned**. Bogle was hanged on **24 October 1865**; **George William Gordon** was hanged on **23 October 1865**. **Both were declared National Heroes in 1965**.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall.** Jamaica became a **Crown Colony in 1866**. Law 20 of 1867 absorbed **St. David** into St. Thomas. The Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

**Foundation of St. Matthew's.** The Anglican Diocese established St. Matthew's as a small mission chapel to serve the Wilmington community as part of its rural network across St. Thomas. The wider parish's nineteenth-century history shaped the social context within which the diocese expanded its mission stations across the parish in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries. No substantive foundational date or detailed architectural history specifically for St. Matthew's Wilmington has been identified in the public heritage sources reviewed.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** Internal self-government came in **1944**. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** fulfilled the aspirations for which Bogle and Gordon died.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. **Hurricane Ivan (2004)** had earlier caused widespread damage across St. Thomas.

## Architecture
St. Matthew's is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for mission stations across the St. Thomas interior.

## Clergy
St. Matthew's Wilmington is served within the Diocese's **Eastern Jamaica Region**.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to **St. Matthew the Evangelist** — apostle, former tax collector, and traditional author of the first Gospel, with a feast day on **21 September** — is a common Anglican choice across the diocese.
