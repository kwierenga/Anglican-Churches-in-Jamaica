# St. Matthias, Kentucky
**Westmoreland** · Church · active

St. Matthias Anglican Church serves the community of Kentucky, an interior district of Westmoreland south-east of Petersfield. The church is listed in the Diocese of Jamaica and the Cayman Islands directory under the Westmoreland Deanery. It does not appear in the 1891 Handbook of Jamaica's Anglican returns nor in the 1900 Handbook — placing its foundation in the late phase of Bishop Enos Nuttall's rural mission expansion, during which the Diocese grew from 26 rural stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1703).** Westmoreland was officially formed in **1703** from the western portion of the older parish of St. Elizabeth, named for its position at the western part of the island. The parish capital, **Savanna-la-Mar**, was founded around 1730. In 1723 the new parish of Hanover was carved out of Westmoreland's northern section.

**Slavery and the sugar economy.** Under English rule, sugar and slavery accelerated in Westmoreland: an inventory in **1722** numbered the estates at **73**, making the parish one of the leading sugar producers of the British Caribbean. The Beckford family — Richard, Peter, and William — were among the most prominent planters; modern **Petersfield** is named in commemoration of Peter Beckford. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds; its parish vestry was composed of the planter class, and its rectors held glebe lands worked by enslaved people.

**The Sam Sharpe Rebellion (December 1831).** The Christmas Rebellion of 1831, led by the Baptist deacon Samuel Sharpe and ignited at the Kensington Estate in St. James on the night of 27 December 1831, spread rapidly through the western parishes (St. James, Trelawny, Hanover, and Westmoreland) with some 60,000 enslaved people involved. Suppressed brutally, the rebellion ended with Sharpe's hanging in Montego Bay on 23 May 1832. The Colonial Church Union — formed in the aftermath as a vigilante alliance of Anglican planters and clergy — undertook a campaign of "riotous destruction of chapels at Stewart Town, Lucea, Brown's Town, Sav.-la-Mar, St. Ann's Bay, Fuller's Field, and other centres of Wesleyan and Baptist activity" (J. B. Ellis, *Diocese of Jamaica*, 1913). Sharpe is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation and post-1838 Westmoreland.** Within two years of Sharpe's hanging the British Parliament passed the Slavery Abolition Act (1833); Emancipation came in 1834, and full freedom followed at the end of the apprenticeship period on **1 August 1838**. By 1854 the number of active sugar estates in Westmoreland had collapsed to 34, less than half the 1722 figure. After 1838, freed people settled the upland districts as small farmers — Kentucky, like Mt. Airy, Cornwall Mountain, and Sheffield, belongs to that wave of post-Emancipation free settlement. Germans were settled at Seaford Town in 1839; East Indian indentured labourers arrived from the 1840s onward.

**1865 to 1900: the post-disestablishment Diocese.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865, Crown Colony rule replaced the planter Assembly (1866); the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica. Under Bishop Enos Nuttall (Bishop of Jamaica 1880–1916), the Diocese expanded its rural mission network from 26 rural stations in 1880 to 122 by 1911. The 1900 Handbook records the Westmoreland cures as Sav.-la-Mar (C. H. Davis & J. J. C. Ormsby), Trinity (S. J. Vaughan), Bluefields & Kings (S. Negus), St. Paul's & Negril (R. L. Reid), Petersfield & Darliston (W. Heaver), and New Road & 5 Mission Stations (E. D. Tinling) — Kentucky is not separately listed, suggesting attachment to one of these cures as one of the unnamed mission stations of the period.

**1912 hurricane, 1938 Frome riots, Independence.** A November **1912** hurricane brought a tidal wave that nearly destroyed Savanna-la-Mar. The **1938 Frome labour riots** at the Frome sugar factory contributed directly to the formation of Jamaica's Trade Union Movement and the rise of Sir Alexander Bustamante. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Matthias a settled mission within the Westmoreland Anglican network.

## Architecture
St. Matthias is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for mission stations across the Westmoreland interior. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal storm surge while remaining exposed to wind and rainfall during major storms.

## Clergy
St. Matthias Kentucky is served within the Montego Bay Region's Westmoreland Deanery.

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** **St. Matthias** was the apostle chosen by lot to replace Judas Iscariot among the twelve, as recorded in **Acts 1:15–26**. His feast day is **14 May** (in the contemporary Anglican calendar; older usage observes 24 February). Matthias is the only apostle whose calling is recorded in Acts rather than the Gospels — an apt patron for a small mission station founded as part of an evangelistic expansion across the post-Emancipation Jamaican countryside.

**Hurricane Melissa (28 October 2025)** made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Interior communities across Westmoreland experienced destructive winds, and the Diocese reported some **50 church buildings destroyed or significantly damaged**. The specific status of St. Matthias Kentucky after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.
