# St. Philip's, Old England
**Manchester** · Church · active

St. Philip's Anglican Church serves Old England, a community in Manchester parish, confirmed Google Places identifies this as 'Old England Anglican Church,' a quaint church noted by reviewers as needing some restoration to its former glory. Old England is a community in the southern part of Manchester with a name that — like many Jamaican communities — preserves a nostalgic connection to the colonists' homeland.

## History
Old England sits in the southern part of Manchester, in the agricultural landscape between Mandeville and the Clarendon border. The name 'Old England' is one of several Jamaican community names that reflect the nostalgia of colonial settlers for their homeland. The Anglican Diocese established St. Philip's to serve this community. The church has served the Old England farming community for several generations.

## Architecture
St. Philip's is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural Manchester in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. Set in the rural interior of Manchester. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
Old England has been served within the Manchester deanery under shared clergy arrangements.

## Notable Facts
Old England is one of several Jamaican community names that express the colonial settlers' longing for their homeland — alongside communities named New England, Old Harbour, and similar. These names are living monuments to the psychology of displacement that shaped the colonial landscape. St. Philip's feast day (1 May, shared with James the Less) opens the month of May in the Anglican calendar.
