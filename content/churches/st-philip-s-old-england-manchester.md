# St. Philip's, Old England
**Manchester** · Church · active

St. Philip's Anglican Church serves Old England, a community in the southern part of Manchester whose name preserves a nostalgic connection to the colonists' homeland. Old England Anglican Church is identified in Google Places and described by reviewers as "a quaint church... needing some restoration to its former glory." It is administered as a mission of the **St. Mark's, Mandeville cure** (the Manchester Parish Church).

## History
**Founding of the parish (1814).** Manchester was created by **Act 55 George III. c. 23** of **1814** in response to a petition of 29 November 1814 by residents of Mile Gully, May Day, and Carpenter's Mountain. The Act came into effect on **13 December 1814**, taking the "hill country" of eastern St. Elizabeth and the western portions of Vere and Clarendon. The new parish was named after **William Montagu, 5th Duke of Manchester** (Governor 1808–1827); **Mandeville**, the capital, was named after his son, **Viscount Mandeville**.

**Slavery, the parish church, and the Sam Sharpe Rebellion (1831).** Manchester's hill-country economy was built on coffee, supplemented by sugar in lower zones, both worked by enslaved African labour. **St. Mark's Anglican Church, Mandeville** — the parish church of Manchester — was completed and consecrated in **1820**; its organ loft was used as a jail during the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**. Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation, 1865, disestablishment.** Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Maidstone** in Manchester (1840) was among Jamaica's earliest Moravian-led free villages. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of St. Philip's: the Mandeville cure.** Old England sits in the southern part of Manchester, in the agricultural landscape between Mandeville and the Clarendon border. The name "Old England" is one of several Jamaican community names that reflect the nostalgia of colonial settlers for their homeland. **Bishop Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. The Mandeville Parish Church website lists St. Philip Old England as a mission of the Mandeville cure.

**Twentieth century: Kendal, bauxite, Independence.** The **Kendal train tragedy** of **1 September 1957** at Kendal Manchester claimed almost 200 lives — Jamaica's worst rail accident. The **bauxite industry** transformed Manchester from 1957 onward. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Philip's a settled mission within the Mandeville cure.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of St. Philip's Old England after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. Philip's is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural Manchester in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries.

## Clergy
Old England has been served as a mission of the St. Mark's Mandeville cure within the Manchester deanery.

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** **Philip the Apostle**, one of the twelve disciples, brought Nathanael to Christ (John 1:43–46) and asked Jesus to "show us the Father" (John 14:8). His feast day in the Anglican calendar falls on **1 May**, shared with James the Less. The Philip of Acts — deacon, evangelist, and baptiser of the Ethiopian eunuch — is a separate figure (feast 11 October); the Old England dedication is ordinarily understood to refer to the Apostle.

**Old England.** The community name belongs to a wider Jamaican toponymic tradition of nostalgic English place-names — Old England, Old Harbour, Westminster, Kingston, Manchester — that record the colonial-era psychology of displacement.
