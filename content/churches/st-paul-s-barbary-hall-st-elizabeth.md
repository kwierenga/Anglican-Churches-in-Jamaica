# St. Paul's, Barbary Hall
**St. Elizabeth** · Church · active

St. Paul’s Anglican Church serves the farming community of Barbary Hall in southern St. Elizabeth. It was founded in the late 1800s to meet the needs of families who settled in the area after gaining freedom in 1838. Despite facing storms and necessary repairs over the years, the church has consistently served its community. Today, St. Paul’s remains a stable and active place of worship for the people of Barbary Hall.

## History
St. Paul’s Anglican Church began as a mission in the post-Emancipation period, established as freed people moved inland from coastal estates to build small farms in the Barbary Hall area. Before 1838, this region was dominated by plantations near Parottee and the south coast, where most of the population was enslaved. After slavery ended, the Anglican Diocese sent clergy from Bull Savannah, Southfield, and Parottee to serve these new free-village communities. By the 1880s, a permanent chapel was completed and dedicated to St. Paul the Apostle. The church quickly became a central hub for baptisms, marriages, and community gatherings, helping families build a new future after slavery.

## Architecture
St. Paul’s is a sturdy limestone church built in the simple Gothic Revival style typical of rural Jamaica. It features a rectangular nave, a small chancel, and a modest bell-cot above the western gable. The steep roof, now covered with metal, was likely originally made of wooden shingles. Inside, the church has an open-timber roof, wooden pews, and narrow pointed windows with colored glass. The surrounding churchyard, with its graves of early community families, contributes to the site's quiet and historic atmosphere.

## Clergy
St. Paul’s has always been part of the Black River Deanery, sharing clergy with nearby churches such as Flagaman, Slipe, and Parottee. Early priests, including Rev. H. C. Wharton and Canon W. R. Campbell, visited Barbary Hall on their regular missionary circuits. The church supported basic education for local children, providing instruction in reading, writing, and scripture at a time when formal schools were scarce. After Jamaica's Independence in 1962, the church continued its mission through youth groups and the Mothers’ Union. Lay readers played a crucial role in leading services when ordained clergy were assigned to multiple congregations.

## Notable Facts
St. Paul’s has endured several major hurricanes, including Charlie (1951) and Gilbert (1988), which caused significant damage to the roof and windows. Each time, the community rallied to rebuild and repair the church. Further upkeep was carried out in the 2000s to maintain the building's structure and interior. Today, St. Paul’s Anglican Church remains active with regular services, weddings, funerals, and harvest festivals, standing as a lasting symbol of faith and community resilience in Barbary Hall.
