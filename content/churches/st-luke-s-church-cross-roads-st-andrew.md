# St. Luke's Church, Cross Roads
**St. Andrew** · Church · active

St. Luke’s Anglican Church is a prominent landmark at 89 Slipe Road in the bustling transport hub of Cross Roads, Kingston. Its origins date to the 1870s, when a small congregation won the right to worship and first gathered under a large tree on the property. The church has been rebuilt twice, evolving from a wooden chapel to a modern structure consecrated in 1980. Today, it is renowned for its extensive outreach—including a feeding program, a basic school, and a seniors' home—and for its strong leadership during crises like Hurricane Melissa.

## History
**English conquest (1655) and St. Andrew parish (1664).** Penn and Venables’s English expedition captured Jamaica from Spain in May **1655**. **St. Andrew parish was established in 1664** with a 600-acre glebe granted in 1667 by Charles II. The **Great Earthquake of 7 June 1692** destroyed two-thirds of Port Royal; the new city of **Kingston** was formally established on **22 July 1692**, with the Assembly constituting Kingston as a parish in **1693**.

**Slavery, Diocese, Sam Sharpe, Emancipation, disestablishment.** Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church. The **Diocese of Jamaica** was established by Letters Patent on **24 July 1824**; **Christopher Lipscomb** was consecrated first Bishop in 1824. During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. Bishop Lipscomb (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. The capital moved from Spanish Town to **Kingston in 1872**.

**Foundation of St. Luke’s (1870s).** St. Luke’s was born from a post-Emancipation struggle for spiritual autonomy, with its first congregation gathering under a tree in the **1870s**. The church’s own history states the "right to worship was fought for and won," leading to the donation of land and the construction of a wooden chapel in **1875**. As the community grew, a larger brick church was dedicated in **1890**.

**The 1907 earthquake and rebuilding.** The **14 January 1907** Kingston earthquake (magnitude 6.2) destroyed much of Kingston; under Nuttall’s £50,000 reconstruction programme, ten churches were consecrated within a single month in **January 1911**. St. Luke’s 1890 brick church was partially destroyed in the 1907 earthquake but was repaired and enlarged, demonstrating the congregation’s resilience. The **Kingston and St. Andrew Corporation (KSAC)** unified the two parishes for civic administration in **1923**. St. Luke’s served as a "mother church," nurturing a mission on Hope Road that later became the independent parish of **St. Margaret’s in Liguanea (1938)**.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** Bishop **Percival Gibson** (1957–1967) was the first native-born Bishop of Jamaica. The current modern church was dedicated in **1968** and consecrated in **1980**, anchoring St. Luke’s life in the post-Independence era (Jamaica became independent on **6 August 1962**).

## Architecture
St. Luke’s is a substantial mid-20th century church built from reinforced materials to serve a large urban congregation. It replaced the original 1890 brick building that was damaged in the 1907 earthquake. The property is a functional campus, including the main worship space, halls for meetings, and a unique pedestrian bridge linking the grounds to Brentford Road, a gift from a local businessman. Situated at a major Kingston intersection, the building serves as a highly visible Christian landmark within a dense urban landscape of shops and offices.

## Clergy
St. Luke's has been shaped by a succession of dedicated clergy, from its first rectors in the 1890s to modern leaders like Venerable Eric L. Maxwell, who oversaw the construction of the current building, and Venerable Dr. Alvin Stone, who guided its consecration. The current rector, Archdeacon Patrick Cunningham, continues this legacy of strong leadership. The parish's life is defined by robust outreach ministries, including a weekly feeding program, St. Simon’s Basic School, and a senior citizens' home. It also maintains an active digital presence, streaming services and hosting public lecture series on faith and society, extending its influence beyond its physical walls.

## Notable Facts
A key part of St. Luke's identity is its history of overcoming disaster, notably the 1907 earthquake that destroyed its earlier building. More recently, during Hurricane Melissa (2025), the church served as a critical hub for relief efforts, collecting donations and providing spiritual support through pastoral messages from its rector. While the church building did not report major structural damage, its role in the community was amplified. From its humble beginnings worshiping under a tree to its 150th anniversary in 2025, St. Luke’s stands as a powerful witness to faith, service, and resilience at the crossroads of Kingston.
