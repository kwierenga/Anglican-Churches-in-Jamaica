# St. Luke's Church, Cross Roads
**St. Andrew** · Church · active

St. Luke’s Anglican Church is a prominent landmark at 89 Slipe Road in the bustling transport hub of Cross Roads, Kingston. Its origins date to the 1870s, when a small congregation won the right to worship and first gathered under a large tree on the property. The church has been rebuilt twice, evolving from a wooden chapel to a modern structure consecrated in 1980. Today, it is renowned for its extensive outreach—including a feeding program, a basic school, and a seniors' home—and for its strong leadership during crises like Hurricane Melissa.

## History
St. Luke’s was born from a post-Emancipation struggle for spiritual autonomy, with its first congregation gathering under a tree in the 1870s. The church’s own history states the 'right to worship was fought for and won,' leading to the donation of land and the construction of a wooden chapel in 1875. As the community grew, a larger brick church was dedicated in 1890. This building was partially destroyed in the 1907 earthquake but was repaired and enlarged, demonstrating the congregation's resilience. St. Luke's also served as a 'mother church,' nurturing a mission on Hope Road that later became the independent parish of St. Margaret's in Liguanea. The current modern church was dedicated in 1968 and consecrated in 1980, anchoring its life in the post-Independence era.

## Architecture
St. Luke’s is a substantial mid-20th century church built from reinforced materials to serve a large urban congregation. It replaced the original 1890 brick building that was damaged in the 1907 earthquake. The property is a functional campus, including the main worship space, halls for meetings, and a unique pedestrian bridge linking the grounds to Brentford Road, a gift from a local businessman. Situated at a major Kingston intersection, the building serves as a highly visible Christian landmark within a dense urban landscape of shops and offices.

## Clergy
St. Luke's has been shaped by a succession of dedicated clergy, from its first rectors in the 1890s to modern leaders like Venerable Eric L. Maxwell, who oversaw the construction of the current building, and Venerable Dr. Alvin Stone, who guided its consecration. The current rector, Archdeacon Patrick Cunningham, continues this legacy of strong leadership. The parish's life is defined by robust outreach ministries, including a weekly feeding program, St. Simon’s Basic School, and a senior citizens' home. It also maintains an active digital presence, streaming services and hosting public lecture series on faith and society, extending its influence beyond its physical walls.

## Notable Facts
A key part of St. Luke's identity is its history of overcoming disaster, notably the 1907 earthquake that destroyed its earlier building. More recently, during Hurricane Melissa (2025), the church served as a critical hub for relief efforts, collecting donations and providing spiritual support through pastoral messages from its rector. While the church building did not report major structural damage, its role in the community was amplified. From its humble beginnings worshiping under a tree to its 150th anniversary in 2025, St. Luke’s stands as a powerful witness to faith, service, and resilience at the crossroads of Kingston.
