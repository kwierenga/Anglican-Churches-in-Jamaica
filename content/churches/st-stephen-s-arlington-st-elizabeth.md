# St. Stephen's, Arlington
**St. Elizabeth** · Church · active

St. Stephen's Anglican Church serves Arlington in St. Elizabeth, one of several Jamaican community names transferred from English places by colonial-era estate proprietors (Arlington being a noted English country house and a London-area place name).

## History
**Founding of the parish (c.1664).** St. Elizabeth was one of the original Jamaican parishes, established under Sir Thomas Modyford and named in honour of **Lady Elizabeth Modyford**, wife of the first Governor of Jamaica (1664–71). Prior to **1703**, the parish stretched across the territory of present-day Westmoreland, Hanover, and Manchester; in 1703 Westmoreland was created from the western portion, and in **1814** the final subdivision created Manchester from the southern section. **Lacovia** was the original parish capital before Black River became the sole capital. The parish church, **St. John the Evangelist** at Black River, dates structurally from around 1700.

**Slavery and the sugar economy.** Under English rule, sugar and slavery defined the parish economy: prominent estates included Holland, Vineyard, and Fullerswood. **Cudjoe**, leader of the Leeward Maroons, signed the **Peace Treaty of 1739** at the Peace Cave in St. Elizabeth's Cockpit Country, securing Maroon land rights — and the Maroon community of **Accompong Town** still exists in the parish today. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**The Sam Sharpe Rebellion (December 1831).** The Christmas Rebellion of 1831, led by the Baptist deacon **Samuel Sharpe** at the Kensington Estate in St. James on **27 December 1831**, spread through the western parishes; some 60,000 enslaved people were involved. Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation and post-1838 settlement.** The British Parliament passed the Slavery Abolition Act in 1833; Emancipation came in 1834, and full freedom followed at the end of the apprenticeship period on **1 August 1838**. Freed people settled the upland districts of St. Elizabeth as small farmers — Arlington belongs to that wave of post-Emancipation free settlement. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865, Crown Colony rule replaced the planter Assembly (1866); the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of St. Stephen's: Nuttall's expansion.** Bishop Enos Nuttall's tenure as Bishop of Jamaica (1880–1916) saw the Diocese grow from 26 rural mission stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. The church does not appear in the 1891 Handbook of Jamaica's Anglican clergy returns, consistent with a foundation date in the late phase of Nuttall's expansion.

**Twentieth century, Independence, hurricanes.** The 1893–94 logwood export boom shaped the parish economy. The bauxite industry developed in St. Elizabeth from **1943**, with **ALPART** later headquartered at Nain. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Stephen's a settled rural mission.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. St. Elizabeth was severely affected, with major Anglican losses including St. Luke's Balaclava and St. Thomas' Lacovia. The specific status of St. Stephen's Arlington after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. Stephen's is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for mission stations across the St. Elizabeth interior. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal storm surge while remaining exposed to wind and rainfall during major storms.

## Clergy
St. Stephen's Arlington is served within the Montego Bay Region's St. Elizabeth deanery network.

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** **St. Stephen the protomartyr** was the first Christian martyr, deacon of the early Jerusalem church, stoned to death for his preaching as recorded in **Acts 7**. His feast day on **26 December** (the day after Christmas) makes "Boxing Day" a particularly significant date for Stephen-dedicated congregations.
