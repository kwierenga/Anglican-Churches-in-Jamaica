# St. Bartholomew's, Kellitts
**Clarendon** · Mission · active

St. Bartholomew's at **Kellitts** is a small Anglican mission in upper Clarendon, set in the limestone hill country between Crofts Hill, Spaldings, and Frankfield. Like its larger neighbour St. Bartholomew's at Frankfield, the chapel takes the name of the apostle Bartholomew (Nathanael), and serves the small farming communities of the Kellitts district within the Clarendon Deanery of the Diocese of Jamaica and the Cayman Islands.

## History
**Clarendon parish (1664) and the Cross Church.** Penn and Venables's English expedition captured Jamaica from Spain in May **1655**. **Clarendon** was created as a parish in **1664**, named for Sir Edward Hyde, first Earl of Clarendon. The original parish church — the **Church of the White Cross** at The Cross — was the third Anglican church in Jamaica and the site of the first House of Assembly elections. As English settlement pushed northward into the interior highlands along the Rio Minho, smaller chapels of ease were built to serve the more distant settlements; **St. Paul's, Chapelton**, the chapel of ease established in **1666**, gave the village of Chapelton its name and eventually became the parish church when the Cross Church was destroyed in 1815.

**Sugar, slavery, and the Cockpit Country frontier.** The fertile lowlands of Clarendon along the Rio Minho became some of Jamaica's wealthiest sugar estates — Suttons, Pennants, Ballards, the Dawkins lands — built on the labour of enslaved Africans. One of Jamaica's earliest slave rebellions broke out at **Suttons Plantation in 1690**, when about 500 enslaved people escaped into the hills and joined Spanish-speaking free Africans, contributing to the formation of the **Maroon** communities under **Cudjoe** in the Cockpit Country. The upper Clarendon hills near Kellitts and Crofts Hill bordered this Maroon frontier; the small upland settlements of the post-Emancipation era grew up on land that had been thinly settled during slavery.

**Anglican clergy, the Colonial Church Union, and Sam Sharpe.** Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church and, in the words of subsequent historians, "a kind of chaplaincy to the plantocracy." Clergy held glebe lands worked by enslaved people and received a share of the £8,823 compensation paid to Jamaican clergy after abolition. After the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion** the Clarendon Anglican clergy joined planters in the **Colonial Church Union** to suppress the non-conformist preachers whom the planters blamed for inciting the enslaved. **Sam Sharpe** was hanged on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. **Emancipation came in 1834**, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**.

**Disestablishment, free villages, and the founding of St. Bartholomew's, Kellitts.** After the **Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865** and the imposition of Crown Colony rule, the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Disestablishment unlocked the funds that built the great Anglican network of elementary schools — **263 schools** by 1900 — and powered the rural mission expansion under Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916), who increased the diocese's mission stations from 26 in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. The Kellitts chapel belongs to this expansion: a small mission served from one of the larger upper-Clarendon parishes (most likely St. Bartholomew's, Frankfield, or St. Paul's, Chapelton), built to serve the descendants of the enslaved who had spread into the limestone hills as smallholders, citrus and provision farmers after Emancipation.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** The completion of the **May Pen-Chapelton railway in 1913**, extended to **Frankfield in 1925**, transformed upper Clarendon's hill economy by giving the citrus and provision farmers of the interior direct rail access to May Pen and the south coast. The Kellitts district benefited indirectly from the railway through nearby station towns at Spaldings and Frankfield. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found Kellitts an established farming community with its small Anglican mission. The railway closed in 1974, and Kellitts settled into the quieter pattern of road-served upper Clarendon farming life it has known ever since.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The exposed ridges and elevated districts of upper Clarendon experienced significant wind damage during Melissa's passage.

## Architecture
St. Bartholomew's, Kellitts, is a modest masonry mission chapel of the kind raised across upper Clarendon and St. Ann in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries: a single nave under a pitched roof, with a small chancel, side windows, and an entrance porch on the gable end. Stone-and-mortar construction, with later cement rendering, is typical for chapels of this size; the elevated terrain channels wind during storm passages and makes good drainage essential to the building's longevity.

## Clergy
St. Bartholomew's, Kellitts, is served within the **Clarendon Deanery** of the Diocese of Jamaica and the Cayman Islands. As a small mission station the chapel is most likely served by a priest of one of the larger upper-Clarendon parishes — St. Bartholomew's, Frankfield, or St. Paul's, Chapelton — under a shared cure arrangement.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to **St. Bartholomew the Apostle** (also identified in John's Gospel as Nathanael) — under whose name several upper Clarendon chapels stand — is uncommon enough in rural Jamaican Anglicanism to suggest a deliberate clustering by the diocese's nineteenth-century mission planners around the Frankfield area. Bartholomew's traditional feast day is **24 August**.
