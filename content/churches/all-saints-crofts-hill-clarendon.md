# All Saints, Crofts Hill
**Clarendon** · Church · active

All Saints' Anglican Church at Crofts Hill is the cure church for a group of rural Anglican congregations in interior northern Clarendon. Together with its mission stations — **St. Bartholomew's at Good Hope, St. Peter's at Rhoden Hall, and St. Michael's at Arthur's Seat** — All Saints' serves the upland farming districts north of the parish capital. The churchyard contains one of the larger inscribed cemeteries in the deanery, with **187 catalogued memorials**.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1664).** Clarendon parish was created in **1664**, nine years after the English seizure of Jamaica from Spain (**1655**), and named for **Sir Edward Hyde, 1st Earl of Clarendon** (1609–1674), Lord High Chancellor of England under Charles II. The original parish church was the **Church of the White Cross** at "The Cross" — Jamaica's third Anglican church and the site of Jamaica's first House of Assembly elections. **St. Paul's Chapelton** was built as a chapel of ease in **1666** in the upland interior, and became the parish church when the Cross Church was destroyed in **1815**. **Vere** was carved out as a separate sugar-plain parish in **1671** with St. Peter's Alley as its parish church; Vere was reabsorbed into Clarendon in **1867**, and **Law 20 of 1887** designated **May Pen** as chief town.

**Slavery and the Suttons rebellion.** Clarendon was one of Jamaica's great sugar parishes. The fertile Rio Minho plain held estates bearing the names of their English founders — **Suttons, Pennants, Ballards** — while the buccaneer **Henry Morgan** owned Morgan's Valley and Danks. The **Dawkins family** alone accumulated seventeen plantations covering over 25,000 acres in Clarendon between 1665 and 1833. The wealth of the parish was built on the labour of enslaved Africans. One of Jamaica's earliest slave rebellions erupted at **Suttons Plantation in 1690**: some 500 enslaved people escaped and joined Spanish-speaking free Africans, forming the Maroon communities eventually led by **Cudjoe** (Kojo) into the Cockpit Country.

**Sam Sharpe and the Colonial Church Union (1831).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion** — the largest slave revolt in British Caribbean history — planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**, a vigilante alliance that sought to "wipe the non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth." Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation, 1865, disestablishment.** Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. The freed population of Clarendon established free villages and provision grounds across the interior, moving into the upland districts to grow coffee, citrus, and provisions. Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb** (1824–1843) had already begun extending Anglican schools across the parish — by 1835 he reported 142 schools with 8,500 scholars across Jamaica. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of the All Saints' cure.** Crofts Hill is an interior community in the hill country of northern Clarendon, settled in the post-Emancipation period as freed people moved into the parish's upland districts in search of land for provision farming. The Anglican Diocese established All Saints' to serve this community as a cure parish, with mission chapels founded across the surrounding districts. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**, and the Crofts Hill cure dates from this expansion phase.

**Twentieth century: bauxite, May Pen, Independence.** Chapelton lost its status as parish capital in **1938**, with administrative functions transferring to **May Pen**. **Alcoa** began bauxite operations in Clarendon in **1959**, with the first shipment in 1963 and the **Jamalco** alumina refinery at Halse Hall opening in 1972 — transforming the parish economy. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found All Saints' a settled cure church.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of All Saints' Crofts Hill after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
All Saints' is a substantial rural masonry church, larger than the surrounding mission chapels in keeping with its role as cure church. The hillside position gives some defensive value against coastal flooding but increases exposure to wind during major storms. The Diocese of Jamaica maintains a directory image of the church among its online photographs.

## Clergy
All Saints' Crofts Hill is the parent church of a four-church cure with mission stations at Good Hope, Rhoden Hall, and Arthur's Seat. The cure is listed as **VACANT** in the most recent diocesan directory, with the parsonage at Croft's Hill P.O. (telephone 966-7609); pastoral oversight has historically rotated among neighbouring Clarendon clergy during vacancies.

## Notable Facts
The All Saints' dedication — commemorating the full company of recognised and unrecognised saints on **1 November** — is consistent with the late-nineteenth-century pattern of dedicating new rural churches to general categories of holiness rather than to single named patrons.
