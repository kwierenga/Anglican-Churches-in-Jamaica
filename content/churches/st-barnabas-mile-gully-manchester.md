# St. Barnabas', Mile Gully
**Manchester** · Church · active

St. Barnabas' Anglican Church serves Mile Gully, confirmed It is the active congregational Anglican church of Mile Gully, in a community that also contains the famous ruined 'Duppy Church' (St. George's ruins) and St. Paul's. Reviewers describe St. Barnabas' as a 'comfortable space with lively worship,' bringing a distinct character from the historic ruins nearby.

## History
Mile Gully's Anglican presence includes both the historic ruins of St. George's Church and two active churches — St. Paul's and St. Barnabas'. St. Barnabas' provides the lively, contemporary dimension of Anglican worship in Mile Gully, maintaining an active congregation in a community defined by its famous ruined church landmark.

## Architecture
St. Barnabas' is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural Manchester in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. Set in the rural interior of Manchester. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
Mile Gully has been served within the Manchester deanery. St. Barnabas' provides active congregational ministry in the Mile Gully community.

## Notable Facts
St. Barnabas' feast day (11 June) is associated with long days — Barnabas Day was the longest day in the Julian calendar before the Gregorian reform. An old English proverb ran 'Barnaby bright, Barnaby bright, the longest day and the shortest night.' The lively worship described by reviewers at St. Barnabas' Mile Gully contrasts sharply with the eerie silence of the nearby Duppy Church ruins, giving Mile Gully a vivid dual character.
