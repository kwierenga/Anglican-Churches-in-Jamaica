# St. Paul's, Nonsuch
**Portland** · Church · active

St. Paul's Anglican Church serves the Nonsuch area of Portland, a community in the interior hill country east of Port Antonio. Nonsuch is a small agricultural and residential district in the limestone karst country of eastern Portland, and St. Paul's has provided Anglican ministry to the local community for several generations. The church is distinct from the ruined St. Peter's Chapel also recorded at Nonsuch, suggesting the community has had more than one Anglican worship point across its history.

## History
The Nonsuch district of Portland lies in the interior hill country inland from the east Portland coast, in a landscape of karst limestone, small farms, and limited road access. The Anglican Diocese established St. Paul's as a mission chapel for the Nonsuch community, serving the local farming families in the post-Emancipation period. The presence of both a ruined chapel (St. Peter's) and an active church (St. Paul's) at Nonsuch suggests the community sustained Anglican worship across multiple sites and periods.

## Architecture
St. Paul's is a small masonry chapel in the limestone karst landscape of interior eastern Portland. The building is set in a community with difficult access — Nonsuch sits in the hill country away from the main coastal road — and the church's construction reflects the practical requirements of a rural interior chapel in this terrain. The karst landscape creates distinctive local conditions, including sink holes and cave systems, that shape the physical environment of the church.

## Clergy
Nonsuch has been served within the Portland deanery under shared clergy arrangements. The interior location means the church depends significantly on lay leaders for its regular pastoral life.

## Notable Facts
Nonsuch is notable for the Nonsuch Caves, a heritage tourism site developed in the limestone cave system of the area. The co-existence of Anglican church ruins and an active church at the same location speaks to the continuity of community worship in Nonsuch across more than a century. Hurricane Melissa (October 2025) brought serious wind and rain impacts to eastern Portland; the Nonsuch community was in the affected zone.
