# St. Helena's, Sheffield
**Westmoreland** · Church · active

St. Helena's Anglican Church serves the Sheffield District of Westmoreland, an interior community near Darliston in the southern uplands of the parish. The church is listed in the Diocese of Jamaica and the Cayman Islands directory under the Westmoreland Deanery. It does not appear in the 1891 Handbook of Jamaica's Anglican returns nor in the 1900 Handbook — placing its foundation in the late phase of Bishop Enos Nuttall's rural mission expansion (1880–1916).

## History
**Founding of the parish (1703).** Westmoreland was officially formed in **1703** from the western portion of the older parish of St. Elizabeth, named for its position at the western part of the island. The parish capital, **Savanna-la-Mar**, was founded around 1730. In 1723 the new parish of Hanover was created from the northern part of Westmoreland.

**Slavery and the sugar economy.** Under English rule, sugar and slavery accelerated in Westmoreland: an inventory in **1722** numbered the estates at **73**, making the parish one of the leading sugar producers of British Caribbean. The Beckford family — Richard, Peter, and William — were among the most prominent planters; modern Petersfield is named for Peter Beckford. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds; its parish vestry was composed of the planter class, and its rectors held glebe lands worked by enslaved people.

**The Sam Sharpe Rebellion (December 1831).** The Christmas Rebellion of 1831, led by the Baptist deacon Samuel Sharpe and ignited at the Kensington Estate in St. James on the night of 27 December 1831, spread rapidly through the western parishes — St. James, Trelawny, Hanover, and Westmoreland — with some 60,000 enslaved people involved. Suppressed brutally, the rebellion ended with Sharpe's hanging in Montego Bay on 23 May 1832. Within two years the British Parliament passed the Slavery Abolition Act (1833); Emancipation came in 1834, and full freedom followed on **1 August 1838**. Sharpe is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Post-Emancipation Westmoreland.** By 1854 the number of active sugar estates in Westmoreland had collapsed to 34, less than half the 1722 figure. Freed people settled the upland districts as small farmers, growing yam, ginger, pimento, and provisions. Germans were settled at Seaford Town in 1839; East Indian indentured labourers arrived from the 1840s onward. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865, Crown Colony rule replaced the planter Assembly (1866); the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**, reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica. Bishop Enos Nuttall's tenure (1880–1916) saw the Diocese grow from 26 rural stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**, and small upland chapels like St. Helena's belong to that wave of late-nineteenth and early-twentieth-century mission planting.

**1912 hurricane, 1938 Frome riots, Independence.** A November **1912** hurricane brought a tidal wave that nearly destroyed Savanna-la-Mar. The **1938 Frome labour riots** at the Frome sugar factory contributed directly to the formation of Jamaica's Trade Union Movement and the rise of Sir Alexander Bustamante. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Helena's a settled rural mission within the southern Westmoreland uplands.

## Architecture
St. Helena's is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for mission stations across the Westmoreland uplands. The interior hill-country position creates wind exposure during storm passages.

## Clergy
St. Helena's Sheffield is served within the Montego Bay Region's Westmoreland Deanery.

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** **St. Helena** (c. 246/248 – c. 330 AD) was the mother of the Emperor Constantine the Great; according to tradition, she discovered the True Cross during a fourth-century pilgrimage to Jerusalem, an event celebrated as "the Invention of the Cross." Her feast day in the Anglican calendar is **18 August**. The dedication of a small Westmoreland chapel to Constantine's mother — the empress whose pilgrimage shaped the Christian sites of the Holy Land — gives St. Helena's an unusual theological character, connecting a remote Jamaican farming community to the foundational events of fourth-century imperial Christianity.

**Hurricane Melissa (28 October 2025)** made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Sheffield District, in interior Westmoreland, was in the direct impact zone. Across the Diocese of Jamaica and the Cayman Islands, some **50 church buildings were destroyed or significantly damaged**. The specific status of St. Helena's after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.
