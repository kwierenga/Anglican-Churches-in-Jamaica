# All Souls', Brompton
**St. Elizabeth** · Church · active

All Souls' Anglican Church serves Brompton, a community in St. Elizabeth parish. Brompton is a small farming community, and All Souls' has provided Anglican ministry here for several generations. The dedication to All Souls — commemorating all the faithful departed on 2 November — gives this church a liturgical identity focused on memory, intercession, and the communion between the living and the dead.

## History
Brompton sits in the interior of St. Elizabeth, in the farming landscape of the parish. The name Brompton is drawn from English place names — Brompton is found in Yorkshire and elsewhere in England. The Anglican Diocese established All Souls' to serve this community. The church has maintained an Anglican presence in this rural community through the post-Emancipation period.

## Architecture
All Souls' is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural St. Elizabeth in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. Set in the rural interior of St. Elizabeth. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
Brompton has been served within the St. Elizabeth deanery under shared clergy arrangements.

## Notable Facts
All Souls' Day (2 November) follows All Saints' Day (1 November) in the Anglican calendar, together forming the 'Allhallowtide' season at the end of October and the start of November. While All Saints' celebrates the saints and heroes of faith, All Souls' commemorates all the faithful departed — the ordinary dead. This distinction gives All Souls' churches a particularly inclusive character, memorialising not only the canonised but everyone who has died in faith.
