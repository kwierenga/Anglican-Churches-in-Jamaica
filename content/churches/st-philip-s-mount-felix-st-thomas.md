# St. Philip's, Mount Felix
**St. Thomas** · Mission · active

St. Philip's Anglican Church provides a second worship point in the **Mount Felix** area of St. Thomas, alongside St. Augustine's also recorded at this community. Mount Felix is a small farming district in the interior of St. Thomas in the hill country between Port Morant and Morant Bay.

## History
**English rule (1655) and the founding parish.** Under English rule from **1655**, St. Thomas was one of Jamaica's original parishes — originally **"St. Thomas-in-the-East"** with the first rector appointed in **1666**.

**Sugar economy and slavery.** St. Thomas was one of Jamaica's great sugar parishes. Large estates — Stokes Hall, Golden Grove, Albion, Holland — dominated the landscape, worked by enslaved Africans throughout the colonial period. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union** to "wipe non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb** (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**The Morant Bay Rebellion (11 October 1865).** On **11 October 1865**, **Paul Bogle** — a small farmer and Native Baptist deacon of **Stony Gut** — led a protest march to the Morant Bay courthouse. Governor **Edward John Eyre's** brutal response: **439 executed**, **600+ flogged**, **1,000+ homes burned**. Bogle was hanged on **24 October 1865**; **George William Gordon** was hanged on **23 October 1865**. **Both were declared National Heroes in 1965**.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall.** Jamaica became a **Crown Colony in 1866**. Law 20 of 1867 absorbed **St. David** into St. Thomas. The Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

**Foundation of St. Philip's.** Two Anglican worship points in the same Mount Felix community name suggest either a geographically dispersed settlement served by two chapels, or a historical recording duplication. St. Philip's serves its part of the Mount Felix community, dedicated to the apostle Philip who brought others to Christ.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** Internal self-government came in **1944**. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** fulfilled the aspirations for which Bogle and Gordon died. **Hurricane Ivan (2004)** caused widespread damage across the interior of St. Thomas.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Hurricane Melissa brought wind and rain impacts to this part of the parish.

## Architecture
St. Philip's is a masonry church of modest proportions, representative of Anglican rural church building in St. Thomas. The structure balances practicality with the dignity appropriate to a place of worship, featuring a straightforward nave with local stone or concrete block construction typical of Jamaica's interior church-building tradition. The elevated terrain channels wind during storm passages and makes good drainage essential to the building's longevity.

## Clergy
Mount Felix has been served within the **St. Thomas deanery** under shared clergy arrangements. The two worship points in the area likely share the same circuit clergy.

## Notable Facts
The two Anglican churches recorded at Mount Felix reflect the Diocese's thorough coverage of St. Thomas's interior communities. The dedication to **St. Philip the Apostle** (feast day **1 May** in the Anglican calendar, jointly with St. James the Less) is found at several other Jamaican Anglican churches.
