# St. James Parish Church, Sam Sharpe Square
**St. James** · Parish Church · active

St. James Parish Church is the historic Anglican church of Montego Bay, standing on Sam Sharpe Square in the heart of Jamaica's second city. Built between 1775 and 1782, it is widely regarded as one of the finest Georgian church buildings in the Caribbean. Its construction was funded by the wealth of the sugar and slave economy that made Montego Bay rich, and it faces — across the square — the memorial to Sam Sharpe, the enslaved Baptist deacon who led the 1831–32 rebellion that accelerated Emancipation. Few church sites in Jamaica hold so sharp a juxtaposition of established ecclesiastical power and grassroots struggle for freedom.

## History
St. James was carved out of the older parishes in 1703, and Montego Bay grew through the eighteenth century into the principal port of Jamaica's north-west coast — the shipping point for the sugar and coffee estates of the Great River valley, the Cockpit Country fringes, and the coastal plain.

Montego Bay's economy was built on slavery. The parish contained some of the most productive sugar estates in the British Empire, including Rose Hall, Barrett Hall, and dozens of others. In the 1770s, the St. James vestry — composed of the planter class — commissioned a new parish church reflective of the town's wealth and status. The foundation stone was laid on 6 May 1775, and the building was completed around 1782 using finely dressed white limestone. The Anglican Church served as the established church of this system — its clergy funded by the colonial government, its parish administration extending to roads, poor relief, and the maintenance of social order.

On 25 December 1831, the Baptist War — also known as the Christmas Rebellion or the Sam Sharpe Rebellion — broke out in St. James. It was the largest slave uprising in the British Caribbean. Sam Sharpe, an enslaved Baptist deacon in Montego Bay, led a planned strike that escalated into armed revolt involving an estimated 60,000 enslaved people across western Jamaica. The rebellion was suppressed with great brutality: Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay's market square on 23 May 1832, reportedly declaring, "I would rather die upon yonder gallows than live in slavery." The rebellion and its suppression shifted British public opinion and directly accelerated passage of the Slavery Abolition Act of 1833. Emancipation followed in 1834, with full freedom on 1 August 1838. The market square where Sharpe was executed was later renamed Sam Sharpe Square, and he was declared a National Hero of Jamaica in 1975.

St. James Parish Church thus stood at the centre of the events that ended slavery in the British Empire. In the post-Emancipation period, the congregation transformed: the church became an institution for the freed population as well as the older planter and merchant families, and the Diocese expanded its mission across the parish.

The church was severely damaged in the Jamaica earthquake of 1 March 1957, centred in the Montego Bay region, and was faithfully restored the same year. Jamaica's independence on 6 August 1962 found St. James Parish Church at the civic centre of a parish whose identity was shaped by its sugar heritage, the legacy of Sam Sharpe, and Montego Bay's twentieth-century emergence as the capital of Jamaican tourism.

## Architecture
The church has a distinctive Greek-cross plan with a prominent western bell tower, built from finely dressed white limestone with large round-headed sash windows. The interior features a carved mahogany gallery and pews, a decorative timber roof, and a Palladian-style east window with stained glass. Its most celebrated interior monument is the 1790s marble memorial to Rosa Palmer by the English sculptor John Bacon the Elder — a work later erroneously linked to the "White Witch of Rose Hall" legend. The churchyard, with graves dating back two centuries, is one of the richer archives of Montego Bay family history.

## Education
The Anglican mission to educate was central to St. James's post-Emancipation development, continuing Bishop Lipscomb's initiative which founded over 80 elementary schools across Jamaica in the 1830s. Church schools were established across the parish, and the rector of St. James Parish Church maintained long-standing partnerships with Cornwall College and other Anglican educational institutions in Montego Bay.

## Clergy
As the principal Anglican church of the Montego Bay Region, St. James holds regional importance: its rector traditionally serves as the Archdeacon of Montego Bay, overseeing Anglican ministry across north-western Jamaica. The church hosts regular worship, weddings, funerals, and major diocesan events, and is a key stop on heritage tours connecting it to Sam Sharpe Square and the National Museum West.

## Notable Facts
St. James Parish Church is consistently cited as one of the most architecturally significant churches in Jamaica, praised for its Georgian proportions and craftsmanship. Sam Sharpe Square, on which it stands, takes its name from the National Hero executed in the same square in 1832 — creating one of the most powerful historical juxtapositions in the island. The Rosa Palmer memorial by John Bacon the Elder is among the finest eighteenth-century English funerary sculptures in the Caribbean. Hurricane Melissa (28 October 2025) tracked across western Jamaica; Montego Bay sustained impact, and the parish church's Georgian fabric — already restored once after the 1957 earthquake — remained a focus of diocesan heritage concern during the recovery.
