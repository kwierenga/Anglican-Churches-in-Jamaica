# St. James Parish Church, Sam Sharpe Square
**St. James** · Parish Church · active

St. James Parish Church is the historic Anglican church of Montego Bay, a landmark of national significance built between 1775 and 1782. Its construction was funded by the wealth of the city's sugar and slave-trading economy, and it is widely regarded as one of the finest Georgian church buildings in the Caribbean. Notable for its white limestone structure, Greek-cross plan, and richly decorated interior, the church has endured for centuries, surviving a major earthquake in 1957 to remain a central place of worship and a premier heritage site in the heart of the city.

## History
In the 1770s, as Montego Bay prospered as a hub for sugar and coffee exports cultivated by enslaved Africans, the St. James vestry commissioned a new parish church reflective of the town's wealth and status. Its foundation stone was laid on May 6, 1775, and the building was completed around 1782 using finely dressed white limestone. The church's sophisticated design, inspired by English pattern books, was a deliberate statement by the planter and mercantile elite, asserting Montego Bay's importance as Jamaica's second city. It stood as a symbol of official power during the tumultuous era of the 1831 Sam Sharpe Rebellion and the abolition of slavery. Although severely damaged in the 1957 earthquake, it was faithfully restored that same year, preserving its original 18th-century form for future generations.

## Architecture
distinctive Greek-cross plan with a prominent western bell tower. Its exterior is defined by finely dressed white limestone walls and large, round-headed sash windows. The interior is celebrated for its ornate features, including a carved mahogany gallery and pews, a decorative timber roof, and a Palladian-style east window with stained glass. It also houses significant historical artifacts, most notably the 1790s marble memorial to Rosa Palmer by sculptor John Bacon the Elder—a work later erroneously linked to the 'White Witch of Rose Hall' legend. The surrounding churchyard, with graves dating back two centuries, serves as an important archive of local and family history.

## Clergy
As the central church of the Montego Bay Region in the Anglican Diocese of Jamaica and the Cayman Islands, St. James Parish Church holds a position of regional importance. This is exemplified by its rector also serving as the Archdeacon of Montego Bay, a role recently held by the Venerable Justin Nembhard. The church hosts regular worship, weddings, and funerals, and is a venue for major diocesan events. Its significance extends beyond the congregation; it is a key stop on heritage tours that connect it to Sam Sharpe Square and the National Museum West, inviting visitors to contemplate the intertwined histories of faith, colonialism, and the struggle for freedom.

## Notable Facts
Architectural Acclaim: The church is consistently cited in guidebooks and by heritage bodies as one of the most architecturally significant and beautiful churches in Jamaica, praised for its harmonious proportions and high-quality craftsmanship. The Rosa Palmer Memorial: A renowned sculpture by John Bacon the Elder, this monument to a planter's wife became entangled with Jamaican folklore when it was incorrectly associated with the legendary 'White Witch of Rose Hall,' adding a layer of popular myth to its art historical importance. A Landscape of Contrast: The church's proximity to Sam Sharpe Square—the site where the national hero was executed—creates a powerful historical juxtaposition. Modern tours often use these two landmarks to explore the contrast between the established Anglican power structure and the grassroots Baptist-led rebellion that fought for emancipation.
