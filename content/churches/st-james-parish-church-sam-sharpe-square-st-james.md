# St. James Parish Church, Sam Sharpe Square
**St. James** · Parish Church · active

St. James Parish Church is the historic Anglican church of Montego Bay, standing on Sam Sharpe Square in the heart of Jamaica's second city. Construction began in 1774 and the building was completed around 1782. It is widely regarded as one of the finest Georgian churches in the Caribbean — ashlar and white limestone, with a Venetian chancel window that was unusual for Jamaican churches of the period. The church faces, across the square, the memorial to Samuel Sharpe, the enslaved Baptist deacon executed in this square in 1832 after leading the Baptist War, which directly accelerated the abolition of British slavery. Few church sites in Jamaica hold so sharp a juxtaposition of established ecclesiastical power and grassroots struggle for freedom.

## History
St. James parish was carved out of the older parishes in 1703, and Montego Bay grew through the eighteenth century into the principal port of Jamaica's north-west coast — the shipping point for the sugar and coffee estates of the Great River valley, the Cockpit Country fringes, and the coastal plain. Its economy was built on slavery. The parish contained some of the most productive sugar estates in the British Empire, including Rose Hall, Cinnamon Hill, and dozens of others.

In 1774, the St. James vestry — composed of the planter class — began constructing a new parish church reflective of the town's wealth and status. The building was finished in ashlar and locally quarried white limestone around 1782, with a chancel featuring a Venetian window possibly influenced by Batty Langley's architectural pattern books — an innovation unusual in Jamaican churches of the period. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds; its vestry was composed of the planter class.

The Baptist War — also known as the Christmas Rebellion — broke out in St. James on 27 December 1831, when the Kensington Estate Great House was set ablaze. Samuel Sharpe, an enslaved Baptist deacon ("daddy") in Montego Bay born around 1801, had planned a nonviolent mass strike to begin after Christmas: enslaved people would refuse to return to work until granted freedom and pay. When planters learned of the plan and deployed troops, the action became armed uprising. Up to 60,000 of the 300,000 enslaved people in Jamaica took part. The rebellion was suppressed by early January 1832; about 14 whites were killed, and more than 500 enslaved Jamaicans died in the fighting or were subsequently executed. Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay's market square on 23 May 1832. In prison before his execution, he told the Methodist missionary Henry Bleby, "I would rather die upon yonder gallows than live in slavery" — words later recorded in Bleby's 1853 *Death Struggles of Slavery*. The rebellion's scale and brutality shifted British public opinion and directly accelerated passage of the Slavery Abolition Act of 1833. Emancipation followed in 1834, with full freedom on 1 August 1838. Sharpe was declared Jamaica's fifth National Hero on 31 March 1982, and the market square where he was executed was renamed Sam Sharpe Square.

Jamaica's independence on 6 August 1962 found St. James Parish Church at the civic centre of a parish whose identity was shaped by its sugar heritage, the legacy of Sam Sharpe, and Montego Bay's twentieth-century emergence as the capital of Jamaican tourism. The church was severely damaged in the Jamaica earthquake of 1 March 1957, centred off the north coast near Montego Bay, and was faithfully restored the same year.

## Architecture
The church is built in ashlar from locally quarried white limestone, with a Venetian chancel window that sets it apart from most Jamaican Anglican buildings of its period. Its most celebrated interior monument is the marble memorial to Rosa Palmer (1718–1790), wife of John Palmer, executed in 1794 by the English sculptor John Bacon the Elder. The memorial became erroneously linked to the "White Witch of Rose Hall" legend through the journalist John Castello's 1868 pamphlet, an inaccurate account that fused Rosa Palmer with the later and unrelated Annie Palmer of Rose Hall folklore. The churchyard contains graves dating back two centuries.

## Education
The Anglican mission to educate expanded across St. James after Emancipation, part of the Diocese's wider effort to provide schools for the freed population.

## Clergy
As the principal Anglican church of the Montego Bay Region, St. James holds regional importance: its rector traditionally serves as the Archdeacon of Montego Bay, overseeing Anglican ministry across north-western Jamaica. By the 1878 Directory of Jamaica the Church of England presence in Montego Bay was divided among Rev. T. Geddes, Rev. Garcia Del Rio, and Rev. James Byne (Jemmott, *Parish History of St. James*). The church hosts regular worship, weddings, funerals, and major diocesan events, and is a key stop on heritage tours connecting it to Sam Sharpe Square and the National Museum West.

## Notable Facts
St. James Parish Church is one of the finest Georgian churches in the Caribbean — ashlar and white limestone with a Venetian chancel window, elements unusual for its period in Jamaica. Sam Sharpe Square, on which it stands, takes its name from the National Hero executed in the same square in 1832. The Rosa Palmer memorial by John Bacon the Elder (1794) is the most significant eighteenth-century English funerary sculpture in the Caribbean; its association with the White Witch of Rose Hall legend dates only to John Castello's 1868 pamphlet. The church was severely damaged in the 1 March 1957 Jamaica earthquake and restored the same year. Hurricane Melissa (28 October 2025) tracked across western Jamaica; Montego Bay sustained impact, and the parish church's Georgian fabric remained a focus of diocesan heritage concern.

## Recent coverage

St James Parish Church — sometimes nicknamed "the Cathedral of St James" — is a Georgian masterpiece consecrated in 1782 whose 1957-earthquake-damaged clock tower remains a Mo'Bay landmark ([Jamaica Gleaner, 24 Jan 2017](https://jamaica-gleaner.com/article/western-focus/20170124/historical-sites-montego-bay)). In 2022 its 1890 silver-piped 18-foot mechanical organ — valued at US$500,000 — entered a US$200,000 restoration by German organ-builder Friedemann Buschbeck under Archdeacon Justin Nembhard ([Gleaner, 21 Nov 2022](http://past.jamaica-gleaner.com/article/lead-stories/20221121/century-old-pipe-organ-getting-new-lease-life)). The church was the consecration venue for Bishop Leon Golding (2012) and the seat to which Canon Collin Reid was elected in April 2026 ([Gleaner, 11 Apr 2026](https://jamaica-gleaner.com/article/news/20260411/canon-collin-reid-anglican-bishop-elect-montego-bay)).
