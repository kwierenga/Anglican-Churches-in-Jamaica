# Transfiguration, Mount Grace
**Westmoreland** · Church · active

The Church of the Transfiguration at Mount Grace is a small Anglican chapel serving a hill community in northern Westmoreland, in the elevated terrain rising toward the Dolphin Head massif. The dedication to the **Transfiguration** — the feast commemorating Christ's appearance in glory on the mountain (Matthew 17, Mark 9, Luke 9), one of the Principal Feasts of the Anglican calendar (6 August) — is particularly fitting for a hilltop church. The chapel is part of the **Holy Trinity Cure (Grange Hill)** alongside St. James' Grange and St. Luke's Cessnock, under Rev. Michael Solomon.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1703).** Westmoreland was officially formed in **1703** from the western portion of the older parish of St. Elizabeth, named for its position at the western part of the island. The parish capital, **Savanna-la-Mar**, was founded around 1730. In 1723 the new parish of Hanover was carved out of Westmoreland's northern section.

**Slavery and the sugar economy.** Under English rule, sugar and slavery accelerated in Westmoreland: an inventory in **1722** numbered the estates at **73**. The Beckford family — Richard, Peter, and William — were among the most prominent planters; modern Petersfield is named for Peter Beckford. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**The Sam Sharpe Rebellion (December 1831).** The Christmas Rebellion of 1831, led by the Baptist deacon Samuel Sharpe and ignited at the Kensington Estate in St. James on 27 December 1831, spread rapidly through the western parishes — St. James, Trelawny, Hanover, and Westmoreland — with some 60,000 enslaved people involved. The Colonial Church Union's vigilante destruction of chapels included Sav.-la-Mar (Ellis, *Diocese of Jamaica*, 1913). Sharpe was hanged on 23 May 1832 and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation and post-1838 hill settlement.** The British Parliament passed the Slavery Abolition Act in 1833; Emancipation came in 1834, and full freedom followed on **1 August 1838**. By 1854 the number of active sugar estates in Westmoreland had collapsed to 34, less than half the 1722 figure. Mount Grace and the surrounding hill communities of northern Westmoreland were settled largely in the post-Emancipation period as freed people moved away from the coastal estate economy and into the interior hills. The Anglican Church followed this movement, establishing mission chapels in upland communities where the terrain made regular travel to the coastal parish church difficult. The Church of the Transfiguration represents this missionary outreach into the hills.

**1865 to 1912 and Nuttall's expansion.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865, Crown Colony rule replaced the planter Assembly (1866); the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica. Bishop Enos Nuttall's tenure (1880–1916) saw the Diocese grow from 26 rural mission stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. The Holy Trinity Grange Hill rectory served as the cure base for the network of small upland chapels — Mount Grace among them. A November **1912** hurricane brought a tidal wave that nearly destroyed Savanna-la-Mar.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** The **1938 Frome labour riots** at the Frome sugar factory contributed directly to the formation of Jamaica's Trade Union Movement and the rise of Sir Alexander Bustamante. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found Transfiguration a settled hill chapel within the Holy Trinity Grange Hill cure.

## Architecture
The Church of the Transfiguration is a small hillside chapel of simple masonry construction, typical of the mission churches erected by the Diocese across the highlands of western Jamaica. Set on the elevated ground of Mount Grace, the church has panoramic views across the Westmoreland hills. The building is modest in scale, suited to a small rural congregation. The hillside churchyard includes the graves of local families. The upland setting provides good natural drainage but exposes the building to strong hilltop winds during storms.

## Clergy
Transfiguration has been served within the **Holy Trinity Grange Hill cure** under Rev. **Michael Solomon**, alongside Holy Trinity Grange Hill (the mother church), St. James' Grange, and St. Luke's Cessnock. The relatively remote location of Mount Grace means that lay leaders — wardens, readers, and Mothers' Union members — have been essential to the weekly life of the congregation.

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** The Transfiguration (Matthew 17:1–8) — Christ's appearance in radiant glory on the mountain, witnessed by Peter, James, and John, with Moses and Elijah on either side — is one of the most theologically resonant events of the Gospels and one of the Principal Feasts of the Anglican calendar (**6 August**). For a hilltop chapel, the dedication is particularly apt.

**Dolphin Head biodiversity.** The Dolphin Head area of northern Westmoreland is one of Jamaica's recognised biodiversity hotspots, a limestone massif with endemic plant and animal species. Mount Grace, in the foothills of this range, sits in a landscape of considerable ecological importance. The hillside communities are exposed to landslide risk during prolonged heavy rainfall.

**Hurricane Melissa (28 October 2025)** made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history — bringing damaging winds and heavy rain to the northern Westmoreland hills. Holy Trinity at Grange Hill (the cure's mother church) was damaged; the specific status of Transfiguration Mount Grace has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed. Hurricane Gilbert (1988) had earlier caused serious landslides and road damage in this part of Westmoreland.
