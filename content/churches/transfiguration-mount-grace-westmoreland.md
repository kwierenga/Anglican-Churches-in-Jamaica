# Transfiguration, Mount Grace
**Westmoreland** · Church · active

The Church of the Transfiguration at Mount Grace is a small Anglican chapel serving a hill community in northern Westmoreland, in the elevated terrain rising toward the Dolphin Head massif. The dedication to the Transfiguration — the feast commemorating Christ's appearance in glory on the mountain — is particularly fitting for a hilltop church. Mount Grace is a small farming community, and the church serves as the spiritual and communal centre for families living across this upland district.

## History
Mount Grace and the surrounding hill communities of northern Westmoreland were settled largely in the post-Emancipation period as freed people moved away from the coastal estate economy and into the interior hills. The Anglican Church followed this movement, establishing mission chapels in upland communities where the terrain made regular travel to the coastal parish church difficult. The Church of the Transfiguration at Mount Grace represents this missionary outreach into the hills, providing a local place of worship for families who might otherwise have no Anglican ministry within practical reach.

## Architecture
The Church of the Transfiguration is a small hillside chapel of simple masonry construction, typical of the mission churches erected by the Diocese across the highlands of western Jamaica. Set on the elevated ground of Mount Grace, the church has panoramic views across the Westmoreland hills. The building is modest in scale, suited to a small rural congregation. The hillside churchyard includes the graves of local families. The upland setting provides good natural drainage but exposes the building to strong hilltop winds during storms.

## Clergy
Transfiguration has been served within the northern Westmoreland circuit, grouped with Holy Trinity at Grange Hill and other nearby hill churches under shared clergy arrangements. The relatively remote location of Mount Grace means that lay leaders — wardens, readers, and Mothers' Union members — have been essential to the weekly life of the congregation. The church is confirmed active in diocesan records.

## Notable Facts
The Dolphin Head area of northern Westmoreland is one of Jamaica's recognised biodiversity hotspots, a limestone massif with endemic plant and animal species. Mount Grace, in the foothills of this range, sits in a landscape of considerable ecological importance. The hillside communities here are exposed to landslide risk during prolonged heavy rainfall, as the steep, forested slopes can become unstable. Hurricane Gilbert (1988) caused serious landslides and road damage in this part of Westmoreland. Hurricane Melissa (October 2025) brought damaging winds and heavy rain to the northern Westmoreland hills; access roads to communities like Mount Grace were blocked by fallen trees, and the church and its congregation were directly affected by the storm.
