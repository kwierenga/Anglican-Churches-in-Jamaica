# St. Saviour's, Milk River
**Clarendon** · Church · closed

St. Saviour's Anglican Church serves the **Milk River** community of southern Clarendon alongside St. Benedict's Mission, also recorded here. The two Milk River entries are approximately 777 metres apart, suggesting they serve different ends of the Milk River community or represent two distinct but closely associated worship points. Milk River is famous for its **mineral spa**, and St. Saviour's serves the community around this unique natural feature. The church is administered within the **St. Peter's Alley / Lionel Town** cure.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1664).** Clarendon parish was created in **1664**, nine years after the English seizure of Jamaica from Spain (**1655**), and named for **Sir Edward Hyde, 1st Earl of Clarendon** (1609–1674), Lord High Chancellor of England under Charles II. The original parish church was the **Church of the White Cross** at "The Cross" — Jamaica's third Anglican church. **St. Paul's Chapelton** was built as a chapel of ease in **1666** and became the parish church when the Cross Church was destroyed in **1815**.

**Vere parish and the southern plain (1673–1867).** **Vere** was carved out as a separate sugar-plain parish in **1673**, taking in the southern coastal plain — including the Milk River area on the southern coast of the merged parish. **St. Peter's Alley** (1671) was Vere's parish church and remains the third-oldest Anglican church in Jamaica. Vere was reabsorbed into Clarendon in **1867**, and **Law 20 of 1867** designated **May Pen** as chief town.

**Slavery, Sam Sharpe, and Emancipation.** The sugar estates of the Vere plain depended on the labour of thousands of enslaved Africans. One of Jamaica's earliest slave rebellions erupted at **Suttons Plantation in 1690**: some 500 enslaved people rose in revolt, and many of those who escaped joined the Maroon communities eventually led by **Cudjoe** into the Cockpit Country. During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**, a vigilante alliance that sought to "wipe the non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation at Milk River.** Milk River's two Anglican entries — St. Benedict's Mission and St. Saviour's — reflect the Diocese's multi-point approach to ministry in this coastal community. The **Milk River mineral spa**, fed by one of the world's most radioactive natural mineral springs, has drawn visitors to this community since the 18th century. St. Saviour's serves the residential community around this unusual landmark. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**, and the Milk River missions belong to that broader expansion phase.

**Twentieth century: bauxite, May Pen, Independence.** Chapelton lost its status as parish capital in **1938**, with administrative functions transferring to **May Pen**. **Alcoa** began bauxite operations in Clarendon in **1959**; the **Jamalco** alumina refinery at Halse Hall opened in **1972**. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Saviour's a settled Lionel Town circuit church.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Hurricane Melissa would have posed a direct threat to this south-coast community; the specific status of St. Saviour's Milk River after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. Saviour's is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural Clarendon in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. The southern coastal-plain position creates exposure to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
Milk River has been served within the Clarendon deanery as part of the St. Peter's Alley / Lionel Town cure. St. Saviour's and St. Benedict's Mission together provide Anglican coverage for the Milk River community.

## Notable Facts
The "Saviour" dedication is a directly Christological name, unique in its explicit reference to the redemptive role of Jesus rather than invoking a saint. St. Saviour's at Milk River thus carries a spiritually charged name in a community already defined by unusual natural powers — the **mineral waters** said to cure ailments of the body, and the church offering salvation for the soul.
