# All Saints', Meylersfield
**Westmoreland** · Church · active

All Saints' Anglican Church serves the community at Meylersfield on the Westmoreland coastal plain, a site with deep roots in the parish's plantation past. Meylersfield was one of the larger Westmoreland sugar estates: UCL's *Legacies of British Slavery* records **506 enslaved Africans** held there in 1823, placing it among the most populous properties on the plain. The church is listed in the Diocese of Jamaica and the Cayman Islands directory under the Westmoreland Deanery; it does not appear in the 1891 Handbook of Jamaica's Anglican returns, placing its foundation post-1891.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1703).** Westmoreland was officially formed in **1703** from the western portion of the older parish of St. Elizabeth, named for its position at the western part of the island. The parish capital, **Savanna-la-Mar**, was founded around 1730. In 1723 the new parish of Hanover was carved out of Westmoreland's northern section.

**Slavery and the sugar economy at Meylersfield.** Under English rule, sugar and slavery accelerated in Westmoreland: an inventory in **1722** numbered the estates at **73**, making the parish one of the leading sugar producers of the British Caribbean. The Beckford family — Richard, Peter, and William — were among the most prominent planters; modern Petersfield is named for Peter Beckford. Meylersfield itself was a substantial sugar property: by **1823** the estate held 506 enslaved Africans (UCL Legacies of British Slavery), among the largest enslaved workforces in the parish at the time. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds; its parish vestry was composed of the planter class, and its rectors held glebe lands worked by enslaved people.

**The Sam Sharpe Rebellion (December 1831).** The Christmas Rebellion of 1831, led by the Baptist deacon Samuel Sharpe and ignited at the Kensington Estate in St. James on the night of 27 December 1831, spread rapidly through the western parishes — St. James, Trelawny, Hanover, and Westmoreland — with some 60,000 enslaved people involved. Suppressed brutally, the rebellion ended with Sharpe's hanging in Montego Bay on 23 May 1832. The Colonial Church Union — formed in the aftermath as a vigilante alliance of Anglican planters and clergy — undertook a campaign of "riotous destruction of chapels" across the western parishes, including Sav.-la-Mar (Ellis, *Diocese of Jamaica*, 1913). Sharpe is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation and the post-1838 landscape at Meylersfield.** Within two years of Sharpe's hanging the British Parliament passed the Slavery Abolition Act (1833); Emancipation came in 1834, and full freedom followed at the end of the apprenticeship period on **1 August 1838**. By 1854 the number of active sugar estates in Westmoreland had collapsed to 34, less than half the 1722 figure. The freed people of estates like Meylersfield settled the surrounding plain as small farmers — a pattern that the Anglican Church would gradually serve through new mission stations. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and the resulting Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica. Bishop Enos Nuttall's tenure (1880–1916) saw the Diocese grow from 26 rural mission stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**; All Saints' Meylersfield belongs to that wave of post-1891 mission planting on the Westmoreland plain.

**1912 hurricane, 1938 Frome riots, Independence.** A November **1912** hurricane brought a tidal wave that nearly destroyed Savanna-la-Mar; the schooner *Latonia* was swept ashore on Great Gorges Street. The **1938 Frome labour riots** at the Frome sugar factory contributed directly to the formation of Jamaica's Trade Union Movement and the rise of Sir Alexander Bustamante. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found All Saints' a settled mission within the Westmoreland Anglican network.

## Architecture
All Saints' is a small rural masonry church of the kind erected by the Diocese for mission stations across the Westmoreland plain in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries. The flat coastal-plain position offers no shelter from storm surge during major hurricanes, a recurring concern for the entire south-coast zone of the parish.

## Clergy
All Saints' Meylersfield is served within the Montego Bay Region's Westmoreland Deanery.

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** All Saints — commemorating the full company of recognised and unrecognised saints on **1 November** — is the most general dedication in the Anglican tradition and is sometimes read as a church-name response to the collective memory of the enslaved ancestors whose labour built the estate landscape that surrounded the chapel. The dedication is also the most common Anglican choice for small mission chapels intended to gather a scattered farming community.

**Hurricane Melissa (28 October 2025)** made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The Westmoreland coastal plain, including Meylersfield, was in the direct impact zone. Across the Diocese of Jamaica and the Cayman Islands, some **50 church buildings were destroyed or significantly damaged**. The specific status of All Saints' Meylersfield after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.
