# St. Martin's, Accompong
**St. Elizabeth** · Mission · active

St. Martin's Anglican Church serves Accompong, a Maroon community in the mountains of St. Elizabeth, confirmed Accompong is one of the most historically significant communities in Jamaica — it is the surviving free Maroon community of western Jamaica, established by the Leeward Maroons under the treaty of 1739 signed with the British Crown. The Maroons of Accompong have maintained their community sovereignty, traditions, and identity for nearly three centuries, and the Anglican church here represents a unique intersection of established religion with one of the Caribbean's most remarkable communities of resistance.

## History
Accompong was established as a free Maroon community in the mountains of St. Elizabeth following the First Maroon War, which ended with the treaty of 1739 negotiated by the legendary Maroon leader Cudjoe with the British governor. The treaty granted the Leeward Maroons land, freedom from slavery, and self-governance in exchange for ceasing hostilities and assisting in the capture of escaped enslaved people — a clause that remains contested in Maroon historical memory. Accompong has maintained its treaty rights and community sovereignty to the present day, governed by a Colonel elected by the community. The Anglican church presence at Accompong reflects the Diocese's ministry to this unique community.

## Architecture
St. Martin's Accompong is Elizabeth, at significant elevation. Accompong is one of the most remote and elevated communities in Jamaica, in the rugged limestone karst landscape of the Cockpit Country. The mountain position creates wind exposure during hurricane passages and significant road access challenges during storm events.

## Clergy
Accompong has been served within the St. Elizabeth deanery under shared clergy arrangements. The community's semi-autonomous status and distinct cultural identity create a unique pastoral context for the Anglican presence here.

## Notable Facts
Accompong is one of only two remaining Maroon communities in Jamaica (the other being Moore Town in Portland) that maintain the formal treaty rights established in the 18th century. The community celebrates Accompong Maroon Day on 6 January each year with festivals and ceremonies that draw visitors from across Jamaica and the diaspora. The Cockpit Country surrounding Accompong is one of Jamaica's most ecologically significant landscapes — a UNESCO-recognised area of biodiversity. The Anglican presence in this historically self-governing community is a reminder of the complex relationships between the established church and the free communities that predated Emancipation.
