# Mount James, Mount James
**St. Andrew** · Mission · active

The Anglican church at Mount James is a small mission chapel perched in the Blue Mountain foothills northeast of Kingston, serving the rural communities above Stony Hill and Irish Town. It developed as an outreach point of the larger St. Andrew Parish Church, providing worship, baptisms, and local gatherings for families living far from town. The building is simple, traditional, and suited to its hillside setting, surrounded by pines, small farms, and views toward the mountains. Though modest, the chapel remains an important spiritual and social anchor for the Mount James community.

## History
The Mount James area grew as a cluster of small hillside settlements in the 19th and early 20th centuries, populated by farmers, labourers, and families who lived too far uphill to easily reach Half-Way-Tree or Constant Spring. In response, the Anglican Church established a series of rural mission stations across St. Andrew, and Mount James became one of these chapels—administered originally from St. Andrew Parish Church, and later grouped within the Mountain/Irish Town region of the diocese. The chapel served as a centre for Sunday worship, children’s classes, and occasional community meetings, giving the highland districts a consistent Anglican presence even as roads and agriculture shifted over time.

## Architecture
The Mount James chapel is a small, rectangular hillside church built in a simple rural Jamaican Anglican style: timber and masonry construction, a gable roof (usually zinc-clad in recent decades), shuttered windows or narrow pointed openings, and a plain entrance porch facing the road. It sits on sloping ground, surrounded by mature trees and a compact yard that blends naturally into the steep terrain. The building’s scale reflects its purpose—an intimate worship space for a small mountain community rather than a formal parish church.

## Clergy
As a mission chapel, Mount James has historically shared clergy with nearby churches in the St. Andrew hills, typically St. Andrew Parish Church, St. Mark’s (Gordon Town), St. Martin (Irish Town), and other mountain congregations. Clergy rotated between outstations for services, confirmations, pastoral visits, and seasonal celebrations. In the diocesan structure, it continues to be part of the St. Andrew region, receiving support from the regional clergy team rather than having its own resident priest.

## Notable Facts
Mount James sits along one of the scenic ridges overlooking the Kingston plain, close to trails and old estate roads leading toward Content Gap and the higher Blue Mountains. The chapel has long served as a landmark for hikers and rural travellers moving between Stony Hill and the upper coffee districts. Because the area is relatively isolated during heavy rains or landslides, the church has often doubled as a local meeting point during community organising or relief efforts.
