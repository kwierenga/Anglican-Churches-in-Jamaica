# St. Thomas', Stewart Town
**Trelawny** · Church · active

St. Thomas Anglican Church in Stewart Town, Trelawny, was built in the mid-19th century to serve the community of freed people after Emancipation in 1838. It is situated in the town's central basin, which was once a thriving market for local produce. Today, St. Thomas' remains an active church within the Diocese, continuing its long tradition of worship and community life in this historic inland market town.

## History
Stewart Town was formally established around 1815 as one of Jamaica's key inland market towns. Following Emancipation in 1838, its population grew as freed families moved inland from coastal plantations to establish independent farms in the surrounding hills. To serve this new community, the Anglican Diocese constructed a stone church dedicated to St. Thomas around 1840. This church quickly became a cornerstone of the community, providing spiritual guidance, education, and a sense of stability for people building a new life in freedom.

## Architecture
St. Thomas's is a cut-limestone church built in a simple Gothic Revival style, featuring a distinctive belfry. It is situated in a scenic basin surrounded by the limestone hills of Trelawny. Its stone walls remain remarkably sturdy. Historic photos from 1931 show interior features such as family pews with 'fly doors' and memorial plaques dedicated to early congregants. Its design is a classic example of a 19th-century Anglican mission church, adapted to its rural Jamaican context.

## Clergy
St. Thomas' is an active part of the Anglican network in Trelawny, with clergy assigned to serve the congregation. The church has played a vital role in the community's life, facilitating baptisms, marriages, and schooling. It is part of the Trelawny Anglican circuit, sharing clergy with churches in Albert Town, Clarks Town, and other inland congregations. Lay readers and wardens continue to sustain regular worship and community activities.

## Notable Facts
St. Thomas' has endured several major hurricanes over its long history. Its sturdy limestone walls have withstood the elements well. The church stands as an important historical monument to the post-Emancipation era, and its 1931 photographs — showing family pews with distinctive 'fly doors' — are a valuable record of 19th-century Anglican church furnishing in rural Jamaica. Today, St. Thomas' continues to serve the Stewart Town community as an active place of worship within the Diocese.
