# St. Thomas', Stewart Town
**Trelawny** · Church · active

St. Thomas' Anglican Church stands at the centre of Stewart Town, an inland market town in the Dry Harbour Mountains of interior Trelawny at the edge of Cockpit Country. Stewart Town was founded by legislative act in 1815 — the second incorporated town in Trelawny after Falmouth — and by 1891 its Anglican congregation numbered 97 registered members under the Rev. J. E. Miller, who served Stewart Town jointly with Gibraltar.

## History
In 1812, with Britain at war with its North American colonies and supplies to Jamaica scarce, the Crown gave royal assent to Law 53 Chapter 19, authorising the magistrates and vestry of Trelawny to lay out a new town on 50 acres of interior land. The commissioners met in April 1815 and incorporated the town on 20 August 1815. It was named Stewart Town after James Stewart, the Custos of Trelawny.

Trelawny was created as a parish in 1770 out of St. James. The parish's economy was built on slavery: the sugar estates of the coastal plain and the Cockpit Country fringes depended on enslaved labour for their production. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church. After Emancipation (1834) and full freedom (1 August 1838), Stewart Town's inland position made it a natural settlement point for freed families moving off the estates. The 1891 Handbook of Jamaica records Rev. J. E. Miller as clergyman at Stewart Town, with 97 registered members, 15 baptisms, and 2 marriages in the year. Miller also served Gibraltar (110 members) — the two stations forming a single charge.

The National Library of Jamaica holds 1931 photographs of the church's interior, documenting family pews with "fly doors" and memorial plaques to early congregants — among the more detailed visual records of a mid-nineteenth-century rural Jamaican Anglican church interior.

## Architecture
St. Thomas' is a cut-limestone church in the simple Gothic Revival style, set in a geographical basin known locally as "Bottom." The building has a rectangular nave, a belfry, and solid walls that have weathered Atlantic storms for more than a century and a half. The 1931 interior photographs show the distinctive family pews with "fly doors" characteristic of nineteenth-century Jamaican church furnishing.

## Clergy
Rev. J. E. Miller served Stewart Town and Gibraltar jointly in 1891 per the Handbook of Jamaica. The church has been served within the Trelawny Deanery under shared clergy arrangements with the wider Trelawny cure.

## Hurricane Melissa (October 2025)
Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on 28 October 2025. Trelawny was among the five most affected parishes. Stewart Town's interior position offered some shelter from surge, but the specific damage status of St. Thomas' after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Notable Facts
Stewart Town was founded in 1815 — making it the second-oldest incorporated town in Trelawny after Falmouth. The town's location in a basin called "Bottom," surrounded by the limestone hills of the Dry Harbour Mountains, gives the church one of the more distinctive settings of any Jamaican Anglican building.
