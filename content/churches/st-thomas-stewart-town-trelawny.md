# St. Thomas', Stewart Town
**Trelawny** · Church · active

St. Thomas Anglican Church in Stewart Town, Trelawny, was built in the mid-19th century to serve the community of freed people after Emancipation in 1838. It was established in the town's central basin, which was once a thriving market for local produce. Over time, as the population changed, the congregation dwindled, and the building fell into a state of disrepair. Although it is no longer used for regular worship, its enduring stone structure remains a powerful historic landmark, symbolizing the faith and resilience of the post-Emancipation community.

## History
Stewart Town was formally established around 1815 as one of Jamaica's key inland market towns. Following Emancipation in 1838, its population grew as freed families moved inland from coastal plantations to establish independent farms in the surrounding hills. To serve this new community, the Anglican Diocese constructed a stone church dedicated to St. Thomas around 1840. This church quickly became a cornerstone of the community, providing spiritual guidance, education, and a sense of stability for people building a new life in freedom.

## Architecture
St. Thomas's is a cut-limestone church built in a simple Gothic Revival style, featuring a distinctive belfry. It is situated in a scenic basin surrounded by the limestone hills of Trelawny. While the building is now derelict, its stone walls remain remarkably sturdy. Historic photos from 1931 show interior features such as family pews with 'fly doors' and memorial plaques dedicated to early congregants. Its design is a classic example of a 19th-century Anglican mission church, adapted to its rural Jamaican context.

## Clergy
St. Thomas's was once an active part of the Anglican network in Trelawny, with clergy assigned to serve the congregation. The church played a vital role in the community's life, facilitating baptisms, marriages, and schooling. However, as Stewart Town's population declined in the latter half of the 20th century, regular services became less frequent. After Jamaica's Independence in 1962, parish activities diminished, leaving lay members to maintain both the building and the community's faith traditions with minimal clerical support.

## Notable Facts
In recent decades, the church has been abandoned and has fallen into severe disrepair. Its sturdy limestone walls have withstood the elements, but the structure continues to deteriorate. While no longer an active house of worship, St. Thomas's stands as a poignant historical monument to the post-Emancipation era. Its current state highlights the need for preservation efforts to prevent the complete loss of this important cultural landmark.
