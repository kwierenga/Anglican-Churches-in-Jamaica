# St. Andrew Parish Church, Half-Way-Tree
**St. Andrew** · Parish Church · active

St. Andrew Parish Church stands as a historic landmark at the bustling center of Half Way Tree, alongside the iconic clock tower and courthouse. Founded in 1664, it is one of Jamaica's oldest churches, witnessing the entire span of the island's history from British colonization through slavery, Emancipation, and Independence. The present stone building was completed in 1700 and has been enlarged over the centuries to serve a growing population. Today, it remains a highly active parish, known for its outreach in communities like Majesty Gardens and for hosting major national services, including Independence celebrations and state funerals.

## History
St. Andrew Parish Church was established in 1664, just as the British began their civil administration of Jamaica. The Crown granted the church 600 acres of glebe land in 1667, embedding it deeply within the colonial plantation system. The first two buildings were lost to a hurricane and the 1692 earthquake, leading to the construction of the current stone church at the Half Way Tree crossroads in 1700. For over 170 years, it was the sole Anglican church in the parish, serving both the plantation elite and the enslaved population on surrounding estates. Its history is intertwined with the nation's story; after Emancipation, the adjacent courthouse was where freed people obtained their certificates of freedom, and the church became a natural place for their baptisms and weddings. The church's own lands later became part of important landmarks, with the rectory land sold to become the site of Devon House.

## Architecture
The core of St. Andrew Parish Church is a 1700 stone structure, making it one of the oldest surviving Anglican churches in Jamaica. Its design features a rectangular nave, a square west tower, and thick masonry walls that have withstood centuries of storms and earthquakes, including the devastating 1907 quake that required major repairs. The church was significantly enlarged in 1879-1880 and has seen several modern additions. The interior houses historic monuments, stained-glass windows, and a large pipe organ. Together with the adjacent Georgian-style courthouse (1807), it forms a heritage cluster that has been the civic and religious heart of Half Way Tree for over three centuries.

## Clergy
With only about 20 rectors in its 350-year history, St. Andrew's has been marked by remarkable stability in leadership. Its clergy have played pivotal roles in Jamaican society, and its cemetery is the final resting place for foundational Anglican figures like Bishops Lipscomb, Nuttall, and Gibson. The church holds a unique place in Caribbean cultural history, as its 18th-century organist, Samuel Felsted, composed 'Jonah,' considered the first oratorio written in the New World. Today, the parish runs a vast array of ministries, most notably the St. Andrew Settlement in Majesty Gardens, which provides critical education, healthcare, and skills training. It continues to serve as a national pulpit, regularly hosting civic services for Independence and other state occasions.

## Notable Facts
The church's cemetery is an open-air museum of Jamaican history, containing the graves of governors-general like Sir Clifford Campbell, pan-Africanist Amy Jacques Garvey, and business pioneer George Stiebel, telling a complex story from the plantation era to Independence. While the building has survived many hurricanes and earthquakes, the church actively supported its community through Hurricane Melissa (2025), canceling services and providing pastoral care and relief, though no major structural damage to the church itself was reported. From its 17th-century origins to its modern role, St. Andrew Parish Church stands as a living witness and active participant in the unfolding story of Jamaica.
