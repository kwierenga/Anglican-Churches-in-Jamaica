# St. Stephen's Church, St. Margaret's Bay
**Portland** · Church · active

St. Stephen's Anglican Church serves **St. Margaret's Bay**, a small north-coast community in Portland parish situated between Port Antonio and Hope Bay. The church is dedicated to the **first Christian martyr**, St. Stephen the Deacon (feast day **26 December**).

## History
**English rule (1655) and the founding of Portland (1723).** Under English rule from **1655**, the future Portland coast remained largely Maroon territory. **Portland parish was created in 1723** by order of the then Governor, **Henry Bentinck, 1st Duke of Portland** (Governor 1722–1726), and named in his honour. The British built **Fort George** at Port Antonio in 1729.

**The First Maroon War (1728–1739) and Nanny.** The **Windward Maroons** under **Quao** and **Nanny** controlled the Blue Mountains and John Crow Mountains. The **1740 treaty** granted **Nanny a 500-acre land grant** that forms the present site of **Moore Town**. **Nanny of the Maroons** became Jamaica's only female National Hero in October 1975.

**Slavery and the planter church.** Sugar estates dominated the fertile coastal lowlands and river valleys; coffee thrived in the hills. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union** to "wipe non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Christ Church Port Antonio** was completed around **1840** under Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb**.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall, and the banana revolution.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. In **1870**, **Captain Lorenzo Dow Baker** purchased bananas in Port Antonio market; Baker's Boston Fruit Company became **United Fruit Company in 1899**.

**Foundation of St. Stephen's.** St. Margaret's Bay developed as a small coastal settlement on the north shore of Portland, one of the series of fishing and farming communities that dot the coastline between Port Antonio and Buff Bay. The Anglican Diocese established St. Stephen's as part of its mission network along the Portland coast, serving the local fishing and farming families.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** **Hurricane Gilbert (1988)** struck Portland's north coast severely. The **1903 hurricane** had earlier damaged Christ Church Port Antonio and devastated the banana industry. Tourism grew at Port Antonio. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Stephen's a settled coastal mission.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Hurricane Melissa tracked along Jamaica's north coast; St. Margaret's Bay was directly in the storm's path and the St. Stephen's congregation experienced direct coastal impact.

## Architecture
St. Stephen's is a coastal church on the north shore of Portland, directly exposed to the Caribbean Sea. Google Places confirms it at its location on the main coastal road. The church's coastal position creates storm surge and wind exposure during hurricane passages along the Portland north coast.

## Clergy
St. Stephen's has been served within the **Portland deanery** under shared clergy arrangements, linked to the Port Antonio circuit. The coastal community has maintained an active congregation through the fishing and farming families of the Bay.

## Notable Facts
**St. Margaret's Bay** is one of several small named bays along Portland's north coast, each with its own distinct community character. The Bay's name reflects the tradition of Marian dedications in Jamaica's colonial geography. The dedication to **St. Stephen** — proto-martyr of the Christian church, stoned to death in Jerusalem (Acts 7), feast day **26 December** — is shared with several other Jamaican Anglican churches.
