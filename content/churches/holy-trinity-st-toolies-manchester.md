# Holy Trinity, St. Toolies
**Manchester** · Church · active

Holy Trinity Anglican Church serves St. Toolies, a community in the rural interior of Manchester. The church is administered as a mission of the **St. Augustine's, Porus cure**, alongside St. Andrew's Harmons, St. Stephen's Chantilly, and St. James' Toll Gate. The cure rectory is at Porus P.O., Manchester.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1814).** Manchester was created by **Act 55 George III. c. 23** of **1814** in response to a petition of 29 November 1814 by residents of Mile Gully, May Day, and Carpenter's Mountain. The Act came into effect on **13 December 1814**, taking the "hill country" of eastern St. Elizabeth and the western portions of Vere and Clarendon. The new parish was named after **William Montagu, 5th Duke of Manchester** (Governor 1808–1827); **Mandeville**, the capital, was named after his son, **Viscount Mandeville**.

**Slavery, the parish church, and the Sam Sharpe Rebellion (1831).** Manchester's hill-country economy was built on coffee, supplemented by sugar in lower zones; both depended on enslaved African labour. **St. Mark's Anglican Church, Mandeville** — the parish church — was completed and consecrated in **1820**, and its organ loft was used as a jail during the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**. Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation, 1865, disestablishment.** Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. The Porus area is itself notable in Jamaican history as a **free village founded for ex-slaves** following Emancipation: missionaries purchased tracts of land and divided them into parcels for sale to ex-slaves who came to grow coffee and other crops (Jamaica Observer, "The story of Manchester part III: Porus," 13 Jul 2014). **Maidstone** in Manchester (1840) was likewise among Jamaica's earliest Moravian-led free villages. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of Holy Trinity: the Porus cure.** The Anglican Diocese established the St. Augustine's Porus cure as the parish church for the post-Emancipation free-village landscape, and extended its ministry into surrounding districts including St. Toolies through small mission stations like Holy Trinity. The **1900 Handbook of Jamaica** records "Porus & Chantilly" as a cure under **Rev. W. Lund**; St. Toolies was likely one of its mission stations. Bishop Enos Nuttall's tenure (1880–1916) saw the Diocese grow from 26 rural mission stations to **122 by 1911**.

**Twentieth century: Kendal, bauxite, Independence.** The **Kendal train tragedy** of **1 September 1957** at Kendal Manchester claimed almost 200 lives — Jamaica's worst rail accident. The **bauxite industry** (**Alcan** from 1957, **Kaiser** later) employed over 3,000 by the 1960s and transformed the parish economy. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found Holy Trinity a settled mission within the Porus cure.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of Holy Trinity, St. Toolies after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
Holy Trinity is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for mission stations across the Manchester interior. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal storm surge while remaining exposed to wind and rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
Holy Trinity, St. Toolies is served as a mission of the St. Augustine's, Porus cure within the Mandeville Region's Manchester Deanery. The 1900 Handbook records the cure under Rev. **W. Lund**.

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** The **Holy Trinity** — Father, Son, and Holy Spirit — is one of the most traditional Anglican dedications, shared with the Diocese's Cathedral on North Street in Kingston (Holy Trinity Cathedral, 1911) and with several other Holy Trinity churches across Jamaica including the historic Holy Trinity Old Harbour and Holy Trinity Westgate (Montego Bay). The choice for a rural mission station gives the small church a theologically central name.
