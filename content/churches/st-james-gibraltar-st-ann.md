# St. James', Gibraltar
**St. Ann** · Church · active

St. James Anglican Church serves the quiet hillside district of Gibraltar in St. Ann. It was established in the late 1800s for the rural communities formed by freed families after Emancipation in 1838. Despite facing storms and other challenges, the church has maintained its role as a meaningful place for the district. Today, it continues to be a cornerstone for worship and community life in Gibraltar and the surrounding areas.

## History
St. James Anglican Church was founded in the late 19th century, as freed people established new villages in the hills of the Gibraltar district after Emancipation. The area was previously part of the plantation landscape around Alexandria, where the population was enslaved. As these post-settlement communities grew, the Anglican Diocese sent clergy from Alexandria and Brown’s Town to hold services in homes and other temporary spaces. By the 1880s, a permanent stone chapel was completed and dedicated to St. James, providing a stable center for baptisms, worship, and community gatherings. The church played a vital role in supporting the spiritual and social needs of families building a new life in freedom.

## Architecture
St. James is a small, sturdy church built from local limestone in the rural Gothic Revival style. It features a simple rectangular nave, a modest chancel, and narrow pointed-arch windows. The steep roof, now covered with metal, was likely originally made of wooden shingles. Inside, the church has an open-timber roof, wooden pews, and a simple pulpit. The surrounding churchyard, with graves of long-standing local families, contributes to the site's peaceful and historic character.

## Clergy
St. James has always been part of the St. Ann Deanery, sharing clergy with neighboring missions in Aboukir, Madras, and Brittonville. Early priests traveled difficult hillside roads to lead worship and support local education and family life. The church was instrumental in promoting literacy and religious instruction, which were especially crucial in the decades after Emancipation. After Jamaica’s Independence in 1962, the congregation remained active through the Mothers’ Union, youth programs, and community events. Lay readers were essential in maintaining regular services when ordained clergy were serving multiple congregations.

## Notable Facts
St. James has endured several major hurricanes, including Charlie (1951) and Gilbert (1988), which caused damage to the roof and windows. Each time, the community, with support from the Diocese, carried out the necessary repairs. The church's thick stone walls have also withstood heavy rains and hillside runoff. Today, St. James Anglican Church remains an active parish, holding weekly services, weddings, and community gatherings, serving as an enduring symbol of faith and community in Gibraltar.
