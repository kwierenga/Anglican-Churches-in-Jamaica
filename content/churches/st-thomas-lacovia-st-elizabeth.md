# St. Thomas', Lacovia
**St. Elizabeth** · Church · active

St. Thomas' Anglican Church stands in Lacovia on the banks of the Black River, in what was for half a century one of the two seats of government of St. Elizabeth parish. The town's name derives from *La Caoban*, its Spanish-period name. The present church is one of the oldest Anglican sites in the parish and is the principal church of the Lacovia Cure, which includes Holy Trinity Whitehall, St. Jude's Slipe, and St. Margaret's Middle Quarters under Rev. David "Tony" Reid, Rural Dean of St. Elizabeth. Hurricane Melissa decimated its roof on 28 October 2025.

## History
In 1723, Lacovia was designated joint capital of St. Elizabeth parish, sharing this status with Black River for fifty years. The Assize Courts and Vestry Meetings alternated between the two towns until 1773, when Black River took sole capital status. Lacovia sits at an important crossing point on the Black River, between the Upper Morass and the Lower Morass, and was surrounded by sugar estates and cattle pens dependent on enslaved labour.

Unusually among Jamaican slave communities, Lacovia was the home of a group of enslaved Madagascans — brought from the Indian Ocean rather than the more common West African sources. A number of them fled to join the Maroons before the First Maroon War, which was ended by the 1739 treaty with Cudjoe.

Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds; its vestry was composed of the planter class. After Emancipation (1834) and full freedom on 1 August 1838, Lacovia continued as a market and service town, and the Anglican Church transformed from planter chaplaincy to a community institution oriented toward worship and education. The Lipscomb episcopate's island-wide schools programme — by 1835 the Diocese reported 142 schools with 8,500 scholars, of which Lipscomb had himself established over 50 as "national schools" — was central to this transformation.

The 1891 Handbook of Jamaica records Rev. J. S. Fraser as clergyman at Lacovia with 643 registered members, 136 baptisms, and 23 marriages — one of the largest Anglican congregations in St. Elizabeth. The 1900 Handbook lists Rev. B. A. S. MacCalla serving Lacovia and Siloah jointly. The current priest-in-charge is the Rev. David "Tony" Reid, who is also Rural Dean of St. Elizabeth.

## Architecture
St. Thomas' is a limestone church in the simple Gothic Revival tradition of nineteenth-century Jamaican parish Anglican architecture — a rectangular nave, tall pointed-arch windows, and a modest belfry. The surrounding churchyard contains graves from the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries. Hurricane Melissa, a Category 5 storm on 28 October 2025, tore away the roof, exposing the interior to the weather.

## Education
St. Thomas' has historically operated community schools as part of the Anglican post-Emancipation educational network. Across St. Elizabeth the Anglican educational legacy is anchored by Munro College (1856) and Hampton School (1858) in the Malvern hills — both funded from the 1797 will of Robert Hugh Munro, a free "gentleman of colour," bequeathed to his nephew Caleb Dickenson and the Churchwardens of St. Elizabeth to endow education for poor children.

## Clergy
- Early eighteenth century: Rev. John Kelly alternated between Black River and Lacovia on successive Sundays — the parish was thinly served in its first decades (Jemmott, *Parish History of St. Elizabeth*)
- Until c.1870: Rev. Michael H. Smith
- 1878: Rev. M. M. Deuwarder (Curate in charge)
- 1891: Rev. J. S. Fraser (643 members)
- 1900: Rev. B. A. S. MacCalla (Lacovia and Siloah)
- Current: Rev. David "Tony" Reid (Rural Dean of St. Elizabeth)

## Hurricane Melissa (October 2025)
Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope, Westmoreland, on 28 October 2025. The Diocese of Jamaica and the Cayman Islands named St. Thomas' Lacovia as one of four churches that "stood little chance" against the storm — alongside St. John's Black River, St. Luke's Balaclava, and St. Thomas' Bluefields. Bishop Garth Minott, Suffragan Bishop of Kingston, reported 120 of the Diocese's ~300 church buildings damaged and 18 destroyed. Major roof reconstruction at Lacovia is required.

## Notable Facts
The Lacovia Tombstones, a short distance from the church, are a designated National Heritage Site. Two flat grave markers from 1738 are embedded in the main road; one identifies Thomas Jordan Spencer (died 17 September 1738), whose crest indicates descent from the Duke of Marlborough and thus connects the grave to the lineage that later produced Sir Winston Churchill. The second stone is uninscribed. Local tradition, recorded by the Jamaica National Heritage Trust, holds that both men were killed in a fight at a nearby tavern and buried side by side. The dedication is to Thomas the Apostle ("Doubting Thomas" — John 20:24–29), feast day 3 July.
