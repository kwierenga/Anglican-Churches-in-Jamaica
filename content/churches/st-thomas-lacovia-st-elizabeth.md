# St. Thomas', Lacovia
**St. Elizabeth** · Church · active

St. Thomas Anglican Church in Lacovia is one of the oldest churches in St. Elizabeth, with a history that dates back to the time of slavery. After Emancipation in 1838, the church became a vital place for freed families who were building new lives in the area. Over the years, it has survived fires, storms, and other challenges but has always remained an active place of worship. Today, St. Thomas continues to serve the people of Lacovia and surrounding communities as part of the Anglican Diocese of Jamaica.

## History
St. Thomas Anglican Church was founded in the 18th century when Jamaica was still a slave society. Lacovia was an important inland crossing point on the Black River, and the church served estate owners, free people of color, and some enslaved people who had limited permission to attend. After Emancipation in 1838, many freed people settled near Lacovia for its river and farmland. The church grew quickly during this period and became a strong center for Anglican worship in the region. Throughout the 1800s, St. Thomas played a major role in education, baptisms, marriages, and community life for people rebuilding their lives after slavery.

## Architecture
The church is constructed from thick limestone blocks in a simple Gothic style. It features a rectangular nave, tall pointed-arch windows, and a modest bell-cot (a small structure to hold the bell). Inside, you find an open wooden roof, simple wooden pews, and a raised chancel at the front where the altar is located. The building has been repaired many times after damage from fires and hurricanes. The surrounding churchyard contains many old graves from the 1800s and early 1900s, marking the long history of the local community.

## Clergy
St. Thomas has always been a key church in the Black River Deanery. It was served by many well-known priests, some of whom also ministered in nearby villages like Mayfield and Middle Quarters. The church once ran its own school, teaching local children reading, writing, and religion. After Jamaica’s Independence in 1962, St. Thomas remained active with youth programs, Mothers’ Union activities, and community outreach. Lay readers (trained volunteers) were essential in leading services when a full-time priest was shared with other churches.

## Notable Facts
St. Thomas has endured many natural disasters, including Hurricane Charlie (1951), Hurricane Gilbert (1988), and floods from the nearby Black River. The church has gone through periods of decline, but each time, the community has worked hard to repair and reopen it. In recent years, small restoration projects have kept the roof, windows, and walls in good condition. The church has a sturdy construction and benefitted from ongoing maintenance. Today, St. Thomas Anglican Church continues to hold regular services, weddings, funerals, and community events, standing as a enduring symbol of faith and history in Lacovia. Hurricane Melissa (October 28, 2025) decimated the roof of St Thomas Anglican Church in Lacovia. The Diocese confirmed it as one of four Anglican churches that 'stood little chance' against the hurricane. The Gleaner (January 2026) reported the roof was 'decimated' with a photograph showing the exposed interior. The Social Development Commission's post-Melissa church restoration programme received applications including churches from the Lacovia area, underscoring the widespread damage. As of early 2026 the church required major roof reconstruction.
