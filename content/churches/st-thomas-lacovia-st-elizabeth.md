# St. Thomas', Lacovia
**St. Elizabeth** · Church · active

St. Thomas' Anglican Church stands in Lacovia, the first capital of St. Elizabeth parish, on the banks of the Black River. Documented since at least 1723 — when Rev. John Kelly served both Lacovia and Black River — it is one of the oldest Anglican foundations in St. Elizabeth. The town's name derives from the Spanish *La Caoba*, meaning mahogany, encoding the economy of extraction that defined colonial Jamaica: the vast mahogany forests were felled by enslaved labour and floated down the Black River for export. St. Thomas' is the mother church of a cure that includes Holy Trinity Whitehall, St. Jude's Slipe, and St. Margaret's Middle Quarters, served by Rev. David A. "Tony" Reid, Rural Dean of St. Elizabeth. Hurricane Melissa decimated the roof on 28 October 2025.

## History
Lacovia served as the capital of St. Elizabeth from approximately 1723 until 1773, when the seat of government transferred to Black River. The town sat at an important crossing point on the Black River, surrounded by sugar estates and cattle pens whose wealth depended entirely on enslaved labour. One of the most distinctive features of Lacovia's history is the presence of a community of enslaved Madagascans — brought from the Indian Ocean rather than the more common West African sources — some of whom fled to join the Maroons before the First Maroon War. Their escape represents the resistance that simmered throughout the era of slavery, and the Maroon treaty of 1739 formalised the freedom that these fugitives had seized for themselves.

The Lacovia Tombstones — a collection of flat grave markers from 1738, now embedded in the main road — are a designated National Heritage Site. They mark the graves of early settlers and likely include vestrymen of St. Thomas' Church. That they are embedded in the road surface tells its own story: as the town declined after losing its capital status, development simply paved over the dead. Their preservation as a heritage site reflects growing awareness of colonial-era historical resources.

The Anglican Church that served Lacovia was the established church of the colony — funded by the government, its vestry composed of the planter class, its clergy often slaveholders themselves. After Emancipation in 1834 and full freedom on 1 August 1838, the church's role shifted fundamentally. Freed people settled near Lacovia for its river access and farmland, and the church grew as a centre of worship and education for families rebuilding their lives after slavery. The church ran its own school, teaching reading, writing, and scripture — part of Bishop Lipscomb's island-wide initiative that established 142 elementary schools across Jamaica.

Jamaica's independence on 6 August 1962 marked the latest chapter in a church that had witnessed the transition from Spanish mahogany trade to English plantation slavery, from slavery to freedom, from colonial outpost to independent nation.

Hurricane Melissa, a Category 5 storm on 28 October 2025, decimated St. Thomas' roof, exposing the interior. The Diocese confirmed it as one of four Anglican churches that "stood little chance" against the hurricane. As of early 2026, major roof reconstruction is required.

## Architecture
St. Thomas' is constructed from thick limestone blocks in a simple Gothic style — a rectangular nave, tall pointed-arch windows, and a modest bell-cot. Inside, an open wooden roof, simple wooden pews, and a raised chancel characterise the interior. The surrounding churchyard contains graves from the 1800s and early 1900s. Pre-Melissa aerial drone photography from December 2024 documents the building before the storm.

## Education
St. Thomas' once operated its own school, providing basic education to children in the Lacovia area — part of the Anglican Church's post-emancipation transformation from planter church to educational institution. Across St. Elizabeth, the Anglican legacy includes Munro College (1856) and Hampton School (1874). Churches in the Lacovia cure continue to "run schools, clinics, homework programmes, and kitchens that feed the homeless."

## Clergy
Rev. David A. "Tony" Reid serves as priest-in-charge and Rural Dean of St. Elizabeth. The 1723 Handbook records Rev. John Kelly serving Lacovia and Black River. The 1891 Handbook lists J. S. Fraser at Lacovia; the 1900 Handbook lists B. A. S. MacCalla serving "Lacovia and Siloah."

## Notable Facts
The dedication to St. Thomas the Apostle — "Doubting Thomas," who demanded to see and touch the risen Christ before believing (John 20:24–29) — connects this church to one of the most human figures in the Gospels: the disciple who questioned, who needed proof, and who was met with grace rather than condemnation. His feast day is 3 July. The Lacovia Tombstones (1738), a few hundred metres from the church, are among the oldest surviving European-era funerary monuments in Jamaica. Lacovia's mahogany gave its name to the town and its timber to the finest churches and great houses of Jamaica and England — all cut by enslaved hands.
