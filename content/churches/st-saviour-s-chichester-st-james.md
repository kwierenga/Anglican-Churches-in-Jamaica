# St. Saviour's, Chichester
**St. James** · Church · active

St. Saviour's Anglican Church serves Chichester, a community in the Ramble district on the border of St. James and Hanover, south of Montego Bay in Jamaica's interior hill country. The church dates to at least 1847, when Rev. W. H. Stewart was ordained for "Chichester, diocese Jamaica," making it one of the earliest post-Emancipation Anglican foundations in the area. By 1878, both the church and an associated school — with teacher N. Campbell — were operational. St. Saviour's is part of the St. Mary's Montpelier Cure under Rev. Don T. Lewis.

## History
St. James was one of Jamaica's premier sugar parishes, and the estates surrounding Chichester were among the largest slaveholdings on the island. The Montpelier Estate — Old and New Montpelier — was owned by Lord Seaford (Charles Rose Ellis, 1st Baron Seaford) and held over 640 enslaved people at emancipation; with Shettlewood Pen across the Great River in Hanover, the total exceeded 1,000. Lord Seaford received over £18,000 in government compensation. The nearby Ramble Estate was owned by William Hudson Heaven, whose godson later used Jamaica wealth to purchase Lundy Island in the Bristol Channel.

St. James was the epicentre of the 1831 Christmas Rebellion — the largest slave uprising in the British Caribbean. Samuel Sharpe, a Baptist deacon in Montego Bay, organised enslaved people in a passive resistance strike that began at Kensington Estate on 27 December 1831. Old Montpelier was among the estates burned; a skirmish there on 28 December saw Colonel Grignon's militia outmanoeuvred. The rebellion involved up to 60,000 of Jamaica's 300,000 enslaved people. Sharpe was hanged on 23 May 1832, and the rebellion directly contributed to the passage of the Slavery Abolition Act in 1833. Sharpe is now a National Hero of Jamaica.

After Emancipation in 1834 and full freedom on 1 August 1838, the Anglican Church expanded into the interior to serve the freed population. St. Mary's Montpelier was built in 1847, and St. Saviour's Chichester appears to date from the same period — Rev. Stewart's ordination for Chichester in 1847–1848 is the earliest documented clergy assignment. The 1878 Jamaica Directory confirms Rev. E. Stewart as "curate St. Saviour Church of England, Chichester" with N. Campbell as teacher at the associated school. By the 1900 Handbook, C. G. McGregory served "Montpelier and 5 Mission Stations" — Chichester was likely among them.

The community's name — shared with the cathedral city in West Sussex — follows the pattern of Jamaican settlements named by English planters after their home towns. Jamaica's independence on 6 August 1962 brought new nationhood to these interior communities.

## Architecture
As a mission station of the Montpelier Cure, St. Saviour's is a smaller, simpler chapel than the cut stone Gothic Revival mother church at Montpelier (cornerstone 1847). No detailed architectural documentation is available.

## Education
The 1878 Jamaica Directory confirms a St. Saviour school at Chichester with teacher N. Campbell — evidence of the Anglican educational mission in action just four decades after Emancipation. The pattern of establishing schools alongside churches was central to the Anglican Church's post-emancipation transformation from planter-class chaplaincy to educational institution serving the freed Black majority.

## Clergy
St. Saviour's is part of the St. Mary's Montpelier Cure, which also includes St. Stephen's Cambridge, All Saints' Chester Castle, and St. Matthew's Catadupa. Current clergy: Rev. Don T. Lewis (retired rector), assisted by Melvin Newsome and Cleve Llewellyn. Rev. W. H. Stewart was ordained for Chichester in 1847–1848; Rev. E. Stewart served as curate by 1878.

## Notable Facts
"St. Saviour" is a directly Christological dedication — referring to Jesus Christ as Saviour — rather than invoking a patron saint. It is one of the dedications classified as being to "one of the persons of God," alongside Holy Trinity, Christ Church, and Good Shepherd. The Montpelier Cure in which St. Saviour's sits encompasses the very estates where the Christmas Rebellion was fought — the rebellion that ended slavery in the British Empire.
