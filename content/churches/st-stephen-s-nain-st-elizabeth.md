# St. Stephen's, Nain
**St. Elizabeth** · Church · active

St. Stephen's Anglican Church stands in Nain, a community in the south-central interior of St. Elizabeth that has been transformed twice — first by Emancipation, when freed people established the settlement, and then by bauxite mining, which arrived in the 1960s and reshaped the landscape and economy. Consecrated in 1864, the church is one of the older surviving Anglican foundations in the parish interior, built during the period when the Church of England was reinventing itself from the established church of the planter class into a church that served the freed Black majority. The dedication to St. Stephen — the first Christian martyr, a deacon who served the poor and spoke truth to power — carries resonance for a community that has endured successive upheavals. St. Stephen's is part of the St. Matthew's Santa Cruz cure under Rev. Hilda Vaughan.

## History
The name Nain comes from the Gospel of Luke (7:11–17), where Jesus encounters a funeral procession at the gate of a town called Nain and raises a widow's only son from the dead — a story of divine intervention for the most vulnerable. The choice of this name, alongside the nearby community of Mount Hermon (the biblical mountain of divine blessing), reflects the pattern of post-Emancipation place-naming in which freed people and missionaries gave settlements names of spiritual hope and new beginnings.

Nain was established in the interior hills of St. Elizabeth after Emancipation in 1834 and full freedom on 1 August 1838. The hills were less valuable for plantation agriculture than the sugar estates along the Black River, making them attractive for free settlement. Freed people established smallholdings, growing provisions, raising livestock, and harvesting pimento (allspice) — a major Jamaican export that sustained communities across the St. Elizabeth interior.

The Anglican Church that consecrated St. Stephen's in 1864 was in the midst of a fundamental transformation. For nearly two centuries, it had served as the established church of the colony — funded by over £37,000 annually in government stipends, serving the planter class, with clergy who were often slaveholders themselves. The Baptist War of 1831–1832, led by National Hero Samuel Sharpe across western Jamaica, had helped accelerate Emancipation — but the Anglican establishment had largely sided with the planters, not the enslaved. After 1838, the church launched an aggressive campaign of church-building and school-founding to serve the freed population it had largely neglected. St. Stephen's consecration in 1864 — six years before disestablishment removed state funding in 1870 — was part of this expansion. By 1891, the Handbook of Jamaica records Rev. A. E. Lewis serving "Mount Hermon and Nain," a pairing that continued through 1900.

The most dramatic transformation of Nain came in the 1960s with the arrival of bauxite mining. Alumina Partners of Jamaica (Alpart) established a major processing plant at Nain itself, converting the red laterite soils of the limestone hills into alumina for export. The industry brought wage employment and infrastructure to an area where small farming offered limited cash income, but at a profound cost: open-pit mining destroyed the landscape, dust pollution affected health and agriculture, and entire communities were displaced from land their families had held since Emancipation. The irony was bitter — communities established by freed people seeking land independence were now displaced by multinational industrial operations, echoing in a different form the original dispossession of slavery.

Jamaica's independence on 6 August 1962 preceded the full bauxite transformation by only a few years. Through the upheavals of mining, displacement, and economic change, St. Stephen's has served as an anchor of continuity and community identity.

## Architecture
St. Stephen's is a local limestone church consecrated in 1864, built in the vernacular Gothic style characteristic of mid-Victorian Anglican churches in Jamaica's interior — a simple rectangular nave with pointed openings, adapted to local materials and climate.

## Education
Nain has schools that likely trace their origins to Anglican educational efforts in the post-Emancipation period. Bishop Lipscomb established 142 elementary schools across Jamaica in the 1830s, and the church's educational mission was central to its purpose in communities like Nain. Across St. Elizabeth, the Anglican legacy includes Munro College (1856) and Hampton School (1874).

## Clergy
St. Stephen's is part of the St. Matthew's Santa Cruz cure under Rev. Hilda Vaughan, who also serves St. Andrew's Gilnock, Church of the Holy Spirit Pepper, and The Transfiguration Leeds. Rev. A. E. Lewis served "Mount Hermon and Nain" from at least 1891 through 1900.

## Notable Facts
St. Stephen — the first Christian martyr, stoned to death in Jerusalem (Acts 6–7) — was one of the seven deacons appointed to ensure fair distribution of food to marginalised widows, making him a figure associated with social justice and service to the poor. His martyrdom speech challenged the religious establishment; he was killed for speaking truth to power. His vision at the moment of death — "I see the heavens opened" — is one of the most luminous passages in the New Testament. His feast day is 26 December (Boxing Day in Jamaica), traditionally a day of community celebration. For a community that has been transformed by forces beyond its control — from slavery to freedom, from farming to mining — the dedication to a saint who served the vulnerable and witnessed to truth under pressure carries enduring meaning.
