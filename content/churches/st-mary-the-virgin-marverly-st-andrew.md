# St. Mary the Virgin, Marverly
**St. Andrew** · Church · active

St. Mary the Virgin Anglican Church serves Marverly, a residential community in southern St. Andrew in the urban residential zone adjoining Kingston. The dedication 'St. Mary the Virgin' — using the full Marian title — distinguishes this church from the many other St. Mary's dedications in Jamaica and marks it as a church with a particular High Church or Anglo-Catholic tradition. Marverly is a neighbourhood in the southern urban area of St. Andrew.

## History
Marverly developed as a residential neighbourhood in southern St. Andrew, in the urban expansion of the Kingston metropolitan area through the 20th century. The Anglican Diocese established St. Mary the Virgin to serve this community. The use of the full title 'the Virgin' in the dedication is more common in High Church Anglican tradition and suggests the church may have been founded with a particular liturgical character.

## Architecture
St. Mary the Virgin is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural St. Andrew in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. Set in the rural interior of St. Andrew. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
Marverly has been served within the southern St. Andrew urban deanery circuit, linked to the Kingston metropolitan ministry network.

## Notable Facts
The dedication 'St. Mary the Virgin' — as distinct from simply 'St. Mary's' — reflects the fuller Marian title used in High Church Anglican tradition, where Marian devotion is expressed within the parameters of Anglican doctrine. This distinguishes the church from the many 'St. Mary's' churches across Jamaica that use a shorter form of the same dedication.
