# St. James', Annotto Bay
**St. Mary** · Church · active

St. James’ Anglican Church is the confirmed principal Anglican church in Annotto Bay, a north-coast town in St. Mary parish at the mouth of the Pencar River. Annotto Bay has a history shaped by the banana trade — it was one of the loading points for banana exports from St. Mary — and the fishing economy that continues in the town today. St. James’ has served as the established Anglican presence in Annotto Bay for well over a century, providing worship and pastoral care to the coastal community.

## History
Annotto Bay developed as a banana-shipping port on St. Mary’s north coast in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, growing in importance as the banana trade expanded across the parish under the United Fruit Company and successor companies. The Anglican Diocese maintained a presence at Annotto Bay from the plantation era, with the church serving planters, merchants, and the broader population of the coastal town. St. James’ became the principal Anglican church in Annotto Bay, with additional mission points — St. Margaret’s and St. Simon’s — serving other parts of the town and district.

## Architecture
St. James’ is a coastal church in a town directly exposed to the Caribbean Sea on the north coast of St. Mary, at the mouth of a river. This compound position creates both storm surge risk from the sea and flooding risk from the river during hurricane rainfall. The building’s construction reflects the durability required for a coastal church in a hurricane-exposed environment.

## Clergy
St. James’ serves as the hub church of the Annotto Bay circuit of the St. Mary deanery, with its clergy responsible for the other Anglican worship points in the town and surrounding district. The church has maintained an active congregation through the fishing and agricultural community of the broader Annotto Bay area.

## Notable Facts
Annotto Bay’s position at the mouth of the Pencar River makes it one of the more flood-prone communities on St. Mary’s north coast. The river can overtop its banks during the heavy rainfall associated with hurricane passages, combining with storm surge to create compound flooding risk for the town and its church. Hurricane Gilbert (1988) struck the north coast with great severity. Hurricane Melissa (October 2025) tracked along Jamaica’s north coast; Annotto Bay was directly in the storm’s path and St. James’ congregation experienced direct storm impact.
