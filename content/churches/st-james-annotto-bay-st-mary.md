# St. James', Annotto Bay
**St. Mary** · Church · active

St. James' Anglican Church is the confirmed principal Anglican church in **Annotto Bay**, a north-coast town in St. Mary parish at the mouth of the **Pencar River**. **Satchell's *Parish History of St. Mary*** identifies St. James' as one of the **four principal Anglican churches of the parish**, alongside the Port Maria parish church, St. Cyprian's Highgate, and Holy Trinity Retreat. Annotto Bay has a history shaped by the **banana trade** — it was one of the loading points for banana exports from St. Mary.

## History
**Pre-English era and the Battle of Rio Nuevo (1658).** **Christopher Columbus** sighted Jamaica in 1494 on his second voyage. The **Battle of Rio Nuevo (June 1658)** saw British forces under **Sir Edward D'Oyley** decisively defeat a Spanish expedition attempting to recapture Jamaica — effectively ending Spanish resistance to British rule.

**English rule (1655) and the early port (Act of 1784).** Under English rule from **1655**, St. Mary became a major sugar-producing parish. **Annotto Bay** developed as a leading shipping port of St. Mary from the early eighteenth century — regulated by an **Act of 1784** for the repair of the harbour and the mooring of ships — and by the early nineteenth century its imports (£234,261 in a reported year) exceeded those of Port Maria. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**Tacky's Revolt (1760).** On Easter Monday, **7 April 1760**, **Tacky's Revolt** broke out on **Frontier and Trinity estates** near Port Maria — a short distance west of Annotto Bay. Tacky — a **Fante royal** from the Gold Coast of West Africa — led a rising of self-emancipated **Coromantee** people intended to overthrow British colonial rule. Over **500 Black men and women** were killed in battle, executed, or took their own lives — the most significant slave rebellion in the British West Indies between the 1733 St. John insurrection and the 1791 Haitian Revolution.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union** to "wipe non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb** (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**Metcalfe parish (1841–1867).** From **1841 to 1867**, **Annotto Bay was the capital of the short-lived parish of Metcalfe**, created by petition of the freeholders of eastern St. Mary and the adjoining parish of St. George and named for **Sir Charles Metcalfe**, then Governor of Jamaica. **Law 20 of 1867** reduced the number of Jamaican parishes and reunited Metcalfe with St. Mary, with **Port Maria** restored as the parish town.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. The Anglican Diocese maintained a presence at Annotto Bay through the plantation era, banana trade, and post-parish reorganisation; St. James' became the established Anglican presence in the town, with additional mission points including St. Margaret's serving other parts of the district.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** The **banana trade** transformed the St. Mary economy in the late 19th and 20th centuries. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. James' a settled town parish.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Annotto Bay's position at the mouth of the **Pencar River** makes it one of the more flood-prone communities on St. Mary's north coast. The river can overtop its banks during the heavy rainfall associated with hurricane passages, combining with storm surge to create compound flooding risk for the town and its church. Annotto Bay was directly in Melissa's path and St. James' congregation experienced direct storm impact.

## Architecture
St. James' is a coastal church in a town directly exposed to the Caribbean Sea on the north coast of St. Mary, at the mouth of a river. This compound position creates both storm surge risk from the sea and flooding risk from the river during hurricane rainfall. The building's construction reflects the durability required for a coastal church in a hurricane-exposed environment.

## Clergy
St. James' serves as the **hub church of the Annotto Bay circuit** of the St. Mary deanery, with its clergy responsible for the other Anglican worship points in the town and surrounding district. For more than a decade in the 21st century, St. James' has been served alongside St. Barnabas Enfield, St. Patrick's Devon Pen, St. Margaret's Long Road, and Grandy Hole by a single Anglican priest, the **Rev. Veronica Thomas**, whose ministry across the five-cure circuit was profiled by the Jamaica Gleaner on 4 July 2015.

## Notable Facts
**Annotto Bay** was the **capital of the short-lived parish of Metcalfe (1841–1867)** before Law 20 of 1867 reunited it with St. Mary. The town's position at the mouth of the **Pencar River** makes it one of the more flood-prone communities on St. Mary's north coast. **Hurricane Gilbert (1988)** had previously struck the north coast with great severity. The dedication to **St. James the Greater** — apostle, brother of John the Evangelist, and patron saint of pilgrims (associated with the great pilgrimage route to Santiago de Compostela), with a feast day on **25 July** — is found across the Anglican world.
