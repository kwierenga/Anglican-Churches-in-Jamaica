# The Transfiguration, Leeds
**St. Elizabeth** · Church · inactive

The Church of the Transfiguration serves Leeds in St. Elizabeth, listed as a cure in the Santa Cruz circuit. Leeds is a community in the interior of St. Elizabeth, and the church's Transfiguration dedication commemorates the moment on Mount Tabor when Jesus' appearance was transformed before Peter, James, and John — one of the most visually dramatic events in the Gospels. The feast of the Transfiguration (6 August) falls in the heart of hurricane season.

## History
Leeds in St. Elizabeth most likely takes its name from the English city of Leeds in Yorkshire — a common practice of naming Jamaican estates after English towns. The Transfiguration church is part of the Santa Cruz cure served by Hilda Vaughan.

## Architecture
The Transfiguration is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural St. Elizabeth in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. Set in the rural interior of St. Elizabeth. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
The Santa Cruz cure covering Leeds is served by Hilda Vaughan from the Santa Cruz rectory.

## Notable Facts
The Transfiguration (6 August) is one of the Principal Feasts of the Anglican calendar, celebrated with particular solemnity. The event — recorded in Matthew 17, Mark 9, and Luke 9 — is understood as a moment of divine disclosure, when Christ's full nature was briefly revealed to his inner circle of disciples. Churches dedicated to the Transfiguration are found across the Anglican world, and this St. Elizabeth example connects a rural Jamaican community to one of the Church's central theological moments.
