# All Saints', West Street
**Kingston** · Church · active

All Saints' Anglican Church stands at 118½ West Street in downtown Kingston, at the western boundary of the city's original colonial grid. Founded in the 1890s as a mission chapel under Archbishop Nuttall's Kingston Church and School Extension Fund, it was consecrated on 17 January 1911 — the same day as Kingston Parish Church — after the 1907 earthquake destroyed the original building. For decades, All Saints' has served some of Kingston's most vulnerable communities in western downtown, including Denham Town, with a ministry that bridged political and social divides through eras of extraordinary violence. Its mission church, St. Alban's in Denham Town, operates one of the oldest schools in the area. The rectorship is currently vacant following the death of Canon Abner Powell in November 2023.

## History
**Parish-historical context.** Penn and Venables's English expedition captured Jamaica from Spain in May **1655**. **St. Andrew parish was established in 1664**, with the parish church at Half Way Tree. The **Great Earthquake of 7 June 1692** destroyed two-thirds of Port Royal; the new city of **Kingston** was formally established on **22 July 1692**, with the Assembly constituting Kingston as a parish in **1693**. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church. The **Diocese of Jamaica** was established by Letters Patent on **24 July 1824**; **Christopher Lipscomb** was consecrated first Bishop in 1824. During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. The capital moved from Spanish Town to **Kingston in 1872**. The **Kingston and St. Andrew Corporation (KSAC)** unified the two parishes for civic administration in **1923**.

All Saints' was established in the 1890s as part of the Kingston Church and School Extension Fund, launched by Archbishop **Enos Nuttall** in **1892** to address Kingston's rapidly growing population — increasing at nearly a thousand persons per year since 1881 — and the expansion of suburbs accessible by the new tram-car lines. The Fund produced two churches and three mission chapels across Kingston and its suburbs. All Saints' does not appear in the 1900 Handbook of Jamaica, which lists Kingston Parish Church, St. George's, St. Michael's, and "Allman Town and 6 Mission Stations" but no All Saints, suggesting it was built between 1892 and 1900 as one of the mission chapels.

West Street, where the church stands, formed the western boundary of Kingston's original grid — laid out by Surveyor John Goffe after the 1692 Port Royal earthquake. Beyond this boundary, the land was undeveloped until Emancipation transformed the city. Full freedom came on 1 August 1838, and within years thousands of formerly enslaved people moved into Kingston seeking employment. West of the grid, Smith's Village grew as an informal settlement of freed people between 1838 and 1843. In 1888, the Rector of Kingston Parish Church reported that residents of this "remote suburb" were attending revival meetings rather than Anglican services, prompting the establishment of the St. Alban's Mission Station to serve their spiritual needs. The Smith Village Elementary School — now St. Alban's Primary School — was founded on the same premises in 1890 and remains one of the oldest schools in western Kingston.

All Saints' thus stood at a boundary: between the colonial merchant grid to the east and the post-Emancipation settlements that grew westward. Its congregation drew from both worlds.

On 14 January 1907, an earthquake of magnitude 6.2 devastated Kingston, destroying approximately 85 per cent of the city's buildings and all its Anglican churches. The present All Saints' was rebuilt and consecrated on 17 January 1911 — the same day as Kingston Parish Church — as one of ten churches consecrated within a single month under Archbishop Nuttall's reconstruction programme. Bishops from England, America, and Canada attended the services. J. B. Ellis described the event as without precedent in the history of the Anglican Church.

In the 1930s and 1940s, Governor Denham's slum clearance programme demolished Smith's Village and replaced it with planned housing — Denham Town. In the 1960s, the adjacent Back-O-Wall settlement was cleared to build Tivoli Gardens (1963–1966), a political garrison community that became synonymous with Jamaica's era of partisan violence. All Saints' and its mission at St. Alban's found themselves ministering at the heart of this volatile geography, serving families caught between political factions in some of Kingston's most dangerous decades.

Bishop **Percival Gibson** (1957–1967) was the first native-born Bishop of Jamaica. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** brought new hope but also new tensions in western downtown Kingston. **Hurricane Melissa** made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The political tribalism that developed around garrison communities like Tivoli Gardens tested the church's commitment to serving all. Through it all, All Saints' maintained its presence on West Street as a place of worship, community support, and pastoral care.

## Architecture
The current building dates from the post-1907 rebuilding programme and was consecrated in 1911. Built in reinforced masonry using the earthquake-resistant construction methods adopted after the disaster, it is a modest church suited to its dense urban setting on West Street. The reconstruction was directed by Rev. F. L. King, who was credited with improving building quality while containing costs across the diocesan programme.

## Education and Community
All Saints' mission church, St. Alban's in Denham Town, operates St. Alban's Primary School — originally founded in 1890 as Smith Village Elementary School and renamed in 1952. The school is over 135 years old and one of the longest-running educational institutions in western Kingston.

Following the May 2010 Tivoli Incursion — the military operation to arrest Christopher "Dudus" Coke that left dozens dead and traumatised the surrounding communities — St. Alban's hosted hundreds of children at a two-week Vacation Bible School to help them readjust. The Diocese introduced a social intervention programme providing social work training for community members and counselling for traumatised residents. The church's response to the incursion demonstrated the depth of its roots in the community.

## Clergy
The most significant figure in All Saints' modern history is Canon Abner Powell, who was ordained deacon in 1973 alongside the future Archbishop Howard Gregory and Bishop Robert Thompson. After serving at St. George's East Street, Powell became rector of All Saints' West Street and chaplain of Kingston College on North Street. He ministered fearlessly through decades of political violence in western Kingston, "bridging the divide" between Denham Town and Tivoli Gardens. The church under his care did not have security windows — even when a computer room was installed, no grilles were added, because the community respected Powell and the work of the church. His ministry spanned approximately fifty years. Canon Powell died on 23 November 2023, aged 74. The rectorship is currently vacant.

## Notable Facts
All Saints' was consecrated on the same day as Kingston Parish Church — 17 January 1911 — linking the modest West Street chapel to the capital's most prominent Anglican church in one of the most remarkable events in Anglican history. The church's location at the boundary of the colonial grid and the post-Emancipation settlements that grew beyond it gives it a particular historical resonance: it has always served the communities that formed after freedom, in a part of Kingston where the legacies of slavery, political violence, and community resilience intersect most sharply.
