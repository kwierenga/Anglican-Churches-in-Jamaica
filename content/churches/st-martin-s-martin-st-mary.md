# St. Martin's, Martin
**St. Mary** · Church · active

St. Martin’s Anglican Church serves the community of Martin in St. Mary parish, a small interior farming district whose name preserves the memory of a colonial estate or proprietor. The dedication to St. Martin of Tours, the 4th-century soldier-saint associated with generosity to the poor, gives the church a patron celebrated for charitable ministry — a fitting dedication for a small rural chapel serving a modest farming community. St. Martin’s has provided Anglican ministry to the local population for several generations.

## History
The Martin district developed from the plantation landscape of interior St. Mary, with its name surviving as a community identity long after the estate economy was replaced by smallholder farming. The Anglican Diocese established St. Martin’s as part of its mission network, serving the freed families and their descendants who settled in the area after Emancipation. The church’s history mirrors the broader transformation of St. Mary’s interior from plantation agriculture to independent farming communities.

## Architecture
St. Martin’s is a small masonry chapel characteristic of Anglican mission buildings in interior St. Mary. The building sits in the hilly terrain of the Martin district, in a landscape of small farms and wooded ridges subject to the heavy orographic rainfall of this part of the island.

## Clergy
Martin has been served within the St. Mary deanery under shared clergy arrangements. As a small interior chapel, its pastoral life depends on dedicated lay leaders who sustain regular worship between clerical visits.

## Notable Facts
The community name ‘Martin’ is unusual in that it matches the church’s dedication exactly — suggesting the community may have taken its identity from the church, or that both derive from the same estate proprietor. Either way, church and community are unusually closely named. Hurricane Melissa (October 2025) brought heavy rain and wind to interior St. Mary; the Martin community and congregation were in the affected zone.
