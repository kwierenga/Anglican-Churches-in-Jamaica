# St. Martin's, Martin
**St. Mary** · Church · active

St. Martin's Anglican Church serves the community of **Martin** in St. Mary parish, a small interior farming district. The dedication to **St. Martin of Tours**, the 4th-century soldier-saint associated with generosity to the poor (feast day **11 November**), gives the church a patron celebrated for charitable ministry.

## History
**Pre-English era and the Battle of Rio Nuevo (1658).** **Christopher Columbus** sighted Jamaica in 1494 on his second voyage. The **Battle of Rio Nuevo (June 1658)** saw British forces under **Sir Edward D'Oyley** decisively defeat a Spanish expedition attempting to recapture Jamaica — effectively ending Spanish resistance to British rule.

**English rule (1655) and the planter church.** Under English rule from **1655**, St. Mary became a major sugar-producing parish. The Martin district developed from the plantation landscape of interior St. Mary, with its name surviving as a community identity long after the estate economy was replaced by smallholder farming. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**Tacky's Revolt (1760).** On Easter Monday, **7 April 1760**, **Tacky's Revolt** broke out on **Frontier and Trinity estates** near Port Maria. Tacky — a **Fante royal** from the Gold Coast of West Africa — led a rising of self-emancipated **Coromantee** people intended to overthrow British colonial rule. Over **500 Black men and women** were killed in battle, executed, or took their own lives — the most significant slave rebellion in the British West Indies between the 1733 St. John insurrection and the 1791 Haitian Revolution.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union** to "wipe non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb** (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**Metcalfe parish (1841–1867) and disestablishment (1870).** A separate parish of **Metcalfe** was created in **1841**, named for **Sir Charles Metcalfe**, then Governor of Jamaica; **Annotto Bay** was its capital. **Law 20 of 1867** reduced parishes from 22 to 14, reuniting Metcalfe with St. Mary. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

**Foundation of St. Martin's.** The Anglican Diocese established St. Martin's as part of its mission network, serving the freed families and their descendants who settled in the area after Emancipation. The church's history mirrors the broader transformation of St. Mary's interior from plantation agriculture to independent farming communities.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** The **banana trade** transformed the St. Mary economy in the late 19th and 20th centuries. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Martin's a settled rural chapel.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Hurricane Melissa brought heavy rain and wind to interior St. Mary; the Martin community and congregation were in the affected zone.

## Architecture
St. Martin's is a small masonry chapel characteristic of Anglican mission buildings in interior St. Mary. The building sits in the hilly terrain of the Martin district, in a landscape of small farms and wooded ridges subject to the heavy orographic rainfall of this part of the island.

## Clergy
Martin has been served within the **St. Mary deanery** under shared clergy arrangements. As a small interior chapel, its pastoral life depends on dedicated lay leaders who sustain regular worship between clerical visits.

## Notable Facts
The community name "Martin" is unusual in that it matches the church's dedication exactly — suggesting the community may have taken its identity from the church, or that both derive from the same estate proprietor. Either way, church and community are unusually closely named. **St. Martin of Tours** — born c. 316 AD, soldier in the Roman army before becoming Bishop of Tours — is famous in Christian iconography for the legend of cutting his cloak in half to share with a beggar; he was a model for monastic life in the Western Church.
