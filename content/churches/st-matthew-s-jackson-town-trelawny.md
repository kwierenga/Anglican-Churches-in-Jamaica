# St. Matthew's, Jackson Town
**Trelawny** · Church · active

St. Matthew's Anglican Church serves Jackson Town, a small rural community in central Trelawny. The church is listed in the Diocese of Jamaica and the Cayman Islands directory within the **Trelawny Deanery** of the Montego Bay Region, and is documented in the National Library of Jamaica's March **1931** photographic survey of Diocesan churches. The church does not appear in the 1891 Handbook of Jamaica's Anglican returns — placing its foundation in the late phase of Bishop Enos Nuttall's rural mission expansion (the Diocese grew from 26 rural stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**).

## History
**Founding of the parish (1770).** The settlers in St. James (then much larger than today) had complained as early as 1733 that the parish was too large; they petitioned the Assembly to divide it, proposing the name "New Brunswick" for the new parish — but the first Bill failed. In **1770** the new parish of Trelawny was finally formed and named after Governor **Sir William Trelawny**. The first capital was **Martha Brae**, but its small size and inland location made it unsuited; around **1790** the seaside village of Martha Brae Point was renamed **Falmouth** (after Falmouth in Cornwall, England — the governor's birthplace) and made the parish capital.

**Slavery, sugar, and the Maroons.** Trelawny became, in time, the parish with **the most sugar estates and sugar factories** in Jamaica — at its peak there were as many as 100 estates with over 40 sugar factories. The interior of the parish was **Cockpit Country**, the Leeward Maroon heartland: Cudjoe's Town (later Trelawny Town in St. James) was the principal Maroon settlement, protected by the **1739 Peace Treaty** that ended the First Maroon War. The **Second Maroon War of 1795–96** ended with the deportation of some 568 Trelawny Town Maroons to Nova Scotia, and their onward transport to Sierra Leone in 1800. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**The Sam Sharpe Rebellion (December 1831).** The Christmas Rebellion of 1831, led by the Baptist deacon **Samuel Sharpe** and ignited at the Kensington Estate in St. James on **27 December 1831**, spread directly into Trelawny — among the western parishes where some 60,000 enslaved people were involved. **Rev. William Knibb**, the Baptist abolitionist born in Kettering, Northamptonshire, who ministered at Falmouth, played a major role in the post-rebellion campaign that helped secure the accelerated end of the apprenticeship scheme in 1838. Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation, 1865, disestablishment.** The British Parliament passed the Slavery Abolition Act in 1833; Emancipation came in 1834, and full freedom followed at the end of the apprenticeship period on **1 August 1838**. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865, Crown Colony rule replaced the planter Assembly (1866); the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of St. Matthew's: Nuttall's expansion.** Bishop Enos Nuttall's tenure (1880–1916) saw the Diocese grow from 26 rural mission stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. The 1891 Handbook records the principal Trelawny Anglican churches as Falmouth, Swanswick (Clark's Town), Rio Bueno, Stewart Town, Gibraltar, and St. Silas, with Albert Town and Wait-a-Bit as missions. Jackson Town does not appear, placing its foundation after 1891. By 1931 the Diocese's photographic survey documents St. Matthew's as an existing church.

**Twentieth century, sugar decline, Independence.** By **1927** the Trelawny sugar estates had declined from a peak of 100 to just 16 — although Trelawny still produced more sugar than any other parish. Banana growing took over as the major agricultural enterprise, and today **yam** is the parish's signature crop (about 60 percent of national production). Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Matthew's a settled rural mission. The Falmouth Historic District was declared a National Monument in **1996** under the Jamaica Heritage Trust Act.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. **Trelawny was among the five most affected parishes**. The specific status of St. Matthew's Jackson Town after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. Matthew's is a rural masonry chapel. No detailed architectural description has been identified in the public heritage sources reviewed; the **1931 National Library of Jamaica image** records the building as it stood early in the twentieth century.

## Clergy
St. Matthew's is served within the Trelawny Deanery under shared clergy arrangements.

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** **Matthew the Evangelist** — apostle, former tax collector ("publican"), traditional author of the first Gospel, with a feast day on **21 September** — is a common Anglican choice for late-nineteenth-century mission foundations.

**Trelawny's notable people.** Trelawny is the birthplace or home of Hugh Lawson Shearer (Jamaica's third Prime Minister), Rev. William Knibb, Rex Nettleford, Usain Bolt, and Veronica Campbell-Brown.
