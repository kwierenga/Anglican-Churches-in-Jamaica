# St. James', Hayes
**Clarendon** · Church · active

St. James' Anglican Church serves **Hayes**, a community in southern Clarendon near the coast and the area's bauxite mining and industrial processing operations. Hayes is near the **Jamalco** alumina processing plant — one of the major industrial facilities on the south coast of Clarendon. The church has served the Hayes community through the industrial transformation of southern Clarendon.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1664).** Clarendon parish was created in **1664**, nine years after the English seizure of Jamaica from Spain (**1655**), and named for **Sir Edward Hyde, 1st Earl of Clarendon** (1609–1674), Lord High Chancellor of England under Charles II. The original parish church was the **Church of the White Cross** at "The Cross" — Jamaica's third Anglican church. **St. Paul's Chapelton** was built as a chapel of ease in **1666** and became the parish church when the Cross Church was destroyed in **1815**.

**Vere parish and the southern plain (1671–1867).** **Vere** was carved out as a separate sugar-plain parish in **1671**, taking in the southern coastal plain — including the Hayes area. **St. Peter's Alley** (1671) was Vere's parish church. Hayes lay within the southern coastal plain that defined Vere's economy of sugar estates worked by enslaved Africans. Vere was reabsorbed into Clarendon in **1867**, and **Law 20 of 1887** designated **May Pen** as chief town.

**Slavery and the Vere sugar economy.** The sugar estates of the Vere plain depended on the labour of thousands of enslaved Africans. The Anglican Church served as the established church of this system — its vestry composed of the planter class, its clergy funded by the colonial government. One of Jamaica's earliest slave rebellions erupted at **Suttons Plantation in 1690**: some 500 enslaved people escaped to form the Maroon communities eventually led by **Cudjoe** into the Cockpit Country.

**Sam Sharpe, Emancipation, disestablishment.** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**, a vigilante alliance that sought to "wipe the non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of St. James' Hayes.** Hayes sits in the southern coastal plain of Clarendon, in an area that has been significantly shaped by the post-1957 bauxite and alumina industry. The Anglican Diocese established St. James' to serve the Hayes community within the Vere/southern Clarendon parochial framework. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**, and St. James' belongs to that broader expansion. The church is recorded in the Jamaican Ancestral Records project's Clarendon index, and a National Library of Jamaica digital photograph captioned "The Anglican Church, Hayes, Clarendon" preserves an earlier view of the building.

**Twentieth century: bauxite and Independence.** Chapelton lost its status as parish capital in **1938**, with administrative functions transferring to **May Pen**. **Alcoa** began bauxite operations in Clarendon in **1959**, with the first shipment leaving in 1963; the **Jamalco** alumina refinery at Halse Hall opened in **1972** — making Hayes one of the communities closest to the industry that has defined the south-coast Clarendon economy for the last half-century. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. James' an active congregation in this transforming landscape.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of St. James' Hayes after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. James' is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural Clarendon in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. Set in the southern coastal plain of Clarendon. The lowland coastal position creates direct exposure to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
Hayes has been served within the Clarendon deanery under shared clergy arrangements.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to **St. James the Greater** — apostle, brother of John the Evangelist, and patron saint of pilgrims (associated with the great pilgrimage route to Santiago de Compostela) — has a feast day on **25 July**. Hayes is one of the communities closest to the **Jamalco** alumina processing facility on the south coast of Clarendon. The bauxite and alumina industry has been a defining feature of the Clarendon south-coast landscape for several decades, and the communities here reflect the mix of agricultural and industrial employment that characterises this part of Jamaica.
