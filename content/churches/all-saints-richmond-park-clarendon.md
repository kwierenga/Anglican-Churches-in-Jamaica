# All Saints', Richmond Park
**Clarendon** · Church · active

All Saints' Anglican Church at Richmond Park is a mission station of the **St. Gabriel's** cure in May Pen, serving a residential district on the western fringe of the parish capital. The church is administered from the cure rectory at 10 Church Street, May Pen P.O.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1664).** Clarendon parish was created in **1664**, nine years after the English seizure of Jamaica from Spain (**1655**), and named for **Sir Edward Hyde, 1st Earl of Clarendon** (1609–1674), Lord High Chancellor of England under Charles II. The original parish church was the **Church of the White Cross** at "The Cross" — Jamaica's third Anglican church. **St. Paul's Chapelton** was built as a chapel of ease in **1666** and became the parish church when the Cross Church was destroyed in **1815**. **Vere** was carved out as a separate sugar-plain parish in **1673**; Vere was reabsorbed into Clarendon in **1867**, and **Law 20 of 1867** designated **May Pen** as chief town.

**Slavery, Sam Sharpe, and Emancipation.** Clarendon was one of Jamaica's great sugar parishes. The fertile Rio Minho plain held estates bearing the names of their English founders — **Suttons, Pennants, Ballards** — and the wealth of the parish was built on the labour of enslaved Africans. One of Jamaica's earliest slave rebellions erupted at **Suttons Plantation in 1690**, sending 500 escaped people into the Cockpit Country to form Maroon communities eventually led by **Cudjoe**. During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union** — a vigilante alliance that sought to "wipe the non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth." Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of the Richmond Park mission.** Richmond Park lies in the May Pen vicinity in the centre of Clarendon parish, with a name that — like many Jamaican communities — preserves the memory of a colonial estate with an English place-name. The Anglican Diocese established All Saints' as a mission station of **St. Gabriel's, May Pen** to serve the residential growth of the area around the parish capital. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**, and the Richmond Park mission belongs to that broader expansion. The Jamaican Ancestral Records project has indexed inscribed memorials in the All Saints' Richmond Park churchyard.

**Twentieth century: May Pen, bauxite, Independence.** Chapelton lost its status as parish capital in **1938**, with administrative functions transferring to **May Pen** — placing Richmond Park in the residential and commercial fringe of the new capital. **Alcoa** began bauxite operations in Clarendon in **1959**; the **Jamalco** alumina refinery at Halse Hall opened in **1972**. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found All Saints' a settled May Pen mission.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of All Saints' Richmond Park after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
All Saints' is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural Clarendon in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries: a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal storm surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
All Saints' Richmond Park has been served as a mission of the St. Gabriel's cure (May Pen). The cure rectory at 10 Church Street, May Pen P.O., is the contact address for both the cure church and its missions.

## Notable Facts
The All Saints' dedication — commemorating the full company of saints on **1 November** — is consistent with the late-nineteenth-century pattern of dedicating new mission churches to general categories of holiness rather than to single named patrons.
