# Peckham (Anglican Mission)
**Clarendon** · Church · active

The Anglican mission at Peckham serves a small community in the upland interior of Clarendon. The chapel is administered as a mission of the St. Bartholomew's, Frankfield cure, alongside Holy Trinity Park Hall, St. Luke's Sanguinetti, St. Matthew's Aenon Town, and St. George's Red Hills. The cure rectory is at Frankfield P.O., Clarendon (telephone 904-4565); the cure was recently listed as **VACANT**.

## History
Peckham is one of the small upland farming districts of central Clarendon, in the broken hill country north of the parish capital at May Pen. The community's name is one of the Jamaican place names transferred from London — Peckham being a suburb in south-east London — likely given by a colonial-era estate proprietor with London connections, following the wider pattern of English place-name transfer that left Jamaica with communities named Manchester, Westminster, Kingston, Sligoville and many others.

The diocesan directory lists the Peckham chapel by community name rather than by a saint dedication; the Diocese of Jamaica has long included a small number of mission stations recorded in this way, where the dedication may be informal, lost to documentary record, or simply known by the place. The chapel was established as part of the comprehensive rural mission expansion that the Diocese carried out from the late nineteenth century onward, extending Anglican ministry to the small farming communities of the Clarendon interior.

## Architecture
The Peckham chapel is a small rural masonry building of the kind erected for mission stations across the Clarendon interior in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries. The hill-country position offers some shelter from coastal storm surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
Peckham is served as a mission of the St. Bartholomew's, Frankfield cure within the Mandeville Region's Clarendon Deanery. The cure is currently vacant; pastoral oversight has historically rotated among neighbouring Clarendon clergy during vacancies.
