# Peckham (Anglican Mission)
**Clarendon** · Church · active

The Anglican mission at **Peckham** serves a small community in the upland interior of Clarendon. The chapel is administered as a mission of the **St. Bartholomew's, Frankfield** cure, alongside Holy Trinity Park Hall, St. Luke's Sanguinetti, St. Matthew's Aenon Town, and St. George's Red Hills. The cure rectory is at Frankfield P.O., Clarendon (telephone 904-4565); the cure was recently listed as **VACANT**.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1664).** Clarendon parish was created in **1664**, nine years after the English seizure of Jamaica from Spain (**1655**), and named for **Sir Edward Hyde, 1st Earl of Clarendon** (1609–1674), Lord High Chancellor of England under Charles II. The original parish church was the **Church of the White Cross** at "The Cross" — Jamaica's third Anglican church. **St. Paul's Chapelton** was built as a chapel of ease in **1666** in the upland interior, and became the parish church when the Cross Church was destroyed in **1815**. **Vere** was carved out as a separate sugar-plain parish in **1671** and was reabsorbed into Clarendon in **1867**; **Law 20 of 1887** designated **May Pen** as chief town.

**Slavery, Sam Sharpe, and Emancipation.** Clarendon was one of Jamaica's great sugar parishes — the fertile Rio Minho plain held the great estates of the Suttons, Pennants, Ballards, and Dawkins. One of Jamaica's earliest slave rebellions erupted at **Suttons Plantation in 1690**: some 500 enslaved people escaped to form the Maroon communities eventually led by **Cudjoe** into the Cockpit Country. During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**, a vigilante alliance that sought to "wipe the non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of the Peckham mission.** Peckham is one of the small upland farming districts of central Clarendon, in the broken hill country north of the parish capital at May Pen. The community's name is one of the Jamaican place names transferred from London — **Peckham** being a suburb in south-east London — likely given by a colonial-era estate proprietor with London connections, following the wider pattern of English place-name transfer that left Jamaica with communities named Manchester, Westminster, Kingston, Sligoville, and many others. The diocesan directory lists the Peckham chapel by community name rather than by a saint dedication; the Diocese of Jamaica has long included a small number of mission stations recorded in this way, where the dedication may be informal, lost to documentary record, or simply known by the place. The chapel was established as part of the comprehensive rural mission expansion that the Diocese carried out from the late nineteenth century onward — Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**, attaching new chapels like Peckham to existing cures such as Frankfield.

**Twentieth century: bauxite, May Pen, Independence.** Chapelton lost its status as parish capital in **1938**, with administrative functions transferring to **May Pen**. **Alcoa** began bauxite operations in Clarendon in **1959**; the **Jamalco** alumina refinery at Halse Hall opened in **1972**. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found the Peckham chapel a settled mission of the Frankfield cure.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of the Peckham chapel after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
The Peckham chapel is a small rural masonry building of the kind erected for mission stations across the Clarendon interior in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries. The hill-country position offers some shelter from coastal storm surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
Peckham is served as a mission of the St. Bartholomew's, Frankfield cure within the Mandeville Region's Clarendon Deanery. The cure is currently vacant; pastoral oversight has historically rotated among neighbouring Clarendon clergy during vacancies.
