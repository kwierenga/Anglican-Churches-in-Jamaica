# St. Mark's Church, Williamsfield
**Manchester** · Church · active

St. Mark's Anglican Church serves Williamsfield, a community in southern Manchester near the Clarendon border. Williamsfield is the home of the **High Mountain Coffee Factory** and the **Pioneer Chocolate Factory** — two products of the Manchester highlands that have received international recognition (NLJ).

## History
**Founding of the parish (1814).** Manchester was created by **Act 55 George III. c. 23** of **1814** in response to a petition of 29 November 1814 by residents of Mile Gully, May Day, and Carpenter's Mountain — who complained that "the closest public building was 40 miles away." The Act came into effect on **13 December 1814**, taking the "hill country" of eastern St. Elizabeth and the western portions of Vere and Clarendon. The new parish was named after **William Montagu, 5th Duke of Manchester** (Governor of Jamaica 1808–1827); the parish capital, **Mandeville**, was named after the Duke's son, **Viscount Mandeville**, on 27 August 1816, on land purchased from Robert Crawford's Caledonia Estate at £10/acre.

**Slavery, the parish church, and the Sam Sharpe Rebellion (1831).** Manchester's hill-country economy was built on coffee from the eighteenth century, supplemented by sugar in lower zones, both worked by enslaved African labour. **St. Mark's Anglican Church, Mandeville** — the parish church of Manchester (and the senior namesake of this Williamsfield mission) — was completed and consecrated in **1820**; its organ loft was used as a jail during the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**. Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church.

**Emancipation, 1865, disestablishment.** Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Maidstone** in Manchester (1840) was among Jamaica's earliest Moravian-led free villages. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of St. Mark's Williamsfield: Nuttall's expansion.** Bishop Enos Nuttall (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. St. Mark's Williamsfield was founded as a mission station within the orbit of the Mandeville parish church.

**Twentieth century: bauxite, coffee, chocolate, Independence.** Williamsfield became home to the **Pioneer Chocolate Factory** and the **High Mountain Coffee Factory** — both producing for international markets and reflecting Manchester's twentieth-century specialty agriculture. The **bauxite industry**, with **Alcan** establishing alumina operations from **1957** and **Kaiser** later joining, employed over 3,000 by the 1960s and led Mandeville to be described as "a town which grew on bauxite." The **Kendal train tragedy** of **1 September 1957** at the Manchester community of Kendal — Jamaica's worst rail accident, with almost 200 dead — marked the parish's twentieth-century history. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Mark's a settled mission within the Manchester network.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of St. Mark's Williamsfield after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. Mark's is a masonry church of modest proportions, representative of Anglican rural church building in Manchester.

## Clergy
Williamsfield has been served within the Manchester deanery under shared clergy arrangements with the Mandeville parish church.

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** **Mark the Evangelist** — author of the second Gospel, traditionally associated with Alexandria, with a feast day on **25 April** — is the patron saint of the Manchester Parish Church (St. Mark's Mandeville, 1820), of which Williamsfield is a satellite.
