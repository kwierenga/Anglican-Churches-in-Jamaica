# St. John's OPC, Guanaboa Vale
**St. Catherine** · Church · active

St. John's (OPC) Anglican Church serves Guanaboa Vale, a community in the hill country of St. Catherine parish. The 'OPC' designation likely indicates 'Old Parish Church' or a similar heritage designation, distinguishing this church from other Anglican worship points in the area. Guanaboa Vale is a small farming community in the interior hills of the parish, and the church has served its community for several generations.

## History
Guanaboa Vale takes its name from the Taino word for soursop, one of the Tainos' favourite foods. After the British took Jamaica in 1655, St. John was made one of the first parishes the new colonial government established; St. John's parish church at Guanaboa Vale was built in 1699, making it among the earliest Anglican parish churches on the island. When Governor Sir John Peter Grant reduced Jamaica's twenty-two parishes to fourteen in 1866, St. John was merged into St. Catherine, and Guanaboa Vale's parish-church status passed to history. By the late twentieth century the village had largely lost its administrative weight: in 2024 the *Jamaica Gleaner* reported the church, the elementary school and other buildings of the township as in ruins or dilapidated apart from the police station. The Manley/Bustamante family — through their grandmother Elsie Clarke Shearer — has documented links to the historic St. John (the St. John district of St. Catherine).

The "OPC" designation in our slug stands for **Old Parish Church**, recording the building's status as the surviving parish-church fabric of the historic St. John parish.

## Architecture
St. John's OPC is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural St. Catherine in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. Set in the hilly interior of St. Catherine. The elevated terrain channels wind during storm passages and makes good drainage essential to the building's longevity.

## Clergy
Guanaboa Vale has been served within the St. Catherine deanery under shared clergy arrangements. The 'Old Parish Church' character suggests it may have served as the hub of a wider circuit in the past.

## Notable Facts
The Guanaboa Vale district of St. Catherine is part of the historic interior farming landscape of the parish. The valley communities between Spanish Town and Linstead developed their own agricultural economies after Emancipation, and the Anglican church network in this area reflects the Diocese's comprehensive coverage of the post-Emancipation countryside. A second St. John's is also recorded at Guanoboa Vale (alternate spelling) in the dataset, suggesting multiple Anglican worship points in this district.
