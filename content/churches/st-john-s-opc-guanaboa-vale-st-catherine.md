# St. John's OPC, Guanaboa Vale
**St. Catherine** · Church · active

St. John's (OPC) Anglican Church serves Guanaboa Vale, a community in the hill country of St. Catherine parish. The 'OPC' designation likely indicates 'Old Parish Church' or a similar heritage designation, distinguishing this church from other Anglican worship points in the area. Guanaboa Vale is a small farming community in the interior hills of the parish, and the church has served its community for several generations.

## History
**Spanish Villa de la Vega (1534) and English conquest (1655).** **Guanaboa Vale** takes its name from the **Taíno** word for soursop, one of the Tainos' favourite foods. The Spanish founded **Villa de la Vega** in 1534 — Jamaica's capital from 1538 until **1872**. After the British took Jamaica in **1655** (Penn and Venables's expedition), **St. John was made one of the first parishes the new colonial government established**; **St. John's parish church at Guanaboa Vale was built in 1699**, making it among the earliest Anglican parish churches on the island.

**Slavery and the planter church.** Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds. **The Diocese of Jamaica** was established by Letters Patent on **24 July 1824**; **Christopher Lipscomb** was consecrated first Bishop in 1824.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838), Sligoville.** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Sligoville** in St. Catherine became Jamaica's **first free village** in 1838, founded with land provided by **Howe Peter Browne, 2nd Marquess of Sligo**, then Governor of Jamaica. Bishop Lipscomb (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall, parish merger (1866/1867).** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. When Governor Sir John Peter Grant reduced Jamaica's twenty-two parishes to fourteen in **1866** (formalised under **Law 20 of 1867**), **St. John was merged into St. Catherine**, and Guanaboa Vale's parish-church status passed to history. By the late twentieth century the village had largely lost its administrative weight: in 2024 the *Jamaica Gleaner* reported the church, the elementary school and other buildings of the township as in ruins or dilapidated apart from the police station. The Manley/Bustamante family — through their grandmother Elsie Clarke Shearer — has documented links to the historic St. John (the St. John district of St. Catherine).

The "OPC" designation in our slug stands for **Old Parish Church**, recording the building's status as the surviving parish-church fabric of the historic St. John parish. Capital moved from Spanish Town to **Kingston in 1872**. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found Guanaboa Vale a quieter rural village. **Hurricane Melissa** made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history; Hurricane Melissa brought wind and rain to interior St. Catherine.

## Architecture
St. John's OPC is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural St. Catherine in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. Set in the hilly interior of St. Catherine. The elevated terrain channels wind during storm passages and makes good drainage essential to the building's longevity.

## Clergy
Guanaboa Vale has been served within the St. Catherine deanery under shared clergy arrangements. The 'Old Parish Church' character suggests it may have served as the hub of a wider circuit in the past.

## Notable Facts
The Guanaboa Vale district of St. Catherine is part of the historic interior farming landscape of the parish. The valley communities between Spanish Town and Linstead developed their own agricultural economies after Emancipation, and the Anglican church network in this area reflects the Diocese's comprehensive coverage of the post-Emancipation countryside. A second St. John's is also recorded at Guanoboa Vale (alternate spelling) in the dataset, suggesting multiple Anglican worship points in this district.
