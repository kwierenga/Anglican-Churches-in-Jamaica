# St. Leonard's, Blue Hole
**St. James** · Church · active

St. Leonard's Anglican Church is a small country church in the rural district of Blue Hole, St. James, situated in the hills inland from Montego Bay. The church serves a community that grew from the historic **Blue Hole Estate**, a sugar plantation heavily involved in the slavery-era economy and the 1831–32 Sam Sharpe Rebellion.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1664).** St. James was one of the second batch of Jamaican parishes formed after the English conquest, established around **1664** by Sir Thomas Modyford and named after **James, Duke of York** (later King James II). At its formation it was much larger than today, including the territory of present-day Trelawny and Hanover. For many years the north side of the island was sparsely settled: in **1673** only **146 persons** resided in the entire parish.

**Eighteenth-century development.** Montego Bay was made a free port in **1738**; Charles Square was laid out in **1755**. Cudjoe, Maroon leader of western Jamaica, signed a peace treaty with the British in **1739**. The Second Maroon War of **1789** saw 300 Maroons fight 1,500 English soldiers. By the 1780s Montego Bay was, next to Kingston, "the most flourishing town in the island" (NLJ).

**The Blue Hole Estate and slavery.** The Blue Hole district takes its name from the eighteenth-century Blue Hole Estate, a sugar plantation owned by **Rains B. Waite** and worked by **over two hundred enslaved Africans**. The UCL *Legacies of British Slavery* database documents the estate's compensation claim filed at the abolition of slavery in 1834. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**The Sam Sharpe Rebellion (December 1831).** The **Christmas Rebellion** of 1831, led by the Baptist deacon **Samuel Sharpe** at the Kensington Estate in St. James on the night of **27 December 1831**, was one of the largest slave rebellions in the British Caribbean — some 60,000 enslaved people across St. James, Trelawny, Hanover, and Westmoreland were involved. The Blue Hole Estate was a site of conflict during the rebellion: rebels set fire to its cane fields and works in the fight for freedom. Sharpe was hanged in **Montego Bay marketplace** (now **Sam Sharpe Square**) on **23 May 1832**, and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation and post-1838 free settlement.** Within two years the British Parliament passed the Slavery Abolition Act (1833); Emancipation came in 1834, and full freedom followed at the end of the apprenticeship period on **1 August 1838**. After Emancipation, the Blue Hole Estate's plantation lands gradually fragmented into the small farms and settlements that characterise the area today. St. Leonard's Anglican Church developed within this rural landscape, establishing itself as a local worship centre for a community shaped by the history of enslavement, resistance, and post-Emancipation settlement.

**1865 to 1900: post-disestablishment Diocese.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865, Crown Colony rule replaced the planter Assembly (1866); the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica. Bishop Enos Nuttall's tenure (1880–1916) saw the Diocese grow from 26 rural mission stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. The 1900 Handbook of Jamaica records the St. James cures as Montego Bay (J. W. Austin), Holy Trinity (G. B. Hall), Marley (H. F. R. Sharpe), and Montpelier & 5 Mission Stations (C. G. McGregory) — St. Leonard's Blue Hole was most likely one of the unnamed mission stations of the Montpelier or Marley cure.

**Twentieth century: hurricanes, Independence.** Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Leonard's a settled rural mission. Major hurricanes — Gilbert (1988), Ivan (2004), Melissa (October 2025) — have repeatedly struck the parish.

## Architecture
No detailed architectural survey or description of St. Leonard's is available in public heritage records. Based on the typical design of similar rural Anglican churches in St. James, the building is most likely a simple, rectangular masonry chapel with a pitched zinc or tile roof, plain or louvred windows, and a small surrounding yard. The church's place in the settled community is reflected in national shelter listings, which note nearby buildings in Blue Hole and Montpelier serving as emergency shelters during major storm events.

## Clergy
Within the diocesan structure, St. Leonard's is part of the Montego Bay Region. Diocesan records list the church as an active congregation, with **Zelphya (or Zelpha) McLaren** serving as a supplementary minister or senior lay leader. Its status is confirmed by its inclusion in financial reports from the Jamaica Church Missionary Society. Week-to-week ministry is sustained by a combination of visiting clergy and dedicated lay leadership.

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** **St. Leonard of Noblac** (c. 496 – c. 559) was a sixth-century Frankish noble who became a hermit, founder of the Benedictine monastery at Noblac near Limoges, and traditional patron of prisoners and captives. His feast day is **6 November**. The dedication is a notably distinctive Anglican choice — uncommon in Jamaica — and carries an apt resonance for a church on the site of a former slave plantation: Leonard, the patron of those held in bondage, presiding over a community whose ancestors were freed in 1838.

**Hurricane Melissa (28 October 2025)** made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Across the Diocese, some **50 church buildings were destroyed or significantly damaged**. The specific status of St. Leonard's Blue Hole after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.
