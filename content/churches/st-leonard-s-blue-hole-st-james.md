# St. Leonard's, Blue Hole
**St. James** · Church · active

St. Leonard’s Anglican Church is a small country church in the rural district of Blue Hole, St. James, situated in the hills inland from Montego Bay. The church is part of the Montego Bay Region of the Anglican Diocese and serves a community that grew from the historic Blue Hole Estate, a sugar plantation heavily involved in the slavery-era economy and the 1831-32 Sam Sharpe Rebellion. Today, it provides a spiritual centre for the farming families and residents of this former plantation landscape.

## History
St. Leonard’s is located in the Blue Hole district, which originated from the 18th-century Blue Hole Estate, a sugar plantation owned by Rains B. Waite and worked by over two hundred enslaved Africans. The estate was a site of conflict during the 1831-32 Sam Sharpe Rebellion, when rebels set fire to its cane fields and works in the fight for freedom. After Emancipation, these plantation lands gradually fragmented into the small farms and settlements that characterize the area today. St. Leonard’s Anglican Church developed within this rural landscape, establishing itself as a local worship centre for a community shaped by this history of enslavement, resistance, and post-Emancipation settlement.

## Clergy
Within the diocesan structure, St. Leonard’s is part of the Montego Bay Region. Diocesan records list the church as an active congregation, with Zelphya (or Zelpha) McLaren serving as a supplementary minister or senior lay leader. Its status is confirmed by its inclusion in financial reports from the Jamaica Church Missionary Society. As with many small rural churches, its week-to-week operations—including Sunday worship, funerals, and community activities—are likely sustained by a combination of visiting clergy and dedicated lay leadership from churchwardens, readers, and Mothers’ Union members.

## Notable Facts
The Blue Hole Estate is well-documented in historical records as a classic St. James sugar plantation, firmly placing the district within Jamaica's slavery-era economy. Its historical significance is heightened by its involvement in the Sam Sharpe Rebellion of 1831-32, directly linking the church's location to a pivotal event in the struggle for emancipation. Today, the area functions as a cluster of small farming and residential communities, with St. Leonard's serving as one of its key institutions alongside local schools.
