# St. Peter's, Old Works
**St. Catherine** · Church · active

St. Peter's Anglican Church serves **Old Works**, a small farming district in the interior hill country of St. Catherine. The "Old Works" name preserves the memory of a colonial sugar-estate processing facility — a "**works**" in Jamaican historical terminology referring to the boiling house and machinery of a sugar estate.

## History
**Spanish Villa de la Vega (1534) and English conquest (1655).** The Spanish founded **Villa de la Vega** in 1534 — Jamaica's capital until **1872**. Penn and Venables's English expedition captured Jamaica in May **1655**.

**Founding parishes.** Under English rule from 1655, **St. Catherine** was one of Jamaica's original parishes. The neighbouring early parishes of **St. John, St. Dorothy, and St. Thomas-in-the-Vale** were merged into expanded St. Catherine under **Law 20 of 1867**.

**Slavery, sugar works, and the planter church.** Old Works is a memory of the plantation era when sugar estates had their "works" — the processing facilities where cane juice was boiled into sugar by enslaved labour. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds. **The Diocese of Jamaica** was established by Letters Patent on **24 July 1824**; **Christopher Lipscomb** was consecrated first Bishop in 1824.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838), Sligoville.** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Sligoville** in St. Catherine became Jamaica's **first free village** in 1838, founded with land provided by **Howe Peter Browne, 2nd Marquess of Sligo**, then Governor of Jamaica. Bishop Lipscomb (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. Capital moved from Spanish Town to **Kingston in 1872**.

**Foundation of St. Peter's.** The Anglican Diocese established St. Peter's to serve this community after Emancipation, when the estate workers settled the surrounding land as smallholders. The "Old Works" name suggests a location associated with a now-defunct or long-transformed estate processing site.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Peter's a settled rural mission.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Hurricane Melissa brought wind and rain to interior St. Catherine.

## Architecture
St. Peter's is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural St. Catherine in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
Old Works has been served within the **St. Catherine deanery** under shared clergy arrangements.

## Notable Facts
The "**Works**" designation in Jamaican place names consistently refers to the machinery and processing facilities of sugar estates, distinguishing the industrial heart of a plantation from its cultivation areas. "**Old Works**" preserves an industrial memory in the community name. The dedication to **St. Peter the apostle** (feast day **29 June** with St. Paul) — chief of the twelve, traditionally the first bishop of Rome — is one of the most common Anglican dedications.
