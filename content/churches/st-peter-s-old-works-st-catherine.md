# St. Peter's, Old Works
**St. Catherine** · Church · active

St. Peter's Anglican Church serves Old Works, a community in the interior of St. Catherine parish. Old Works is a small farming district, and the church has provided Anglican ministry here for several generations. The 'Old Works' name likely preserves the memory of a colonial estate manufacturing facility — a 'works' in Jamaican historical terminology typically referring to the boiling house and machinery of a sugar estate.

## History
Old Works sits in the interior of St. Catherine, in the hill country of the parish. The name 'Old Works' is a memory of the plantation era when sugar estates had their 'works' — the processing facilities where cane juice was boiled into sugar. The Anglican Diocese established St. Peter's to serve this community after Emancipation, when the estate workers settled the surrounding land as smallholders.

## Architecture
St. Peter's is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural St. Catherine in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. Set in the rural interior of St. Catherine. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
Old Works has been served within the St. Catherine deanery under shared clergy arrangements.

## Notable Facts
The 'Works' designation in Jamaican place names consistently refers to the machinery and processing facilities of sugar estates, distinguishing the industrial heart of a plantation from its cultivation areas. 'Old Works' suggests a location associated with a now-defunct or long-transformed estate processing site, preserving an industrial memory in the community name.
