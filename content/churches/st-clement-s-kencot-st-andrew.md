# St. Clement's, Kencot
**St. Andrew** · Church · active

St. Clement's Anglican Church serves the Kencot community in St. Andrew, a residential area on the western edge of the parish adjoining Kingston. Kencot is a dense urban neighbourhood in the Kingston metropolitan area, and St. Clement's has served its community for several generations. The dedication to St. Clement of Rome, the third Bishop of Rome and an early church father, gives this urban parish a patron associated with the earliest generation of Christian leadership.

## History
Kencot developed as a residential neighbourhood in the urban expansion of Kingston and St. Andrew through the 20th century. The Anglican Diocese established St. Clement's to serve this growing community as the metropolitan area expanded westward. The church sits in a community with mixed residential and commercial character, in the urban landscape connecting Half-Way-Tree to the Kingston waterfront.

## Architecture
St. Clement's is an urban church in the Kencot neighbourhood, in the densely settled residential and commercial zone of western St. Andrew. Note that a second Anglican entry — St. Clement's Mission — is also recorded in Kencot nearby, suggesting the community has both a main church and a mission chapel.

## Clergy
Kencot has been served within the St. Andrew deanery. The urban density of the area supports a more substantial congregation than rural mission chapels, and the church has maintained an active pastoral presence in the community.

## Notable Facts
Kencot is notable in Jamaican cultural history as one of the communities associated with the development of Kingston's urban popular culture. The neighbourhood is within the broader west Kingston zone that has produced significant contributions to Jamaican music, street culture, and community life. The church's presence in this neighbourhood reflects the Anglican Diocese's commitment to urban ministry alongside its rural mission network.
