# St. Clement's Mission, Kencot
**St. Andrew** · Mission · active

St. Clement's Mission provides a third Anglican worship point in the Kencot area of St. Andrew, alongside St. Clement's and St. Barnabas' also recorded in this community. The Mission status distinguishes it from the main St. Clement's church, suggesting it serves as a chapel-of-ease or mission chapel within the broader Kencot Anglican community. The three Anglican entries in Kencot reflect the neighbourhood's density and the Diocese's layered pastoral coverage of the area.

## History
The Kencot community in western St. Andrew supports three Anglican worship points, reflecting both the population density of this urban neighbourhood and the Diocese's practice of establishing mission chapels to serve specific parts of larger communities. St. Clement's Mission operates as part of the St. Clement's parish network.

## Architecture
St. Clement's Mission is a masonry church of modest proportions, representative of Anglican rural church building in St. Andrew. The structure balances practicality with the dignity appropriate to a place of worship, featuring a straightforward nave with local stone or concrete block construction typical of Jamaica's interior church-building tradition. Set in the rural interior of St. Andrew. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
The Kencot mission chapel has been served by the clergy of St. Clement's, Kencot, as part of the same parish circuit.

## Notable Facts
The three Anglican entries for Kencot — St. Clement's, St. Barnabas', and St. Clement's Mission — illustrate the Diocese's approach to dense urban communities, where multiple worship points serve different streets and sub-communities within what is administratively a single neighbourhood. This pattern of layered urban coverage is found across the Kingston/St. Andrew metropolitan area.
