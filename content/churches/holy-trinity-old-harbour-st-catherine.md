# Holy Trinity, Old Harbour
**St. Catherine** · Church · inactive

The Church of the Holy Trinity is one of two Anglican congregations in Old Harbour, the historic coastal town of southern St. Catherine. The historic seat of the parish is St. Dorothy's, dating back to a 1681 land donation; Holy Trinity is the second Anglican worship point in the town and serves as a cure church in its own right, with the Diocese of Jamaica's directory listing the rectory at P.O. Box 370, Old Harbour P.O. The dedication to the Holy Trinity is among the most traditional Anglican dedications and is shared with the diocese's cathedral on North Street, Kingston.

## History
Old Harbour — known to seventeenth-century mariners as a sheltered anchorage on Jamaica's south coast — was an important pre-Kingston port and one of the early settled towns of St. Catherine after the English took the island in 1655. As the town grew, Anglican ministry came to be shared between St. Dorothy's (the colonial-era parish church) and Holy Trinity, which the church's own public communications describe as having been a community of worship "for countless souls for over 200 years." The two churches together have provided comprehensive Anglican coverage of the town and its hinterland.

## Architecture
Holy Trinity stands in the centre of Old Harbour town, with the architectural footprint of a town parish-style church rather than a small mission chapel. Its low-lying coastal position makes the church more exposed than the interior St. Catherine chapels to storm surge and salt air during major hurricanes; robust masonry has been required to maintain the building.

## Clergy
Holy Trinity is grouped within the Mandeville Region of the Diocese, with the cure rectory at P.O. Box 370, Old Harbour P.O. (telephone 745-2464). Pastoral responsibility has historically been shared with St. Dorothy's for the wider Old Harbour Anglican community.
