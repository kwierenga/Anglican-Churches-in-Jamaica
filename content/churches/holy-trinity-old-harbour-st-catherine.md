# Holy Trinity, Old Harbour
**St. Catherine** · Church · inactive

The Church of the Holy Trinity is one of two Anglican congregations in **Old Harbour**, the historic coastal town of southern St. Catherine. The historic seat of the parish is **St. Dorothy's**, dating back to a 1681 land donation; Holy Trinity is the second Anglican worship point in the town and serves as a cure church in its own right.

## History
**Spanish Villa de la Vega (1534) and English conquest (1655).** The Spanish founded **Villa de la Vega** in 1534 — Jamaica's capital until **1872**. Penn and Venables's English expedition captured Jamaica in May **1655**. Old Harbour — known to seventeenth-century mariners as a sheltered anchorage on Jamaica's south coast — was an important pre-Kingston port and one of the early settled towns of St. Catherine after the English took the island.

**Founding parishes — and the old St. Dorothy.** Under English rule from 1655, **St. Catherine** was one of Jamaica's original parishes. **Old Harbour** sat in the old parish of **St. Dorothy**, where Colonel Thomas Fuller and his wife Catherine donated the land for **St. Dorothy's Church** in **1681**. The old parishes of **St. John, St. Dorothy, and St. Thomas-in-the-Vale** were merged into expanded St. Catherine under **Law 20 of 1867** (Governor Sir John Peter Grant's reduction from 22 to 14 parishes).

**Slavery and the planter church.** Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds. **The Diocese of Jamaica** was established by Letters Patent on **24 July 1824**; **Christopher Lipscomb** was consecrated first Bishop in 1824.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838), Sligoville.** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Sligoville** in St. Catherine became Jamaica's **first free village** in 1838, founded with land provided by **Howe Peter Browne, 2nd Marquess of Sligo**, then Governor of Jamaica. Bishop Lipscomb (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. Capital moved from Spanish Town to **Kingston in 1872**.

**Foundation of Holy Trinity.** As the town grew, Anglican ministry came to be shared between St. Dorothy's (the colonial-era parish church) and Holy Trinity, which the church's own public communications describe as having been a community of worship "for countless souls for over 200 years." The two churches together have provided comprehensive Anglican coverage of the town and its hinterland.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found Holy Trinity an active town parish.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The low-lying coastal position of Old Harbour made it vulnerable to storm surge.

## Architecture
Holy Trinity stands in the centre of Old Harbour town, with the architectural footprint of a town parish-style church rather than a small mission chapel. Its low-lying coastal position makes the church more exposed than the interior St. Catherine chapels to storm surge and salt air during major hurricanes; robust masonry has been required to maintain the building.

## Clergy
Holy Trinity is grouped within the **Mandeville Region** of the Diocese, with the cure rectory at P.O. Box 370, Old Harbour P.O. (telephone 745-2464). Pastoral responsibility has historically been shared with **St. Dorothy's** for the wider Old Harbour Anglican community.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to the **Holy Trinity** — Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, celebrated on Trinity Sunday — is shared with the diocese's cathedral on North Street, Kingston, and with Holy Trinity at Linstead and at Westgate, Montego Bay. **Old Harbour** was one of Jamaica's earliest English ports — a sheltered south-coast anchorage that pre-dated the rise of Port Royal and Kingston.
