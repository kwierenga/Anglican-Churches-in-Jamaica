# St. James', Birnamwood
**Portland** · Church · active

St. James' Anglican Church at **Birnamwood** serves a community in the western interior of Portland parish, near the boundary with St. Mary. Birnamwood (also spelled Birnam Wood) is a small farming community in the hill country of western Portland. St. James' was specifically named in **Hurricane Melissa (October 2025)** coverage, with a landslide reported near the church during the storm.

## History
**English rule (1655) and the founding of Portland (1723).** Under English rule from **1655**, the future Portland coast remained largely Maroon territory. **Portland parish was created in 1723** by order of the then Governor, **Henry Bentinck, 1st Duke of Portland** (Governor 1722–1726), and named in his honour. The British built **Fort George** at Port Antonio in 1729.

**The First Maroon War (1728–1739) and Nanny.** The **Windward Maroons** under **Quao** and **Nanny** controlled the Blue Mountains and John Crow Mountains. The **1740 treaty** granted **Nanny a 500-acre land grant** that forms the present site of **Moore Town**. **Nanny of the Maroons** became Jamaica's only female National Hero in October 1975.

**Slavery and the planter church.** Sugar estates dominated the fertile coastal lowlands and river valleys; coffee thrived in the hills. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union** to "wipe non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Christ Church Port Antonio** was completed around **1840** under Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb**.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall, and the banana revolution.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. In **1870**, **Captain Lorenzo Dow Baker** purchased bananas in Port Antonio market; Baker's Boston Fruit Company became **United Fruit Company in 1899**.

**Foundation of St. James'.** Birnamwood sits in the western interior of Portland, in the hill country that transitions towards the St. Mary border. The community's name echoes the **Shakespearean forest of Birnam Wood** (from *Macbeth*) — one of many literary and English landscape names transplanted to Jamaican estate communities during the colonial period. The Anglican Diocese established St. James' as a mission chapel to serve this interior farming community.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** The **1903 hurricane** damaged Christ Church Port Antonio and the banana industry. Tourism grew at Port Antonio from the late 19th century. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. James' a settled rural mission.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. **A landslide was reported in the immediate vicinity of St. James' Birnamwood during the storm** — direct documentation that makes it one of the better-evidenced storm-impact records for a Portland Anglican church in the 2025 season. The incident illustrates the particular landslide risk for hill-country churches in western Portland during major hurricane passages.

## Architecture
St. James' is a masonry chapel in the western interior hills of Portland. The building is set in hilly terrain that is particularly susceptible to landslides during heavy rainfall — a fact confirmed by the landslide reported near the church during Hurricane Melissa. Portland's interior receives some of the highest rainfall in Jamaica, and the steep slopes of western Portland amplify this risk.

## Clergy
Birnamwood has been served within the **Portland deanery** under shared clergy arrangements, connected to the Buff Bay circuit given its western Portland location. Lay leaders sustain the church's regular pastoral life.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to **St. James the Greater** — apostle, brother of John the Evangelist, and patron saint of pilgrims (associated with the great pilgrimage route to Santiago de Compostela), with a feast day on **25 July** — is found across the Anglican world. The community name **Birnamwood** is a literary reference to the prophesied moving forest in Shakespeare's *Macbeth* — Birnam Wood comes to Dunsinane.
