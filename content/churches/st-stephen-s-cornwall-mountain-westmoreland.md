# St. Stephen's, Cornwall Mountain
**Westmoreland** · Church · active

St. Stephen's Anglican Church serves the community of Cornwall Mountain in the upland country of northern Westmoreland, in the rolling agricultural terrain that rises toward the boundary with St. James and Hanover. The placename carries a double colonial echo: **Cornwall** was the historic county of western Jamaica (alongside Middlesex and Surrey, the three counties created in 1758), and the mountain district takes its name from that imposed administrative geography. The church does not appear in the 1891 Handbook of Jamaica's Anglican returns nor in the 1900 Handbook — placing its foundation firmly in the great rural mission expansion of Bishop Enos Nuttall (Bishop of Jamaica 1880–1916), under whose tenure the Diocese grew from 26 rural mission stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1703).** Westmoreland was officially formed in **1703** from the western portion of the older parish of St. Elizabeth — its name reflecting its position at the western part of the island. The parish capital, **Savanna-la-Mar** (Spanish *sabana de la mar*, "the savannah by the sea"), was founded around 1730; before that the chief town of the parish was Queen's Town, now called Cross Path. In **1723**, a further division created the new parish of Hanover from the northern section of Westmoreland.

**Slavery and the sugar economy.** Under English rule, sugar and slavery accelerated in Westmoreland: an inventory taken in **1722** numbered the estates of the parish at **73**, making Westmoreland one of the chief sugar-producing parishes of British Caribbean. The Beckford family — Richard, Peter, and William — were among the most prominent planters; the modern town of **Petersfield** is named in commemoration of Peter Beckford. The parish church (St. George's, Savanna-la-Mar) functioned as both spiritual centre and administrative seat. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds; its parish vestry was composed of the planter class, and its rectors held glebe lands worked by enslaved people.

**The Sam Sharpe Rebellion (December 1831).** The Christmas Rebellion of 1831 — led by the Baptist deacon Samuel Sharpe and ignited at the Kensington Estate in St. James on the night of 27 December 1831 — spread rapidly through the western parishes (St. James, Trelawny, Hanover, and Westmoreland) with some 60,000 enslaved people involved. Suppressed brutally, the rebellion ended with Sharpe's hanging in Montego Bay on 23 May 1832. The Colonial Church Union, formed in the aftermath as a vigilante alliance of Anglican planters and clergy, undertook a campaign of "riotous destruction of chapels at Stewart Town, Lucea, Brown's Town, Sav.-la-Mar, St. Ann's Bay, Fuller's Field, and other centres of Wesleyan and Baptist activity" (J. B. Ellis, *Diocese of Jamaica*, 1913). Sharpe is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation and post-1838 settlement.** Within two years of Sharpe's hanging the British Parliament passed the Slavery Abolition Act (1833); Emancipation came in 1834, and full freedom followed at the end of the apprenticeship period on **1 August 1838**. By 1854 the number of active sugar estates in Westmoreland had collapsed to 34, less than half the 1722 figure. After 1838, freed people settled the upland districts of the parish in search of provision land — communities like Cornwall Mountain, Mt. Airy, Sheffield, and Kentucky belong to that wave of post-Emancipation free settlement.

**1865, 1870, 1891 records and Nuttall's expansion.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865, Crown Colony rule replaced the planter-dominated Assembly (1866); the Anglican Church was disestablished in 1870 and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica. The **1891 Handbook** records the Westmoreland Anglican churches as Trinity (J. S. Vaughan, 515 members), Bluefields and Kings (S. G. Shrimpton, 234 + 385), St. Paul's (B. A. S. MacCalla, 450), Negril, Petersfield (558 members), Darliston (W. Heaver, 632), and New Road (W. Heaver, 206) — Cornwall Mountain is not recorded. The **1900 Handbook** records the cures as Sav.-la-Mar (C. H. Davis & J. J. C. Ormsby), Trinity (S. J. Vaughan), Bluefields & Kings (S. Negus), St. Paul's & Negril (R. L. Reid), Petersfield & Darliston (W. Heaver), and New Road & 5 Mission Stations (E. D. Tinling) — likewise no Cornwall Mountain. St. Stephen's was therefore established after 1900, during the late phase of Nuttall's mission expansion.

**1912 hurricane, 1938 labour riots, Independence.** A November **1912** hurricane brought a tidal wave that nearly wiped out Savanna-la-Mar; the schooner *Latonia* was swept ashore and "sailed for some time on Great Gorges Street." Only Mannings School, the teacher's residence, and the Bank of Nova Scotia were spared. Westmoreland's twentieth century was further marked by the **1938 Frome labour riots** at the Frome sugar factory — riots that contributed directly to the formation of Jamaica's Trade Union Movement and the rise of Sir Alexander Bustamante. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Stephen's a settled rural mission within the New Road circuit's wider hinterland.

## Architecture
St. Stephen's is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for mission stations across the Westmoreland uplands in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries: a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small adjoining churchyard. The interior hill-country position offers some shelter from coastal storm surge, but the western Jamaican parishes have repeatedly been struck by major hurricanes — and Cornwall Mountain's ridge-and-valley terrain creates significant wind exposure during major storm passages.

## Clergy
St. Stephen's Cornwall Mountain is served within the Montego Bay Region's Westmoreland Deanery.

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** Stephen the protomartyr was the first Christian martyr, deacon of the early Jerusalem church, stoned to death for his preaching as recorded in Acts 7. His feast day on **26 December** (the day after Christmas) makes "Boxing Day" a particularly significant day in Stephen-dedicated congregations. The traditional iconographic mark of his martyrdom is the stone.

**Hurricane Melissa (28 October 2025)** made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history — and caused catastrophic damage across the parish. Across the Diocese of Jamaica and the Cayman Islands, some **50 church buildings were destroyed or significantly damaged**. The specific status of St. Stephen's Cornwall Mountain after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.
