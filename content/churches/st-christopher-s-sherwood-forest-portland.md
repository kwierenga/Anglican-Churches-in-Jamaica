# St. Christopher's, Sherwood Forest
**Portland** · Church · active

St. Christopher's Anglican Church serves Sherwood Forest, a community in the interior hill country of Portland parish in the hilly terrain east of Port Antonio. Sherwood Forest is a small agricultural community whose evocative English name, like many Jamaican place names, preserves a colonial estate naming convention. St. Christopher's, dedicated to the patron saint of travellers, has served this interior community for several generations.

## History
Sherwood Forest developed as an interior community of Portland in the hilly country east of Port Antonio, part of the post-Emancipation agricultural landscape of the parish interior. The Anglican Diocese established St. Christopher's as a mission chapel to serve the local farming community, dedicated to the patron of travellers — a fitting choice for a church in the hills where road access was difficult and congregation members travelled on foot or by mule to worship. The church has served the community through the transformations of Portland's interior economy.

## Architecture
St. Christopher's is a small masonry chapel in the interior hill country of Portland. The building is set in the hilly terrain of Sherwood Forest, in a landscape subject to the heavy rainfall of Portland's interior — some of the highest annual totals in Jamaica. The church's elevated position creates wind exposure and landslide risk during major storm events.

## Clergy
Sherwood Forest has been served within the Portland deanery under shared clergy arrangements. Lay leaders sustain the church's regular pastoral life between clerical visits.

## Notable Facts
Sherwood Forest's English name — echoing the legendary home of Robin Hood — is one of many Jamaican place names that transplant English landscape names into the tropical interior. The Portland interior is dense with such names, reflecting the estate-naming conventions of the plantation era. Hurricane Melissa (October 2025) brought heavy rain and wind to the interior hills of Portland; the Sherwood Forest community and congregation were in the affected zone.
