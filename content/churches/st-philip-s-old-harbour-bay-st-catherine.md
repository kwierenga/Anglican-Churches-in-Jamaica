# St. Philip's, Old Harbour Bay
**St. Catherine** · Church · active

St. Philip's Anglican Church serves **Old Harbour Bay**, a fishing village at the waterfront of the Old Harbour area, distinct from Old Harbour town further inland.

## History
**Spanish Villa de la Vega (1534) and English conquest (1655).** The Spanish founded **Villa de la Vega** in 1534 — Jamaica's capital until **1872**. Penn and Venables's English expedition captured Jamaica in May **1655**.

**Founding parishes — and the old St. Dorothy.** Under English rule from 1655, **St. Catherine** was one of Jamaica's original parishes. **Old Harbour Bay** sat in the old parish of **St. Dorothy** (where Colonel Thomas Fuller and Catherine Fuller donated the land for **St. Dorothy's Church** in **1681**). The old parishes of **St. John, St. Dorothy, and St. Thomas-in-the-Vale** were merged into expanded St. Catherine under **Law 20 of 1867**.

**Slavery and the planter church.** Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds. **The Diocese of Jamaica** was established by Letters Patent on **24 July 1824**; **Christopher Lipscomb** was consecrated first Bishop in 1824.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838), Sligoville.** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Sligoville** in St. Catherine became Jamaica's **first free village** in 1838, founded with land provided by **Howe Peter Browne, 2nd Marquess of Sligo**, then Governor of Jamaica. Bishop Lipscomb (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. Capital moved from Spanish Town to **Kingston in 1872**.

**Foundation of St. Philip's.** Old Harbour Bay developed as a fishing community on the south coast of St. Catherine, at the actual bay from which Old Harbour takes its name. The community has maintained a fishing tradition alongside the agricultural economy of the surrounding area. The Anglican Diocese established St. Philip's to serve this waterfront community.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Philip's a settled coastal mission.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The waterfront position on the Caribbean Sea creates direct storm surge vulnerability during hurricane passages — coastal fishing villages like Old Harbour Bay are among the most exposed communities on Jamaica's south coast.

## Clergy
Old Harbour Bay has been served within the **St. Catherine deanery** under shared clergy arrangements, linked to the Old Harbour circuit.

## Notable Facts
**Old Harbour Bay** is the fishing village at the actual harbour that gave Old Harbour town its name. The separation of the town (further inland) from its associated bay community is a common pattern on Jamaica's south coast. The dedication to **St. Philip the Apostle** (feast day **1 May** with St. James the Less) — who brought Nathanael to Christ — is found at several Jamaican Anglican churches.
