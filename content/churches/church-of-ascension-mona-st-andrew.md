# Church of the Ascension, Mona
**St. Andrew** · Church · active

The Church of the Ascension stands at 1a Daisy Avenue in Mona Heights, Kingston 6, serving the suburban communities around the University of the West Indies. It began on Whitsunday 1961 when Anglicans in the new Mona Heights housing scheme gathered for worship under a mango tree in an open field at the top of Palmetto Avenue. Over six decades it has grown into a vibrant parish known for its steel orchestra, its ties to the university, and its annual Classics in June concert series. The current rector is Canon Michael Allen.

## History
**Parish-historical context.** Penn and Venables's English expedition captured Jamaica from Spain in May **1655**. **St. Andrew parish was established in 1664** with a 600-acre glebe granted in 1667 by Charles II. The **Great Earthquake of 7 June 1692** destroyed two-thirds of Port Royal; **Kingston** was formally established on **22 July 1692**, constituted a parish in **1693**. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church. The **Diocese of Jamaica** was established by Letters Patent on **24 July 1824**; **Christopher Lipscomb** was consecrated first Bishop in 1824. During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. The capital moved from Spanish Town to **Kingston in 1872**. The **14 January 1907 earthquake** destroyed much of Kingston; ten churches were consecrated within a single month in **January 1911**. The **Kingston and St. Andrew Corporation (KSAC)** unified the two parishes for civic administration in **1923**.

Mona Heights was developed in 1958–1959 by West Indies Home Contractors (WIHCON), the company created by the Matalon family, as 630 houses on 200 acres originally earmarked for the West Indian Federation. The flat-roofed concrete houses became Jamaica's first large middle-class suburban development. The land on which they were built had a far older history: it was formerly part of Mona Estate, a sugar plantation active from the early 1700s until sugar production ceased around 1908. The estate's enslaved population numbered 160 to 190 people; an 1817 census recorded 187 individuals — 88 men, 99 women, aged from three months to 81 years — about two-thirds of them Creole (Jamaican-born). The plantation ruins — boiling house, mill, and a cut stone aqueduct of 51 arches connecting the Mona, Papine, and Hope estates — survive on the adjacent UWI campus, where a modern obelisk commemorates the enslaved people who lived and died on the estate.

On Whitsunday, 21 May 1961, Anglicans in the new housing scheme gathered for their first service under a mango tree on an empty lot. Funds were raised, and a permanent church was built and consecrated. The founding rector, Canon John McNab — ordained by Bishop Percival Gibson in 1957 — combined his work at Ascension with chaplaincy to the University and St. Peter's Theological College, tying the parish to academic life from the outset. McNab also served as School Chaplain at Jamaica College, where he inspired Robert Thompson, later Suffragan Bishop of Kingston, to enter the priesthood. He subsequently became Principal of Kingston College (1974–1978), leading the school's recovery from a devastating fire. He died on 17 March 2011.

Canon Peter Mullings succeeded McNab in 1974 and served as rector for 37 years until 2011 — a tenure that spanned the major growth of the Mona community. He also served as Rural Dean of St. Andrew for 29 years and as Chaplain of Jamaica College, continuing the church's connection to education. Installed as Honorary Canon of the Cathedral of St. Jago de la Vega in 1987, Mullings was known for teaching church management and ecumenical relationships across 59 years of ordained ministry. He died in Canada on 11 January 2022, aged 85.

Jamaica's independence on 6 August 1962 — just a year after the church's founding — accelerated the transformation of the Mona area from plantation land to university campus and suburb. When UWI had opened in 1948, St. Margaret's, Liguanea had been the only Anglican church nearby; by 1961, Ascension provided a parish home directly adjacent to campus. Among the current assistant clergy is Rev. Veront Satchell, Professor of Economic History at UWI Mona and a published scholar on sugar, slavery, and technological change in Jamaica — an embodiment of the church's continuing bond with the university.

## Architecture
The Church of the Ascension is a modernist concrete building with clean angular lines, flat and gently sloping roof forms, and large areas of glass — a striking contrast to the older stone parish churches of Jamaica. One long exterior wall is a patterned screen of breeze blocks providing texture and ventilation, while the main entrance features a high glazed facade that floods the interior with light. The church sits on a residential street framed by gardens and trees, and includes a columbarium. The interior is arranged to accommodate the steel orchestra, musical groups, microphones, projection, and streaming technology, reflecting its adaptation for contemporary worship.

## Education and Community
The church hosts regular health and wellness clinics and a series of "5th Wednesday Health Talks." Since 2005, the annual Classics in June concert — held on Father's Day and organised by Soroptimist International Jamaica (Kingston Club) — has featured prominent Jamaican classical musicians and donated over half a million dollars to children's charities over twenty years. The church's acoustic qualities have made it a sought-after venue for classical performances. St. Matthew's, Jack's Hill, a sister congregation under the same rector, has maintained scholarship funds for over 30 years supporting young people in creative arts and professional fields.

## Clergy
Canon Michael Allen has served as rector since 2011. He holds a BA in Theology from UWI and an MA in Christian Education from Virginia Theological Seminary, and served a decade as Divisional Director for Christian Education at Church House before coming to Ascension. He was appointed Canon of the Cathedral (stall of St. Columba) in 2018. Assistant clergy include Rev. Veront Satchell, Seymour Blackwood, and Christopher Givans.

## Notable Facts
The church is widely known for its steel orchestra, which accompanies services with rhythmic pan music — a distinctive expression of Caribbean Anglican worship that sets Ascension apart from most other parishes in the Diocese. The nearby UWI Panoridim Steel Orchestra, with over 60 years of pan culture on the Mona campus, reflects the broader musical environment in which the church sits. The Ascension — commemorating Jesus's ascension to heaven forty days after the Resurrection — is one of the principal feasts of the Christian calendar. The founding of the church on Whitsunday (Pentecost) 1961, ten days after Ascension Day in the liturgical calendar, places its birth between the two great feasts that close the Easter season.

The Church of the Ascension was shaped by an extraordinarily long incumbency: the Rev'd Canon Peter Mullings served as rector for 38 years, from 1973 to his retirement in 2011.
