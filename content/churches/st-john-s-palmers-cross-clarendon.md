# St. John's, Palmers Cross
**Clarendon** · Church · active

St. John's Anglican Church serves **Palmers Cross**, a community on the south coastal plain of Clarendon — historically part of the old parish of Vere — and St. John's has served it for several generations.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1664).** Clarendon parish was created in **1664**, nine years after the English seizure of Jamaica from Spain (**1655**), and named for **Sir Edward Hyde, 1st Earl of Clarendon** (1609–1674), Lord High Chancellor of England under Charles II. The original parish church was the **Church of the White Cross** at "The Cross" — Jamaica's third Anglican church. **St. Paul's Chapelton** was built as a chapel of ease in **1666** and became the parish church when the Cross Church was destroyed in **1815**.

**Vere parish and the southern plain (1673–1867).** **Vere** was carved out as a separate sugar-plain parish in **1673**, taking in the southern coastal plain — including Palmers Cross. **St. Peter's Alley** (1671) was Vere's parish church, and Palmers Cross sat within Vere's economy of sugar estates worked by enslaved Africans. Vere was reabsorbed into Clarendon in **1867**, and **Law 20 of 1867** designated **May Pen** as chief town.

**Slavery, Sam Sharpe, and Emancipation.** The sugar estates of the Vere plain depended on the labour of thousands of enslaved Africans. One of Jamaica's earliest slave rebellions erupted at **Suttons Plantation in 1690**: some 500 enslaved people escaped to form the Maroon communities eventually led by **Cudjoe** into the Cockpit Country. During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**, a vigilante alliance that sought to "wipe the non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of St. John's.** Palmers Cross sits on the south coast of Clarendon, in the flat coastal plain near the Caribbean Sea. The community has been shaped by coastal farming and fishing. The Anglican Diocese established St. John's to serve this community as part of the Vere/southern Clarendon parochial framework. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**, and St. John's Palmers Cross belongs to that broader expansion phase.

**Twentieth century: bauxite, May Pen, Independence.** Chapelton lost its status as parish capital in **1938**, with administrative functions transferring to **May Pen**. **Alcoa** began bauxite operations in Clarendon in **1959**; the **Jamalco** alumina refinery at Halse Hall opened in **1972**, with the alumina exported via south-coast ports near the Palmers Cross hinterland. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. John's an active congregation in this transforming landscape.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of St. John's Palmers Cross after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. John's is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural Clarendon in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. The lowland coastal-plain position creates direct exposure to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
Palmers Cross has been served within the Clarendon deanery under shared clergy arrangements.

## Notable Facts
Palmers Cross is one of the south-coast communities of Clarendon on the Caribbean Sea, in an area of coastal wetlands and farming that has historically supported both agriculture and fishing. The dedication to **St. John** — most commonly the Evangelist (feast 27 December), occasionally the Baptist (24 June) — is one of the most common Anglican dedications.
