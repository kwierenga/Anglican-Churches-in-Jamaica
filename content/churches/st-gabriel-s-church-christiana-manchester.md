# St. Gabriel's Church, Christiana
**Manchester** · Church · active

St. Gabriel's Anglican Church serves Christiana, a major town in the highlands of Manchester parish at significant elevation in the central Jamaican hills. Christiana is one of the cooler and more prominent interior towns of Jamaica, known for its highland farming, including Irish potatoes grown in the cooler climate. St. Gabriel's provides Anglican ministry in this highland community alongside Christ Church, also recorded in Christiana.

## History
Christiana developed as a substantial highland town in Manchester at elevations that produce some of the coolest temperatures in lowland Jamaica. The town has been a centre for the surrounding hill farming communities, and the cool climate has made it a destination for those escaping the coastal heat. The Anglican Diocese established St. Gabriel's to serve the Christiana community.

## Architecture
St. Gabriel's Church is a masonry church of modest proportions, representative of Anglican rural church building in Manchester. The structure balances practicality with the dignity appropriate to a place of worship, featuring a straightforward nave with local stone or concrete block construction typical of Jamaica's interior church-building tradition. Set in the rural interior of Manchester. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
Christiana has been served within the Manchester deanery. St. Gabriel's and Christ Church together provide Anglican coverage for this highland market town.

## Notable Facts
Christiana is one of the few Jamaican towns where Irish potatoes are successfully cultivated — the cool highland climate mimics the temperature conditions needed for the crop. This has given Christiana a distinctive agricultural character, and the town's farmers supply potatoes to markets across Jamaica. The archangel Gabriel's dedication gives St. Gabriel's a celestial character that suits its cloud-level highland position.
