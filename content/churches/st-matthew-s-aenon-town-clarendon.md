# St. Matthew's, Aenon Town
**Clarendon** · Church · active

St. Matthew's Anglican Church serves Aenon Town, a community in the north-western interior of Clarendon parish near the Frankfield area. Aenon Town has a name of biblical origin — Aenon was the place near Salim where John the Baptist baptised in the Gospel of John — making it one of the more theologically named communities in Clarendon.

## History
Aenon Town sits in the north-western interior of Clarendon, in the hill country near Frankfield and the St. Elizabeth border. The biblical name of the community suggests it was founded or named by a religious community, possibly after Emancipation when freed people often gave their settlements biblical or aspirational names. The Anglican Diocese established St. Matthew's to serve this community.

## Architecture
St. Matthew's is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural Clarendon in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. Set in the rural interior of Clarendon. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
Aenon Town has been served within the Clarendon deanery under shared clergy arrangements.

## Notable Facts
Aenon Town's biblical name is part of a tradition of spiritually named Jamaican communities, particularly common in the post-Emancipation period when freed people established new settlements with names reflecting their religious hopes and identities. The name Aenon — from the Gospel of John — carries an association with baptism and new beginnings that would have been particularly resonant for a community founded in the first generation of freedom.
