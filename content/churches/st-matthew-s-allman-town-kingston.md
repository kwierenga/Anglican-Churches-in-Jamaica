# St. Matthew's, Allman Town
**Kingston** · Church · active

St. Matthew's Anglican Church stands at 24–26 Gordon Street (formerly Hitchen Street) in the heart of Allman Town, Kingston 4, a short distance from the National Heroes Park. Consecrated in 1911 after the devastating 1907 earthquake destroyed an earlier building, the church has served as a spiritual anchor in one of Kingston's oldest post-Emancipation communities for well over a century. With its stone walls, stained glass windows, mahogany pews, and pipe organ, St. Matthew's embodies a tradition of Anglican worship that has endured through decades of profound change in the surrounding neighbourhood.

## History
**Parish-historical context.** Penn and Venables's English expedition captured Jamaica from Spain in May **1655**. **St. Andrew parish was established in 1664**. The **Great Earthquake of 7 June 1692** destroyed two-thirds of Port Royal; **Kingston** was constituted a parish in **1693**. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church. The **Diocese of Jamaica** was established by Letters Patent on **24 July 1824**; **Christopher Lipscomb** was consecrated first Bishop in 1824. During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero. The capital moved from Spanish Town to **Kingston in 1872**. The **Kingston and St. Andrew Corporation (KSAC)** unified the two parishes for civic administration in **1923**.

The Anglican presence in Allman Town dates to the 1880s or 1890s, when the parish was established as part of Bishop **Enos Nuttall's** ambitious programme of church expansion across Kingston and St. Andrew. By 1900, the Handbook of Jamaica records Allman Town as a functioning parish with three clergy — J. Massiah, C. H. Coles, and A. B. Mullings — and six associated mission stations, indicating a substantial operation serving the growing community east of the Race Course (now National Heroes Park).

The neighbourhood itself was one of Jamaica's earliest post-Emancipation suburban developments — its very existence a consequence of freedom. Full emancipation came on 1 August 1838, and within five years the demand for housing from newly free Black Jamaicans and the growing class of free people of colour was reshaping Kingston. In 1843, a large livestock pen was subdivided and sold in lots, creating what was described as "the largest and most ambitious scheme yet" of its kind. Initially lying outside Kingston's official boundaries in lower St. Andrew, Allman Town was formally incorporated into the city in 1867. By the early 1900s, the area had developed a character of modest respectability, with residential streets laid out around the expanding Race Course.

The 1907 Kingston earthquake, a magnitude 6.5 event on 14 January, devastated the city and damaged or destroyed approximately thirty churches. St. Matthew's was among the casualties. Under Archbishop Nuttall's reconstruction programme — funded by congregational contributions, a Pan-Anglican Congress grant, and the Jamaica Church Aid Association at a total cost of some £50,000 — the church was rebuilt and consecrated on 18 January 1911 alongside nine other restored churches across the diocese.

Over the following century, the fortunes of Allman Town shifted dramatically. Once a respectable residential district, the neighbourhood experienced increasing overcrowding from the 1920s onward, as tenement housing replaced family homes. By the late twentieth century, Allman Town had become part of Kingston's inner-city belt, facing the challenges of poverty, unemployment, and community violence that characterise much of downtown Kingston. The church witnessed Jamaica's independence in 1962 and the optimism of nationhood, even as the neighbourhood around it continued to struggle. Through all of this, St. Matthew's has remained a constant — a place of worship, community gathering, and continuity amid upheaval.

## Architecture
St. Matthew's is a substantial stone church consistent with the Jamaican hybrid of Georgian and Gothic elements typical of post-1907 Anglican rebuilding. Stained glass windows above the altar are described by visitors as "stunning," flooding the chancel with coloured light. Warm mahogany pews line the nave, and a pipe organ — still in use — anchors the church's tradition of choral worship. The Flower Guild provides fresh arrangements at services, maintaining a practice common to Anglican churches across the island.

The church's position on the low-lying Kingston coastal plain creates vulnerability to flooding during major storm events, a risk shared by many downtown Kingston buildings.

## Education and Community
St. Matthew's has long understood its ministry as extending beyond Sunday worship into the daily life of Allman Town. Two Anglican-affiliated schools — Allman Town Infant School and Allman Town Primary School — have educated generations of children from the surrounding communities. Allman Town Primary celebrated its centenary in 2021, marking a hundred years of service to the neighbourhood.

The St. Matthew's Optimist Club has operated from the church hall on Gordon Street for over fifteen years, providing after-school programmes, scholarships, mentorship, and food assistance to local basic schools. The club has organised Peace Day commemorations in which young people use dance and drama to advocate against violence — a pointed statement in a community where crime has been an enduring challenge. A 2020 community report described Greater Allman Town as "a medium density inner city community in the forgotten mid-town section of the greater Kingston metropolitan area," yet the church's sustained investment in education and youth development has made it a stabilising presence in the lives of families who might otherwise have few institutional supports.

## Clergy
The most notable figure in St. Matthew's modern history is Canon the Honourable Weeville McHervin Gordon, OJ, CD, who served as rector for approximately forty years from the 1960s into the early 2000s. In 1991, Canon Gordon was appointed Custos of Kingston — the first clergyman ever elevated to that office in Jamaica — and between 1994 and 2009 he served as Acting Governor-General on seventy-two occasions. He was awarded the Order of Distinction (Commander Class) in 1995 and the Order of Jamaica in 2007. In recognition of his work in the community, the Kingston and St. Andrew Corporation renamed Hitchen Street to Weeville Gordon Street in 2005. Canon Gordon died in November 2010. At a 2016 Festal Evensong commemorating the patron saint St. Matthew, the Suffragan Bishop of Kingston recalled how Canon Gordon had "prepared me for Confirmation" as a nine-year-old — a testament to the depth of his pastoral influence in Allman Town.

The rectorship is currently vacant.

## Notable Facts
The National Heroes Park, immediately adjacent to St. Matthew's, is Jamaica's principal national memorial space, containing the tombs of National Heroes including Norman Manley, Alexander Bustamante, and Marcus Garvey. The church's position beside this site gives it a unique connection to Jamaica's commemorative landscape. St. Matthew's has been a venue for memorial services, funerals, and community events of significance, with visitors describing the setting of stained glass, mahogany, and organ music as "a beautiful celebration" of life.
