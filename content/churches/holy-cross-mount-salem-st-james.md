# Holy Cross, Mount Salem
**St. James** · Church · active

Holy Cross Anglican Church serves Mount Salem, one of the larger inner-community areas of Montego Bay, situated on the hillside rising above the city’s western districts. Mount Salem is a well-established residential community with a mixed social character, home to working families, small businesses, and a dense network of community institutions. Holy Cross has been the Anglican presence in Mount Salem for several decades, providing worship, pastoral care, and a community anchor in a neighbourhood that has faced both economic pressures and serious security challenges in recent years.

## History
Mount Salem developed as a hillside residential community in the expanding western suburbs of Montego Bay, growing rapidly in the latter half of the 20th century as internal migration brought families from rural Jamaica into the city. The Anglican Diocese established Holy Cross as a mission church to serve Mount Salem’s growing population, following the pattern of church planting in new urban communities across the Diocese. The dedication to the Holy Cross — a deeply traditional Anglican Christological dedication — reflects the church’s character as a planted urban mission. Mount Salem has in more recent decades been identified in national security reports as one of the communities of concern in the greater Montego Bay area, a social reality that has shaped the pastoral challenges facing the church.

## Architecture
Holy Cross is a functional church building on the Mount Salem hillside, set within a community of dense residential development above the Montego Bay coastal plain. The elevated hillside position gives the church some protection from direct coastal storm surge, though the steep terrain creates landslide and flood risk during heavy rainfall. The building serves as both a place of worship and a community gathering point, with the church’s presence on the hillside making it a visible landmark in the Mount Salem community.

## Clergy
Holy Cross has been served within the Montego Bay Region, grouped with other western Montego Bay congregations in the Diocese’s clergy cluster. The church’s ministry in a community facing significant social pressures has required a pastoral approach that combines liturgical worship with active community engagement and support. Lay leaders — wardens, Mothers’ Union members, and youth workers — have been important to sustaining the church’s life and its connections to the wider Mount Salem community.

## Notable Facts
Mount Salem’s appearance in national security discourse as a community of concern in St. James reflects the broader challenges facing urban communities in western Jamaica, where economic marginalisation and organised crime have created serious social pressures. The Anglican Church’s presence in Mount Salem through Holy Cross represents a commitment to ministry in difficult community contexts, consistent with the Diocese’s history of urban mission from St. Francis Glendevon to the inner-city congregations of Kingston. The hillside setting of Mount Salem, while reducing storm-surge exposure, means the community experiences significant landslide and access risks during major hurricanes. Hurricane Melissa (October 2025), which made landfall in Westmoreland and affected the wider St. James area, brought heavy rainfall to the hillside communities of Montego Bay; Holy Cross and its congregation were in the affected zone.
