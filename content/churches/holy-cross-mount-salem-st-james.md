# Holy Cross, Mount Salem
**St. James** · Church · active

Holy Cross Anglican Church serves Mount Salem, one of the larger inner-community areas of Montego Bay, situated on the hillside rising above the city's western districts. Mount Salem is a well-established residential community with a mixed social character — working families, small businesses, and a dense network of community institutions — and Holy Cross has provided the Anglican presence there for several decades. The community gained particular national attention in **September 2017**, when the Jamaican Government declared it the country's **first Zone of Special Operations (ZOSO)**.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1664).** St. James was one of the second batch of Jamaican parishes formed after the English conquest, established around **1664** by Sir Thomas Modyford and named after **James, Duke of York** (later King James II). At its formation it was much larger than today, including the territory of present-day Trelawny and Hanover. For many years the north side of the island was sparsely settled: in **1673** only **146 persons** resided in the entire parish.

**Eighteenth-century development.** Montego Bay was made a free port in **1738**; Charles Square was laid out by James Lawrence (custos) in **1755**. Cudjoe, Maroon leader of western Jamaica, signed a peace treaty with the British in **1739**, after which the Maroons were given almost 1,500 acres of land at **Trelawny Town** in St. James. The Second Maroon War of **1789** saw 300 Maroons fight 1,500 English soldiers. By the 1780s Montego Bay was, next to Kingston, "the most flourishing town in the island" (NLJ).

**Slavery and the Sam Sharpe Rebellion (December 1831).** Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds. The **Christmas Rebellion** of 1831, led by the Baptist deacon **Samuel Sharpe** at the Kensington Estate in St. James on the night of **27 December 1831**, was one of the largest slave rebellions in the British Caribbean. Sharpe was hanged in **Montego Bay marketplace** (now **Sam Sharpe Square**) on **23 May 1832**. Sharpe is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation, 1865, disestablishment.** Within two years the British Parliament passed the Slavery Abolition Act (1833); Emancipation came in 1834, and full freedom followed on **1 August 1838**. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865, Crown Colony rule replaced the planter Assembly (1866); the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of Holy Cross: a twentieth-century urban mission.** Mount Salem developed as a hillside residential community in the expanding western suburbs of Montego Bay, growing rapidly in the latter half of the twentieth century as internal migration brought families from rural Jamaica into the city. The Anglican Diocese established Holy Cross as a mission church to serve Mount Salem's growing population, following the pattern of church-planting in new urban communities across the Diocese, alongside Holy Trinity Westgate, Mary Magdalene Granville, St. Francis Glendevon, and St. Augustine's Coral Gardens. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found these new urban Anglican missions in their early decades.

**The Mount Salem ZOSO and modern Montego Bay.** Mount Salem has in more recent decades been identified in national security reporting as one of the communities of concern in the greater Montego Bay area. In **September 2017** the Government declared it Jamaica's **first Zone of Special Operations (ZOSO)** under the Law Reform (Zones of Special Operations) (Special Security and Community Development Measures) Act, a social reality that has shaped the pastoral challenges facing the church. Montego Bay received city status by act of Parliament on **9 October 1980** and officially became Jamaica's second city in 1982. Major hurricanes — Gilbert (1988), Ivan (2004), Melissa (October 2025) — have repeatedly struck the parish.

## Architecture
Holy Cross is a functional church building on the Mount Salem hillside, set within a community of dense residential development above the Montego Bay coastal plain. The elevated hillside position gives the church some protection from direct coastal storm surge, though the steep terrain creates landslide and flood risk during heavy rainfall.

## Clergy
Holy Cross has been served within the Montego Bay Region, grouped with other western Montego Bay congregations in shared clergy arrangements typical of urban Anglican administration in Jamaica. Lay leaders — wardens, Mothers' Union members, and youth workers — have been important to sustaining the church's life and its connections to the wider Mount Salem community.

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** "Holy Cross" — a deeply traditional Anglican Christological dedication — reflects the church's character as a planted urban mission rather than an inherited colonial-period parish church. The Feast of the Holy Cross (Holy Cross Day, **14 September**) commemorates the cross of Christ.

**Hurricane Melissa (28 October 2025)** made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history — and brought heavy rainfall and damaging winds to the hillside communities of Montego Bay. Holy Cross and its congregation were in the affected zone, though specific damage to this church has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed. Across the Diocese, some **50 church buildings were destroyed or significantly damaged**.
