# St. Luke's, Wood Hall
**Clarendon** · Chapel · active

St. Luke's Anglican Chapel of Ease serves **Wood Hall**, a rural community in central Clarendon roughly 50 km west of Kingston. The chapel is administered as a chapel of ease of the **St. Paul's, Chapelton** cure, alongside St. Mark's Beckford Kraal, St. James' Rock River, and All Saints' Mt. Providence. The cure rectory is at Chapelton P.O., Clarendon (telephone 785-0782).

## History
**Founding of the parish (1664).** Clarendon parish was created in **1664**, nine years after the English seizure of Jamaica from Spain (**1655**), and named for **Sir Edward Hyde, 1st Earl of Clarendon** (1609–1674), Lord High Chancellor of England under Charles II. The original parish church was the **Church of the White Cross** at "The Cross" — Jamaica's third Anglican church. **St. Paul's Chapelton** was built as a chapel of ease in **1666** and became the parish church when the Cross Church was destroyed in **1815**. **Vere** was carved out as a separate sugar-plain parish in **1671** and was reabsorbed into Clarendon in **1867**; **Law 20 of 1887** designated **May Pen** as chief town.

**Slavery, Sam Sharpe, and Emancipation.** Clarendon was one of Jamaica's great sugar parishes — the fertile Rio Minho plain held the great estates of the Suttons, Pennants, Ballards, and Dawkins, and the wealth of the parish was built on the labour of enslaved Africans. One of Jamaica's earliest slave rebellions erupted at **Suttons Plantation in 1690**: some 500 enslaved people escaped to form the Maroon communities eventually led by **Cudjoe** into the Cockpit Country. During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**, a vigilante alliance that sought to "wipe the non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of the Wood Hall chapel of ease.** Wood Hall is one of the small upland farming districts that developed in the post-Emancipation period in central Clarendon, in the agricultural country between Chapelton and the southern parish boundary. The name preserves a colonial estate name (variously also spelled "Woodhall" or "Woodall" in older records) — "Hall" being a common designation for the proprietor's house at the centre of a colonial-era estate. The Diocese of Jamaica established St. Luke's as a chapel of ease of the **St. Paul's Chapelton** cure to serve this scattered rural community, following the pattern of Bishop **Enos Nuttall's** late-nineteenth-century rural mission expansion (1880–1916), which extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

The "chapel of ease" designation in the diocese listing distinguishes St. Luke's from a full mission station — a chapel of ease is a building "for the ease of parishioners who cannot conveniently reach the parish church," a term inherited from the established Church of England's parochial system.

**Twentieth century: bauxite, May Pen, Independence.** Chapelton lost its status as parish capital in **1938**, with administrative functions transferring to **May Pen**. **Alcoa** began bauxite operations in Clarendon in **1959**; the **Jamalco** alumina refinery at Halse Hall opened in **1972**. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Luke's a settled chapel of ease of the Chapelton cure.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of St. Luke's Wood Hall after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. Luke's Wood Hall is a small rural chapel of the kind built by the Diocese for chapel-of-ease use across rural Clarendon. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal storm surge while remaining exposed to wind and rainfall during major storms.

## Clergy
St. Luke's Wood Hall is served from the St. Paul's, Chapelton cure rectory; pastoral oversight is shared across the cure's network of mission stations and chapels of ease.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to **St. Luke** — physician, evangelist, and traditional patron of physicians and artists, whose feast day is **18 October** — is a common Anglican dedication, found at several other points in the Clarendon and parish-wide network (including the cure-mother church St. Luke's, Cross Roads in Kingston).
