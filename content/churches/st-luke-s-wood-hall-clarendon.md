# St. Luke's, Wood Hall
**Clarendon** · Chapel · active

St. Luke's Anglican Chapel of Ease serves Wood Hall, a rural community in central Clarendon roughly 50 km west of Kingston. The chapel is administered as a chapel of ease of the St. Paul's, Chapelton cure, alongside St. Mark's Beckford Kraal, St. James' Rock River, and All Saints' Mt. Providence. The cure rectory is at Chapelton P.O., Clarendon (telephone 785-0782).

## History
Wood Hall is one of the small upland farming districts that developed in the post-Emancipation period in central Clarendon, in the agricultural country between Chapelton and the southern parish boundary. The name preserves a colonial estate name (variously also spelled "Woodhall" or "Woodall" in older records) — "Hall" being a common designation for the proprietor's house at the centre of a colonial-era estate. The Diocese of Jamaica established St. Luke's as a chapel of ease of the St. Paul's, Chapelton cure to serve this scattered rural community, following the pattern of the diocese's late-nineteenth-century rural mission expansion under Bishop Enos Nuttall.

The "chapel of ease" designation in the diocese listing distinguishes St. Luke's from a full mission station — a chapel of ease is a building "for the ease of parishioners who cannot conveniently reach the parish church," a term inherited from the established Church of England's parochial system. The dedication to St. Luke — physician, evangelist, and traditional patron of physicians and artists, whose feast day is 18 October — is a common Anglican dedication, found at several other points in the Clarendon and parish-wide network (including the cure-mother church St. Luke's, Cross Roads in Kingston).

## Architecture
St. Luke's Wood Hall is a small rural chapel of the kind built by the Diocese for chapel-of-ease use across rural Clarendon. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal storm surge while remaining exposed to wind and rainfall during major storms.

## Clergy
St. Luke's Wood Hall is served from the St. Paul's, Chapelton cure rectory; pastoral oversight is shared across the cure's network of mission stations and chapels of ease.
