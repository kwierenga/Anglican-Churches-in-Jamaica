# St. Paul's, Kentish
**St. Catherine** · Church · active

St. Paul's Anglican Church serves Kentish, a small community in the rural interior of St. Catherine parish.

## History
Kentish is one of the small farming districts of the St. Catherine interior. The community's name preserves a colonial-era estate name — one of the many Jamaican community names whose English origins (in this case, Kent) became attached to colonial properties as proprietors named their plantations after their English home counties.

The Anglican Diocese established St. Paul's as a small mission chapel to serve the Kentish community as part of the wider rural church-planting expansion that the diocese carried out from the late nineteenth century onward, after the disestablishment of the Anglican Church in 1870 freed the diocese to organise its mission network on its own canons and resources. No substantive foundational date or architectural history for St. Paul's Kentish has been identified in the public heritage sources reviewed; the church does not appear in the 1891 Handbook of Jamaica clergy listings, which is consistent with a foundation date in the early twentieth century.

The dedication to St. Paul the apostle — author of much of the New Testament, missionary across the Mediterranean world, with feast days on 25 January (his conversion) and 29 June (with St. Peter) — is one of the most common Anglican dedications.

## Architecture
St. Paul's is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for mission stations across the St. Catherine interior. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal storm surge while remaining exposed to wind and rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
St. Paul's Kentish has been served within the Kingston Region's St. Catherine Deanery.
