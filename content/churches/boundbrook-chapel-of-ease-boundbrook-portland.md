# Boundbrook Chapel-of-Ease, Boundbrook
**Portland** · Chapel · active

Boundbrook Chapel-of-Ease serves the **Boundbrook** community of Portland, a district adjacent to **Port Antonio** on the western edge of the parish capital's urban area. A **Chapel-of-Ease** is an Anglican church built to serve a community at some distance from the parish church, providing a more accessible place of worship without full parish status. Boundbrook's Chapel-of-Ease is linked to **Christ Church** parish church as its parent congregation.

## History
**English rule (1655) and the founding of Portland (1723).** Under English rule from **1655**, the future Portland coast remained largely Maroon territory. **Portland parish was created in 1723** by order of the then Governor, **Henry Bentinck, 1st Duke of Portland** (Governor 1722–1726), and named in his honour. The British built **Fort George** at Port Antonio in 1729 on the western flank of the harbour.

**The First Maroon War (1728–1739) and Nanny.** The **Windward Maroons** under **Quao** and **Nanny** controlled the Blue Mountains and John Crow Mountains. The **1740 treaty** granted **Nanny a 500-acre land grant** that forms the present site of **Moore Town**. **Nanny of the Maroons** became Jamaica's only female National Hero in October 1975.

**Slavery and the planter church.** Sugar estates dominated the fertile coastal lowlands and river valleys; coffee thrived in the hills. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838), and the parish church (c. 1840).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union** to "wipe non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Christ Church Port Antonio**, the parish church, was completed around **1840** under Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb** (1824–1843) and Rev. **Griffiths**, designed by **Annesley Voysey**.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall, and the banana revolution.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. In **1870**, **Captain Lorenzo Dow Baker** purchased bananas in Port Antonio market; the first shipment of **1,450 stems** reached Jersey City in **1871**, launching the international banana trade. Baker's **Boston Fruit Company** became the **United Fruit Company in 1899**, and Port Antonio grew into one of the busiest banana ports in the Caribbean.

**Foundation of the Boundbrook Chapel-of-Ease.** Boundbrook developed as a residential and commercial area on the western approaches to Port Antonio, growing as the town expanded along the coastal plain. The Anglican Diocese established the Chapel-of-Ease to serve Boundbrook's population who would otherwise have had to travel to the parish church for worship. **Chapels-of-Ease** were a common feature of the Diocese's parish system in Jamaica, providing local accessibility while maintaining the administrative structure of the larger parish. Boundbrook's chapel has served the community through the growth and decline of Port Antonio's banana-trade prosperity.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** The **1903 hurricane** damaged Christ Church Port Antonio and devastated the banana industry. Tourism grew at Port Antonio from the late 19th century, with **Errol Flynn** owning Navy Island from the 1940s. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found Boundbrook a settled chapel-of-ease.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Hurricane Melissa struck Port Antonio and its immediate surroundings directly; Boundbrook's coastal position made it particularly vulnerable to surge and flooding.

## Architecture
The Boundbrook Chapel-of-Ease is a modest Anglican chapel in the coastal community west of Port Antonio town centre. Its position near the coast exposes it to storm surge during hurricane passages, and the flat coastal plain of Boundbrook is particularly vulnerable to flooding during major rainfall events.

## Clergy
As a **Chapel-of-Ease**, Boundbrook has been served by the clergy of Christ Church parish, Port Antonio, without independent parish status. The chapel's pastoral life is closely linked to the parish church and its deanery circuit.

## Notable Facts
The **Chapel-of-Ease** designation reflects a medieval English ecclesiastical tradition that was transplanted throughout the British colonial church system. In Jamaica, many communities that lacked full parish status were served by Chapels-of-Ease that provided regular worship within a larger parish structure. Boundbrook's chapel is one of several such chapels in the Portland deanery.
