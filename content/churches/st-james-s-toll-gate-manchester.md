# St. James', Toll Gate
**Manchester** · Church · unknown

St. James' Anglican Church serves Toll Gate in Manchester, a co-cure with St. Augustine's Porus and St. Stephen's Chantilly under the Porus rectory. Toll Gate is a junction community in south Manchester where the road system intersects, and the name preserves the memory of the colonial toll-gate system that charged travellers at key road junctions.

## History
Toll Gate sits at a road junction in south Manchester near the Clarendon border. The community name directly records the colonial practice of collecting road tolls at key passage points — a system operated in Jamaica from the 18th century. The church is part of the Porus cure alongside Chantilly and the main Porus church.

## Architecture
St. James' is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural Manchester in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. Set in the rural interior of Manchester. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
The Porus/Chantilly/Toll Gate cure has Charles Danvers (SM) as assistant.

## Notable Facts
Road tolls were a significant feature of Jamaican colonial infrastructure. The roads were maintained by toll revenue collected at gates across the island. When the toll system was abolished in the 19th century, many communities that had formed around toll gates retained the name. Toll Gate in Manchester is one of several such communities on the island preserving this piece of colonial transportation history in their name.
