# St. Luke's, Balaclava
**St. Elizabeth** · Church · active

St. Luke's Anglican Church served Balaclava, a small town in northern St. Elizabeth on the historic Kingston–Montego Bay railway line. It was destroyed by Hurricane Melissa on 28 October 2025 — one of four Anglican churches the Diocese reported as having "stood little chance" against the Category 5 storm, and among nine Diocesan buildings lost entirely. St. Luke's churchyard had been, since 1957, one of the resting places of victims of the Kendal Train Crash — the worst rail disaster in Jamaica's history.

## History
Balaclava was established in the late eighteenth century around the plantations of the Arscott and Sherman families. The town was renamed after the Battle of Balaclava (25 October 1854), fought during the Crimean War and famous for the Charge of the Light Brigade.

Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, drawing over £40,000 a year from government funds — £37,284 from the Island's Local Government plus £7,100 from the Imperial Government — to support its clergy, buildings, and parochial administration. Out of a Jamaican population of about 400,000, only some 48,000 claimed Anglican membership, and the government could no longer afford to maintain the Church. Law 30 of 1870 enacted the gradual disendowment and reconstituted the Anglican Church in Jamaica as the self-supporting Diocese. A wave of rural mission expansion followed under Bishop Enos Nuttall (1880–1916), whose Diocese grew from 26 rural stations in 1880 to 122 by 1911. St. Luke's Balaclava fits this post-disestablishment pattern; the church does not appear in the 1891 Handbook of Jamaica's main-church returns for St. Elizabeth, placing its foundation (or formal organisation) after 1891.

The Balaclava railway station opened in 1892 on the line connecting Kingston to Montego Bay, making the town a junction point for passenger and freight traffic. It closed in October 1992, when all passenger rail services in Jamaica abruptly ceased.

On the night of 1 September 1957 at about 11:30 pm, a train returning to Kingston from a Montego Bay excursion derailed near Kendal in neighbouring Manchester. The Kendal Train Crash killed 187 people and injured around 700 — the worst rail disaster in Jamaica's history and, at the time, the second worst in world history. Many of the passengers had been members of the Holy Name Society of St. Anne's Roman Catholic Church. While a mass grave at the crash site holds many of the dead, others were buried in the St. Luke's Anglican churchyard at Balaclava, where a monument commemorates them.

Jamaica's independence on 6 August 1962 found the church serving a farming, railway, and market community. Hurricane Melissa destroyed the building on 28 October 2025.

## Architecture
Before Melissa, St. Luke's was a modest rural Gothic Revival stone church with a simple nave, pitched roof, and churchyard containing the Kendal Train Crash commemorative monument. Photographic documentation survives.

## Education
The Anglican mission to educate was central to the post-Emancipation church's purpose, and rural elementary schools were established alongside chapels across St. Elizabeth. The parish's anchor institutions are Munro College (1856) and Hampton School (1858), both in the Malvern hills and both funded from the 1797 will of Robert Hugh Munro — a free "gentleman of colour" whose bequest to his nephew Caleb Dickenson and the Churchwardens of St. Elizabeth established the endowment.

## Clergy
St. Luke's is served within the St. Elizabeth Deanery.

## Hurricane Melissa (October 2025)
Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope, Westmoreland, on 28 October 2025. The Diocese reported St. Luke's Balaclava as one of four Anglican churches that "stood little chance" against the storm, alongside St. John's Black River, St. Thomas' Lacovia, and St. Thomas' Bluefields. Bishop Garth Minott, Suffragan Bishop of Kingston, reported that about 120 of the Diocese's ~300 Anglican churches sustained damage and 18 were completely destroyed; St. Luke's was among the nine (some sources cite the figure more conservatively) buildings lost outright.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to St. Luke — the physician, companion of Paul, traditionally author of the third Gospel and the Acts of the Apostles — is one of the more common Anglican dedications. Luke's Gospel is distinguished by its attention to the poor, to women, and to outsiders; his feast day is 18 October. The Battle of Balaclava (1854), for which the town is named, produced both the heroic Charge of the Light Brigade and — through the knitted wool headgear originally made by charitable women in England for soldiers suffering in the Crimean winter — the word "balaclava" in its modern sense.
