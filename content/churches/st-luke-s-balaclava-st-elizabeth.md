# St. Luke's, Balaclava
**St. Elizabeth** · Church · active

St. Luke’s Anglican Church serves the town of Balaclava, an important railway and farming community in northern St. Elizabeth. The church was founded in the 1800s, when the area grew after Emancipation in 1838 and more families settled around the new road and railway lines. Over the years, St. Luke’s has survived storms, rebuilding, and changes in the town but has remained active. Today, the church continues to serve Balaclava and nearby districts with regular worship and community events.

## History
Balaclava developed as a busy settlement during the 19th century, especially after Emancipation, when freed families moved into the northern plains and hills of St. Elizabeth. Before 1838, the area was linked to large estates where enslaved people lived and worked, especially near the Appleton and Maggotty valleys. As the town grew, the Anglican Diocese sent clergy from Lacovia and Maggotty to hold services for the expanding population. By the mid-to-late 1800s, a permanent church was built and dedicated to St. Luke the Evangelist, known for his writings on compassion and healing. The church became a central place for baptisms, marriages, schooling, and community meetings in Balaclava.

## Architecture
St. Luke’s is a sturdy limestone church built in the simple Gothic revival style common across Jamaica. It features a long rectangular nave, a small chancel, and pointed arched windows with wooden shutters and colored glass. The roof, once made of wooden shingles, is now covered with metal sheets to withstand heavy rains. Inside, the church has wooden pews, an open-timber roof, and a small pulpit used for readings and sermons. The churchyard contains the graves of early Balaclava families, adding to the historic feel of the site.

## Clergy
St. Luke’s has always been part of the Black River Deanery, with priests often serving several churches in northern St. Elizabeth, including Siloah, Maggotty, and Thornton. Early clergy such as the Rev. H. C. Wharton and Canon W. R. Campbell supported the church during its early years. St. Luke’s helped support education in Balaclava, especially at a time when rural schooling was limited. After Jamaica’s Independence in 1962, the church continued to host Mothers’ Union meetings, youth groups, and community events. Lay readers helped keep services going whenever priests were shared among multiple congregations.

## Notable Facts
St. Luke’s has faced several major storms over the decades. Hurricane Charlie (1951) and Hurricane Gilbert (1988) damaged the roof and windows, but repairs were made by parishioners and the Diocese. Smaller restorations in the 2000s improved the structure and appearance of the building. Today, St. Luke’s Anglican Church remains active with Sunday worship, weddings, funerals, and harvest festivals, serving as a steady and important part of the Balaclava community. Hurricane Melissa (October 28, 2025) destroyed the church. The Diocese confirmed St Luke's in Balaclava as one of four Anglican churches that 'stood little chance' against Melissa's winds, placing it alongside St John's Black River, St Thomas Lacovia, and St Thomas Bluefields as the worst Anglican losses. Balaclava, set in the hills of St Elizabeth, was exposed to severe wind as Melissa tracked across the island. As of late 2025 the structure required full assessment and major reconstruction.
