# St Mark’s, Craighton
**St. Andrew** · Chapel · active

St. Mark’s Anglican Church is a small, historic church perched on a hillside in Craighton, near Irish Town in the Blue Mountains. It overlooks Kingston from a landscape once dominated by coffee plantations worked by enslaved Africans. The current building, constructed in 1882 on the ruins of an earlier chapel, and its graveyard with mid-1800s headstones connect it directly to both the slavery and post-Emancipation eras. Today, it serves its small mountain community and is linked to the nearby Craighton Primary School, which was severely affected by landslides triggered by Hurricane Melissa (2025).

## History
St. Mark’s is situated in the heart of Jamaica's Blue Mountain coffee region, an area developed by Irish and Scottish planters on the backs of enslaved labor. The church's history is deeply intertwined with the Craighton Estate, a coffee plantation established around 1800. Heritage sources indicate the present church was built in 1882 'on the ruins of the original church,' suggesting an earlier chapel served the plantation community during slavery and the immediate post-Emancipation period. This church would have ministered to estate owners, managers, and the enslaved and newly freed population. After slavery, St. Mark's evolved into a rural mission church for small farmers and workers, a role it maintains alongside its stewardship of the historic Craighton Primary School.

## Architecture
St. Mark’s is a classic example of a modest 19th-century Jamaican countryside church. It is a simple, rectangular building with plain walls, a gable roof, and a small porch, designed for function in a rural setting. The churchyard is of great historical significance, with gravestones dating back to the mid-1800s, confirming the site's long use as a sacred burial ground. Its breathtaking location offers sweeping views of Kingston but also places it in a vulnerable position on a steep, landslide-prone slope, a danger that was realized during recent hurricanes and landslide events in the Blue Mountains.

## Clergy
As part of the Anglican network in rural St. Andrew, St. Mark's has historically been served by clergy responsible for multiple mountain churches. The congregation is small and tightly-knit, with week-to-week life revolving around Sunday services, funerals, and community gatherings. Its connection to Craighton Primary School remains strong, with the priest often serving on the school's board. The church also attracts visitors and hikers, adding a transient but appreciative element to its congregation and highlighting its role as a quiet, spiritual landmark in a popular ecotourism area.

## Notable Facts
St. Mark’s churchyard serves as a tangible record of the area's complex past, likely holding the graves of those who lived through slavery and its aftermath. The church's modern challenge is its vulnerability to extreme weather. During Hurricane Melissa (2025), the Blue Mountains experienced devastating landslides that damaged infrastructure, including the affiliated Craighton Primary School and the access roads around Irish Town. While there is no public report of the church building being destroyed, its location makes it highly susceptible to water damage and isolation during such storms. The story of St. Mark’s is one of enduring faith in a beautiful but demanding landscape, from the era of coffee plantations to the threat of modern climate disasters.
