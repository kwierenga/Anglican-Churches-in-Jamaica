# St. Mary's, Negril
**Westmoreland** · Church · active

St. Mary's Anglican Church serves the Negril community at the westernmost tip of Jamaica, a beach and coastal settlement that has transformed over the past half-century from a remote fishing village into one of the Caribbean's most visited resort destinations. While Negril's fame today rests on its Seven Mile Beach and relaxed tourist character, St. Mary's continues to minister to the permanent resident community — the descendants of the fishing families and small farmers who have lived here across generations — providing worship, baptisms, funerals, and pastoral care that anchor the local community's identity amid the surrounding tourism industry.

## History
Negril was historically one of the most isolated communities in western Jamaica, separated from the rest of Westmoreland by the Great Morass, the large wetland that stretches across the neck of land connecting the Negril peninsula to the mainland. This isolation meant that Negril's community was long self-sufficient, based on fishing, small farming, and modest trade. The Anglican Church established St. Mary's to serve this remote coastal community, making it part of the Diocese's wider mission network in western Westmoreland. For most of its history, the church served a small, close-knit fishing village; the development of the tourism sector from the 1970s onward brought dramatic changes to the community's economy and character while St. Mary's continued its ministry to permanent residents.

## Architecture
St. Mary's is a modest masonry church serving a coastal community in an area of very low elevation. Negril sits at sea level, with the Great Morass to the east and the Caribbean Sea to the west and north-west; the church is one of the most sea-level-exposed Anglican buildings in Jamaica. This extreme low-elevation coastal setting creates exceptional vulnerability to storm surge. The church's architecture reflects its role as a community church for a modest congregation rather than a tourist facility, maintaining its character as a working parish church within a dramatically changed resort environment.

## Clergy
St. Mary's has been served within the western Westmoreland circuit of the Diocese's Montego Bay Region. The church's ministry navigates the complex social landscape of a resort town, serving a permanent community that coexists with a large transient tourist population. Lay leaders and wardens have been central to maintaining the church's identity and community connections. The church serves as a pastoral anchor for Negril's residents through both the prosperity and the disruptions that tourism-driven development has brought.

## Notable Facts
Negril's extreme exposure at the western tip of Jamaica makes it one of the most hurricane-vulnerable communities on the island. Storms approaching from the Caribbean — particularly those tracking north-eastward toward western Jamaica — hit Negril first and with full force. Hurricane Charlie (1951) caused severe damage on the west coast. Hurricane Gilbert (1988) made landfall very close to Negril, bringing catastrophic winds and surge that devastated the area. Hurricane Ivan (2004) again struck the western tip. Hurricane Melissa made landfall in Westmoreland in October 2025; Negril and the western tip of Jamaica were among the most severely affected areas, with major storm surge inundating the low-lying coastal strip and causing serious damage across the resort community and the surrounding residential areas where St. Mary's congregation lives.
