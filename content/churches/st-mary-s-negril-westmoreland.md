# St. Mary's, Negril
**Westmoreland** · Church · active

St. Mary the Virgin Church at Hermitage serves the Anglican community of Negril, at the westernmost tip of Jamaica. The Diocese of Jamaica and the Cayman Islands lists it under the Westmoreland Deanery within the Montego Bay Region. For most of its history Negril was among the most isolated settlements on the island — cut off from the rest of Jamaica by the Great Morass — and St. Mary's has served a permanent fishing-and-farming community through the dramatic transformation of the area from remote village to international resort from the 1970s onward.

## History
Westmoreland parish was formed in 1703, named after the English county. Negril occupies the parish's western peninsula, separated from the mainland by the Great Morass — a vast wetland east of the beach that dominates the interior of the peninsula. Until 1959, when the coast road connecting Negril to the rest of the island was built, the village was cut off from overland contact with Westmoreland's capital. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds. No precise founding date for St. Mary's has been identified in public heritage sources reviewed, but as an Anglican station in a remote fishing village it was most likely established in the post-Emancipation nineteenth century to serve a small resident community.

Negril's character changed dramatically from the 1970s onwards. Road improvements between Montego Bay and Negril in the early 1970s opened the village to tourism; Seven Mile Beach drew international attention, the Negril Aerodrome welcomed its first passengers in 1976, and Negril Beach Village — the area's first large-scale resort — opened in 1977. Through this transformation, St. Mary's has continued to minister to the permanent residents — the descendants of the fishing families and small farmers — alongside the transient tourist population that now dominates the peninsula's economy.

## Architecture
St. Mary's is a small masonry church serving a coastal community. Negril sits at very low elevation, with the Great Morass to the east and the Caribbean Sea to the west and north-west. The extreme low-lying coastal setting leaves the church unusually exposed to storm surge from hurricanes tracking into western Jamaica.

## Clergy
St. Mary's is served within the Westmoreland Deanery under shared clergy arrangements, with lay leaders and wardens sustaining regular worship.

## Hurricane Melissa (October 2025)
Hurricane Melissa made landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on 28 October 2025 as a Category 5 storm. Negril and the western tip of Jamaica were among the most severely affected areas, with major storm surge inundating the low-lying coastal strip. The specific damage to St. Mary's at Hermitage has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed, though Diocesan recovery coordination across the Westmoreland Deanery continues.

## Notable Facts
Negril's 1959 road is the key date in the village's modern history: before it, Negril was one of the most isolated communities on the island; after, it became one of the most visited. St. Mary's has straddled this transformation, remaining a working parish church for the small permanent community within a resort landscape.
