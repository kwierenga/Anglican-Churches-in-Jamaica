# All Saints', Chester Castle
**St. James** · Church · active

All Saints' Anglican Church serves Chester Castle, a community in the interior of St. James parish south of Montego Bay. Part of the St. Mary's Montpelier Cure — the same cure as St. Saviour's Chichester, St. Stephen's Cambridge, and St. Matthew's Catadupa — the church sits in a landscape shaped by some of the largest slaveholdings in Jamaica and by the Christmas Rebellion that ended slavery in the British Empire.

## History
Chester Castle lies in the interior hill country of St. James, in the heart of one of Jamaica's great sugar parishes. The estates surrounding this area — including the Montpelier properties of Lord Seaford, which held over 1,000 enslaved people — were among the most productive and brutal in Jamaica. The Christmas Rebellion of 1831–1832, led by National Hero Samuel Sharpe, erupted across these estates: Old Montpelier was burned, and the rebellion — involving up to 60,000 of Jamaica's 300,000 enslaved people — directly led to the passage of the Slavery Abolition Act in 1833.

After Emancipation in 1834 and full freedom on 1 August 1838, freed people settled across the interior of St. James, establishing farming communities in the hills. The Anglican Church expanded into these settlements as part of its post-emancipation transformation — from the established church of the planter class to a mission church serving the freed Black majority. The Montpelier Cure, centred on St. Mary's Montpelier (cornerstone 1847), grew outward to include mission stations and chapels of ease across the surrounding districts. By the 1900 Handbook of Jamaica, C. G. McGregory served "Montpelier and 5 Mission Stations" — Chester Castle was likely among them.

The community name — "Chester Castle" — follows the English pattern of naming Jamaican settlements after places in England. Jamaica's independence on 6 August 1962 marked the transition from colonial to national church.

## Architecture
All Saints' is a modest chapel characteristic of the rural Anglican mission stations established across St. James's interior in the nineteenth century.

## Education
The Anglican mission to educate was central to the Montpelier Cure's purpose. Schools were established alongside churches across the interior to serve communities of freed people. Bishop Lipscomb established over 50 national schools (by 1835 he reported 142 schools with 8,500 scholars), and by 1900 the Diocese operated 263 elementary schools island-wide.

## Clergy
All Saints' is part of the St. Mary's Montpelier Cure, served by Rev. Don T. Lewis (retired rector) with assistants Melvin Newsome and Cleve Llewellyn. The cure is based at Montpelier P.O. (tel: 995-0261).

## Notable Facts
The All Saints' dedication — commemorating all the saints of the Church, known and unknown, on 1 November — is one of the most inclusive Anglican dedications. In a community where many of the faithful departed were enslaved people whose names were never recorded in any church register, the dedication to "all saints" carries particular weight. The Montpelier Cure encompasses the very estates where the Christmas Rebellion was fought — the uprising that ended slavery in the British Empire.
