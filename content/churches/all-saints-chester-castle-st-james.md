# All Saints', Chester Castle
**St. James** · Church · active

All Saints' Anglican Church serves Chester Castle, a community in the interior of St. James parish south of Montego Bay. The church is part of the **St. Mary's Montpelier Cure** — alongside St. Saviour's Chichester, St. Stephen's Cambridge, and St. Matthew's Catadupa — and sits in a landscape shaped by some of the largest slaveholdings in Jamaica and by the Christmas Rebellion that helped end slavery in the British Empire.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1664).** St. James was one of the second batch of Jamaican parishes formed after the English conquest, established around **1664** by Sir Thomas Modyford and named after **James, Duke of York** (later King James II). At its formation it was much larger than today, including the territory of present-day Trelawny and Hanover. For many years the north side of the island was sparsely settled: in **1673** only **146 persons** resided in the entire parish.

**The Maroons and eighteenth-century development.** Cudjoe, Maroon leader of western Jamaica, signed a peace treaty with the British in **1739**, after which the Maroons were given almost 1,500 acres of land at **Trelawny Town** in St. James. Montego Bay was made a free port in **1738**. The Second Maroon War of **1789** saw 300 Maroons fight 1,500 English soldiers. By the 1780s Montego Bay was, next to Kingston, "the most flourishing town in the island" (NLJ).

**Slavery and the great Montpelier estates.** Chester Castle lies in the interior hill country of St. James, in the heart of one of Jamaica's great sugar parishes. The estates surrounding this area — including the **Montpelier properties of Lord Seaford**, which held over 1,000 enslaved people — were among the most productive and brutal in Jamaica. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds; its parish vestry was composed of the planter class.

**The Sam Sharpe Rebellion (December 1831).** The **Christmas Rebellion** of 1831–1832, led by National Hero **Samuel Sharpe** at the Kensington Estate in St. James on the night of **27 December 1831**, erupted across the Montpelier estates: **Old Montpelier was burned**, and the rebellion — involving up to 60,000 of Jamaica's 300,000 enslaved people — directly led to the passage of the Slavery Abolition Act in 1833. Sharpe was hanged in **Montego Bay marketplace** (now **Sam Sharpe Square**) on **23 May 1832**.

**Emancipation and the founding of the Montpelier cure.** Within two years the British Parliament passed the Slavery Abolition Act (1833); Emancipation came in 1834, and full freedom followed on **1 August 1838**. After Emancipation, freed people settled across the interior of St. James, establishing farming communities in the hills. The Anglican Church expanded into these settlements as part of its post-Emancipation transformation — from the established church of the planter class to a mission church serving the freed Black majority. The **Montpelier Cure**, centred on St. Mary's Montpelier (cornerstone **1847**), grew outward to include mission stations and chapels of ease across the surrounding districts.

**1865 to 1900: post-disestablishment Diocese.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865, Crown Colony rule replaced the planter Assembly (1866); the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica. By the **1900 Handbook of Jamaica**, **C. G. McGregory** served "Montpelier and 5 Mission Stations" — Chester Castle was likely among them.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** Bishop Lipscomb established over 50 national schools in his tenure, and by 1900 the Diocese operated 263 elementary schools island-wide. The St. James Parish Church in central Montego Bay was wrecked by the **1957 earthquake** but restored. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** marked the transition from colonial to national church. Major hurricanes — Gilbert (1988), Ivan (2004), Melissa (October 2025) — have repeatedly struck the parish.

## Architecture
All Saints' is a modest chapel characteristic of the rural Anglican mission stations established across St. James's interior in the nineteenth century.

## Education
The Anglican mission to educate was central to the Montpelier Cure's purpose. Schools were established alongside churches across the interior to serve communities of freed people. Bishop Lipscomb established over 50 national schools (by 1835 he reported 142 schools with 8,500 scholars), and by 1900 the Diocese operated **263 elementary schools** island-wide.

## Clergy
All Saints' is part of the **St. Mary's Montpelier Cure**, served by Rev. **Don T. Lewis** (retired rector) with assistants Melvin Newsome and Cleve Llewellyn. The cure is based at Montpelier P.O. (telephone 995-0261). Documented historical clergy include **C. G. McGregory** (rector at Montpelier with 5 mission stations, 1900 Handbook).

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** All Saints — commemorating all the saints of the Church, known and unknown, on **1 November** — is one of the most inclusive Anglican dedications. In a community where many of the faithful departed were enslaved people whose names were never recorded in any church register, the dedication carries particular weight.

**Christmas Rebellion landscape.** The Montpelier Cure encompasses the very estates where the Christmas Rebellion was fought — the uprising that helped end slavery in the British Empire.

**Hurricane Melissa (28 October 2025)** made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Across the Diocese, some **50 church buildings were destroyed or significantly damaged**. The specific status of All Saints' Chester Castle after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.
