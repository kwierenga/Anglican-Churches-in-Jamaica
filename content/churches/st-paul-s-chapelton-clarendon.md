# St. Paul's, Chapelton
**Clarendon** · Church · active

St. Paul's Anglican Church at Chapelton is confirmed as the oldest church in Chapelton, the administrative capital of Clarendon parish. As the oldest church in Clarendon's parish capital, St. Paul's has been at the centre of the town's Anglican life since the colonial period.

## History
Chapelton is the administrative capital of Clarendon parish, and St. Paul's has been its oldest and principal Anglican church since the colonial period. The church has served through the full span of Clarendon's history as an administered parish, witnessing the sugar era, Emancipation, the development of the interior road network, and the modernisation of the parish's agricultural economy. Chapelton's market serves the hill farming communities of interior Clarendon.

## Architecture
St. Paul's is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural Clarendon in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. Set in the rural interior of Clarendon. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
Chapelton has been served within the Clarendon deanery. St. Paul's, as the oldest church in the parish capital, has traditionally been the most prominent church in the Chapelton circuit.

## Notable Facts
The distinction of being the oldest church in Chapelton places St. Paul's among the historic colonial-era Anglican churches of Jamaica. Chapelton's role as the administrative capital of Clarendon — despite being smaller than May Pen — is a legacy of the colonial parish organisation system that determined administrative centres based on criteria other than population size.
