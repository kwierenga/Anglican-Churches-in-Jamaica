# St. John the Divine, Alston
**Manchester** · Church · active

St. John the Divine Anglican Church serves Alston in the highland country of northern Manchester. The church is administered as a mission of the **Christ Church, Christiana cure**, alongside St. Jude's Battersea — both attached to the cure rectory at P.O. Box 15, Christiana P.O., Manchester (telephone 964-2274). The 1900 Handbook of Jamaica records "Christiana" as a cure under **Rev. J. Cass**.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1814).** Manchester was created by **Act 55 George III. c. 23** of **1814** in response to a petition of 29 November 1814 by residents of Mile Gully, May Day, and Carpenter's Mountain. The Act came into effect on **13 December 1814**, taking the "hill country" of eastern St. Elizabeth and the western portions of Vere and Clarendon. The new parish was named after **William Montagu, 5th Duke of Manchester** (Governor 1808–1827); **Mandeville**, the capital, was named after his son, **Viscount Mandeville**.

**Slavery, the parish church, and the Sam Sharpe Rebellion (1831).** Manchester's hill-country economy was built on coffee — the cool, elevated terrain favoured coffee growing — and depended on enslaved African labour. **St. Mark's Anglican Church, Mandeville** — the parish church — was completed and consecrated in **1820**, and its organ loft was used as a jail during the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**. Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation, 1865, disestablishment.** Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Maidstone** in Manchester (founded 1840 by the Nazareth Moravian Church on 341 acres of Thomas Frith's coffee estate) was among Jamaica's earliest free villages. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of St. John the Divine: the Christiana cure.** Christiana developed as one of the highland market towns of Manchester, where the cool elevated climate supported coffee, citrus, and provision farming. The Christiana area was one of the focal points of the post-Emancipation small-farmer movement: cool, elevated, suited to coffee and provision crops, and accessible enough that freed people could establish viable farming communities. The Anglican Diocese established Christ Church Christiana as the cure church for this elevated landscape and extended its ministry into outlying districts including Alston through small mission stations like St. John the Divine. Bishop Enos Nuttall's tenure (1880–1916) saw the Diocese grow from 26 rural mission stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. The 1900 Handbook records "Christiana" under Rev. J. Cass.

**Twentieth century: Kendal, bauxite, Independence.** The **Kendal train tragedy** of **1 September 1957** at Kendal Manchester claimed almost 200 lives — Jamaica's worst rail accident. The **bauxite industry** (Alcan from 1957, Kaiser later) employed over 3,000 by the 1960s. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. John the Divine a settled mission within the Christiana cure.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of St. John the Divine Alston after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. John the Divine is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for highland mission stations across northern Manchester. The elevated terrain creates wind exposure during storm passages and can make road access difficult during heavy rainfall.

## Clergy
- **1900** — Rev. **J. Cass** (rector at Christiana, with St. John the Divine as a mission station of the cure).
- Today — served as a mission of the Christ Church Christiana cure within the Mandeville Region's Manchester Deanery.

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** The dedication "**St. John the Divine**" — distinct from "St. John the Evangelist" or "St. John the Baptist" — refers to John the Theologian, traditionally identified as the author of the Book of Revelation and the visionary of Patmos. The dedication carries a particularly contemplative, mystical character, fitting for a remote highland congregation. The dedication is shared with the great Cathedral Church of St. John the Divine in New York (1892–present), the largest Anglican cathedral in the world.
