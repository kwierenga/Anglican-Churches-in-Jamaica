# St. Barnabas', Beeston Spring
**Westmoreland** · Church · active

St. Barnabas' Anglican Church serves Beeston Spring, a community in the interior of Westmoreland between Savanna-la-Mar and the eastern hill country. The settlement takes its name from a natural spring in the district, one of the modest water sources that sustained inland communities in this part of the parish. St. Barnabas' has ministered to the families of Beeston Spring and its surrounding farms for several generations, providing a consistent Anglican presence in a quiet interior community.

## History
Beeston Spring developed in the post-Emancipation landscape of interior Westmoreland, as freed people settled away from the coastal estates and established small-scale farming communities across the plain and lower hills. The district's spring gave it a degree of self-sufficiency and helped anchor a stable population. The Anglican Diocese established mission churches across this interior zone, and St. Barnabas' was founded to serve Beeston Spring and its surrounding settlements as part of this wider outreach effort.

## Architecture
St. Barnabas' is a small masonry chapel characteristic of Anglican mission buildings in the Westmoreland interior. The church is likely a simple rectangular structure with a pitched zinc roof and plain windows, set in a modest churchyard. Its location on the interior plain, while not at the coast, places it in a landscape that can experience significant flooding when major storms bring heavy sustained rainfall to Westmoreland.

## Clergy
The church has been served within the Westmoreland Anglican circuit, sharing clergy with other interior missions under the oversight of the Savanna-la-Mar deanery. Lay leaders — wardens, Mothers' Union members, and lay readers — have been central to maintaining regular worship and community activities at a church that receives clerical visits rather than having a resident priest.

## Notable Facts
The interior of Westmoreland, while protected from direct storm surge by its distance from the coast, is vulnerable to intense rainfall flooding during major hurricanes. The plain's flat topography means that water drains slowly after sustained heavy rain. Hurricane Charlie (1951) brought prolonged rainfall to inland Westmoreland, causing flooding across the agricultural interior. Hurricane Gilbert (1988) similarly inundated the plain. Hurricane Ivan (2004) brought serious flooding inland. Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on 28 October 2025. Beeston Spring and the interior plain experienced damaging winds and heavy rain, and the congregation was part of the wider diocesan recovery response.
