# St. Barnabas', Beeston Spring
**Westmoreland** · Church · active

St. Barnabas' Anglican Church serves Beeston Spring, a community in the interior of Westmoreland between Savanna-la-Mar and the eastern hill country. The settlement takes its name from a natural spring in the district — one of the modest water sources that sustained inland communities in this part of the parish during the post-Emancipation period.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1703).** Westmoreland was officially formed in **1703** from the western portion of the older parish of St. Elizabeth, named for its position at the western part of the island. The parish capital, **Savanna-la-Mar**, was founded around 1730. In 1723 the new parish of Hanover was carved out of Westmoreland's northern section.

**Slavery and the sugar economy.** Under English rule, sugar and slavery accelerated in Westmoreland: an inventory in **1722** numbered the estates at **73**, making the parish one of the leading sugar producers of the British Caribbean. The Beckford family — Richard, Peter, and William — were among the most prominent planters; modern Petersfield is named for Peter Beckford. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**The Sam Sharpe Rebellion (December 1831).** The Christmas Rebellion of 1831 — led by the Baptist deacon Samuel Sharpe and ignited at the Kensington Estate in St. James on 27 December 1831 — spread rapidly through the western parishes (St. James, Trelawny, Hanover, and Westmoreland) with some 60,000 enslaved people involved. The Colonial Church Union, formed in the aftermath as a vigilante alliance of Anglican planters and clergy, undertook a campaign of "riotous destruction of chapels" across the western parishes, including Sav.-la-Mar (Ellis, *Diocese of Jamaica*, 1913). Sharpe was hanged on 23 May 1832 and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation and post-1838 settlement.** The British Parliament passed the Slavery Abolition Act in 1833; Emancipation came in 1834, and full freedom followed on **1 August 1838**. By 1854 the number of active sugar estates in Westmoreland had collapsed to 34, less than half the 1722 figure. Beeston Spring developed in the post-Emancipation landscape of interior Westmoreland, as freed people settled away from the coastal estates and established small-scale farming communities across the plain and lower hills. The district's spring gave it a degree of self-sufficiency and helped anchor a stable population. Germans were settled at Seaford Town in **1839**; East Indian indentured labourers arrived from the 1840s onward.

**1865 to 1900: Nuttall's rural expansion.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865, Crown Colony rule replaced the planter Assembly (1866); the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica. Bishop Enos Nuttall's tenure (1880–1916) saw the Diocese grow from 26 rural mission stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. St. Barnabas' Beeston Spring belongs to that wave of late-nineteenth and early-twentieth-century mission planting, founded as part of the diocese's outreach to the small-farming communities of the Westmoreland interior.

**1912 hurricane, 1938 Frome riots, Independence.** A November **1912** hurricane brought a tidal wave that nearly destroyed Savanna-la-Mar. The **1938 Frome labour riots** at the Frome sugar factory contributed directly to the formation of Jamaica's Trade Union Movement and the rise of Sir Alexander Bustamante. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Barnabas' a settled mission within the Westmoreland Anglican network.

## Architecture
St. Barnabas' is a small masonry chapel characteristic of Anglican mission buildings in the Westmoreland interior — most likely a simple rectangular structure with a pitched zinc roof and plain windows, set in a modest churchyard. Its location on the interior plain, while not at the coast, places it in a landscape that can experience significant flooding when major storms bring heavy sustained rainfall.

## Clergy
The church has been served within the Westmoreland Anglican circuit, sharing clergy with other interior missions under the oversight of the Savanna-la-Mar deanery. Lay leaders — wardens, Mothers' Union members, and lay readers — have been central to maintaining regular worship and community activities.

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** **St. Barnabas** ("son of encouragement") was the early-Christian Cypriot Levite who introduced Saul (Paul) to the apostles after his conversion (Acts 9:27) and accompanied him on the first missionary journey. His feast day is **11 June**. The dedication is one of the more common Anglican choices for mission stations, and St. Barnabas appears as the dedication of the Westmoreland cure church of St. Barnabas at George's Plain.

**Major hurricane history.** Hurricane Charlie (1951) brought prolonged rainfall to inland Westmoreland, causing flooding across the agricultural interior. Hurricane Gilbert (1988) similarly inundated the plain. Hurricane Ivan (2004) brought serious flooding inland. **Hurricane Melissa** made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Beeston Spring and the interior plain experienced damaging winds and heavy rain, and the congregation was part of the wider diocesan recovery response. Across the Diocese, some **50 church buildings were destroyed or significantly damaged**.
