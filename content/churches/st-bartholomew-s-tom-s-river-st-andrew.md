# St. Bartholomew's, Tom's River
**St. Andrew** · Church · active

St. Bartholomew's Anglican Church serves Tom's River, a community in northern St. Andrew in the hill country above the suburban fringe. Tom's River is a small residential and agricultural district, and the church has provided Anglican ministry here for several generations. The dedication to Bartholomew the Apostle, traditionally identified with Nathanael whom Philip brought to Jesus, is found in several Jamaican Anglican churches.

## History
Tom's River sits in the northern hill country of St. Andrew, in the elevated terrain beyond the Stony Hill area. The Anglican Diocese established St. Bartholomew's to serve this community. The name Tom's River likely preserves the memory of a colonial estate owner or settler.

## Architecture
St. Bartholomew's is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural St. Andrew in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. Set in the rural interior of St. Andrew. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
Tom's River has been served within the northern St. Andrew deanery circuit under shared clergy arrangements.

## Notable Facts
Bartholomew's feast day (24 August) falls in the height of the Atlantic hurricane season, and St. Bartholomew's churches across the Caribbean have historically faced their patron's feast day under the shadow of storm season. Tom's River's hill position means direct surge is not a primary risk, but the heavy orographic rainfall of the Blue Mountain approaches creates significant flood and landslide risk during major hurricane events.
