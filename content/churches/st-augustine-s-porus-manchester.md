# St. Augustine's, Porus
**Manchester** · Church · active

St. Augustine's Anglican Church serves Porus, the market town at the junction of the main road from May Pen to Mandeville. Porus (also written historically as "Porous") is a significant commercial crossroads in eastern Manchester near the Clarendon border, and the church has served this gateway community for several generations. The Diocese of Jamaica assigns Porus to the Manchester Deanery; the preferred spelling of the town in the diocesan directory is **Porus**.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1814).** Manchester was created by **Act 55 George III. c. 23** of **1814** in response to a petition of 29 November 1814 by residents of Mile Gully, May Day, and Carpenter's Mountain. The Act came into effect on **13 December 1814**, taking the "hill country" of eastern St. Elizabeth and the western portions of Vere and Clarendon. The new parish was named after **William Montagu, 5th Duke of Manchester** (Governor 1808–1827); **Mandeville**, the capital, was named after his son, **Viscount Mandeville**.

Porus sits at the junction of Manchester and Clarendon, on the main road between May Pen and Mandeville. This makes it a commercial crossroads serving travellers and the surrounding farming communities. The Anglican Diocese established St. Augustine's to serve Porus, and the church's presence at this road junction has given it a wider community reach than a purely rural interior church.

**Slavery, the parish church, and the Sam Sharpe Rebellion (1831).** Manchester's economy was built on coffee in the hill country and sugar in lower zones, both worked by enslaved African labour. **St. Mark's Anglican Church, Mandeville** — the parish church — was completed and consecrated in **1820**, and its organ loft was used as a jail during the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**. Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Porus as a free village.** Porus was founded as a **free village for ex-slaves** following Emancipation in 1834 and full freedom on **1 August 1838**: missionaries purchased tracts of land and divided them into parcels for sale to ex-slaves, and many freed people migrated here to grow coffee and other crops (Jamaica Observer, "The story of Manchester part III: Porus," 13 Jul 2014). **Maidstone** in Manchester (1840) was likewise a Moravian-led free village. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of the Porus cure.** The Anglican Diocese established St. Augustine's as the cure church for the Porus free-village landscape. The 1900 Handbook of Jamaica records "Porus & Chantilly" as a cure under **Rev. W. Lund**. Bishop Enos Nuttall's tenure (1880–1916) saw the Diocese grow from 26 rural mission stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

**Twentieth century: Kendal, bauxite, Independence.** The **Kendal train tragedy** of **1 September 1957** at Kendal Manchester claimed almost 200 lives — Jamaica's worst rail accident. The **bauxite industry** transformed Manchester from 1957 onward. **Porus High School** celebrated its 50th anniversary at the church in 2013 (Jamaica Gleaner). Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Augustine's an active cure church.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of St. Augustine's Porus after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. Augustine's is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural Manchester in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. Set in the rural interior of Manchester. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
St. Augustine's Porus is the cure church of the Porus circuit, which also covers St. Stephen's Chantilly and St. James' Toll Gate. The cure rectory operates from Porus.

## Notable Facts
Porus is one of the commercial crossroads of central Jamaica, where the road from Kingston and the coast meets the road up to Mandeville and the highlands. Porus was founded as a free village for ex-slaves following Emancipation in 1838; missionaries purchased tracts of land and divided them into parcels for sale to ex-slaves, and many freed people migrated here to grow coffee and other crops.
