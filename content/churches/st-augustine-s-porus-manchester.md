# St. Augustine's, Porus
**Manchester** · Church · active

St. Augustine's Anglican Church serves Porus, the market town at the junction of the main road from May Pen to Mandeville. Porus (also written historically as "Porous") is a significant commercial crossroads in eastern Manchester near the Clarendon border, and the church has served this gateway community for several generations. The Diocese of Jamaica assigns Porus to the Manchester Deanery; the preferred spelling of the town in the diocesan directory is **Porus**.

## History
Porus sits at the junction of Manchester and Clarendon, on the main road between May Pen and Mandeville. This makes it a commercial crossroads serving travellers and the surrounding farming communities. The Anglican Diocese established St. Augustine's to serve Porus, and the church's presence at this road junction has given it a wider community reach than a purely rural interior church.

## Architecture
St. Augustine's is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural Manchester in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. Set in the rural interior of Manchester. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
St. Augustine's Porus is the cure church of the Porus circuit, which also covers St. Stephen's Chantilly and St. James' Toll Gate. The cure rectory operates from Porus.

## Notable Facts
Porus is one of the commercial crossroads of central Jamaica, where the road from Kingston and the coast meets the road up to Mandeville and the highlands. Porus was founded as a free village for ex-slaves following Emancipation in 1838; missionaries purchased tracts of land and divided them into parcels for sale to ex-slaves, and many freed people migrated here to grow coffee and other crops.
