# St. Mark's, Hopewell
**Westmoreland** · Church · active

St. Mark's Anglican Church serves Hopewell, a community in the Sheffield District of interior Westmoreland — not to be confused with the more familiar coastal Hopewell in Hanover on the Montego Bay–Lucea corridor. The CSV coordinates (18.25, –78.29) place this church in the interior near Darliston. The church is listed in the Diocese of Jamaica and the Cayman Islands directory under the Westmoreland Deanery.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1703).** Westmoreland was officially formed in **1703** from the western portion of the older parish of St. Elizabeth, named for its position at the western part of the island. The parish capital, **Savanna-la-Mar**, was founded around 1730. In 1723 the new parish of Hanover was carved out of Westmoreland's northern section.

**Slavery and the sugar economy.** Under English rule, sugar and slavery accelerated in Westmoreland: an inventory in **1722** numbered the estates at **73**. The Beckford family — Richard, Peter, and William — were among the most prominent planters; modern Petersfield is named for Peter Beckford. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**The Sam Sharpe Rebellion (December 1831).** The Christmas Rebellion of 1831 — led by the Baptist deacon Samuel Sharpe and ignited at the Kensington Estate in St. James on 27 December 1831 — spread rapidly through the western parishes (St. James, Trelawny, Hanover, and Westmoreland) with some 60,000 enslaved people involved. The Colonial Church Union's vigilante destruction of chapels included Sav.-la-Mar (Ellis, *Diocese of Jamaica*, 1913). Sharpe was hanged on 23 May 1832 and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation and post-1838 Westmoreland.** The British Parliament passed the Slavery Abolition Act in 1833; Emancipation came in 1834, and full freedom followed on **1 August 1838**. By 1854 the number of active sugar estates in Westmoreland had collapsed to 34, less than half the 1722 figure. Freed people settled the upland districts as small farmers — communities like the interior Hopewell belong to that wave of post-Emancipation free settlement, alongside Mt. Airy, Cornwall Mountain, Sheffield, and Kentucky.

**Foundation of St. Mark's.** A **"Hopewell"** mission station is recorded in the 1891 Handbook of Jamaica under the Hanover cure of Rev. J. Ord at St. Saviour's — but that entry refers to the **Hanover/coastal Hopewell**, not the Westmoreland one. The Westmoreland Hopewell does not appear in the 1891 list, indicating a later foundation, most likely during Bishop Enos Nuttall's rural mission expansion (Diocese grew from 26 rural stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**). After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and the resulting Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**1912 hurricane, 1938 Frome riots, Independence.** A November **1912** hurricane brought a tidal wave that nearly destroyed Savanna-la-Mar. The **1938 Frome labour riots** at the Frome sugar factory contributed directly to the formation of Jamaica's Trade Union Movement and the rise of Sir Alexander Bustamante. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Mark's an established mission within the Westmoreland network.

## Architecture
St. Mark's is a small masonry rural chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for mission stations across the Westmoreland uplands. No detailed architectural description has been identified in the public sources reviewed.

## Clergy
St. Mark's is served within the Westmoreland Deanery under shared clergy arrangements, with lay leaders and wardens sustaining regular worship.

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** Mark the Evangelist — author of the second Gospel, traditionally associated with Alexandria, with a feast day on **25 April** — is the patron of three other Jamaican Anglican churches we know of (St. Mark's at Mandeville, the Manchester Parish Church 1820; St. Mark's Siloah in St. Elizabeth; St. Mark's Hopewell in St. James).

**Three Hopewells.** There are three separate Hopewells in western Jamaica with Anglican associations — in **Hanover** (coastal, on the north coast near Round Hill); in **St. James** (central parish); and in **Westmoreland** (Sheffield District). This entry is the Westmoreland Hopewell.

**Hurricane Melissa (28 October 2025)** made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Interior communities across Westmoreland experienced destructive winds. Across the Diocese of Jamaica and the Cayman Islands, some **50 church buildings were destroyed or significantly damaged**. The specific status of St. Mark's Hopewell after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.
