# St. Mark's, Hopewell
**Hanover** · Church · active

St. Mark's Anglican Church in Hopewell serves as a significant coastal parish church strategically located along the main corridor between Montego Bay and Lucea. Positioned just inland from the picturesque bays that once facilitated sugar exports and now host luxury resorts like Round Hill, the church embodies the transition from Jamaica's plantation era to its modern tourism economy. Recognized as an important component of Hanover's heritage landscape, it appears in official listings of Jamaica's notable historic churches. Today, it maintains an active local congregation while simultaneously attracting visitors interested in genealogical research and coastal village history.

## History
Hopewell evolved as a crucial north coast port and service village situated between Sandy Bay and Lucea, historically connected to surrounding sugar estates and later to the development of the exclusive Round Hill resort community. Within this evolving social and economic context, St. Mark's established itself as the primary Anglican institution, providing essential sacramental services including baptisms, marriages, and burials for generations of coastal families. The church's graveyard holds particular significance, with its historic tombstones having been systematically photographed and indexed for genealogical research, creating a valuable resource for descendants and historians. By the late twentieth century, the congregation had transformed to include both longstanding village families and hospitality workers from the growing tourism sector, leading to its official designation among Hanover's protected heritage sites.

## Architecture
Photographic evidence reveals a modest yet distinctive rural church constructed primarily from local stone, featuring a straightforward rectangular plan with low eaves and a characteristically steeply pitched roof now covered in modern red sheeting. The building exhibits subtle Gothic influences through its narrow pointed-arch windows, minimal buttressing, and modest porch entrance, while the unpainted natural stonework maintains an authentically Jamaican aesthetic. The surrounding churchyard, dotted with well-preserved nineteenth and early twentieth-century tombs, creates a cohesive historical environment that reinforces the building's long-standing role as a generational community anchor. The structure's simple yet durable design reflects traditional Jamaican ecclesiastical architecture adapted to the coastal environment.

## Clergy
Within the current diocesan organizational structure, St. Mark's falls under the Montego Bay Region, with the diocesan directory identifying Reverend Isaac Nugent as the priest in charge and grouping the church with other western coastal parishes. Historically, a church of this size and location would have operated under a shared ministry model, with rectors and curates regularly traveling between Hopewell, Negril, Little London, and other points along the coastal corridor to conduct services and provide pastoral care. This tradition of collaborative ministry continues in contemporary practice, effectively integrating the Hopewell congregation into the broader Anglican network across western Jamaica and ensuring its connection to regional religious life despite its relatively small size.

## Notable Facts
The Jamaica National Heritage Trust officially lists St. Mark's among Hanover's key historic churches and specifically notes its prominent location along the main road between Hopewell and Sandy Bay, emphasizing its visibility to travelers along the north coast corridor. Community heritage tours based in Hopewell frequently highlight local history, including the Round Hill development and general village life, often using the Anglican church and its cemetery as primary reference points when narrating the area's historical evolution. For genealogists and family historians, the published index of tombstone photographs from St. Mark's churchyard has established the site as an essential research destination for people tracing ancestral connections across both Hanover and neighboring Westmoreland parishes, adding academic significance to its religious and architectural importance. Hurricane Melissa (October 2025) made landfall in western Jamaica; Hopewell, on the north Hanover coast, was on the less-exposed side of the storm's track but experienced significant wind and rain. The confirmed Anglican loss in Hanover from the storm was the Ramble Anglican Church (not in this dataset), which was demolished; St Mark's Hopewell's specific damage status has not been separately reported.
