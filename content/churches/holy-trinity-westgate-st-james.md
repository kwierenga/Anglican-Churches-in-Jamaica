# Holy Trinity, Westgate
**St. James** · Church · active

Holy Trinity Anglican Church at Westgate serves a community in the western urban fringe of Montego Bay, in the district between the city centre and the expanding western suburbs. Westgate — named for its position at the western approach to the town — developed as a mixed residential and commercial area as Montego Bay grew through the twentieth century. The church has been a launching pad for diocesan leaders: the Rt Rev'd Canon **Garth Minott** served at Holy Trinity from **1992–94** before going on to be elected **Suffragan Bishop of Kingston** in April 2022.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1664).** St. James was one of the second batch of Jamaican parishes formed after the English conquest, established around **1664** by Sir Thomas Modyford and named after **James, Duke of York** (later King James II). At its formation it was much larger than today, including the territory of present-day Trelawny and Hanover. For many years the north side of the island was sparsely settled: in **1673** only **146 persons** resided in the entire parish.

**Eighteenth-century development.** Montego Bay was made a free port in **1738**; Charles Square was laid out by James Lawrence (custos) in **1755**. Cudjoe, Maroon leader of western Jamaica, signed a peace treaty with the British in **1739**. The Second Maroon War of **1789** saw 300 Maroons fight 1,500 English soldiers. By the 1780s Montego Bay was, next to Kingston, "the most flourishing town in the island" (NLJ).

**Slavery and the Sam Sharpe Rebellion (December 1831).** Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds. The **Christmas Rebellion** of 1831, led by the Baptist deacon **Samuel Sharpe** at the Kensington Estate in St. James on the night of **27 December 1831**, was one of the largest slave rebellions in the British Caribbean — some 60,000 enslaved people involved. Sharpe was hanged in **Montego Bay marketplace** (now **Sam Sharpe Square**) on **23 May 1832**, and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation, 1865, disestablishment.** Within two years the British Parliament passed the Slavery Abolition Act (1833); Emancipation came in 1834, and full freedom followed on **1 August 1838**. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865, Crown Colony rule replaced the planter Assembly (1866); the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of Holy Trinity: a twentieth-century urban mission.** The Westgate area of Montego Bay grew as the town expanded westward beyond its colonial core, and the Anglican Diocese responded by planting mission churches in the new residential districts. Holy Trinity was established to serve the Westgate community, providing Anglican worship and pastoral care to families who found themselves beyond the immediate catchment of St. James Parish Church in the town centre. The church's history mirrors the rapid growth of Montego Bay as Jamaica's second city through the twentieth century.

**Modern era and tourism economy.** Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found Holy Trinity an established western-suburb mission. Montego Bay received city status by act of Parliament on **9 October 1980** and officially became Jamaica's second city in 1982. The St. James Parish Church in central Montego Bay had been wrecked by the **1957 earthquake** but restored.

## Architecture
Holy Trinity is a functional urban church building in the Westgate district of Montego Bay. Its location on the western approach to the city gives it a prominent position in the urban streetscape. The building serves both as a place of worship and as a community facility, with the church and its associated spaces used for a range of pastoral and community activities consistent with Anglican urban ministry. The coastal proximity of the greater Montego Bay area means all churches in the city's western corridor share exposure to hurricane conditions.

## Clergy
Holy Trinity Westgate has been served within the Montego Bay Region clergy cluster, grouped with other western Montego Bay congregations. Notably, the **Rt Rev'd Canon Garth Minott** served at Holy Trinity from **1992–94** before going on to be elected **Suffragan Bishop of Kingston** in April 2022. Lay leaders and wardens have been central to the church's community life, sustaining worship and ministry alongside visiting or shared clergy.

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** The **Holy Trinity** — Father, Son, and Holy Spirit — is one of the most traditional Anglican dedications, shared with the Diocese's Cathedral on North Street, Kingston (Holy Trinity Cathedral, 1911) and with several other Holy Trinity churches in western Jamaica including Old Harbour and Linstead.

**Major hurricane history.** The north-west coast of Jamaica, of which Montego Bay is the focal point, has experienced multiple major hurricane impacts. **Hurricane Gilbert** (1988) struck the north-west coast with extreme severity, causing extensive damage across Montego Bay and the surrounding area. **Hurricane Ivan** (2004) again affected the region. **Hurricane Melissa** (28 October 2025) made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The western corridor of Montego Bay, including Westgate, experienced damaging winds and coastal conditions, and Holy Trinity's congregation was in the storm's impact zone. Across the Diocese, some **50 church buildings were destroyed or significantly damaged**.
