# St. David's, Brittonville
**St. Ann** · Church · active

St. David’s Anglican Church serves the small hillside community of Brittonville in St. Ann. It began in the late 1800s, when freed families were forming new villages after Emancipation in 1838. Over time, the church has experienced storms, population changes, and periods of rebuilding but has remained an important part of local worship. Today, St. David’s continues to serve Brittonville as a place of faith and community gathering.

## History
Before Emancipation, most people around Brittonville lived on estates where they worked under slavery. After 1838, many freed families moved into the hills and valleys of central St. Ann to build farms and create new settlements. As these free villages grew, the Anglican Diocese sent clergy from nearby Alexandria and Moneague to hold services under trees, in homes, or in small meeting sheds. By the late 19th century, a stone mission chapel was built in Brittonville and dedicated to St. David, becoming a recognized worship center for the district. The church helped support baptisms, schooling efforts, and community life as people adjusted to freedom.

## Architecture
St. David’s is a simple stone church built in the rural Gothic style common across Jamaica. It has a rectangular nave, narrow pointed windows with wooden shutters, and a small chancel at the eastern end. The roof, once likely covered with wooden shingles, is now made of metal sheets to handle heavy rainfall in the hills. Inside, the church includes wooden pews, an open-timber roof, and a modest pulpit used for readings and sermons. The churchyard contains a number of older graves from long-time Brittonville families, adding to its historic feel.

## Clergy
St. David’s has long been part of the St. Ann Anglican Deanery, sharing clergy with churches in Alexandria, Moneague, and Claremont. Early priests traveled by horse or cart to lead services and support children’s schooling in the community. The church helped promote literacy and moral guidance during the decades after Emancipation, when education was limited in rural areas. After Jamaica’s Independence in 1962, St. David’s continued hosting Mothers’ Union meetings, youth programs, and harvest celebrations. Lay readers regularly supported the church, especially when clergy were serving several missions at once.

## Notable Facts
St. David’s has faced challenges from storms over the years. Hurricane Charlie (1951) and Hurricane Gilbert (1988) caused roof damage that the community later repaired. Occasional flooding and landslides in the Brittonville area have also affected access to the church, though the building itself sits on slightly raised ground. Today, St. David’s Anglican Church continues to hold weekly services, weddings, funerals, and harvest events, serving as a symbol of history, faith, and resilience in Brittonville.
