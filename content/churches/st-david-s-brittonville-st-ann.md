# St. David's, Brittonville
**St. Ann** · Church · active

St. David's Anglican Church serves the small hillside community of **Brittonville** in St. Ann. It began in the late 1800s, when freed families were forming new villages after Emancipation in 1838. Today, St. David's continues to serve Brittonville as a place of faith and community gathering.

## History
**Pre-English settlement: Sevilla la Nueva (1509–1534).** St. Ann has a deeper colonial past than almost any other Jamaican parish. **Sevilla la Nueva**, the first permanent European settlement in Jamaica and the colony's first Spanish capital, was founded in **1509** by Juan de Esquivel near present St. Ann's Bay and served as Spanish capital until **1534**. **Christopher Columbus** had been marooned at St. Ann's Bay for over a year (1503–1504).

**English rule (1655) and the planter church.** Under English rule from **1655**, St. Ann became a major **sugar and livestock** region. Before Emancipation, most people around Brittonville lived on estates where they worked under slavery. Until **1870**, the Church of England was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**Sam Sharpe Rebellion and the Colonial Church Union (1831–32).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, **Hamilton Brown** of Brown's Town led a Colonial Church Union mob that destroyed the Brown's Town Baptist chapel in 1832 — the **Colonial Church Union** sought to "wipe the non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth." **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation (1838) and free villages.** Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. After 1838, many freed families moved into the hills and valleys of central St. Ann to build farms and create new settlements at Brittonville and other districts. **Sturge Town** (1839, founded by the English Quaker abolitionist Joseph Sturge) and **Clarksonville** (1835, founded by Baptist missionary Rev. John Clark, named for the abolitionist Thomas Clarkson) were among Jamaica's earliest free villages — both in St. Ann (NLJ, *History of St. Ann*). Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb** (1824–1843) had begun extending Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**Disestablishment (1870) and the founding of St. David's.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. As the Brittonville free village grew, the Anglican Diocese sent clergy from nearby Alexandria and Moneague to hold services under trees, in homes, or in small meeting sheds. By the late 19th century, a stone mission chapel was built in Brittonville and dedicated to **St. David**, becoming a recognised worship centre for the district.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** Bauxite mining began in St. Ann around **1952** (Reynolds, Kaiser). **Hurricane Charlie (1951)** and **Hurricane Gilbert (1988)** caused roof damage that the community later repaired. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. David's an active mission.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of St. David's Brittonville after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. David's is a simple stone church built in the rural Gothic style common across Jamaica. It has a rectangular nave, narrow pointed windows with wooden shutters, and a small chancel at the eastern end. The roof, once likely covered with wooden shingles, is now made of metal sheets to handle heavy rainfall in the hills. Inside, the church includes wooden pews, an open-timber roof, and a modest pulpit. The churchyard contains a number of older graves from long-time Brittonville families, adding to its historic feel.

## Clergy
St. David's has long been part of the **St. Ann Anglican Deanery**, sharing clergy with churches in Alexandria, Moneague, and Claremont. Early priests travelled by horse or cart to lead services and support children's schooling in the community. Lay readers regularly supported the church, especially when clergy were serving several missions at once.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to **St. David** — patron saint of Wales (feast day **1 March**) — is one of only a small number of Welsh saint dedications in Jamaica's Anglican network, perhaps reflecting the Welsh origins of a colonial proprietor. Brittonville's name itself evokes the British origins of the colonial estate from which the community took its post-Emancipation form.
