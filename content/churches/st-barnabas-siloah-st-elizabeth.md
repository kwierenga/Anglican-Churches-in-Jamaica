# St. Barnabas', Siloah
**St. Elizabeth** · Church · active

St. Barnabas Anglican Church serves the community of Siloah in northern St. Elizabeth, near the Appleton Valley. It was founded in the late 1800s by freed families who settled the area after Emancipation in 1838. Despite facing hurricanes and other challenges over the years, the church has remained a strong part of village life. Today, it continues to be a steady place for worship and community gatherings in Siloah.

## History
St. Barnabas Anglican Church was established in the late 19th century, as the community of Siloah grew with families leaving large sugar estates like Appleton to build independent lives after Emancipation. The area's history was deeply shaped by the Appleton Estate, where generations had lived under slavery. In response to this new settlement, the Anglican Diocese began sending clergy from Maggotty and Lacovia to hold services. By the 1880s, a permanent stone chapel was completed and dedicated to St. Barnabas, a biblical figure known for encouragement. The church quickly became a central hub for baptisms, marriages, education, and village meetings, supporting families in the post-slavery era.

## Architecture
St. Barnabas is a simple, sturdy church built from local limestone in the rural Gothic Revival style. It features a rectangular nave, a small chancel, and a modest bell-cot on the western gable. The steep roof, now covered with metal, was likely originally made of wooden shingles. Inside, the church has an open-timber roof, wooden pews, and narrow pointed windows with colored glass. The surrounding churchyard, with graves of early Siloah families, contributes to the site's peaceful and historic character.

## Clergy
St. Barnabas has always been part of the Black River Deanery, sharing clergy with other churches in Maggotty, Thornton, and the local Wesleyan mission. Early priests, such as Rev. H. C. Wharton and Canon W. R. Campbell, included Siloah on their missionary circuits. The church was instrumental in supporting early education, providing local children with reading, writing, and religious instruction when schools were scarce. After Jamaica’s Independence in 1962, the congregation remained active through youth programs and the Mothers’ Union. Lay readers were essential in maintaining regular worship when ordained clergy were serving multiple parishes.

## Notable Facts
St. Barnabas has endured several major hurricanes, including Hurricane Charlie (1951) and Hurricane Gilbert (1988), which damaged the roof and windows. Each time, the community, with support from the Diocese, carried out repairs. Further restoration work in more recent years has helped maintain the building's structure. Today, St. Barnabas Anglican Church remains active, holding weekly services, weddings, funerals, and harvest festivals, continuing its role as a enduring symbol of faith and resilience in Siloah.
