# St. Joseph's, The Grove
**St. Andrew** · Church · active

St. Joseph's Anglican Church serves The Grove, a hillside community on Gordon Town Road in eastern St. Andrew where the Hope River meets the Mammee River. Originally constructed in 1827 — just two years after the first Bishop of Jamaica arrived — the church is one of the earliest Anglican foundations in St. Andrew outside Half-Way-Tree. Rebuilt after the 1907 earthquake and consecrated on 22 January 1911, it is part of a grouped parish with St. Margaret's, Liguanea and St. Cyprian's, August Town, under the current rector the Rev. Franklyn Jackson.

## History
St. Joseph's was built in 1827 during the episcopate of Bishop Christopher Lipscomb, Jamaica's first Anglican bishop, who arrived in February 1825 and consecrated 31 churches during his nineteen-year tenure. Before Lipscomb, St. Andrew Parish Church at Half-Way-Tree (established 1664) was the sole Anglican church in the entire parish. St. Joseph's was among the earliest chapels established to serve communities too distant from Half-Way-Tree for regular attendance — in this case, the settlements along Gordon Town Road in the Hope River valley.

The original building was constructed using white lime, sand, and molasses — the same Georgian-era mortar technique used for the nearby Alberga Bridge, built around 1789. The church and the bridge are described as the only structures remaining in Jamaica of this type of construction, making the church fabric itself a piece of Georgian architectural heritage.

The Grove sits in the heart of Jamaica's Hope Estate landscape, surrounded by land with deep roots in slavery. Hope Estate, founded by Major Richard Hope in the 1660s on 2,600 acres of the Liguanea Plain, was one of the island's largest, most productive, and most technologically advanced slave sugar estates. By 1753, Thomas Hope Elletson owned 530 acres with 215 enslaved people. The neighbouring Papine Estate, established in 1756 by Alexander Grant — a Scot who named it after the Mill of Papine in Scotland — housed nearly 200 enslaved labourers. Mona Estate, adjacent to Papine, would later become the site of the University of the West Indies in 1948.

St. Joseph's was built in 1827, seven years before the Emancipation Act of 1834. Its congregation would have been drawn from the communities of enslaved and free people on these estates and in the surrounding hills. After full freedom came on 1 August 1838, the church served as a spiritual home through the transformation from plantation labour to free settlement. The founding of Grove Primary School by the Anglican Church in 1894 extended this mission into education for the descendants of the enslaved.

By the 1891 Handbook of Jamaica, The Grove had 288 registered members, 34 baptisms, and 9 marriages, served by H. S. Isaacs, M.A., who also served Woodford. By 1900, D. W. Bland had been assigned to the same cure.

On 14 January 1907, an earthquake devastated Kingston and eastern St. Andrew, destroying or damaging 130 church-owned buildings across the Diocese. The 1827 structure at The Grove was among the casualties. It was rebuilt and consecrated on 22 January 1911 — the same day as the extension to St. Andrew Parish Church at Half-Way-Tree — as one of ten churches consecrated within a single month under Archbishop Nuttall's reconstruction programme. The rebuilding employed reinforced concrete for earthquake resistance, a significant departure from the original lime-and-molasses construction. Jamaica's independence in 1962 brought new development to the surrounding area, though The Grove itself has remained a modest hillside settlement.

## Architecture
The original 1827 church was built in the Georgian style using white lime, sand, and molasses mortar — a construction method shared with the Alberga Bridge and now exceedingly rare in Jamaica. The post-1907 rebuilding produced the current structure in reinforced concrete, consistent with the Jamaican hybrid of Georgian and Gothic elements typical of the reconstruction era. The church sits on a hillside overlooking the Hope River at the junction of Gordon Town Road.

## Education
Grove Primary and Infant School, founded in 1894 by the Anglican Church, is located on a hillside overlooking the Hope River. Its wooden and zinc classrooms have remained largely unchanged since its founding. Current enrolment is approximately 110 students, though the school has capacity for over 300. Students come from surrounding communities including Mud Town, Kintyre, Mount Industry, Cornfield, and as far as Molynes Road. Principal Francine Taylor Arnett, who took charge in 2021, has led improvements to lighting, piping, and reading and mathematics intervention programmes. The church also historically operated an early childhood programme, St. Joseph the Grove Basic School, whose building the school administration aims to rehabilitate.

## Clergy
The Grove and Woodford have been served together since at least 1891. Today, St. Joseph's is part of a grouped parish with St. Margaret's, Liguanea and St. Cyprian's, August Town, under the Rev. Franklyn Jackson, with assistant Melrose Wiggan.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to St. Joseph — the foster father of Jesus and patron saint of workers, carpenters, families, and fathers — is relatively uncommon among Jamaican Anglican churches. For a community whose members were largely manual labourers and farmers descended from enslaved plantation workers, the patronage of St. Joseph the Worker carried particular resonance. His feast day is 19 March. The church's 1827 lime-sand-molasses construction links it to a Georgian building tradition that has almost entirely disappeared from the Jamaican landscape.
