# St. George's, Savanna-la-Mar (Parish Church)
**Westmoreland** · Parish Church · active

St. George's Anglican Church is the Parish Church of Westmoreland, situated in Savanna-la-Mar, the parish capital on Jamaica's south-western coast. It is one of the oldest churches in western Jamaica, its origins reaching back to the 18th century when the town was a centre for the sugar and rum trade. Few Anglican churches in Jamaica have faced as many catastrophic events as St. George's: it has been repeatedly destroyed by hurricanes and rebuilt, each time restoring the congregation's spiritual home. Today it remains the mother church of the Westmoreland deanery, serving both the town and as the hub of the Diocese's pastoral network across the parish.

## History
The Anglican presence in Savanna-la-Mar dates to the early 18th century. The town's very name — 'Savannah of the Sea' — reflects its exposed coastal position, and this geography shaped the church's history profoundly. The original parish church was destroyed in the catastrophic hurricane of 1780, one of the deadliest Atlantic hurricanes ever recorded, which levelled much of Savanna-la-Mar and killed hundreds. A replacement was built but suffered severe damage in the 1818 hurricane. Further destruction came with the great hurricane of October 1944, which devastated the south coast. Each time the congregation rebuilt. The church's post-Emancipation history saw it serve newly freed people who had been enslaved on the vast sugar estates of the Westmoreland plain, the flattest and most intensively cultivated landscape in Jamaica.

## Architecture
The present St. George's is a solid masonry church in the tradition of Jamaican parish churches, rebuilt and modified multiple times following hurricane damage. Its structure reflects both the need for resilience — thick walls, a low profile where possible — and the civic dignity expected of a parish church. The interior retains historic memorials and fittings accumulated over nearly three centuries of parish life. The churchyard contains graves dating to the colonial period, including planters, clergy, and leading families of the old sugar economy, making it one of the more historically significant burial grounds in western Jamaica.

## Clergy
St. George's has been served by rectors of considerable standing in the Diocese, given its status as the parish church of one of Jamaica's most significant agricultural parishes. Clergy here oversaw not only the town congregation but missions across the Westmoreland plain and into the interior hills. The church has been central to major civic and national occasions in the parish, including services for Independence, national disasters, and memorial observances for prominent Westmoreland figures. It continues to lead the parish's Anglican network, coordinating ministry across the many smaller churches and chapels of the deanery.

## Notable Facts
Savanna-la-Mar holds a grim distinction as the most hurricane-battered town in Jamaica, and St. George's has been destroyed and rebuilt more than once as a direct result. The 1780 hurricane was so catastrophic it is still considered among the deadliest natural disasters in Caribbean history, and the parish church was completely obliterated. Hurricane Charlie in 1951 again caused severe damage to church buildings in Savanna-la-Mar. Hurricane Ivan in 2004 brought destructive storm surge to the town. George's served as a coordination point for relief. Hurricane Melissa (October 28, 2025) peeled the roof from St George's Church while leaving the walls structurally intact. The Jamaica Gleaner reported that Anglican Bishop Hartley Perrin stood before the building and described the damage: 'The physical structure, in terms of the walls and so on, they are still there. It's a matter of rafters, sarking and recovering. It can be done.' Savanna-la-Mar, the parish capital of Westmoreland, was within Melissa's direct impact zone as the storm made landfall near New Hope, Westmoreland. The congregation continued worship efforts in the damaged building while reconstruction planning began. St George's, as the parish church of Westmoreland, serves a key pastoral and civic role in the post-Melissa recovery of the town.
