# St. George's, Savanna-la-Mar (Parish Church)
**Westmoreland** · Parish Church · active

St. George's Anglican Church is the parish church of Westmoreland, standing in Savanna-la-Mar on Jamaica's south-western coast. Its origins reach back to the early eighteenth century, when the town was a principal port for the sugar and rum trade of the Westmoreland plain — the flattest and most intensively cultivated plantation landscape on the island. Few Anglican churches in Jamaica have endured as many catastrophic events as St. George's: repeatedly destroyed by hurricanes and rebuilt each time, it remains today the mother church of the Westmoreland deanery.

## History
The Anglican presence in Savanna-la-Mar dates to the early eighteenth century. The town's very name — "Savannah of the Sea" — reflects its exposed coastal position on the south-western plain, and this geography has shaped the church's history profoundly.

Westmoreland's economy was built on slavery. The parish encompassed some of the largest and most productive sugar estates in the British Caribbean, their wealth concentrated in a planter class who governed from Savanna-la-Mar and shipped their produce through its harbour. The Anglican Church served as the established church of this system — its vestry composed of the planter class, its clergy funded by the colonial government, its parish administration extending to roads, poor relief, and the maintenance of social order.

The original parish church was destroyed in the catastrophic hurricane of 3 October 1780, one of the deadliest Atlantic hurricanes ever recorded, which levelled much of Savanna-la-Mar and killed hundreds. A replacement was built but suffered severe damage in the 1818 hurricane.

Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on 1 August 1838. The freed population of Westmoreland — who had laboured on the sugar estates of the coastal plain — established free villages across the parish, and the Anglican Church expanded its mission to serve these communities. Further destruction of the parish church came with the great hurricane of October 1944. Each time the congregation rebuilt.

Jamaica's independence on 6 August 1962 found St. George's at the civic centre of Westmoreland, serving a parish whose identity was shaped by its sugar heritage, its exposed coastline, and the recurring cycle of storm damage and reconstruction.

## Architecture
The present St. George's is a solid masonry church in the tradition of Jamaican parish churches, rebuilt and modified multiple times following hurricane damage. Its structure reflects both the need for resilience — thick walls, a low profile where possible — and the civic dignity expected of a parish church. The interior retains historic memorials and fittings accumulated over nearly three centuries of parish life. The churchyard contains graves dating to the colonial period, including planters, clergy, and leading families of the old sugar economy, making it one of the more historically significant burial grounds in western Jamaica.

## Education
The Anglican mission to educate was central to Westmoreland's post-Emancipation development, continuing Bishop Lipscomb's initiative which founded over 80 elementary schools across Jamaica in the 1830s. Church schools were established alongside chapels across the parish, providing literacy and education to the communities of freed people.

## Clergy
St. George's has been served by rectors of considerable standing in the Diocese, given its status as the parish church of one of Jamaica's most significant agricultural parishes. Clergy here oversaw not only the town congregation but missions across the Westmoreland plain and into the interior hills, coordinating the Diocese's presence across the many smaller churches and chapels of the deanery.

## Notable Facts
Savanna-la-Mar holds a grim distinction as the most hurricane-battered town in Jamaica. The 1780 hurricane was so catastrophic it remains among the deadliest natural disasters in Caribbean history, and the parish church was completely obliterated. Subsequent storms — including the hurricane of 1818, the October 1944 hurricane, Hurricane Charlie (1951), and Hurricane Ivan (2004) — each caused significant damage. Hurricane Melissa (28 October 2025) made landfall near New Hope, Westmoreland, and peeled the roof from St. George's while leaving the walls structurally intact. The Jamaica Gleaner reported Anglican Bishop Hartley Perrin standing before the damaged building: "The physical structure, in terms of the walls and so on, they are still there. It's a matter of rafters, sarking and recovering. It can be done." Savanna-la-Mar, as Westmoreland's parish capital, was within Melissa's direct impact zone, and St. George's serves a central pastoral and civic role in the post-Melissa recovery of the town.
