# St. George's, Savanna-la-Mar (Parish Church)
**Westmoreland** · Parish Church · active

St. George's Anglican Church is the parish church of Westmoreland, standing on Great George Street in Savanna-la-Mar on Jamaica's south-western coast. Anglican worship on the site dates to 1739, and the present stone church is the third on the ground — its foundation stone laid on St George's Day 1903 and consecrated on St George's Day 1904. Its first church was destroyed in the catastrophic hurricane of 1780, and the building has been damaged and restored in every major storm of the last century, most recently by Hurricane Melissa in October 2025.

## History
The town's very name — "Savannah of the Sea" — reflects its exposed coastal position on the south-western plain. The parish of Westmoreland was formed in 1703 from a division of St. Elizabeth, whose expanse was considered large enough for two parishes; in 1723 a further section of Westmoreland was carved off to form Hanover. Savanna-la-Mar itself was founded around 1730 on a narrow strip of land between mangrove swamps; before that, the chief town of the area was Queen's Town (now Cross Path). Anglican records for Savanna-la-Mar begin in 1739, when the first church was erected near the shoreline to serve a town that was becoming one of the principal ports of the sugar economy. Westmoreland contained some of the largest and most productive sugar estates in the British Caribbean, and until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, its vestry composed of the planter class and its parish administration extending to roads, poor relief, and the maintenance of social order.

Savanna-la-Mar has long been the most hurricane-battered town in Jamaica. In 1748, the town was almost decimated by a hurricane. On 8 October 1780 — in one of the deadliest Atlantic hurricanes ever recorded — a catastrophic storm and tidal wave levelled Savanna-la-Mar, destroying the town and the parish church. For some years, services were held in temporary accommodations. In 1797, the foundation stone of a second church was laid; the wooden structure, originally intended as temporary, opened for services in 1799 and served the congregation for more than a century. In 1790 another storm-surge event saw the sea "sweep up the beach for nearly a mile."

Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on 1 August 1838. The freed population of Westmoreland — who had laboured on the sugar estates of the coastal plain — established free villages across the parish. In 1870 the Church of England was disestablished in Jamaica, and parish oversight passed to the Diocese of Jamaica.

By the early twentieth century the wooden second church had fallen into disrepair. The foundation stone of the present building was laid on St George's Day 1903, and the new stone church was consecrated on St George's Day 1904. In November 1912, after nine days of rain, another hurricane struck Savanna-la-Mar with a tidal wave that swept the schooner *Latonia* ashore to sail along Great Georges Street; according to contemporary accounts, only Mannings School, the teacher's residence, and the Bank of Nova Scotia were spared. Exactly a century after the new church's consecration — in September 2004 — the building was extensively damaged by Hurricane Ivan. Restoration, costing J$15.5 million, began in April 2005 and was completed in September 2006.

Jamaica's independence on 6 August 1962 found St. George's at the civic centre of Westmoreland, and the restored 1904 building remains the mother church of the Westmoreland deanery.

## Architecture
The present church is a solid cut-stone building measuring 105 feet 3 inches in length by 56 feet in width, with an apse of 18 feet 6 inches by 23 feet. The foundation stone of the original 1780 church was recovered during excavation for the present building; a brass plate commemorating the discovery is set into a column at the western entrance. The interior retains memorials and fittings accumulated across three churches and nearly three centuries of parish life.

## Education
The Anglican mission to educate expanded across Westmoreland after Emancipation, part of the Diocese's wider effort to provide schools for the freed population. Church schools were established alongside chapels across the parish, providing literacy and education to the communities of freed people.

## Clergy
St. George's has been served by rectors of considerable standing in the Diocese, given its status as the parish church of one of Jamaica's most significant agricultural parishes. Clergy here have overseen the town congregation as well as missions across the Westmoreland plain and the interior hills.

## Notable Facts
The 8 October 1780 hurricane and tidal wave remains among the deadliest natural disasters in Caribbean history, and the parish church was completely obliterated. Hurricane Ivan (September 2004) damaged the present building exactly one hundred years after its 1904 consecration; the J$15.5 million restoration took from April 2005 to September 2006. Hurricane Melissa (28 October 2025) made landfall near New Hope, Westmoreland, and Savanna-la-Mar sustained catastrophic damage across public buildings, schools, and places of worship. St. George's was among the churches reported with roof damage in the Diocese's post-Melissa appeal, and the Custos of Westmoreland, Rev. Canon Hartley Perrin — Rector of St. Peter's, Petersfield — was widely quoted describing the scale of the town's destruction.
