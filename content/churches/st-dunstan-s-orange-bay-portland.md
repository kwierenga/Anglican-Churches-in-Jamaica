# St. Dunstan's, Orange Bay
**Portland** · Church · active

St. Dunstan's Anglican Church serves Orange Bay, a community on the coast of Portland parish. Orange Bay is a small coastal settlement on Portland's north coast, and St. Dunstan's is one of the most unusually dedicated churches in Jamaica's Anglican network — St. Dunstan of Canterbury is rarely commemorated in Jamaican churches. The church has served the Orange Bay community for several generations.

## History
Orange Bay sits on the Portland north coast, a small community in the coastal plain of western Portland. The Anglican Diocese established St. Dunstan's as part of its coastal mission network, choosing the unusual dedication to the 10th-century Archbishop of Canterbury who reformed the English church and monastic life. The rarity of this dedication in Jamaica suggests a specific connection to an early patron or clergyman with ties to the Dunstan tradition.

## Architecture
St. Dunstan's is a coastal church on the Portland north coast, exposed to the Caribbean Sea. The coastal plain position creates storm surge vulnerability during hurricane passages, and the proximity of hills creates compound landslide risk during heavy rainfall.

## Clergy
Orange Bay has been served within the western Portland deanery circuit. The coastal community maintains its congregation through the local fishing and farming families.

## Notable Facts
St. Dunstan's unusual dedication — commemorating a medieval Archbishop of Canterbury revered for his craftsmanship (he was a smith) as well as his ecclesiastical reforms — makes it one of the most distinctive church names in Jamaica's Anglican network. St. Dunstan is the patron saint of goldsmiths, locksmiths, and armourers. Hurricane Melissa (October 2025) brought serious coastal impacts to Portland's north coast; Orange Bay was in the storm's direct path.
