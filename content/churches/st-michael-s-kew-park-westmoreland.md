# St. Michael and All Angels, Kew Park (Rat Trap)
**Westmoreland** · Church · active

The Anglican Church of St. Michael and All Angels at Rat Trap (Kew Park) served the interior farming community of central Westmoreland, in the country between Savanna-la-Mar and the hill districts to the north and east. It was effectively destroyed by Hurricane Melissa on **28 October 2025** — *"only a few stones remain,"* in the words of *Jamaica Gleaner* columnist Peter Espeut, who passed through the site in the storm's aftermath. The church was built in **1865** on the 939-acre Kew Park Estate at Bethel Town, a property continuously held by the Williams family since 1850.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1703).** Westmoreland was officially formed in **1703** from the western portion of the older parish of St. Elizabeth, named for its position at the western part of the island. The parish capital, **Savanna-la-Mar**, was founded around 1730. In 1723 the new parish of Hanover was carved out of Westmoreland's northern section.

**Slavery and the sugar economy.** Under English rule, sugar and slavery accelerated in Westmoreland: an inventory in **1722** numbered the estates at **73**, making the parish one of the leading sugar producers of the British Caribbean. The Beckford family — Richard, Peter, and William — were among the most prominent planters; modern Petersfield is named for Peter Beckford. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds; its parish vestry was composed of the planter class.

**The Sam Sharpe Rebellion (December 1831).** The Christmas Rebellion of 1831, led by the Baptist deacon Samuel Sharpe and ignited at the Kensington Estate in St. James on 27 December 1831, spread rapidly through the western parishes — St. James, Trelawny, Hanover, and Westmoreland — with some 60,000 enslaved people involved. The Colonial Church Union's vigilante destruction of chapels included Sav.-la-Mar (Ellis, *Diocese of Jamaica*, 1913). Sharpe was hanged on 23 May 1832 and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation, the Williams family, and the church's foundation in 1865.** The British Parliament passed the Slavery Abolition Act in 1833; Emancipation came in 1834, and full freedom followed on **1 August 1838**. By 1854 the number of active sugar estates in Westmoreland had collapsed to 34, less than half the 1722 figure. The Williams family acquired the **939-acre Kew Park Estate at Bethel Town in 1850** — twelve years after full freedom — and have held the property continuously since. James Rowland Williams is recorded in colonial-era documentation as a planter and cattleman of Kew Park. The local name "Rat Trap" is said by parish tradition to derive from a shop-owner who boasted that his shop could attract anyone in the district whether they liked him or not. **St. Michael and All Angels was built on the Kew Park Estate in 1865** — the year of the Morant Bay Rebellion, and just before disestablishment of the Anglican Church in **1870**.

**The post-1870 Diocese.** After disestablishment the church was reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica. Bishop Enos Nuttall (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 rural stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**, of which St. Michael's was an early and continuing member.

**1912 hurricane, 1938 Frome riots, Independence.** A November **1912** hurricane brought a tidal wave that nearly destroyed Savanna-la-Mar. The **1938 Frome labour riots** at the Frome sugar factory contributed directly to the formation of Jamaica's Trade Union Movement and the rise of Sir Alexander Bustamante. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Michael and All Angels a settled rural church on the Williams family's continuing Kew Park Estate.

## Architecture
St. Michael and All Angels was a modest masonry church with an associated churchyard, built in 1865. No detailed architectural record beyond contemporary accounts has been identified in the public heritage sources reviewed.

## Clergy
Kew Park has been served within the Westmoreland Deanery under shared clergy arrangements. The Diocese of Jamaica and the Cayman Islands lists St. Michael's as one of the cures of the Westmoreland Deanery within the Montego Bay Region.

## Hurricane Melissa (October 2025)
Hurricane Melissa made landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** as a Category 5 storm — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history — tracking through the interior of the parish. Peter Espeut, writing in the *Jamaica Gleaner* on 7 November 2025 (column "Hope springs eternal"), described the scene: *"Only a few stones remain of the Anglican Church of St Michael and All Angels at Rat Trap (Kew Park)."* This is one of the most complete Anglican church losses reported in any account of the storm in Jamaica, and one of the some **50 church buildings destroyed or significantly damaged** across the Diocese of Jamaica and the Cayman Islands.

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** The full form **"St. Michael and All Angels"** — Michael, the archangel "leader of the heavenly host," together with the entire angelic company — is celebrated on **29 September** at Michaelmas, jointly with Gabriel and Raphael in the Anglican calendar. The dedication is relatively common in rural Anglican foundations in the British tradition, and the full form was in use at Rat Trap before the storm that destroyed the building.
