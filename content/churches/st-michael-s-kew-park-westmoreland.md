# St. Michael's, Kew Park
**Westmoreland** · Church · active

The Anglican Church of St. Michael and All Angels at Rat Trap (Kew Park) served the interior farming community of central Westmoreland, in the country between Savanna-la-Mar and the hill districts to the north and east. It was effectively destroyed by Hurricane Melissa on 28 October 2025 — "only a few stones remain," in the words of the Gleaner columnist Peter Espeut, who passed through the site in the storm's aftermath.

## History
Kew Park takes its name from the English estate-naming traditions of Jamaica's plantation era, echoing the Royal Botanic Gardens at Kew. The area was known as a cattle and provision property during the colonial period, with James Rowland Williams recorded as a planter and cattleman of Kew Park. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church; after disestablishment, rural mission stations like Kew Park became part of the Diocese's expanded network in the post-Emancipation decades. The community is also known locally as Rat Trap — a name said by local tradition to derive from a shop-owner who boasted that his shop could attract anyone in the district whether they liked him or not.

## Architecture
St. Michael and All Angels was a modest masonry church with an associated churchyard. No detailed historical architectural record has been identified in the public heritage sources reviewed.

## Clergy
Kew Park has been served within the Westmoreland Deanery under shared clergy arrangements. The Diocese of Jamaica and the Cayman Islands lists St. Michael's as one of the cures of the Westmoreland Deanery within the Montego Bay Region.

## Hurricane Melissa (October 2025)
Hurricane Melissa made landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on 28 October 2025 as a Category 5 storm, tracking through the interior of the parish. Peter Espeut, writing in the Jamaica Gleaner on 7 November 2025, described the scene: "Only a few stones remain of the Anglican Church of St Michael and All Angels at Rat Trap (Kew Park)." This is one of the most complete Anglican church losses reported in any account of the storm in Jamaica.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to the Archangel Michael and All Angels is celebrated on 29 September — Michaelmas — jointly with Gabriel and Raphael. The dedication is relatively common in rural Anglican foundations in the British tradition, and the full form "Michael and All Angels" was in use at Rat Trap before the storm that destroyed the building.
