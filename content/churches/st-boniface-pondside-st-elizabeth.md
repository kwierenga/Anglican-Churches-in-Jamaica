# St. Boniface, Pondside
**St. Elizabeth** · Church · active

St. Boniface Anglican Church serves Pondside, a community in St. Elizabeth parish.

## History
Pondside is one of the small farming districts of St. Elizabeth, in the parish whose southern lowlands and central uplands together formed one of the most heavily enslaved sugar landscapes of the British Caribbean. After Emancipation, freed people settled the upland districts as small farmers, and the Anglican Diocese extended its mission network across these post-Emancipation communities. St. Boniface at Pondside is one of these mission chapels.

The dedication to St. Boniface (Wynfrith) is a distinctive Anglican choice. Born in southern England in 675, baptised with the name Wynfrith ("Friend of Peace"), Boniface became the most famous English missionary of the early Middle Ages — sent by Pope Gregory II to evangelise the Germans, he reorganised the Frankish church, founded monasteries across Germany and the Low Countries, and was martyred in 754 in Frisia. He is regarded as the apostle of the Germans. The Diocese of Jamaica retains an article on its website explaining the saint's biography in connection with its Jamaican Boniface dedications. The Diocese has at least three Boniface dedications: this Pondside chapel, St. Boniface Church on St. Thomas Road in Kingston (St. Andrew), and St. Boniface, Whitehall in St. Thomas.

No substantive foundational date or architectural history specifically for St. Boniface Pondside has been identified in the public heritage sources reviewed; the church does not appear in the 1891 Handbook of Jamaica clergy listings, suggesting a foundation date in the early twentieth century.

## Architecture
St. Boniface is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for mission stations across the St. Elizabeth interior. The position offers some shelter from coastal storm surge while remaining exposed to wind and rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
St. Boniface Pondside is served within the Montego Bay Region's St. Elizabeth deanery network.
