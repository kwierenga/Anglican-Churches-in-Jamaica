# St. Boniface, Pondside
**St. Elizabeth** · Church · active

St. Boniface Anglican Church serves Pondside in St. Elizabeth, one of three diocesan dedications to the great English missionary in Jamaica (alongside St. Boniface, St. Thomas Road in Kingston/St. Andrew, and St. Boniface's, Whitehall in St. Thomas).

## History
**Founding of the parish (c.1664).** St. Elizabeth was one of the original Jamaican parishes, established under Sir Thomas Modyford and named in honour of **Lady Elizabeth Modyford**, wife of the first Governor of Jamaica (1664–71). Prior to **1703**, the parish stretched across the territory of present-day Westmoreland, Hanover, and Manchester; in 1703 Westmoreland was created from the western portion, and in **1814** the final subdivision created Manchester from the southern section. **Lacovia** was the original parish capital before Black River became the sole capital. The parish church, **St. John the Evangelist** at Black River, dates structurally from around 1700.

**Slavery and the sugar economy.** Under English rule, sugar and slavery defined the parish economy: prominent estates included Holland, Vineyard, and Fullerswood. **Cudjoe**, leader of the Leeward Maroons, signed the **Peace Treaty of 1739** at the Peace Cave in St. Elizabeth's Cockpit Country, securing Maroon land rights — and the Maroon community of **Accompong Town** still exists in the parish today. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**The Sam Sharpe Rebellion (December 1831).** The Christmas Rebellion of 1831, led by the Baptist deacon **Samuel Sharpe** at the Kensington Estate in St. James on **27 December 1831**, spread through the western parishes; some 60,000 enslaved people were involved. Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation, 1865, disestablishment.** The British Parliament passed the Slavery Abolition Act in 1833; Emancipation came in 1834, and full freedom followed at the end of the apprenticeship period on **1 August 1838**. After 1838, freed people settled the upland districts of southern St. Elizabeth as small farmers — Pondside belongs to that wave of post-Emancipation free settlement. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865, Crown Colony rule replaced the planter Assembly (1866); the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of St. Boniface: Nuttall's expansion.** Bishop Enos Nuttall's tenure as Bishop of Jamaica (1880–1916) saw the Diocese grow from 26 rural mission stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. The church does not appear in the 1891 Handbook of Jamaica's Anglican clergy returns, consistent with a foundation date in the early twentieth century.

**Twentieth century, Independence, hurricanes.** The 1893–94 logwood export boom shaped the parish economy. The bauxite industry developed in St. Elizabeth from **1943**, with **ALPART** later headquartered at Nain. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Boniface a settled rural mission.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. St. Elizabeth was severely affected, with major Anglican losses including St. Luke's Balaclava and St. Thomas' Lacovia. The specific status of St. Boniface Pondside after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. Boniface is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for mission stations across the St. Elizabeth interior. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal storm surge while remaining exposed to wind and rainfall during major storms.

## Clergy
St. Boniface Pondside is served within the Montego Bay Region's St. Elizabeth deanery network.

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** **St. Boniface** (born **Wynfrith** in 675 in southern England, baptised "Friend of Peace") was the most famous English missionary of the early Middle Ages. Sent by Pope Gregory II to evangelise the Germans, he reorganised the Frankish church, founded monasteries across Germany and the Low Countries, and was martyred in 754 in Frisia. He is regarded as the **apostle of the Germans**, and his feast day is **5 June**. The Diocese of Jamaica retains an article on its website explaining the saint's biography in connection with its three Boniface dedications: this Pondside chapel; St. Boniface Church on St. Thomas Road in Kingston (St. Andrew); and St. Boniface's, Whitehall in St. Thomas. The choice of an English missionary saint dedicated to the conversion of the Germans is a quietly evocative fit for a small mission chapel in the Jamaican countryside.
