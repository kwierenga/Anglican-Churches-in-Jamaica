# Church of the Holy Trinity, Linstead
**St. Catherine** · Church · active

The Church of the Holy Trinity provides one of the two Anglican worship points in **Linstead** — alongside St. John's — in St. Catherine's main interior market town, located on the road north from Spanish Town towards Ewarton. The Trinity dedication, naming Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, is shared with the diocese's cathedral on North Street, Kingston.

## History
**Spanish Villa de la Vega (1534) and English conquest (1655).** The Spanish founded **Villa de la Vega** in 1534 — Jamaica's capital until **1872**. Penn and Venables's English expedition captured Jamaica in May **1655**.

**Founding parishes.** Under English rule from 1655, **St. Catherine** was one of Jamaica's original parishes. The neighbouring early parishes of **St. John, St. Dorothy, and St. Thomas-in-the-Vale** were merged into expanded St. Catherine under **Law 20 of 1867**. Linstead was the principal town of the old **St. Thomas-in-the-Vale** parish before the 1867 merger.

**Slavery and the planter church.** Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds. **The Diocese of Jamaica** was established by Letters Patent on **24 July 1824**; **Christopher Lipscomb** was consecrated first Bishop in 1824.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838), Sligoville.** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Sligoville** in St. Catherine became Jamaica's **first free village** in 1838, founded with land provided by **Howe Peter Browne, 2nd Marquess of Sligo**, then Governor of Jamaica. Bishop Lipscomb (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. Capital moved from Spanish Town to **Kingston in 1872**.

**Foundation of Holy Trinity Linstead.** Linstead developed as the principal interior town of St. Catherine, anchored by its weekly market — celebrated in the Jamaican folk song **"Linstead Market"** — and by its position on the road from Spanish Town to the interior. The Anglican community in the town grew large enough to sustain a second worship point alongside St. John's, and the Diocese established Holy Trinity to serve that need. The 1891 **Handbook of Jamaica** clergy listing does not record a separate Holy Trinity Linstead, which is consistent with a foundation date in the later nineteenth or early twentieth century — the period in which Bishop **Nuttall** pushed extensive rural and small-town church-planting across the island. The Diocese of Jamaica's online directory image collection includes a photograph of the building (attachment_id 863).

**Twentieth century and Independence.** Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found Holy Trinity a settled small-town parish.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Hurricane Melissa brought wind and rain to interior St. Catherine.

## Architecture
Holy Trinity is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for small-town Anglican congregations in late-nineteenth- and early-twentieth-century St. Catherine: a simple nave with a modest entrance and a small churchyard. Linstead's interior position offers some shelter from coastal storm surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
Linstead's two Anglican churches share clergy and lay leadership within the **St. Catherine portion of the Mandeville Region**.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to the **Holy Trinity** — Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, celebrated on Trinity Sunday — is shared with the diocese's cathedral on North Street, Kingston (Holy Trinity Cathedral, 1911), and with Holy Trinity at Old Harbour, Holy Trinity at Westgate, Montego Bay, and Holy Trinity at Trinityville, St. Thomas. **Linstead Market** is one of Jamaica's most celebrated traditional markets, immortalised in the eponymous folk song.
