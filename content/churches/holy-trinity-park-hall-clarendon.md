# Holy Trinity, Park Hall
**Clarendon** · Church · active

Holy Trinity Anglican Church serves **Park Hall**, a farming community in the interior hills of Clarendon parish. Holy Trinity has provided Anglican ministry here for several generations. The Trinity dedication — Father, Son, and Holy Spirit — is one of the most traditional Anglican church names.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1664).** Clarendon parish was created in **1664**, nine years after the English seizure of Jamaica from Spain (**1655**), and named for **Sir Edward Hyde, 1st Earl of Clarendon** (1609–1674), Lord High Chancellor of England under Charles II. The original parish church was the **Church of the White Cross** at "The Cross" — Jamaica's third Anglican church and the site of Jamaica's first House of Assembly elections. **St. Paul's Chapelton** was built as a chapel of ease in **1666** in the upland interior, and became the parish church when the Cross Church was destroyed in **1815**. **Vere** was carved out as a separate sugar-plain parish in **1673** and was reabsorbed into Clarendon in **1867**; **Law 20 of 1867** designated **May Pen** as chief town.

**Slavery and the Suttons rebellion.** Clarendon was one of Jamaica's great sugar parishes. The fertile Rio Minho plain held estates bearing the names of their English founders — **Suttons, Pennants, Ballards** — while the buccaneer **Henry Morgan** owned Morgan's Valley and Danks. The **Dawkins family** alone accumulated seventeen plantations covering over 25,000 acres in Clarendon between 1665 and 1833. One of Jamaica's earliest slave rebellions erupted at **Suttons Plantation in 1690**: some 500 enslaved people escaped and joined Spanish-speaking free Africans, forming the Maroon communities eventually led by **Cudjoe** into the Cockpit Country.

**Sam Sharpe and the Colonial Church Union (1831).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**, a vigilante alliance that sought to "wipe the non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth." Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation, 1865, disestablishment.** Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. The freed population of Clarendon established free villages and provision grounds across the interior, moving into the upland districts to grow coffee, citrus, and provisions. Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb** (1824–1843) had begun extending Anglican schools across the parish — by 1835 he reported 142 schools with 8,500 scholars across Jamaica. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of Holy Trinity.** Park Hall sits in the interior of Clarendon, in the hill country of the parish; the name preserves the memory of a colonial estate, the "hall" designation being common in Jamaican estate naming (e.g. Harewood Hall, Somerset Hall). The estate would have had its "park" — an enclosed area for cattle or horses — giving the community a name that combines two common colonial-estate landscape features. The Anglican Diocese established Holy Trinity to serve this community as part of its post-Emancipation interior mission network. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**, and Holy Trinity Park Hall belongs to that broader expansion phase.

**Twentieth century: bauxite, May Pen, Independence.** Chapelton lost its status as parish capital in **1938**, with administrative functions transferring to **May Pen**. **Alcoa** began bauxite operations in Clarendon in **1959**; the **Jamalco** alumina refinery at Halse Hall opened in **1972**. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found Holy Trinity a settled rural mission.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of Holy Trinity Park Hall after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
Holy Trinity is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural Clarendon in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. Set in the rural interior of Clarendon. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
Park Hall has been served within the Clarendon deanery under shared clergy arrangements.

## Notable Facts
Park Hall's name preserves the memory of a colonial estate, the "hall" designation being common in Jamaican estate naming. The estate would have had its "park" — an enclosed area for cattle or horses — giving the community a name that combines two common colonial-estate landscape features. The dedication to the **Holy Trinity** — celebrated on Trinity Sunday, the Sunday after Pentecost — is one of the most traditional Anglican dedications, declaring the church's commitment to the central Trinitarian doctrine of orthodox Christian faith.
