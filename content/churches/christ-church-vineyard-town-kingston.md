# Christ Church, Vineyard Town
**Kingston** · Church · inactive

Christ Church, Vineyard Town is one of the Anglican churches of Kingston, serving the **Vineyard Town** community in eastern Kingston at 6 Antrim Road, Kingston 3. The church is nationally identified with **Fr Sean Major-Campbell's annual Human Rights Service**, held on the Sunday nearest 10 December each year since 2014.

## History
**English conquest (1655) and Port Royal.** Penn and Venables's English expedition captured Jamaica from Spain in May **1655**. Under English rule, **Port Royal** at the tip of the Palisadoes became the wealthiest English city in the Caribbean — "the wickedest city on earth" — and a major slave-trading port.

**The Great Earthquake (7 June 1692) and the founding of Kingston (1693).** On **7 June 1692**, two-thirds of Port Royal sank into Kingston Harbour; approximately 2,000 died immediately and another 3,000 in the following days. Survivors relocated to the north shore and the new city of **Kingston** was formally established on **22 July 1692**, with the Assembly constituting Kingston as a parish in **1693**. **Kingston Parish Church (St. Thomas the Apostle)** was built in **1699** at the corner of King Street and Parade. **St. Andrew parish** had been established earlier, in **1664**, with **Half-Way-Tree** as parish capital and a 600-acre glebe granted in 1667 by Charles II.

**Slavery and the planter church.** Kingston contained the largest enslaved population in British America. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church. The **Diocese of Jamaica** was established by Letters Patent on **24 July 1824**; **Christopher Lipscomb** was consecrated first Bishop in 1824. During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation (1838) and disestablishment (1870).** Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838** — approximately 311,000 formerly enslaved people were freed. Bishop Lipscomb (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

**The 1907 earthquake and the 1923 KSAC merger.** The capital moved from Spanish Town to **Kingston in 1872**. The **14 January 1907** earthquake (magnitude 6.2) destroyed much of Kingston, killing over 1,000. Reconstruction under Nuttall's £50,000 programme included the consecration of **ten churches within a single month in January 1911**. The **Kingston and St. Andrew Corporation (KSAC)** unified the two parishes for civic administration in **1923**.

**Foundation of Christ Church.** Vineyard Town is an inner-city community of eastern Kingston whose name preserves the memory of market gardening that once characterised the flat land east of the city centre. Christ Church has served this community through the development of Kingston's eastern residential areas.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** under Bishop Percival Gibson — the first native-born Bishop of Jamaica — found Christ Church a settled urban parish.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Hurricane Melissa brought wind and rain to Kingston.

## Architecture
Christ Church is a masonry church in the urban landscape of eastern Kingston, serving the Vineyard Town community at 6 Antrim Road, Kingston 3.

## Clergy
The cure has been served by **Sean Major-Campbell** within the Kingston deanery.

## Notable Facts
Although the building is currently listed inactive, Christ Church Vineyard Town remains nationally identified with **Fr Sean Major-Campbell's annual Human Rights Service**, held on the Sunday nearest **10 December** each year since **2014** when his foot-washing of two lesbian congregants made front-page news. Bishop Leon Golding preached at the 2025 service.
