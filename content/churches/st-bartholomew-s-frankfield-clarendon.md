# St. Bartholomew's, Frankfield
**Clarendon** · Church · active

St. Bartholomew's Anglican Church is the **cure church** for **Frankfield**, the principal town of the interior highlands of Clarendon. St. Bartholomew's serves the Frankfield community in the upper Rio Minho valley and is the parent of a circuit covering Frankfield, St. Luke's Sanguinetti, St. George's Red Hills, St. Matthew's Aenon Town, Holy Trinity Park Hall, and Peckham. The cure rectory is at Frankfield P.O., Clarendon (telephone 904-4565); the cure was recently listed as **VACANT**.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1664).** Clarendon parish was created in **1664**, nine years after the English seizure of Jamaica from Spain (**1655**), and named for **Sir Edward Hyde, 1st Earl of Clarendon** (1609–1674), Lord High Chancellor of England under Charles II. The original parish church was the **Church of the White Cross** at "The Cross" — Jamaica's third Anglican church. **St. Paul's Chapelton** was built as a chapel of ease in **1666** in the upland interior, and became the parish church when the Cross Church was destroyed in **1815**. **Vere** was carved out as a separate sugar-plain parish in **1671** and was reabsorbed into Clarendon in **1867**; **Law 20 of 1887** designated **May Pen** as chief town.

**Slavery and the Suttons rebellion.** Clarendon was one of Jamaica's great sugar parishes. The fertile Rio Minho plain held estates bearing the names of their English founders — **Suttons, Pennants, Ballards** — while the buccaneer **Henry Morgan** owned Morgan's Valley and Danks. The **Dawkins family** alone accumulated seventeen plantations covering over 25,000 acres in Clarendon between 1665 and 1833. One of Jamaica's earliest slave rebellions erupted at **Suttons Plantation in 1690**: some 500 enslaved people escaped to form the Maroon communities eventually led by **Cudjoe** into the Cockpit Country.

**Sam Sharpe and the Colonial Church Union (1831).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**, a vigilante alliance that sought to "wipe the non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth." Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation, 1865, disestablishment.** Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. The freed population of Clarendon established free villages and provision grounds across the interior, moving into the upland districts to grow coffee, citrus, and provisions. Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb** (1824–1843) had begun extending Anglican schools across the parish — by 1835 he reported 142 schools with 8,500 scholars across Jamaica. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of the Frankfield cure.** Frankfield developed as the principal town of the interior highlands of Clarendon — the market hub for the surrounding hill communities and a centre of post-Emancipation farming. The Anglican Diocese established St. Bartholomew's to serve this substantial community, with St. John's also recorded at Frankfield. The upper Rio Minho valley around Frankfield has been a farming area for the surrounding hill communities for well over a century. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**, and the Frankfield cure consolidated Anglican ministry across the hill country with its network of mission chapels at Sanguinetti, Red Hills, Aenon Town, Park Hall, and Peckham.

**Twentieth century: railway, bauxite, Independence.** The May Pen to Chapelton branch railway, opened in **1913** and extended to **Frankfield in 1925**, made Frankfield the upper terminus of the Clarendon hill-country line, transforming it into a transport hub for the citrus industry that grew rapidly across the upper Rio Minho valley. Chapelton lost its status as parish capital in **1938**, with administrative functions transferring to **May Pen**. **Alcoa** began bauxite operations in Clarendon in **1959**; the **Jamalco** alumina refinery at Halse Hall opened in **1972**. The Frankfield railway closed in **1974**, ending six decades of rail service. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Bartholomew's a settled cure church.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of St. Bartholomew's Frankfield after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. Bartholomew's is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural Clarendon in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. The interior hill-country position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events. The Find a Grave project records 70 memorials in the churchyard.

## Clergy
St. Bartholomew's Frankfield is the cure church for the Frankfield circuit (Sanguinetti, Red Hills, Aenon Town, Park Hall, Peckham). The cure rectory at Frankfield P.O., Clarendon (telephone 904-4565) is the contact point; the cure has recently been listed as **VACANT**.

## Notable Facts
**St. Bartholomew's** feast day (**24 August**) falls in the height of hurricane season. Frankfield's position in the upper Rio Minho valley makes it central to the agricultural economy of interior Clarendon, and the town has historically served as the market hub for the surrounding hill communities.
