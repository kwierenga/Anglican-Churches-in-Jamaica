# St. Mark's, Craigton
**St. Andrew** · Church · active

St. Mark's Anglican Church at Craigton (note: distinct from St. Mark's at Craighton, which is a separate confirmed location) serves a community approximately 2.8 km distant from the Craighton church. Craigton and Craighton appear to be separate communities with similar names in different parts of St. Andrew, or the same community recorded under two spelling variants for locations that are genuinely 2.8 km apart. This St. Mark's serves its particular Craigton community.

## History
The Craigton/Craighton distinction in St. Andrew may reflect a genuine splitting of what was once a single community into two distinct settlements at different elevation bands, or may represent the same community recorded under two different spelling conventions. Both St. Mark's churches are in the eastern hill country of St. Andrew.

## Architecture
St. Mark's is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural St. Andrew in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. Set in the rural interior of St. Andrew. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
Craigton has been served within the eastern St. Andrew deanery circuit under shared clergy arrangements.

## Notable Facts
The dual presence of Craighton and Craigton in the St. Andrew church records reflects the broader phenomenon of spelling variants in Jamaican place names, where colonial-era names were recorded inconsistently and different spellings have persisted in parallel. Both communities support Anglican worship, suggesting the hill country of eastern St. Andrew has maintained a dense network of worship points.
