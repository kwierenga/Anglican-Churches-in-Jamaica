# St. Alban's, Ashton
**Westmoreland** · Church · active

St. Alban's Anglican Church is a small, historic country church in the rural district of **Ashton**, Westmoreland, south of Bethel Town. It serves a community that emerged from the parish's sugar belt and the post-Emancipation establishment of free villages. Once a vibrant centre of community life, the congregation by 2025 had dwindled to fewer than eight regular attendees — a decline documented in vivid detail by *Jamaica Gleaner* staff reporter Rochelle Clayton in her **17 February 2025** feature, *"Bell Tolls for Traditional Churches?"*

## History
**Founding of the parish (1703).** Westmoreland was officially formed in **1703** from the western portion of the older parish of St. Elizabeth, named for its position at the western part of the island. The parish capital, **Savanna-la-Mar**, was founded around 1730. In 1723 the new parish of Hanover was carved out of Westmoreland's northern section.

**Slavery and the sugar economy.** Under English rule, sugar and slavery accelerated in Westmoreland: an inventory in **1722** numbered the estates at **73**, making the parish one of the leading sugar producers of the British Caribbean. The Beckford family — Richard, Peter, and William — were among the most prominent planters; modern Petersfield is named for Peter Beckford. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds; its parish vestry was composed of the planter class, and its rectors held glebe lands worked by enslaved people.

**The Sam Sharpe Rebellion (December 1831).** The Christmas Rebellion of 1831, led by the Baptist deacon **Samuel Sharpe** and ignited at the Kensington Estate in St. James on the night of **27 December 1831**, spread rapidly through the western parishes — St. James, Trelawny, Hanover, and Westmoreland — with some 60,000 enslaved people involved. The Colonial Church Union's vigilante destruction of chapels included Sav.-la-Mar (Ellis, *Diocese of Jamaica*, 1913). Sharpe was hanged on 23 May 1832 and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation, post-1838 free villages, and Ashton.** Within two years of Sharpe's hanging the British Parliament passed the Slavery Abolition Act (1833); Emancipation came in 1834, and full freedom followed at the end of the apprenticeship period on **1 August 1838**. By 1854 the number of active sugar estates in Westmoreland had collapsed to 34, less than half the 1722 figure. Ashton developed in this post-Emancipation landscape, alongside its near neighbour **Bethel Town** — a documented former slave village that became a free village after Emancipation. The Anglican Diocese established St. Alban's as a small mission chapel to serve this newly free community.

**1865, 1870, and Nuttall's expansion.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865, Crown Colony rule replaced the planter Assembly (1866); the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica. Bishop Enos Nuttall's tenure (1880–1916) saw the Diocese grow from 26 rural mission stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. St. Alban's belongs to that wave of late-nineteenth-century rural mission planting; *Jamaica Gleaner* funeral notices from the early 2000s and earlier document services at St. Alban's followed by burial in local family plots, attesting to its long-standing role.

**Twentieth century, Independence, and the long decline.** Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Alban's a settled rural mission. The 2025 *Gleaner* feature contrasts the church's earlier vitality — *"a full congregation and a packed calendar of events,"* including well-attended Harvest Thanksgiving Services, rallies, monthly meetings, and communion services — with its current state of decline.

**The 2025 Gleaner report.** Rochelle Clayton's *"Bell Tolls for Traditional Churches?"* feature (Jamaica Gleaner, 17 February 2025) recorded a congregation of fewer than **eight regular attendees**, with only **five people** present at her January visit. The Rev. **Isaac Nugent** is the rector, visiting once monthly on the third Sunday to administer Holy Communion; the gate to the churchyard was locked during the *Gleaner*'s visit, but basic maintenance continued. **Ralston Shakes** — a longtime member who maintains the church grounds as a gardener — described the experience: *"Sometimes it's only five of us, sometimes two, and sometimes just me alone"* when the elder does not attend. There is no Sunday school (the youth stopped attending), no choirs, no instruments, no night services, and no prayer meetings; the last Holy Communion observed by the *Gleaner* report was the first of 2025, in January. Rev. Nugent attributed the membership loss to *"deaths of older members and the church's remote location,"* compounded by the pandemic effect when *"people started to do services online."*

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Social-media reports detailed serious damage to homes and the local school in Ashton; while there is no specific structural report on St. Alban's, the church is located within a district that suffered both severe weather damage and longer-term economic pressures.

## Architecture
No published architectural survey or photograph of St. Alban's is available in major heritage collections. Based on the typical design of similar rural Anglican churches in Westmoreland, it is most likely a modest, rectangular masonry chapel, with features such as a simple porch or small bell-cote, a pitched zinc roof, and plain or louvred windows, set in a yard bordered by family burial plots rather than a large, formal cemetery. The hilly Ashton terrain makes raised, stable ground important.

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** **St. Alban** — the first recorded Christian martyr of Britain (3rd or 4th century) — was a Romano-British layperson at Verulamium (modern St Albans, Hertfordshire) who sheltered a fugitive Christian priest, converted, and was beheaded after surrendering himself in the priest's place. His feast day is **22 June**. The dedication is a quiet thread between this small Westmoreland congregation and the very origins of Christianity in the British Isles.

**A community case study.** The 2025 *Gleaner* feature used St. Alban's as a case study for *"Bell Tolls for Traditional Churches?"* — highlighting the widespread challenge of ageing congregations and declining attendance in Jamaica's historic denominations. Despite the decline in weekly attendance, the church remains a meaningful venue for funerals and memorial services for Ashton residents, indicating its enduring role for major life events even as its regular congregation ages and shrinks.

**Bethel Town context.** Ashton lies just south of **Bethel Town**, one of Westmoreland's documented post-Emancipation free villages established after 1838 as freed people purchased land — through Baptist and other missionary support — and built independent communities apart from the great estates.
