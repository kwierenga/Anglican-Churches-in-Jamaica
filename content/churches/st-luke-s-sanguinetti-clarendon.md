# St. Luke's, Sanguinetti
**Clarendon** · Church · active

St. Luke's Anglican Church serves **Sanguinetti**, a community in the north-western interior of Clarendon, lying in the hill country close to the Manchester border. **Spalding** is the nearest significant town, with **Christiana** in Manchester just to the west. Sanguinetti is an unusual community name — of Italian origin — reflecting the diverse backgrounds of colonial-era estate proprietors in this part of Jamaica's interior.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1664).** Clarendon parish was created in **1664**, nine years after the English seizure of Jamaica from Spain (**1655**), and named for **Sir Edward Hyde, 1st Earl of Clarendon** (1609–1674), Lord High Chancellor of England under Charles II. The original parish church was the **Church of the White Cross** at "The Cross" — Jamaica's third Anglican church. **St. Paul's Chapelton** was built as a chapel of ease in **1666** and became the parish church when the Cross Church was destroyed in **1815**. **Vere** was carved out as a separate sugar-plain parish in **1673** and was reabsorbed into Clarendon in **1867**; **Law 20 of 1867** designated **May Pen** as chief town.

**Slavery, Sam Sharpe, and Emancipation.** Clarendon was one of Jamaica's great sugar parishes — the fertile Rio Minho plain held the great estates of the Suttons, Pennants, Ballards, and Dawkins, and the wealth of the parish was built on the labour of enslaved Africans. One of Jamaica's earliest slave rebellions erupted at **Suttons Plantation in 1690**: some 500 enslaved people escaped to form the Maroon communities eventually led by **Cudjoe** into the Cockpit Country. During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**, a vigilante alliance that sought to "wipe the non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of St. Luke's Sanguinetti.** Sanguinetti sits in the north-western interior of Clarendon, in the upland farming country near Spalding and the Manchester border. The community takes its name from **Jacob Sanguinetti**, an Italian proprietor who owned land here — one of a number of Jamaican communities that bear the surnames of their original estate owners. The Anglican Diocese established St. Luke's to serve this farming community as part of the post-Emancipation rural mission expansion. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. The Sanguinetti area is known for its steep terrain, waterfalls, and relative isolation from the main road network — characteristics that have shaped both the community's character and the church's role as a local anchor.

**Twentieth century: bauxite, May Pen, Independence.** Chapelton lost its status as parish capital in **1938**, with administrative functions transferring to **May Pen**. **Alcoa** began bauxite operations in Clarendon in **1959**; the **Jamalco** alumina refinery at Halse Hall opened in **1972**. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Luke's a settled mission of the Frankfield cure.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of St. Luke's Sanguinetti after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. Luke's is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across the upland interior of Clarendon in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The building is functional in character, suited to a small rural congregation in a remote hill community. The steep terrain of the Sanguinetti area and its elevation create wind exposure during storm passages and can make road access difficult in heavy rain.

## Clergy
St. Luke's Sanguinetti is administered as a mission of the **St. Bartholomew's Frankfield** cure (currently vacant), with the cure rectory at Frankfield P.O., Clarendon (telephone 904-4565). The remote location means the church depends substantially on lay leaders.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to **St. Luke** — physician and evangelist, whose feast day is **18 October** — falls in the calmer period after hurricane season. **Sanguinetti's Italian-origin name** is one of the more unusual community names in Jamaica. Jamaica has several communities with Italian or Sephardic Jewish names reflecting the diverse origins of colonial-era proprietors — Sanguinetti stands out as one of the most distinctively non-English names in the interior parish landscape.
