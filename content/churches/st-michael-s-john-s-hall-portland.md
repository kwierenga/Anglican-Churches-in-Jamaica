# St. Michael's, John's Hall
**Portland** · Church · active

St. Michael's Anglican Church serves the small hillside community of **John's Hall** in eastern Portland, situated just inland from Fellowship and Port Antonio. The Diocese of Jamaica & the Cayman Islands officially lists 'St. Michael's – John's Hall' among Portland churches within the Eastern Jamaica Region. Multiple gazetteer and mapping sources identify John's Hall as a distinct settlement near Bellevue and Garland Grove, differentiating it from several other Jamaican localities sharing the same name.

## History
**English rule (1655) and the founding of Portland (1723).** Under English rule from **1655**, the future Portland coast remained largely Maroon territory. **Portland parish was created in 1723** by order of the then Governor, **Henry Bentinck, 1st Duke of Portland** (Governor 1722–1726), and named in his honour. The British built **Fort George** at Port Antonio in 1729.

**The First Maroon War (1728–1739) and Nanny.** The **Windward Maroons** under **Quao** and **Nanny** controlled the Blue Mountains and John Crow Mountains. The **1740 treaty** granted **Nanny a 500-acre land grant** that forms the present site of **Moore Town**. **Nanny of the Maroons** became Jamaica's only female National Hero in October 1975.

**Slavery and the planter church.** Sugar estates dominated the fertile coastal lowlands and river valleys; coffee thrived in the hills. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union** to "wipe non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Christ Church Port Antonio** was completed around **1840** under Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb**.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall, and the banana revolution.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. In **1870**, **Captain Lorenzo Dow Baker** purchased bananas in Port Antonio market; Baker's Boston Fruit Company became **United Fruit Company in 1899**. The church represents the historical expansion of Anglican presence into Portland's eastern valleys during this post-Emancipation period.

**Foundation of St. Michael's.** Within the diocesan organisational structure, St. Michael's at John's Hall appears in the 'Churches by Deanery' tables under Portland's Eastern Jamaica Region grouping, alongside other small interior missions such as St. John's at Cooper's Hill and St. Jude's at Bourbon. This placement reflects Portland's long-established pattern of maintaining rural chapels that extend Anglican ministry into the parish's interior communities while remaining connected to the regional deanery centred around Port Antonio.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** The **1903 hurricane** damaged Christ Church Port Antonio and devastated the banana industry. Tourism grew at Port Antonio. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Michael's a settled rural mission.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The chapel, like others in Portland's interior, faced operational challenges during Hurricane Melissa, when the parish's eastern regions experienced significant landslides, road closures, and utility disruptions.

## Architecture
No dedicated Jamaica National Heritage Trust site card or published photographic collection for St. Michael's at John's Hall is currently available in digital archives. Based on comparable Anglican chapels throughout rural Portland, the structure likely follows the typical pattern of a compact masonry nave with timber roofing and simple pointed-arch openings, accompanied by a small churchyard cemetery.

## Clergy
St. Michael's operates under the administrative oversight of the **Eastern Jamaica Region's Portland deanery**, as confirmed by its listing in diocesan 'churches by parish' directories. The church's designation as 'C/E' (Church of England) on regional indices identifies it among Portland's outstation missions. All clerical assignments, worship schedules, and pastoral coordination for this chapel are managed through the Eastern Jamaica Region's administrative channels.

## Notable Facts
The church's location requires careful geographical distinction, as several Jamaican settlements share the name **John's Hall**, though map sources precisely place this community in Portland near Fellowship and Bellevue. The dedication to **St. Michael the Archangel** — leader of the heavenly host, commemorated at **Michaelmas on 29 September** — is a common Anglican choice for hilltop sites.
