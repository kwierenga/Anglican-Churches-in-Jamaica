# St. Peter's, Blue Mountain Valley
**St. Thomas** · Church · unknown

St. Peter's Anglican Church serves Blue Mountain Valley, a community in the interior of St. Thomas parish, in the hill country on the southern flanks of the Blue Mountains. Blue Mountain Valley is a farming community in the elevated terrain of northern St. Thomas, and the church provides Anglican ministry to a congregation in one of the more remote and scenically dramatic parts of the parish — the approach country to Jamaica's highest mountain range.

## History
Blue Mountain Valley sits in the interior of St. Thomas, in the hill country that ascends toward the Blue Mountain peaks. This part of the parish was settled by freed people after Emancipation who established small farms in the cooler highland environment. The Anglican Diocese established St. Peter's as part of its mission network in the northern interior communities of St. Thomas, serving the farming community in the valley landscape.

## Architecture
St. Peter's is a masonry chapel in the elevated Blue Mountain Valley area of St. Thomas. The highland position creates significant wind exposure during hurricane passages, and the valley terrain channels rainfall and creates flooding and landslide risk during major storm events. The Blue Mountains receive some of the highest rainfall in Jamaica from the north-east trade winds.

## Clergy
Blue Mountain Valley has been served within the St. Thomas deanery under shared clergy arrangements. The remote interior location means the church depends heavily on lay leaders for its regular pastoral life.

## Notable Facts
Blue Mountain Valley's position in the approach country to Jamaica's highest mountains places it in a landscape of extraordinary natural beauty and significant ecological importance. The Blue Mountains are a UNESCO World Heritage Site, recognised for their biodiversity and the distinctive Blue Mountain coffee produced on their upper slopes. The valley communities on the southern Blue Mountain flanks have maintained Anglican worship in a demanding environment for well over a century. Hurricane Ivan (2004) caused serious landslide damage across the Blue Mountain foothills. Hurricane Melissa (October 2025) brought extraordinary rainfall to this region.
