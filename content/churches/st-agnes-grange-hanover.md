# St. Agnes', Grange
**Hanover** · Church · active

St. Agnes' at Grange is a mission church of the Hanover Deanery within the Montego Bay Region of the Diocese of Jamaica and the Cayman Islands. It is attached to the principal churches of the deanery — St. Mary's, Lucea; Holy Trinity, Green Island; and St. Bartholomew's, Dalmally — and serves the rural community of Grange in Hanover's interior.

## History
Hanover was established on 12 November 1723 from parts of Westmoreland and St. James, named for the ruling House of Hanover, and became one of the major sugar-producing parishes of the Jamaican north-west. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds; disestablishment in 1870 reconstituted it as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

The Anglican network in Hanover in 1865 was anchored by Lucea (the parish church) and Green Island, with a missionary presence at Church Hill and an Island Curate at Newport. By the 1891 Handbook, the main churches of the deanery were Lucea, Green Island, and St. Saviour's, with mission stations at Church Hill, Dalmally, Chester Castle, and Hopewell — but Grange is not recorded. St. Agnes' at Grange was therefore established after 1891, most likely during Bishop Enos Nuttall's expansion of mission stations across rural Jamaica (the Diocese grew from 26 rural stations in 1880 to 122 by 1911). No founding date has been verified.

The dedication is to St. Agnes of Rome (c.291–304), the virgin-martyr of the early Church whose feast day is 21 January — a dedication relatively uncommon in Jamaican Anglican foundations.

## Architecture
St. Agnes' is a modest rural parish church with an associated cemetery — the Find A Grave record documents burials on the property. No substantive architectural description is recorded in the public heritage sources reviewed.

## Clergy
As a mission church of the Hanover Deanery, St. Agnes' is served under shared clergy arrangements with the principal churches of the cure.

## Notable Facts
Hurricane Melissa (28 October 2025) made landfall in Westmoreland and caused severe damage across Hanover. Across the Diocese of Jamaica and the Cayman Islands, some 50 church buildings were destroyed or significantly damaged; the most prominent confirmed loss in Hanover was the 100-year-old Ramble Anglican Church, reported by the Jamaica Gleaner as demolished. The specific status of St. Agnes' after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources.
