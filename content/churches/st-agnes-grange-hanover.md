# St. Agnes', Grange
**Hanover** · Church · active

St. Agnes' at Grange is a mission church of the Hanover Deanery within the Montego Bay Region of the Diocese of Jamaica and the Cayman Islands. It is attached to the principal churches of the deanery — St. Mary's, Lucea; Holy Trinity, Green Island; and St. Bartholomew's, Dalmally — and serves the rural community of Grange in Hanover's interior.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1723).** Hanover was established on **12 November 1723** from a section of Westmoreland (with later boundary adjustments incorporating land from St. James), in response to a petition complaining of the expense and inconvenience — up to fifty miles of travel — endured by residents of the remote north-west "to pay taxes." The new parish was named for the House of Hanover, then newly on the British throne under George I; a proposal to name it **"St. Sophia"** for George I's mother was overruled by the Assembly. The parish capital, Lucea, was established at the natural harbour on the north-west coast, and Fort Charlotte (named for the queen of George III) was built to defend the harbour from French raiders.

**Slavery and the sugar economy.** Hanover became a major sugar parish: by 1768 it had **71 sugar plantations, 13,571 enslaved Africans, 8,942 cattle**, and produced 7,500 barrels of sugar that year. A census conducted in **1824** recorded **502 whites, 1,438 free coloureds, and 22,945 enslaved people** — the proportions typical of a late-period Caribbean sugar parish, where the enslaved African population vastly outnumbered free residents. Henry Morgan, the buccaneer who became Governor of Jamaica, owned 4,000 acres at Riley outside Lucea. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds, and the parish vestry was composed of the planter class.

**The Sam Sharpe Rebellion (December 1831).** The Christmas Rebellion of 1831, led by the Baptist deacon Samuel Sharpe and ignited at the Kensington Estate in St. James on the night of 27 December 1831, spread rapidly through the western parishes — St. James, Trelawny, Hanover, and Westmoreland — with some 60,000 enslaved people involved. Suppressed brutally, the rebellion ended with Sharpe's hanging in Montego Bay on 23 May 1832. The Colonial Church Union — a vigilante alliance of Anglican planters and clergy formed in the aftermath — undertook a campaign of "riotous destruction of chapels at Stewart Town, Lucea, Brown's Town, Sav.-la-Mar, St. Ann's Bay, Fuller's Field, and other centres of Wesleyan and Baptist activity," as J. B. Ellis recorded in his 1913 *History of the Diocese of Jamaica*. The Union was eventually declared "by proclamation to be an illegal association."

**Emancipation, post-1838 economic shift.** Within two years of Sharpe's hanging the British Parliament passed the Slavery Abolition Act (1833); Emancipation came in 1834, and full freedom followed at the end of the apprenticeship period on **1 August 1838**. After 1838, freed people across Hanover settled the upland districts as small farmers; the **Lucea yam** became a key parish export to Cuban and Central American markets, partly compensating for the post-Emancipation decline of the great sugar estates. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865, Crown Colony rule replaced the planter-dominated Assembly (1866); the Anglican Church was disestablished in 1870, reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of St. Agnes', Grange.** The 1865 and 1870 Almanacs and the 1891 Handbook of Jamaica do not record Grange among the Hanover Anglican stations. St. Agnes' was therefore established after 1891 — most likely during Bishop Enos Nuttall's expansion of mission stations across rural Jamaica (the Diocese grew from 26 rural stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**). No founding date for the chapel has been verified.

**Independence and the modern era.** Jamaica's independence on 6 August 1962 found St. Agnes' a settled rural mission within Hanover's Anglican network. The parish remained one of the smaller Jamaican parishes by population, and its Anglican structure had taken its modern form by the early twentieth century.

## Architecture
St. Agnes' is a modest rural parish church with an associated cemetery — the Find A Grave record documents burials on the property. No substantive architectural description is recorded in the public heritage sources reviewed.

## Clergy
As a mission church of the Hanover Deanery, St. Agnes' is served under shared clergy arrangements with the principal churches of the cure.

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** St. Agnes of Rome (c. 291 – c. 304) was a virgin-martyr of the early Church, executed under the Diocletian persecution at age twelve or thirteen. Her feast day is 21 January. The dedication is relatively uncommon among Jamaican Anglican foundations — a small mission station's choice of an early-Christian virgin-martyr as patron is theologically distinctive.

**Hurricane Melissa (28 October 2025)** made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history — and caused severe damage across Hanover. Across the Diocese of Jamaica and the Cayman Islands, some 50 church buildings were destroyed or significantly damaged; the most prominent confirmed loss in Hanover was the 100-year-old Ramble Anglican Church, reported by the Jamaica Gleaner as demolished. The specific status of St. Agnes' after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed. Hanover Parish Church Hall later served as the venue for the Government's "ROOFS" recovery cheque hand-over to Hanover households (Gleaner, 27 February 2026).
