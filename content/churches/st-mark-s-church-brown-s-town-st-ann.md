# St. Mark's Church, Brown's Town
**St. Ann** · Church · active

St. Mark's Anglican Church is the historic Anglican parish church of **Brown's Town**, St. Ann. Founded in **1805** by the prominent Irish planter and slave-owner **Hamilton Brown**, the church's origins are inextricably linked to the history of slavery. Its churchyard, containing **Brown's grave** and other 19th-century tombs, serves as a permanent physical record of this past. Today, St. Mark's continues its role as an active parish church and a central institution in the civic and spiritual life of the bustling market town.

## History
**Pre-English settlement: Sevilla la Nueva (1509–1534).** St. Ann has a deeper colonial past than almost any other Jamaican parish. **Sevilla la Nueva**, the first permanent European settlement in Jamaica and the colony's first Spanish capital, was founded in **1509** by Juan de Esquivel near present St. Ann's Bay and served as Spanish capital until **1534**. **Christopher Columbus** had been marooned at St. Ann's Bay for over a year (1503–1504).

**English rule (1655) and Brown's Town's foundation (1805).** Under English rule from **1655**, St. Ann became a major **sugar and livestock** region. **Hamilton Brown**, an Irish-born planter and major sugar planter who enslaved over a thousand people, founded the town that bears his name in **1805** and built St. Mark's as the official Church of England presence in the new settlement. Until **1870**, the Church of England was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds; its vestry was composed of the planter class.

**Sam Sharpe Rebellion, the Colonial Church Union, and the Brown's Town Baptist chapel (1831–32).** Brown's legacy is marked by violent religious conflict. During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, Brown led a mob from the **Colonial Church Union** that destroyed the local Baptist chapel — which ministered to enslaved and free Black congregants. The **Colonial Church Union**, formed by planters and Anglican clergy after the rebellion, sought to "wipe the non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth." **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation (1838) and the local irony of Clarksonville.** Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. Following Emancipation, St. Mark's gradually evolved from a planter's chapel to a town parish, serving the community of freed people who remained in the area as small farmers and traders. In a pointed local irony, **the free village of Clarksonville** — 15 km south of Brown's Town — was founded in **1835** (just three years after the chapel-burning) by the Baptist missionary **Rev. John Clark** and named for the English abolitionist **Thomas Clarkson** (per NLJ *History of St. Ann*). Other free villages of St. Ann include **Sturge Town** (1839, founded by the English Quaker abolitionist **Joseph Sturge**). Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb** (1824–1843) had begun extending Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**Disestablishment (1870) and the parish-town role.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. St. Mark's role as the principal Anglican church of the Brown's Town market town gave it leadership over a wider deanery network of mission chapels.

**Twentieth century: bauxite, Independence.** Bauxite mining began in St. Ann around **1952** (Reynolds, Kaiser); the bauxite economy reshaped the parish, with major operations to the north and east of Brown's Town. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Mark's a settled town parish over 150 years old.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of St. Mark's Brown's Town after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
The original St. Mark's was a small stone building with a shingled roof, completed in **1805**. The present church, as shown in the National Library of Jamaica's digital collection, is a modest Georgian-influenced structure with solid masonry walls and a simple bell-cote. The churchyard remains a key feature, containing the grave of **Hamilton Brown** and other historic tombs. Despite the town's expansion, placing the church near schools and shops, the grounds retain a quiet, historic atmosphere with mature trees, creating a reflective space at the heart of the busy community.

## Clergy
St. Mark's is listed as the main Anglican church for Brown's Town and surrounding districts. It remains an active parish over two centuries after its founding, with regular Sunday worship and special services. The church hosts funerals and civic events, including services linked to local institutions like Brown's Town Community College.

## Notable Facts
The church's founder, **Hamilton Brown**, was a recruiter of Irish migrants and is a documented ancestor of some branches of the Harris family — a connection that has drawn international genealogical interest. The **1832 destruction of the Brown's Town Baptist chapel** by a mob led by Brown highlights the violent religious conflicts of the era, pitting planter-aligned Anglicans against nonconformist churches sympathetic to the freedom movement. St. Mark's and Brown's Town have been used in recent commentary as a case study in how communities can acknowledge a slavery-founded church while continuing to worship in it. The dedication to **St. Mark** — the Evangelist, author of the shortest of the four Gospels (feast **25 April**) — is shared with the Manchester parish church at Mandeville.
