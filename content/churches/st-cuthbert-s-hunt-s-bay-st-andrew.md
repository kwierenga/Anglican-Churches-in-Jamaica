# St. Cuthbert's, Hunt's Bay
**St. Andrew** · Mission · inactive

St. Cuthbert's Anglican Church serves **Hunt's Bay**, a low-lying coastal and industrial community at the western end of the Kingston waterfront. The dedication to **St. Cuthbert of Lindisfarne** — the hermit-monk and bishop who became one of the most beloved saints of the early English church (feast day **20 March**) — is rare in Jamaica.

## History
**English conquest (1655) and St. Andrew parish (1664).** Penn and Venables's English expedition captured Jamaica from Spain in May **1655**. **St. Andrew parish was established in 1664** with a 600-acre glebe granted in 1667 by Charles II.

**Port Royal, the 1692 earthquake, and the founding of Kingston (1693).** The **Great Earthquake of 7 June 1692** destroyed two-thirds of Port Royal; the new city of **Kingston** was formally established on **22 July 1692**, with the Assembly constituting Kingston as a parish in **1693**.

**Slavery, Diocese, Sam Sharpe.** Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church. The **Diocese of Jamaica** was established by Letters Patent on **24 July 1824**; **Christopher Lipscomb** was consecrated first Bishop in 1824. During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation (1838) and disestablishment (1870).** Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. Bishop Lipscomb (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

**1872 capital, 1907 earthquake, 1923 KSAC.** The capital moved from Spanish Town to **Kingston in 1872**. The **14 January 1907 earthquake** destroyed much of Kingston; under Nuttall's £50,000 reconstruction programme, ten churches were consecrated within a single month in **January 1911**. The **Kingston and St. Andrew Corporation (KSAC)** unified the two parishes for civic administration in **1923**.

**Foundation of St. Cuthbert's.** Hunt's Bay sits on the southern coast of St. Andrew at the edge of Kingston Harbour, in the low-lying coastal industrial zone. The Anglican Diocese established St. Cuthbert's to serve this harbour-side community. The unusual dedication to the **7th-century Northumbrian monk-bishop** may reflect a founding clergyman with personal devotion to the north English monastic tradition.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Cuthbert's a coastal mission.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The low-lying coastal position creates significant storm surge vulnerability — Hunt's Bay and the southern coastline of St. Andrew/Kingston are among the areas most directly exposed to surge from storms approaching from the south.

## Architecture
St. Cuthbert's is a coastal church at Hunt's Bay directly exposed to Kingston Harbour. The low-lying coastal position creates significant storm surge vulnerability during hurricane passages.

## Clergy
Hunt's Bay has been served within the **southern St. Andrew deanery circuit** under shared clergy arrangements.

## Notable Facts
**St. Cuthbert of Lindisfarne** (c. 634–687) is one of the most celebrated saints of the early English church, known for his simple life as a hermit on the **Farne Islands** and his pastoral care as Bishop of Lindisfarne. The **Lindisfarne Gospels** were produced in his honour. His dedication to a church in a Jamaican harbour community creates an unusual connection between the remote northern English monastic tradition and the Caribbean urban coast.
