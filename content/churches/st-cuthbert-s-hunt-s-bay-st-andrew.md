# St. Cuthbert's, Hunt's Bay
**St. Andrew** · Mission · inactive

St. Cuthbert's Anglican Church serves Hunt's Bay, a community in southern St. Andrew on the coast of Kingston Harbour. Hunt's Bay is a low-lying coastal and industrial community at the western end of the Kingston waterfront, and St. Cuthbert's provides Anglican ministry to this harbour-side community. The dedication to St. Cuthbert of Lindisfarne — the hermit-monk and bishop who became one of the most beloved saints of the early English church — is rare in Jamaica.

## History
Hunt's Bay sits on the southern coast of St. Andrew at the edge of Kingston Harbour, in the low-lying coastal industrial zone. The Anglican Diocese established St. Cuthbert's to serve this harbour-side community. The unusual dedication to the 7th-century Northumbrian monk-bishop may reflect a founding clergyman with personal devotion to the north English monastic tradition.

## Architecture
St. Cuthbert's is a coastal church at Hunt's Bay Andrew directly exposed to Kingston Harbour. The low-lying coastal position creates significant storm surge vulnerability during hurricane passages — Hunt's Bay and the southern coastline of St. Andrew/Kingston are among the areas most directly exposed to surge from storms approaching from the south.

## Clergy
Hunt's Bay has been served within the southern St. Andrew deanery circuit under shared clergy arrangements.

## Notable Facts
St. Cuthbert of Lindisfarne (c. 634–687) is one of the most celebrated saints of the early English church, known for his simple life as a hermit on the Farne Islands and his pastoral care as Bishop of Lindisfarne. The Lindisfarne Gospels were produced in his honour. His dedication to a church in a Jamaican harbour community creates an unusual connection between the remote northern English monastic tradition and the Caribbean urban coast.
