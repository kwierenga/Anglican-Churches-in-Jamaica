# St. James', Mount James
**St. Andrew** · Church · active

St. James' Anglican Church serves Mount James, a community in the hill country of northern St. Andrew. Mount James is a residential and agricultural district, and the church has provided Anglican ministry here for several generations. The dedication to St. James the Apostle — patron of pilgrims and travellers — gives this hillside church a patron associated with the journey of faith through difficult terrain.

## History
Mount James sits in the northern hills of St. Andrew, in the elevated terrain approaching the Blue Mountains. The Anglican Diocese established St. James' to serve this hill community. The name Mount James may reflect the church's dedication, with the community taking its name from the church rather than the reverse.

## Architecture
St. James' is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural St. Andrew in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. Set in the hilly interior of St. Andrew. The elevated terrain channels wind during storm passages and makes good drainage essential to the building's longevity.

## Clergy
Mount James has been served within the northern St. Andrew deanery circuit under shared clergy arrangements with the Stony Hill cluster.

## Notable Facts
The name Mount James — potentially derived from the church's patron — exemplifies a common Jamaican naming pattern in which communities take their identity from the local church, particularly in hill country where the church was often the first substantial institution in a newly settled area. St. James' Day (25 July) is one of the major feasts in the Anglican calendar.
