# St. Joseph's, Innswood/McCook's Pen
**St. Catherine** · Church · active

St. Joseph's Anglican Church serves the **Innswood/McCook's Pen** area of St. Catherine, a community in the south-eastern interior of the parish between Spanish Town and the south coast. **McCook's Pen** preserves the "pen" designation — a livestock-raising estate.

## History
**Spanish Villa de la Vega (1534) and English conquest (1655).** The Spanish founded **Villa de la Vega** in 1534 — Jamaica's capital until **1872**. Penn and Venables's English expedition captured Jamaica in May **1655**.

**Founding parishes.** Under English rule from 1655, **St. Catherine** was one of Jamaica's original parishes. The neighbouring early parishes of **St. John, St. Dorothy, and St. Thomas-in-the-Vale** were merged into expanded St. Catherine under **Law 20 of 1867**.

**Slavery and the planter church.** Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds. **The Diocese of Jamaica** was established by Letters Patent on **24 July 1824**; **Christopher Lipscomb** was consecrated first Bishop in 1824.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838), Sligoville.** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Sligoville** in St. Catherine became Jamaica's **first free village** in 1838, founded with land provided by **Howe Peter Browne, 2nd Marquess of Sligo**, then Governor of Jamaica. Bishop Lipscomb (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. Capital moved from Spanish Town to **Kingston in 1872**. The 1845 Jamaica Almanac records McCook's Pen among the parish proprietors.

**Foundation of St. Joseph's.** Innswood and McCook's Pen sit in the south-eastern interior of St. Catherine, in the agricultural landscape between Spanish Town and the south coast. The Anglican Diocese established St. Joseph's to serve this community.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Joseph's a settled rural mission. The area is now also associated with industrial and residential development on the outskirts of the Greater Spanish Town area.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Hurricane Melissa brought wind and rain to St. Catherine.

## Architecture
St. Joseph's is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural St. Catherine in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
Innswood/McCook's Pen has been served within the **St. Catherine deanery** under shared clergy arrangements.

## Notable Facts
**McCook's Pen** preserves the **"pen"** designation that was common across Jamaica's agricultural landscape in the colonial era — a "pen" was a livestock-raising estate, supplying working animals, beef, and dairy products to the plantation system. The dedication to **St. Joseph** (feast day **19 March**) — the foster father of Jesus — is relatively uncommon in Jamaican Anglican practice.
