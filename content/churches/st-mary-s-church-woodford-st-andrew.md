# St. Mary's Church, Woodford
**St. Andrew** · Church · active

St. Christopher's Anglican Church is a small rural church serving the community of Woodford in the hills of St. Andrew parish. Like many Anglican churches in Jamaica's interior, it grew out of the Diocese's effort to establish mission stations beyond the main parish towns following Emancipation. Woodford sits in the agricultural hinterland of St. Andrew, historically associated with coffee and provision farming. St. Christopher's has served as the spiritual anchor for this quiet community for generations, providing worship and pastoral care to families in the surrounding hills.

## History
The origins of St. Christopher's lie in the post-Emancipation expansion of Anglican ministry into the rural districts of St. Andrew. Until the mid-19th century, St. Andrew Parish Church at Half Way Tree was the sole Anglican church in the parish, but it established mission stations across the interior to reach a dispersed farming population. The Diocese's connection to Woodford is documented through clergy such as Reverend Canon Harpaul, who served as priest in charge of Woodford and other St. Andrew missions. The church reflects the broader story of how freed people and their descendants built new community institutions in the decades after 1838.

## Architecture
St. Christopher's is a modest rural church typical of Anglican mission buildings erected in Jamaica's interior during the late 19th and early 20th centuries. Such structures generally featured a simple rectangular nave, pitched roof, louvred windows for ventilation, and plain timber or masonry construction suited to local materials. Set in the Woodford hills, the church is surrounded by the agricultural landscape that has defined this part of St. Andrew for centuries. Its architecture is functional rather than ornate, reflecting the modest means of the rural congregation it was built to serve.

## Clergy
St. Christopher's has been served by clergy of the Diocese of Jamaica, often shared across multiple rural mission stations in the St. Andrew hills. The ministry of Reverend Canon Harpaul in Woodford illustrates the pattern by which the Diocese sustained rural congregations through itinerant, multi-charge ministry — one priest serving several small communities. This model was common across Jamaica's interior parishes and required clergy of considerable dedication. The church continues today as part of the Anglican Diocese of Jamaica and the Cayman Islands.

## Notable Facts
Woodford is part of the rural interior of St. Andrew that produced coffee, provisions, and livestock — a very different world from the commercial suburbs closer to Kingston. The Anglican presence there is part of the Diocese's wider effort to reach Jamaica's hills through mission stations, many of which have endured for over a century. The church's dedication to Saint Christopher, patron saint of travellers, is a fitting choice for a hilltop community connected to the wider world by winding country roads.
