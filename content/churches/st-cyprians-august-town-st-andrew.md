# St Cyprian’s, August Town
**St. Andrew** · Church · active

St. Cyprian’s Anglican Church serves the historic community of August Town, a hillside settlement along the Hope River below the University of the West Indies. The community grew from the labourers of the Mona Estate, and many congregants are descendants of enslaved Africans. The church has provided a steady presence for worship and sacraments in a district that has long faced poverty and social challenges. During crises like Hurricane Melissa (2025), the church and its networks were vital in organizing food and support for residents who suffered significant losses.

## History
St. Cyprian’s roots lie in the post-Emancipation era, serving the community of August Town, which was established by laborers from the Mona Estate. By 1891, records show 'August Town' as an Anglican mission station under St. Andrew Parish Church, ministering to a population whose recent ancestors had lived through slavery. The church's development occurred alongside the rise of influential religious movements in August Town, most notably the Jamaica Native Baptist Church led by the charismatic Alexander Bedward. While Bedwardism offered a different form of spiritual expression, the Anglican mission provided a traditional Christian presence, and over the 20th century, it evolved into the full parish of St. Cyprian’s, enduring as a stable institution through the community's complex social history.

## Architecture
While detailed architectural records are scarce, St. Cyprian’s is characteristic of a small, functional Anglican church in a hillside community. It is likely a simple, rectangular masonry structure with louvered windows for ventilation, designed for practicality rather than architectural grandeur. The property includes a churchyard that serves as the local cemetery, a common feature of such parishes. Its location on the steep slopes of August Town means the building and its grounds are intrinsically shaped by the landscape, requiring careful management of drainage and access, especially during heavy rains.

## Clergy
Historically, August Town was served by clergy from larger parishes like St. Andrew's in Half Way Tree, who traveled to the mission for services. Today, St. Cyprian’s is part of a modern clergy cluster with St. Margaret’s in Liguanea and St. Joseph’s in The Grove, sharing pastoral resources. The day-to-day ministry relies heavily on dedicated lay leaders—wardens, choir members, and Mothers’ Union members—who sustain worship, pastoral care, and community ties. The church also connects with local institutions like the August Town Primary School, which serves as a community hub and emergency shelter.

## Notable Facts
August Town holds a significant place in Jamaican religious history as the center of Alexander Bedward's movement, with his tomb designated a National Heritage Site. This context makes the enduring presence of St. Cyprian’s a testament to the diverse spiritual landscape of the community. During Hurricane Melissa (2025), while the church building was not reported as destroyed, the community suffered greatly, with many residents losing roofs and walls. The church and its networks became critical support centers, helping to organize meals and relief efforts for affected families, continuing its long-standing role of standing with the community through times of crisis.
