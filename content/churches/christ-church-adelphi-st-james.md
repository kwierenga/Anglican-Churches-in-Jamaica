# Christ Church, Adelphi
**St. James** · Church · active

Christ Church Anglican serves Adelphi, a rural community in the interior hills of St. James parish, southeast of Montego Bay. The church has long provided Anglican ministry to the surrounding farming districts that make up the Adelphi cure.

## History
Adelphi sits in the elevated interior of St. James, in the hill country east of the Great River valley and inland from the coast. The settlement traces its name to a former sugar estate, one of many properties in the area whose place-names survive long after the plantations themselves were broken up. The Anglican Diocese established Christ Church to serve the community of small-holders and labouring families that grew up around the estate lands following Emancipation in 1838. The church became a focal point for worship, baptism, and education in a district where the nearest larger centres of Cambridge and Montpelier lay several hours away by foot or cart.

## Architecture
Christ Church is a modest masonry church in the practical style typical of rural Anglican congregations in the St. James interior. The building follows a simple rectangular plan with a nave, a small chancel, and pointed-arch windows. Construction in local stone and rendered block, together with a pitched metal roof, reflects the materials and weather considerations of Jamaica's hill country. The site sits within a small churchyard which serves the surrounding districts.

## Clergy
Christ Church Adelphi falls within the St. James Deanery of the Diocese of Jamaica and the Cayman Islands, sharing clergy with neighbouring congregations in the Cambridge and Montpelier cures. Lay readers and visiting priests have long been essential to maintaining a regular pattern of worship.

## Notable Facts
Adelphi takes its name from the Greek "adelphoi" ("brothers"), a name applied to a number of Jamaican estates by 18th-century proprietors and reflecting the classical naming fashion of the colonial era. The community's setting in the hills places it well clear of coastal storm surge but exposed to the rainfall and wind of major hurricanes that periodically cross the island.
