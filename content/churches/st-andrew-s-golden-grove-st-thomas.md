# St. Andrew's, Golden Grove
**St. Thomas** · Church · active

St. Andrew's Anglican Church serves **Golden Grove**, a community in the eastern coastal plain of St. Thomas parish near the **Plantain Garden River**. Golden Grove is a significant community in the sugar-producing district of eastern St. Thomas — historically one of the most productive sugar landscapes in the British Caribbean.

## History
**English rule (1655) and the founding parish.** Under English rule from **1655**, St. Thomas was one of Jamaica's original parishes — originally **"St. Thomas-in-the-East"** to distinguish from St. Thomas-in-the-Vale (now part of St. Catherine), with the first rector appointed in **1666**.

**Sugar economy and slavery — the Plantain Garden River valley.** The **Plantain Garden River valley** was intensively cultivated for sugar from the 17th century onwards, and the community of Golden Grove developed within this plantation landscape. Great estates of the area included **Stokes Hall, Golden Grove, Albion, and Holland**, all worked by enslaved Africans. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds. **Captain Bligh** landed at Port Morant with **breadfruit plants** brought from Tahiti in **1793** — the breadfruit was introduced to Jamaica as a food source for the enslaved population.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union** to "wipe non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb** (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**The Morant Bay Rebellion (11 October 1865).** On **11 October 1865**, **Paul Bogle** — a small farmer and Native Baptist deacon of **Stony Gut** — led a protest march to the Morant Bay courthouse. Custos Baron von Ketelhodt was killed. Governor **Edward John Eyre's** brutal response: **439 executed**, **600+ flogged**, **1,000+ homes burned**. Bogle was hanged on **24 October 1865**; **George William Gordon** was hanged on **23 October 1865**. **Both were declared National Heroes in 1965** at a centenary ceremony in Morant Bay.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall.** The House of Assembly voted itself out of existence; Jamaica became a **Crown Colony in 1866**. Law 20 of 1867 absorbed **St. David** into St. Thomas. The Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

**Foundation of St. Andrew's.** The Anglican Diocese established St. Andrew's to serve the estate community at Golden Grove and later the post-Emancipation farming families of the district.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** Internal self-government came in **1944**. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** fulfilled the aspirations for which Bogle and Gordon died. The Plantain Garden River valley of eastern St. Thomas was devastated by **Hurricane Ivan (2004)**, which caused catastrophic flooding across the river's lower course.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Hurricane Melissa brought heavy rain to south-eastern Jamaica; the Golden Grove area and congregation were in the affected zone.

## Architecture
St. Andrew's is a coastal-plain church in the low-lying eastern St. Thomas landscape. The area around Duckenfield and Golden Grove is directly exposed to the Caribbean Sea and to flooding from the Plantain Garden River, creating compound hurricane vulnerability from surge and inland flooding.

## Clergy
Golden Grove has been served within the **St. Thomas deanery** under shared clergy arrangements, connected to the Port Morant circuit given its eastern St. Thomas location.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to **St. Andrew the apostle** (feast day **30 November**) — patron of Scotland and brother of Simon Peter — reflects the significant Scottish presence in the colonial-era plantation economy. The Plantain Garden River valley's intensive sugar cultivation made it one of the most productive plantation landscapes in the British Caribbean.
