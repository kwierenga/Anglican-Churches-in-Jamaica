# St. Peter's, Wait-a-Bit
**Manchester** · Church · inactive

St. Peter's Anglican Church serves Wait-a-Bit in northern Manchester, part of the Albert Town cure. Wait-a-Bit is one of Jamaica's most characteristically named communities — the name is believed to derive from the wait-a-bit thorn (Acacia nilotica or similar thorny shrub), which grows prolifically in the Jamaican interior and catches travellers on their clothing, compelling them to stop and disentangle. The name perfectly captures the rugged, thorny landscape of the Cockpit Country edge.

## History
Wait-a-Bit is a community in the hills of northern Manchester. The name is found in several Caribbean territories and refers to the thorny scrub vegetation that catches travellers. The community is part of the Albert Town cure in the remote northern interior of Manchester.

## Architecture
St. Peter's is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural Manchester in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. Set in the rural interior of Manchester. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
The Albert Town cure covering Wait-a-Bit is served from the Albert Town rectory.

## Notable Facts
Wait-a-Bit is among the most evocative community names in Jamaica — the thorny shrub that names it was a literal obstacle for travellers through the interior before road-clearing. The name joins a tradition of expressive Jamaican place names including Come See, Turn Back, Lookout, Rest and Be Thankful, and others that describe a traveller's experience of the landscape. St. Peter, the gatekeeper of heaven, has an appropriate association with a community whose name is about being stopped in your tracks.
