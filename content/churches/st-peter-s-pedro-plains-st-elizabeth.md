# St. Peter's, Pedro Plains
**St. Elizabeth** · Church · active

St. Peter’s Anglican Church serves the farming communities of Pedro Plains and Flagaman in southern St. Elizabeth. Founded in the late 1800s, it provided a place of worship for freed families who had settled on the fertile plains after Emancipation. Despite facing hurricanes and other challenges over the years, the church has remained a constant presence in the community. Today, it continues to be an important spiritual center for the people living in the Southfield–Flagaman–Pedro Plains area.

## History
St. Peter’s Anglican Church was founded in the late 19th century, as freed people established new communities in the post-Emancipation period. The Pedro Plains region, known for its small farms, was served by Anglican clergy who traveled from Southfield and Bull Savannah. Initially, congregations met in simple buildings, but by the 1880s a permanent stone chapel was completed and dedicated to St. Peter the Apostle. The church quickly became a central place for worship, baptisms, and community meetings, helping to strengthen the new free villages in the area.

## Architecture
St. Peter's is a classic example of a rural Jamaican Anglican church, built from local limestone in a simple Gothic Revival style. It features a rectangular nave, a small chancel, and a modest bell-cot above the western entrance. The steeply pitched roof, originally made of wooden shingles and now covered with metal, is designed for the rainy climate. Inside, the church has an open wooden roof, wooden pews, and narrow pointed windows with colored glass. The surrounding churchyard contains graves of many long-established local families.

## Clergy
St. Peter’s has always been part of the Black River Deanery, served by priests who also ministered in Southfield, Bull Savannah, and Barbary Hall. Early clergy, such as Rev. H. C. Wharton and Canon W. R. Campbell, oversaw several rural congregations at once. The church once supported a small school, providing local children with education in reading, writing, and religion. Following Jamaica’s Independence in 1962, the church remained active with youth groups and the Mothers’ Union. Lay readers were essential in leading services when ordained clergy were not available.

## Notable Facts
St. Peter’s has endured many hurricanes, including Hurricane Charlie (1951) and Hurricane Gilbert (1988), which caused damage to the roof and windows. Each time, the congregation made repairs through local fundraising and diocesan support. The church received further structural reinforcements to its roof and bell-cot in the 2000s. There are no reports of damage to the church from Hurricane Melissa (2025). Today, St. Peter’s continues to hold weekly services, weddings, and community events, standing as a stable symbol of faith and community in the Pedro Plains and Flagaman districts.
