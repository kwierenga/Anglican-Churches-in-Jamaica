# St. Peter's, Pedro Plains
**St. Elizabeth** · Church · active

St. Peter's Anglican Church serves Pedro Plains, a farming community on the dry south coast of St. Elizabeth near Treasure Beach. Built in 1941 on a site where Anglican worship had been established since at least the 1890s, the church is the mother church of a six-church cure under Rev. Marjorie Downer. In July 2024, Hurricane Beryl caused a complete roof collapse, leaving St. Peter's — which can hold 300 people — in ruins with a multimillion-dollar repair bill. Hurricane Melissa, a Category 5 storm, followed in October 2025. The dedication to St. Peter the Fisherman resonates with a community that sits just inland from the fishing villages of the south coast.

## History
Pedro Plains takes its name from the Spanish colonial period (1509–1655). The Spanish established cattle ranches called *hatos* across Jamaica's interior plains, and *Hato Pereda* — anglicised to "Pedro" after the English conquest in 1655 — was the name for this area. The Taino people preceded the Spanish: a cave on the nearby Pedro Bluff contains archaeological remains of their occupation. Under English rule, the south coast economy was built on enslaved labour. By 1820, St. Elizabeth held 19,129 enslaved people across all listed properties. While the Pedro Plains area was drier and more suited to cattle and provisions than the sugar estates around Black River, enslaved workers remained fundamental to the economy.

The Anglican Church's presence in the area began in the colonial era, when the established church served the planter class. As the state church, it received over £37,000 annually in government stipends and was widely known as the "church of slave-owners." After Emancipation in 1834 and full freedom on 1 August 1838, freed people established smallholdings across Pedro Plains and the south coast, building the farming communities that the church would eventually serve. The transformation from planter church to mission church was gradual: disestablishment in 1870 removed state funding, and under Bishop Nuttall (1880–1916) mission stations expanded from 26 to 122 across Jamaica.

By the 1900 Handbook of Jamaica, Rev. C. T. Rickard served "Plains, Mayfield, and 8 mission stations" — "Plains" almost certainly referring to Pedro Plains, given the geography. The current building was constructed in 1941, replacing earlier mission structures. Post-pandemic attendance had fallen to 30–40 worshippers in a church built for 300 — a reflection of the rural depopulation that has challenged small Anglican churches across Jamaica since independence on 6 August 1962.

Hurricane Beryl struck in July 2024, causing a complete roof collapse that left St. Peter's in ruins. Rev. Downer, who arrived in November 2019 from the Church of the Holy Spirit in Portmore, drew comparisons to Hurricane Gilbert (1988), which had also damaged the church. Before Beryl, the church had received a "much needed facelift" in 2018. Then Hurricane Melissa, a Category 5 storm, struck western Jamaica on 28 October 2025, devastating St. Elizabeth further — the 300-year-old parish church at Black River was largely destroyed, and St. Thomas' Lacovia lost its roof. The double blow of Beryl and Melissa has left St. Peter's facing an uncertain future.

## Architecture
St. Peter's was built in 1941 in the local limestone and masonry tradition of rural St. Elizabeth Anglican churches. The building features a rectangular nave with capacity for 300, a modest chancel, and a bell-cot. The roof, which collapsed under Hurricane Beryl, was metal-sheeted over what was originally a steep timber-framed structure designed for the south coast's rainy season.

## Education
Pedro Plains Primary School serves the community. The Anglican mission to educate has been central to the church's purpose across St. Elizabeth, where Bishop Lipscomb established 142 elementary schools in the 1830s. Munro College (1856) and Hampton School (1874), both Anglican-founded, are the premier educational institutions in the parish. A US$200-million irrigation project from the Black River is planned to serve over 4,000 farmers across 4,000 hectares in the Pedro Plains area — a development that could revitalise the farming community the church serves.

## Clergy
Rev. Marjorie Downer serves St. Peter's as mother church of a six-church cure that includes St. Paul's Barbary Hall, All Saints' Newell, St. Matthew's Orange Grove, St. Augustine's Mountainside, and The Epiphany Big Woods. She was ordained in St. Elizabeth after previously leading the Church of the Holy Spirit in Portmore.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to St. Peter — the Jewish fisherman from Bethsaida whom Jesus called to be a "fisher of men" and named Cephas, "the rock" — is deeply fitting for a community near the fishing villages of Treasure Beach. The south coast has a distinctive Scottish heritage: shipwrecked Scottish sailors in the 1830s settled and intermarried with the local population, giving Treasure Beach its unique character. Pedro Plains itself is part of Jamaica's "breadbasket" — the driest area in the parish, where farmers grow watermelons, scotch bonnet peppers, onions, and provisions. St. Peter's feast day is 29 June.
