# St. Ann Parish Church, St. Ann's Bay
**St. Ann** · Parish Church · active

St. Ann Parish Church is the principal Anglican church of St. Ann's Bay, the capital town of St. Ann parish — the "Garden Parish" on Jamaica's north coast. Completed in 1871, the current building stands on a site with Anglican worship stretching back to the earlier eighteenth century, in a parish whose history encompasses the first Spanish settlement on the island, the birthplace of a National Hero, and the richest agricultural land on Jamaica's north side.

## History
St. Ann has a deeper colonial past than almost any other Jamaican parish. Sevilla la Nueva, the first Spanish capital of Jamaica, was established in 1509 near the present town of St. Ann's Bay, and Columbus made landfall at Discovery Bay on his second voyage in 1494. Under British rule from 1655, the parish became a major sugar and livestock region, its coastal plain given over to sugar estates and its interior plateau to "pens" — the Jamaican term for livestock properties.

The Anglican Church served as the established church of this plantation system. The vestry was composed of the planter class; the clergy were paid by the colonial government; the parish administration handled roads, poor relief, and the maintenance of social order alongside ecclesiastical duties. The enslaved population of St. Ann — who laboured on the sugar estates of Drax Hall, Seville, and dozens of other properties — produced the wealth that built St. Ann's Bay as a north-coast export town.

Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on 1 August 1838. The freed population of St. Ann established free villages and provision grounds across the parish's interior, and the Anglican Church expanded its mission to serve these new communities. The present parish church was completed in September 1871, replacing an earlier building and constructed to meet the needs of a post-Emancipation congregation that had grown substantially in the preceding decades.

St. Ann's Bay is the birthplace of Marcus Mosiah Garvey, born 17 August 1887 — Jamaica's first National Hero and the founder of the Universal Negro Improvement Association, whose ideas shaped twentieth-century Pan-Africanism. Jamaica's independence on 6 August 1962 found St. Ann Parish Church at the civic centre of a parish whose identity was shaped by its Spanish origins, its plantation history, and Garvey's legacy.

## Architecture
St. Ann Parish Church is a solid cut-stone building in the Gothic Revival style common for important Anglican churches of the later nineteenth century. It features a rectangular nave, a steeply pitched roof, and pointed-arch windows. The interior contains memorial tablets dating from the 1700s and 1800s, some of which reference enslaved Africans — a tangible link to the plantation-era congregation. The churchyard contains graves spanning the colonial and post-colonial periods.

## Education
The Anglican mission to educate was central to St. Ann's post-Emancipation development, continuing Bishop Lipscomb's initiative which founded over 80 elementary schools across Jamaica in the 1830s. The clergy of St. Ann Parish Church oversaw Sunday schools and supported local primary education across the parish, a tradition that continued into the twentieth century through partnerships with Anglican schools in St. Ann's Bay and the surrounding communities.

## Clergy
As the principal Anglican church of St. Ann, the parish church has been the seat of the St. Ann deanery and a hub for ministry across the north coast. Its clergy oversaw a network of mission chapels extending from the coast to the interior plateau, coordinating the Diocese's presence across one of Jamaica's largest and most populous parishes.

## Notable Facts
St. Ann is the birthplace of Marcus Garvey — the only Jamaican parish capital to claim a National Hero among its natives. The parish's Spanish origins at Sevilla la Nueva make it the oldest documented European settlement site on the island. St. Ann Parish Church has weathered more than a century and a half of Atlantic hurricanes, including Hurricane Gilbert (1988), which struck the north coast with severe force. Hurricane Melissa (28 October 2025) tracked along Jamaica's north coast; St. Ann's Bay sustained impact, with wider damage reported across the parish.
