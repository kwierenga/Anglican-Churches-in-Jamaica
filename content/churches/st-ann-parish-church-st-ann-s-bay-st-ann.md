# St. Ann Parish Church, St. Ann's Bay
**St. Ann** · Parish Church · active

St. Ann Parish Church stands at the corner of Main and Church Street in **St. Ann's Bay**, the capital of the "Garden Parish" on Jamaica's north coast. The present building was completed in **September 1871** and consecrated as the Church of "St. Anne" by the **Rt. Rev. Reginald Courtenay**, Bishop of Kingston, on Wednesday **20 September 1871**. It stands in a parish whose history encompasses the first Spanish settlement in Jamaica, the birthplace of Marcus Garvey, and some of the richest agricultural land on the north side of the island.

## History
**Pre-English settlement: Sevilla la Nueva (1509–1534).** St. Ann has a deeper colonial past than almost any other Jamaican parish. **Sevilla la Nueva**, the first permanent European settlement in Jamaica and the colony's first Spanish capital, was founded in **1509** by Juan de Esquivel near present St. Ann's Bay. It served as the Spanish capital until **1534**, when the settlement was relocated to Villa de la Vega (Spanish Town). **Christopher Columbus** had been marooned at St. Ann's Bay for over a year (1503–1504) on his fourth voyage.

**English rule (1655) and the planter church.** Under English rule from **1655**, the parish became a major sugar and livestock region. The coastal plain was given over to sugar estates — **Drax Hall, Seville**, and dozens of others — and the interior plateau to "**pens**," the Jamaican term for livestock properties. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds; its vestry was composed of the planter class, its parish administration extending to roads, poor relief, and the maintenance of social order. The enslaved population of St. Ann, who laboured on the sugar estates, produced the wealth that built the parish capital.

**Sam Sharpe Rebellion and the Colonial Church Union (1831–32).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, **Hamilton Brown** of Brown's Town (founder 1805 of St. Mark's there) led a Colonial Church Union mob that destroyed the Brown's Town Baptist chapel in 1832 — the **Colonial Church Union** sought to "wipe the non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth." **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation (1838) and free villages.** Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. The freed population established free villages and provision grounds across the parish's interior. **Sturge Town** (founded 1839 by the English Quaker abolitionist **Joseph Sturge**) and **Clarksonville** (founded 1835 by Baptist missionary Rev. **John Clark**, named for the abolitionist **Thomas Clarkson**) were among Jamaica's earliest free villages — both in St. Ann (NLJ, *History of St. Ann*). Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb** (1824–1843) had begun extending Anglican schools across Jamaica — by 1835 he reported 142 schools with 8,500 scholars.

**Building the present parish church (1871).** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica. The present parish church was completed in **September 1871**, replacing an earlier building to meet the needs of a congregation that had grown substantially since Emancipation. The contract was awarded to **Climie, Colonial Engineer**, who built the main walls of cut stone in the Gothic Revival manner characteristic of late-nineteenth-century Jamaican Anglican architecture. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

**Marcus Garvey, bauxite, Independence.** **Marcus Mosiah Garvey** was born on **17 August 1887 at 32 Market Street** in St. Ann's Bay, a short distance from the parish church. The founder of the Universal Negro Improvement Association and African Communities League, Garvey became Jamaica's first **National Hero**, conferred with the Order of National Hero in 1969; his remains were returned to Jamaica in 1964 and rest at National Heroes Park in Kingston. **Bob Marley** was born on **6 February 1945** at **Nine Mile** in St. Ann. Bauxite mining began in St. Ann around **1952** (Reynolds, Kaiser); **Discovery Bay** (named after Columbus's landing) became a major bauxite port. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Ann Parish Church at the civic centre of a parish whose identity was shaped by its Spanish origins, its plantation history, and Garvey's legacy.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Melissa tracked across western and northern Jamaica, causing widespread damage across the Diocese.

## Architecture
The church is a cut-stone Gothic Revival building with a series of pointed-arch windows along each elevation, separated by buttresses. The apse is semi-octagonal with buttresses at the corners, flanked by pointed-arch stained-glass windows, the largest at the east end. The churchyard contains memorial tablets dating from the 1700s and 1800s, some of which reference enslaved Africans — a tangible link to the plantation-era congregation.

## Education
The Anglican mission to educate was central to St. Ann's post-Emancipation development, part of the Diocese's wider effort to provide schools for the freed population. The clergy of St. Ann Parish Church oversaw Sunday schools and supported local primary education across the parish.

## Clergy
As the principal Anglican church of St. Ann, the parish church has been a hub for ministry across the north coast. Its clergy coordinated a network of mission chapels extending from the coast to the interior plateau — the Diocese's presence across one of Jamaica's largest parishes. The 1900 **Handbook of Jamaica** records the cure attached to the parish church, and the 1891 Handbook lists J. B. Ellis among the parish's clergy.

## Notable Facts
St. Ann is the birthplace of **Marcus Garvey** — the only Jamaican parish capital to claim the island's first National Hero among its native sons. The parish's Spanish origins at **Sevilla la Nueva (1509)** make it the oldest documented European settlement in Jamaica, predating the English takeover by almost a century and a half. The parish church's contractor, Climie, is documented by the Jamaica National Heritage Trust as a Colonial Engineer. Hurricane Gilbert (1988) struck the north coast with severe force.
