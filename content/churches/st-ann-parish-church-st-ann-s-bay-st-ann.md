# St. Ann Parish Church, St. Ann's Bay
**St. Ann** · Parish Church · active

St. Ann Parish Church stands at the corner of Main and Church Street in St. Ann's Bay, the capital of the "Garden Parish" on Jamaica's north coast. The present building was completed in September 1871 and consecrated as the Church of "St. Anne" by the Bishop of Kingston, the Rt. Rev. Reginald Courtenay, on Wednesday 20 September 1871. It stands in a parish whose history encompasses the first Spanish settlement in Jamaica, the birthplace of Marcus Garvey, and some of the richest agricultural land on the north side of the island.

## History
St. Ann has a deeper colonial past than almost any other Jamaican parish. Sevilla la Nueva, the first permanent European settlement in Jamaica and the colony's first Spanish capital, was founded in 1509 by Juan de Esquivel near the present town of St. Ann's Bay. It served as the Spanish capital until 1534, when the settlement was relocated to Villa de la Vega (Spanish Town).

Under English rule from 1655, the parish became a major sugar and livestock region. The coastal plain was given over to sugar estates — Drax Hall, Seville, and dozens of others — and the interior plateau to "pens," the Jamaican term for livestock properties. Until 1870, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds; its vestry was composed of the planter class, its parish administration extending to roads, poor relief, and the maintenance of social order. The enslaved population of St. Ann, who laboured on the sugar estates, produced the wealth that built the parish capital.

Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on 1 August 1838. The freed population established free villages and provision grounds across the parish's interior. The present parish church was completed in 1871, replacing an earlier building to meet the needs of a congregation that had grown substantially since Emancipation. The contract was awarded to Climie, Colonial Engineer, who built the main walls of cut stone in the Gothic Revival manner characteristic of late-nineteenth-century Jamaican Anglican architecture.

Marcus Mosiah Garvey was born on 17 August 1887 at 32 Market Street in St. Ann's Bay, a short distance from the parish church. The founder of the Universal Negro Improvement Association and African Communities League, Garvey became Jamaica's first National Hero, conferred with the Order of National Hero in 1969; his remains were returned to Jamaica in 1964 and rest at National Heroes Park in Kingston.

Jamaica's independence on 6 August 1962 found St. Ann Parish Church at the civic centre of a parish whose identity was shaped by its Spanish origins, its plantation history, and Garvey's legacy.

## Architecture
The church is a cut-stone Gothic Revival building with a series of pointed-arch windows along each elevation, separated by buttresses. The apse is semi-octagonal with buttresses at the corners, flanked by pointed-arch stained-glass windows, the largest at the east end. The churchyard contains memorial tablets dating from the 1700s and 1800s, some of which reference enslaved Africans — a tangible link to the plantation-era congregation.

## Education
The Anglican mission to educate was central to St. Ann's post-Emancipation development, part of the Diocese's wider effort to provide schools for the freed population. The clergy of St. Ann Parish Church oversaw Sunday schools and supported local primary education across the parish.

## Clergy
As the principal Anglican church of St. Ann, the parish church has been a hub for ministry across the north coast. Its clergy coordinated a network of mission chapels extending from the coast to the interior plateau — the Diocese's presence across one of Jamaica's largest parishes.

## Notable Facts
St. Ann is the birthplace of Marcus Garvey — the only Jamaican parish capital to claim the island's first National Hero among its native sons. The parish's Spanish origins at Sevilla la Nueva (1509) make it the oldest documented European settlement in Jamaica, predating the English takeover by almost a century and a half. The parish church's contractor, Climie, is documented by the Jamaica National Heritage Trust as a Colonial Engineer. Hurricane Gilbert (1988) struck the north coast with severe force. Hurricane Melissa (28 October 2025) tracked across western and northern Jamaica, causing widespread damage across the Diocese.
