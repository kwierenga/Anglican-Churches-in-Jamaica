# St. Peter's, Rhoden Hall
**Clarendon** · Church · active

St. Peter's Anglican Church is a Chapel of Ease at Rhoden Hall in the Kellits area of northern Clarendon, near the St. Ann border. It is part of the Good Hope cure circuit administered from Crofts Hill, alongside St. Bartholomew's Good Hope and St. Michael's Arthur's Seat. The community name almost certainly derives from the Rodon family, prominent Clarendon slaveholders who held multiple estates in the interior of the parish including Frankfield, Rosewell, and Union.

## History
Rhoden Hall sits in the interior hill country of upper Clarendon — terrain suited to provisions farming, coffee, and small-scale agriculture rather than the sugar estates that dominated the Rio Minho lowlands. The Rodon family, from whom the community likely takes its name, were major Clarendon property holders. The 1831 Jamaica Almanac records H. and F. Rodon holding Frankfield (219 enslaved people), Rosewell (17 enslaved), and Union with Burrell (69 enslaved). George Rodon, who died at Rosewells Estate on 15 May 1813, had co-owned properties across Clarendon and Vere. The nearby Kellits area takes its name from Moses Kellet, a Clarendon assemblyman in the 1750s; the 1831 Almanac records William Shand holding "Kellit's" with 449 enslaved people.

After Emancipation in 1834 and full freedom on 1 August 1838, upper Clarendon became an area of extensive peasant settlement. Across Jamaica, 465 coffee plantations covering 188,400 acres were abandoned between 1832 and 1847 as the plantation economy collapsed. Freed people moved into the interior hills, establishing smallholdings and provision grounds. The Anglican Church followed these communities with chapels of ease — subsidiary worship points served by itinerant clergy from neighbouring cures.

St. Peter's Rhoden Hall does not appear in the 1891 or 1900 Handbooks of Jamaica, though the 1900 listing for Clarendon includes "10 Mission Stations" that are not named individually. The church was likely established in the late nineteenth or early twentieth century as part of the Anglican expansion into the interior, following the transformation from established church of the planter class to mission church serving the freed Black majority.

## Architecture
No detailed architectural records or photographs of St. Peter's Rhoden Hall are currently available.

## Education
No school specifically associated with St. Peter's has been confirmed. The Anglican mission to educate was a major driver of school establishment across rural Clarendon — Bishop Lipscomb founded over 80 elementary schools in the 1830s, and by 1900 the Diocese operated 263 schools island-wide.

## Clergy
St. Peter's is part of the Good Hope cure circuit, administered from the Crofts Hill rectory. The cure has been listed as vacant in recent diocesan records. The chapel's status as a Chapel of Ease (C/E) means it has always depended on clergy from the circuit rather than having its own resident priest.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to St. Peter — the fisherman whom Jesus called to be "the rock" on which the church was built — is one of the most common Anglican dedications in Jamaica. The Rhoden surname, likely derived from the Rodon planter family, is disproportionately concentrated in Jamaica: approximately 22 per cent of all people worldwide bearing the surname Rhoden live in Jamaica, reflecting the way enslaved and freed people adopted or were assigned the surnames of their former owners.
