# Christ Church, Moneague
**St. Ann** · Church · active

Christ Church Anglican serves the historic inland town of **Moneague** in St. Ann, an area known for its seasonal lake and old military post. The town was a well-known rest stop for travellers and soldiers moving between St. Ann's Bay and the interior, and Moneague Training Camp remains a Jamaica Defence Force facility.

## History
**Pre-English settlement: Sevilla la Nueva (1509–1534).** St. Ann has a deeper colonial past than almost any other Jamaican parish. **Sevilla la Nueva**, the first permanent European settlement in Jamaica and the colony's first Spanish capital, was founded in **1509** by Juan de Esquivel near present St. Ann's Bay. It served as Spanish capital until **1534** when relocated to Villa de la Vega (Spanish Town). **Christopher Columbus** had been marooned at St. Ann's Bay for over a year (1503–1504) on his fourth voyage.

**English rule (1655) and the planter church.** Under English rule from **1655**, St. Ann became a major **sugar and livestock** region — the coastal plain to sugar estates (Drax Hall, Seville, Windsor) and the interior plateau to "**pens**," the Jamaican term for livestock properties. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds; its vestry was composed of the planter class. The enslaved population of St. Ann produced the wealth that built the parish.

**Sam Sharpe Rebellion and the Colonial Church Union (1831–32).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, **Hamilton Brown** of Brown's Town (founder of St. Mark's there) led a Colonial Church Union mob that destroyed the Brown's Town Baptist chapel in 1832 — the **Colonial Church Union** sought to "wipe the non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth." **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation (1838) and free villages.** Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. The freed population settled the hills of inland St. Ann, establishing farms and new villages. **Sturge Town** (founded 1839 by the English Quaker abolitionist **Joseph Sturge**) and **Clarksonville** (founded 1835 by Baptist missionary Rev. John Clark, named for the abolitionist Thomas Clarkson) were among Jamaica's earliest free villages — both in St. Ann (NLJ, *History of St. Ann*). Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb** (1824–1843) had begun extending Anglican schools across Jamaica — by 1835 he reported 142 schools with 8,500 scholars.

**Foundation of Christ Church Moneague.** The Anglican Diocese built Christ Church in the 19th century to serve a growing post-Emancipation Moneague community as well as personnel from the nearby **Moneague Training Camp**. By the late 1800s, it had become an established centre for worship, baptism, education, and community life.

**Disestablishment (1870) and Nuttall expansion.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**, and Christ Church Moneague was consolidated within that broader expansion.

**Twentieth century: bauxite, tourism, Independence.** Bauxite mining began in St. Ann around **1952** (Reynolds, Kaiser); **Discovery Bay** (named after Columbus's landing) became a major bauxite port. Tourism grew at **Ocho Rios** as a key north-coast resort. **Hurricane Charlie (1951)** and **Hurricane Gilbert (1988)** required roof and window repairs at Christ Church. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found Christ Church a settled rural town parish.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. There are no separate reports of damage to Christ Church Moneague in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
Christ Church is a sturdy stone building constructed in the simple Gothic Revival style typical of rural Anglican churches. It features a rectangular nave, a small chancel, and tall pointed-arch windows with coloured glass. The steep roof, now clad in metal, was likely originally shingled, a design suited to the area's heavy rainfall. Inside, the church has an open-timber roof, wooden pews, and a raised pulpit. The churchyard, with graves of early settlers and soldiers, contributes to the site's historic character.

## Clergy
Christ Church has always been part of the St. Ann Deanery, sharing clergy with neighbouring parishes in Claremont, St. Ann's Bay, and Brown's Town. Early priests travelled along old military roads to lead services and provide spiritual and educational support. After Jamaica's Independence in 1962, it continued its mission through the Mothers' Union, youth programs, and Sunday school.

## Notable Facts
Moneague's location near the **Moneague Lake** — a seasonal water body that periodically appears and disappears — has long given the town a reputation for unusual hydrology. The **Moneague Training Camp** is a Jamaica Defence Force training facility with origins in the colonial-era military post, giving Christ Church a longstanding connection to soldiers who came to worship here.
