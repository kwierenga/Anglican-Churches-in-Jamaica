# Christ Church, Moneague
**St. Ann** · Church · active

Christ Church Anglican serves the historic inland town of Moneague in St. Ann, an area known for its seasonal lake and old military post. The church was established in the 1800s, as Jamaican society transitioned from slavery to freedom after Emancipation in 1838. It has endured hurricanes and community changes throughout its long history. Today, Christ Church remains both an active place of worship and a significant local landmark.

## History
Christ Church was founded in the 19th century to serve a diverse and growing Moneague community. The town was a well-known rest stop for travelers and soldiers moving between St. Ann's Bay and the interior. After Emancipation in 1838, freed families settled in the surrounding hills, establishing farms and new villages. The Anglican Diocese built this church to serve these new residents as well as personnel from the nearby Moneague Training Camp. By the late 1800s, it had become an established center for worship, baptism, education, and community life.

## Architecture
Christ Church is a sturdy stone building constructed in the simple Gothic Revival style typical of rural Anglican churches. It features a rectangular nave, a small chancel, and tall pointed-arch windows with colored glass. The steep roof, now clad in metal, was likely originally shingled, a design suited to the area's heavy rainfall. Inside, the church has an open-timber roof, wooden pews, and a raised pulpit. The churchyard, with graves of early settlers and soldiers, contributes to the site's historic character.

## Clergy
Christ Church has always been part of the St. Ann Deanery, sharing clergy with neighboring parishes in Claremont, St. Ann's Bay, and Brown's Town. Early priests traveled along old military roads to lead services and provide spiritual and educational support. The church played a key role in promoting literacy and religious education, particularly in the post-Emancipation era. After Jamaica's Independence in 1962, it continued its mission through the Mothers’ Union, youth programs, and Sunday school. Lay readers were crucial in maintaining regular services when ordained clergy were assigned to multiple congregations.

## Notable Facts
Christ Church has weathered many storms, including Hurricane Charlie (1951) and Hurricane Gilbert (1988), which required repairs to the roof and windows. Its location has also made it susceptible to flooding from the nearby Moneague Lake, though the building itself is situated on relatively high ground. There are no reports of damage from Hurricane Melissa (2025). Today, Christ Church Anglican remains a vibrant parish, hosting weekly services, weddings, funerals, and community events, serving as a living connection between Moneague's storied past and its present.
