# St. Boniface's, Whitehall
**St. Thomas** · Church · active

St. Boniface's Anglican Church serves Whitehall, a community in St. Thomas parish.

## History
Whitehall is one of the small farming districts of St. Thomas. The community's name preserves a colonial-era estate name — "Whitehall" being a recurring estate name in Jamaica drawn from the famous London street and royal palace area. The Anglican Diocese established St. Boniface's as a small mission chapel to serve this community as part of its broader rural mission network across St. Thomas, the parish whose history is bound up with the 1865 Morant Bay Rebellion led by Paul Bogle and the subsequent re-organisation of Jamaican governance.

The dedication to St. Boniface (born Wynfrith in 675 in southern England, baptised "Friend of Peace") is a distinctive Anglican choice. Boniface became the great English missionary to the Germans in the early eighth century, founder of monasteries across Germany and the Low Countries, and was martyred in Frisia in 754; the Diocese of Jamaica retains an article on its website explaining the saint's biography in connection with its three Boniface dedications: this Whitehall chapel, St. Boniface at Pondside in St. Elizabeth, and St. Boniface Church on St. Thomas Road in Kingston (St. Andrew). The choice of an English missionary saint dedicated to the conversion of the Germans is a quietly evocative fit for a small mission chapel in a Jamaican parish.

No substantive foundational date or detailed architectural history specifically for St. Boniface's Whitehall has been identified in the public heritage sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. Boniface's is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for mission stations across the St. Thomas interior.

## Clergy
St. Boniface's Whitehall is served within the Diocese's Eastern Jamaica Region.
