# St. Boniface's, Whitehall
**St. Thomas** · Church · active

St. Boniface's Anglican Church serves **Whitehall**, a community in St. Thomas parish. The community's name preserves a colonial-era estate name — "Whitehall" being a recurring estate name in Jamaica drawn from the famous London street and royal palace area.

## History
**English rule (1655) and the founding parish.** Under English rule from **1655**, St. Thomas was one of Jamaica's original parishes — originally **"St. Thomas-in-the-East"** with the first rector appointed in **1666**.

**Sugar economy and slavery.** St. Thomas was one of Jamaica's great sugar parishes. Large estates — Stokes Hall, Golden Grove, Albion, Holland — dominated the landscape, worked by enslaved Africans throughout the colonial period. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union** to "wipe non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb** (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**The Morant Bay Rebellion (11 October 1865).** On **11 October 1865**, **Paul Bogle** — a small farmer and Native Baptist deacon of **Stony Gut** — led a protest march to the Morant Bay courthouse. Governor **Edward John Eyre's** brutal response: **439 executed**, **600+ flogged**, **1,000+ homes burned**. Bogle was hanged on **24 October 1865**; **George William Gordon** was hanged on **23 October 1865**. **Both were declared National Heroes in 1965**.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall.** Jamaica became a **Crown Colony in 1866**. Law 20 of 1867 absorbed **St. David** into St. Thomas. The Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

**Foundation of St. Boniface's.** Whitehall is one of the small farming districts of St. Thomas. The Anglican Diocese established St. Boniface's as a small mission chapel to serve this community as part of its broader rural mission network across St. Thomas. The dedication to **St. Boniface** (born Wynfrith in 675 in southern England, baptised "Friend of Peace") is a distinctive Anglican choice. Boniface became the great English missionary to the Germans in the early eighth century, founder of monasteries across Germany and the Low Countries, and was martyred in Frisia in **754**; the Diocese of Jamaica retains an article on its website explaining the saint's biography in connection with its three Boniface dedications: this Whitehall chapel, **St. Boniface at Pondside in St. Elizabeth**, and **St. Boniface Church on St. Thomas Road in Kingston (St. Andrew)**. The choice of an English missionary saint dedicated to the conversion of the Germans is a quietly evocative fit for a small mission chapel in a Jamaican parish. No substantive foundational date or detailed architectural history specifically for St. Boniface's Whitehall has been identified in the public heritage sources reviewed.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** Internal self-government came in **1944**. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** fulfilled the aspirations for which Bogle and Gordon died.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. **Hurricane Ivan (2004)** had earlier caused widespread damage across St. Thomas.

## Architecture
St. Boniface's is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for mission stations across the St. Thomas interior.

## Clergy
St. Boniface's Whitehall is served within the Diocese's **Eastern Jamaica Region**.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to **St. Boniface** (feast day **5 June**) — born Wynfrith in southern England in 675 — connects this small Jamaican chapel to the great English missionary tradition. Boniface ("Friend of Peace") evangelised the Germanic peoples of central Europe and was martyred in Frisia in 754, becoming the patron of Germany. The Diocese of Jamaica has three Boniface dedications: this Whitehall chapel, Pondside (St. Elizabeth), and St. Thomas Road (Kingston/St. Andrew).
