# Epiphany, Bigwoods District
**St. Elizabeth** · Church · active

The Church of the Epiphany serves the Bigwoods District of St. Elizabeth, a community in the interior of the parish. The Epiphany dedication — commemorating the revelation of Christ to the Gentiles, celebrated on 6 January — is one of the more theologically rich church dedications in Jamaica's Anglican network, and one of very few Epiphany churches on the island. Bigwoods is a community in the interior of the parish, its name evoking the forested landscape of the interior hills.

## History
Bigwoods District sits in the interior of St. Elizabeth, in the hill country of the parish. The Anglican Diocese established the Church of the Epiphany to serve this community, choosing a liturgical dedication that marks the beginning of the season of light in the Christian year. The Epiphany (6 January) is the feast of the Magi's visit to the infant Christ — traditionally associated with the opening of the Gospel to all peoples and nations.

## Architecture
Epiphany is a masonry church of modest proportions, representative of Anglican rural church building in St. Elizabeth. The structure balances practicality with the dignity appropriate to a place of worship, featuring a straightforward nave with local stone or concrete block construction typical of Jamaica's interior church-building tradition. Set in the rural interior of St. Elizabeth. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
Bigwoods District has been served within the St. Elizabeth deanery under shared clergy arrangements.

## Notable Facts
The Epiphany (6 January) has a rich liturgical tradition in both Western and Eastern Christianity. In the Eastern church it is the principal feast of Christmas, celebrating the Baptism of Christ. In the Western tradition it marks the visit of the Magi — 'the manifestation of Christ to the Gentiles.' Epiphany also inaugurates the season of Epiphany, a period of 'ordinary time' when the church reflects on the nature of Christ's identity. The dedication of this church to the Epiphany is one of the most theologically specific in Jamaica's Anglican network.
