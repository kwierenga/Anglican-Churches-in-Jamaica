# St. Francis', Glendevon
**St. James** · Mission · active

St. Francis Mission is the Anglican church in **Glendevon**, a densely populated and often-challenged inner-city community above Montego Bay. Founded as an outreach mission of St. James Parish Church, it provides a critical anchor of stability, offering not only worship but also vital youth programmes, a basic school, and community support. The mission holds international renown as the spiritual home of **The Rt. Rev. Rose Hudson-Wilkin**, who grew up there before becoming a Chaplain to Queen Elizabeth II and later **Bishop of Dover** in the Church of England.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1664).** St. James was one of the second batch of Jamaican parishes formed after the English conquest, established around **1664** by Sir Thomas Modyford and named after **James, Duke of York** (later King James II). At its formation it was much larger than today, including the territory of present-day Trelawny and Hanover. For many years the north side of the island was sparsely settled: in **1673** only **146 persons** resided in the entire parish.

**Eighteenth-century development.** Montego Bay was made a free port in **1738**; Charles Square was laid out by James Lawrence (custos) in **1755**. Cudjoe, Maroon leader of western Jamaica, signed a peace treaty with the British in **1739**. The Second Maroon War of **1789** saw 300 Maroons fight 1,500 English soldiers. By the 1780s Montego Bay was, next to Kingston, "the most flourishing town in the island" (NLJ).

**Slavery and the Sam Sharpe Rebellion (December 1831).** Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds. The **Christmas Rebellion** of 1831, led by the Baptist deacon **Samuel Sharpe** at the Kensington Estate in St. James on the night of **27 December 1831**, was one of the largest slave rebellions in the British Caribbean — some 60,000 enslaved people involved. Sharpe was hanged in **Montego Bay marketplace** (now **Sam Sharpe Square**) on **23 May 1832**, and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation, 1865, disestablishment.** Within two years the British Parliament passed the Slavery Abolition Act (1833); Emancipation came in 1834, and full freedom followed on **1 August 1838**. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865, Crown Colony rule replaced the planter Assembly (1866); the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of St. Francis Mission: a twentieth-century urban mission.** St. Francis Mission was established by **St. James Parish Church** to serve the rapidly expanding hillside communities like Glendevon, which grew throughout the late twentieth century as people moved from rural areas into the city. Over time, Glendevon became known as one of St. James's most socially stressed areas, frequently cited in national reports on urban poverty and violence. Within this context, St. Francis was planted as a mission church; funeral notices from the 1980s confirm its early presence, documenting services at the church with burials at Pye River cemetery. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** had found Montego Bay's urban frontier still expanding, and St. Francis represents the diocese's response.

**Modern era and tourism economy.** Montego Bay received city status by act of Parliament on **9 October 1980** and officially became Jamaica's second city in 1982. The St. James Parish Church in central Montego Bay (the cure parent of St. Francis) had been wrecked by the **1957 earthquake** but restored. Major hurricanes have repeatedly struck the parish — Gilbert (1988), Ivan (2004), Melissa (October 2025).

## Architecture
No formal architectural survey of St. Francis exists in public heritage records. Community and media reports consistently place the church and its hall on **Sun Valley Road**, describing it as a venue for league presentations, trade training, and meetings — confirming its role as a multi-use community complex. Based on similar post-war urban missions in Jamaica, St. Francis is most likely a modest, functional structure built from concrete block, with a low-pitched roof and an attached hall for its basic school and community programmes, rather than a traditional, ornate church building.

## Education
**St. Francis Basic School** operates on the mission's premises, underlining its deep commitment to early childhood education and family support. A 2011 Gleaner report quoted the priest of St. Francis championing training programmes for young men and mothers, and supporting childcare to enable adult education — demonstrating a holistic approach to community transformation.

## Clergy
St. Francis is listed under the Montego Bay Region as a **mission of St. James Parish Church**, with the rector serving as the priest in charge (recently the Very Rev. **Justin Nembhard**). The mission has a strong tradition of lay and ordained leadership, most notably **Rose Hudson-Wilkin**, who often credits her upbringing and early ministry at St. Francis for nurturing her vocation.

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** **St. Francis of Assisi** (1181/82–1226) — the founder of the Franciscan Order, patron of animals, the poor, and ecology, with a feast day on **4 October** — is one of the most beloved saints of the Western Christian tradition. The dedication is theologically apt for an inner-city Anglican mission committed to ministry among the marginalised.

**The cradle of a bishop.** St. Francis is internationally recognised as the **childhood church of The Rt. Rev. Rose Hudson-Wilkin**. Her journey from Sunday school at the mission to serving as a Chaplain to Queen Elizabeth II — and later her election as the first Black female bishop in the Church of England (Bishop of Dover, 2019) — highlights the global impact of this local Glendevon congregation. Hudson-Wilkin participated in the 2023 coronation of King Charles III.

**Stability in a challenged environment.** While social surveys note Glendevon's struggles with violence and crime, they also acknowledge the presence of strong institutions like St. Francis. The church, with its hall and school, provides a crucial pillar of stability and hope for families amidst these significant pressures.

**Hurricane Melissa (28 October 2025)** made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Across the Diocese, some **50 church buildings were destroyed or significantly damaged**. The specific status of St. Francis Mission Glendevon after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.
