# St. Bartholomew's, Dalmally
**Hanover** · Church · active

St. Bartholomew's at Dalmally is one of the three principal churches of the Hanover Deanery within the Montego Bay Region of the Diocese of Jamaica and the Cayman Islands, alongside St. Mary's, Lucea (the parish church) and Holy Trinity, Green Island. Its documented history as an Anglican station reaches back at least to the 1891 Handbook of Jamaica, when it was recorded as a mission with 71 registered members.

## History
Hanover was established on 12 November 1723 from parts of Westmoreland and St. James, named for the ruling House of Hanover, and grew through the eighteenth century as one of the major sugar-producing parishes of the Jamaican north-west, its economy built on the labour of enslaved Africans. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds; disestablishment that year reconstituted it as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

The Dalmally name refers to the Scottish village of Dalmally in Argyll — one of many transplanted Scottish toponyms in Jamaica's interior, reflecting the significant Scots planter presence in the parish. The 1865 and 1870 Jamaica Almanacs do not record Dalmally as a separate station; by 1891, however, the Handbook of Jamaica lists Dalmally as a mission of the Hanover cure, with Rev. J. K. Collymore (rector at Green Island) exercising oversight — 71 registered members, 38 baptisms, and no marriages are recorded for that year. The congregation has since been promoted to principal-church status within the Hanover Deanery.

## Architecture
No substantive architectural description of St. Bartholomew's has been identified in the public heritage sources reviewed. It is a rural parish church serving the community of Dalmally and the surrounding districts.

## Clergy
Historically served within the Hanover cure — in 1891 under Rev. J. K. Collymore at Green Island — St. Bartholomew's now sits within the Hanover Deanery as one of the three principal churches.

## Notable Facts
The dedication is to Bartholomew the Apostle, one of the twelve, traditionally identified with Nathanael in the Gospel of John. His feast day is 24 August. Hurricane Melissa (28 October 2025) made landfall in Westmoreland and caused severe damage across Hanover; across the Diocese of Jamaica and the Cayman Islands, some 50 church buildings were destroyed or significantly damaged, and the 100-year-old Ramble Anglican Church nearby was reported demolished by the Jamaica Gleaner. The specific status of St. Bartholomew's after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources.
