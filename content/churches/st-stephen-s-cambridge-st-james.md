# St. Stephen's, Cambridge
**St. James** · Church · active

St. Stephen's Anglican Church serves Cambridge, the main township of southern St. James, located in the County of Cornwall and nestled in the mountains some fifteen miles south-east of Montego Bay.

## History
Cambridge developed as a market town in the southern hills of St. James, on the road that leads from the Montego Bay coastal plain into the parish's interior. The town's name was transferred from the English university city — one of several Jamaican places whose names commemorate English universities or counties (alongside Oxford, Manchester, Westminster, Sligo, and others). Residents of Cambridge are by tradition called "Cantabrigians," following the Latinised Cambridge form.

The Anglican Diocese established St. Stephen's as the parish church for Cambridge — Cambridge being a substantial enough township to support its own Anglican worship point in the wider Montego Bay Region network. The dedication to St. Stephen — the first Christian martyr, deacon of the early Jerusalem church, stoned to death for his preaching as recorded in Acts 7 — is among the most ancient Christian dedications. His feast day on 26 December (the day after Christmas) is observed across the Anglican world.

## Architecture
St. Stephen's is a small town masonry church set in the hills of southern St. James. The mountain position creates significant wind exposure during major storm passages — the western Jamaican parishes have repeatedly been struck by major hurricanes, including Gilbert (1988), Ivan (2004), and most recently Hurricane Melissa (October 2025), whose Category 5 landfall in Westmoreland brought damaging winds across the southern St. James interior.

## Clergy
St. Stephen's Cambridge is served within the Montego Bay Region's St. James Deanery.
