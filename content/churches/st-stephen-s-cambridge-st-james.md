# St. Stephen's, Cambridge
**St. James** · Church · active

St. Stephen's Anglican Church serves Cambridge, the main township of southern St. James, located in the County of Cornwall and nestled in the mountains some fifteen miles south-east of Montego Bay. Residents of Cambridge are by tradition called **"Cantabrigians,"** following the Latinised form of the English university city's name.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1664).** St. James was one of the second batch of Jamaican parishes formed after the English conquest, established around **1664** by Sir Thomas Modyford and named after **James, Duke of York** (later King James II). At its formation it was much larger than today, including the territory of present-day Trelawny and Hanover. For many years the north side of the island was sparsely settled: in **1673** only **146 persons** resided in the entire parish, and in 1711–12 the citizens of St. James were excused from taxation because of its few inhabitants.

**Eighteenth-century development and the Maroons.** Montego Bay was made a free port in **1738**; James Lawrence (custos) laid out Charles Square in 1755, named after Sir Charles Knowles (Governor 1752–56). Cudjoe, Maroon leader of western Jamaica, signed a peace treaty with the British in **1739**, and the Maroons were given almost 1,500 acres of land at **Trelawny Town**, named after Governor Edward Trelawny. The Second Maroon War of **1789** saw 300 Maroons fight 1,500 English soldiers. By the 1780s Montego Bay was, next to Kingston, "the most flourishing town in the island" (NLJ).

**Slavery and the Sam Sharpe Rebellion (December 1831).** Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds. The **Christmas Rebellion** of 1831, led by the Baptist deacon **Samuel Sharpe** at the Kensington Estate in St. James on the night of **27 December 1831**, was one of the largest slave rebellions in the British Caribbean, with some 60,000 enslaved people across St. James, Trelawny, Hanover, and Westmoreland involved. Sharpe was hanged in **Montego Bay marketplace** on **23 May 1832**. The Colonial Church Union, formed in the aftermath, undertook a campaign of chapel destruction across the western parishes (Ellis, *Diocese of Jamaica*, 1913). Sharpe is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation and the foundation of upland mission stations.** Within two years the British Parliament passed the Slavery Abolition Act (1833); Emancipation came in 1834, and full freedom followed at the end of the apprenticeship period on **1 August 1838**. Cambridge developed as a market town in the southern hills of St. James, on the road that leads from the Montego Bay coastal plain into the parish's interior. The town's name was transferred from the English university city — one of several Jamaican places whose names commemorate English universities or counties (alongside Oxford, Manchester, Westminster, Sligo, and others).

**1865 to 1900: post-disestablishment Diocese.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865, Crown Colony rule replaced the planter Assembly (1866); the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica. Bishop Enos Nuttall's tenure (1880–1916) saw the Diocese grow from 26 rural mission stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. The 1900 Handbook of Jamaica records the St. James cures as Montego Bay (J. W. Austin), Holy Trinity (G. B. Hall), Marley (H. F. R. Sharpe), and Montpelier & 5 Mission Stations (C. G. McGregory). St. Stephen's Cambridge most likely emerged in this period of expansion.

**Twentieth century: hurricanes, earthquake, Independence.** The St. James Parish Church in Montego Bay was wrecked by the **1957 earthquake** but restored "with few changes from the original" (NLJ). Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Stephen's a settled town church in southern St. James. Montego Bay received city status by act of Parliament on **9 October 1980** and officially became Jamaica's second city in 1982. Major hurricanes have repeatedly struck the parish — Hurricane Gilbert (1988), Hurricane Ivan (2004), and most recently Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).

## Architecture
St. Stephen's is a small town masonry church set in the hills of southern St. James. The mountain position creates significant wind exposure during major storm passages, while the elevation provides some protection from coastal storm surge.

## Clergy
St. Stephen's Cambridge is served within the Montego Bay Region's St. James Deanery.

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** Stephen the protomartyr was the first Christian martyr, deacon of the early Jerusalem church, stoned to death for his preaching as recorded in Acts 7. His feast day on **26 December** (the day after Christmas) makes "Boxing Day" a particularly significant date for Stephen-dedicated congregations.

**Cambridge's English connection.** The town's name and the Cantabrigian designation for its residents preserve a quietly persistent connection to the English university city — one of many Jamaican places whose toponymy still records the colonial naming practice of the eighteenth century.

**Hurricane Melissa (28 October 2025)** made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history — and brought damaging winds across the southern St. James interior. Across the Diocese of Jamaica and the Cayman Islands, some **50 church buildings were destroyed or significantly damaged**. The specific status of St. Stephen's Cambridge after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.
