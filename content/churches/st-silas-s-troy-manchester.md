# St. Silas', Troy
**Manchester** · Church · inactive

St. Silas' Anglican Church serves Troy in Manchester, part of the Albert Town cure. Troy is a community in the Cockpit Country-adjacent landscape of northern Manchester/St. Elizabeth border area. St. Silas' dedication is one of the rarer in Jamaica's Anglican network — Silas was Paul's companion on his second missionary journey, whose most famous moment was singing hymns in a Philippian jail at midnight.

## History
Troy is a community in the interior hills of northern Manchester near the St. Elizabeth border. It is in the same general area as Comfort Hall, which reviewers described as one of the 'coolest of cool places.' The Albert Town cure covers Troy alongside Craighead, Wait-a-Bit, Warsop, and the main Albert Town church.

## Architecture
St. Silas' is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural Manchester in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. Set in the rural interior of Manchester. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
The Albert Town cure covering Troy is served from the Albert Town rectory.

## Notable Facts
Troy is a classical naming choice — the ancient city of Troy from Homer's Iliad was a common source of Jamaican community names, reflecting the classical education of colonial proprietors. Jamaica has multiple Troys in different parishes. St. Silas, who accompanied Paul through Macedonia and wrote with Paul to the Thessalonians, is one of the least-celebrated figures in the New Testament, making St. Silas' Troy a distinctively unusual dedication.
