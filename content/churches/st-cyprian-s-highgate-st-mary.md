# St. Cyprian's, Highgate
**St. Mary** · Church · active

St. Cyprian's Anglican Church is the principal Anglican church of **Highgate**, one of the largest interior market towns of St. Mary parish. Set on the main road through Highgate, the church serves the town's market community and the surrounding farming districts. Highgate occupies a strategic position in interior St. Mary as a commercial hub for the hill communities of the central parish, and St. Cyprian's has been its established Anglican presence for well over a century. **Satchell's *Parish History of St. Mary*** identifies St. Cyprian's as one of the **four principal Anglican churches** of the parish, alongside the Port Maria parish church, St. James' Annotto Bay, and Holy Trinity Retreat.

## History
**Pre-English era and the Battle of Rio Nuevo (1658).** **Christopher Columbus** sighted Jamaica in 1494 on his second voyage. The **Battle of Rio Nuevo (June 1658)** saw British forces under **Sir Edward D'Oyley** decisively defeat a Spanish expedition attempting to recapture Jamaica — effectively ending Spanish resistance to British rule.

**English rule (1655) and the planter church.** Under English rule from **1655**, St. Mary became a major sugar-producing parish. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**Tacky's Revolt (1760).** On Easter Monday, **7 April 1760**, **Tacky's Revolt** broke out on **Frontier and Trinity estates** near Port Maria. Tacky — a **Fante royal** from the Gold Coast of West Africa — led a rising of self-emancipated **Coromantee** people intended to overthrow British colonial rule. Over **500 Black men and women** were killed in battle, executed, or took their own lives — the most significant slave rebellion in the British West Indies between the 1733 St. John insurrection and the 1791 Haitian Revolution.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union** to "wipe non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb** (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**Metcalfe parish (1841–1867) and disestablishment (1870).** A separate parish of **Metcalfe** was created in **1841**, named for **Sir Charles Metcalfe**, then Governor of Jamaica; **Annotto Bay** was its capital. **Law 20 of 1867** reduced parishes from 22 to 14, reuniting Metcalfe with St. Mary. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

**Foundation of St. Cyprian's.** Highgate developed as the main market town for the interior communities of central St. Mary, growing in commercial importance as **coffee** and **banana** cultivation spread across the parish hills in the 19th century. The Anglican Diocese established St. Cyprian's as the principal church for this interior hub, dedicated to the **3rd-century bishop of Carthage** who is revered for his writings on church unity and his courage as a martyr (feast day **16 September**).

**Twentieth century and Independence.** The **banana trade** transformed the St. Mary economy in the late 19th and 20th centuries. The Anglican Church established **St. Cyprian's Preparatory School** in the town. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Cyprian's a settled hub church serving Highgate's market families and surrounding farming community.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. **Hurricane Gilbert (1988)** had previously caused serious damage to interior St. Mary, with landslides affecting the roads into Highgate; Melissa brought heavy rain and wind impacts to interior St. Mary, with Highgate and St. Cyprian's congregation directly affected.

## Architecture
Reviewer descriptions confirm St. Cyprian's as a **stone-built church** in a "rustic setting," characterised as an "old style country church" with an authentic historical character and **"beautiful and inspiring" stained-glass windows** (per Satchell's *Parish History of St. Mary*). The building sits in the Highgate townscape, accessible from the main road, and is set within a churchyard. The church's moderate elevation in the interior hills gives it some natural protection from direct coastal storm surge while exposing it to the heavy orographic rainfall of interior St. Mary and wind during hurricane passages.

## Clergy
St. Cyprian's serves as the **hub church of the Highgate circuit** of the St. Mary deanery, with its clergy responsible for the network of smaller mission chapels in the surrounding interior communities. The church has maintained a more substantial congregation than the smaller mission chapels, given Highgate's market-town character and population.

## Notable Facts
St. Cyprian's is identified in Satchell's *Parish History of St. Mary* as one of the **four principal Anglican churches of the parish**, alongside the Port Maria parish church, St. James' Annotto Bay, and Holy Trinity Retreat — noted for its "beautiful and inspiring" stained-glass windows. **Highgate** gave its name to a widespread Jamaican soil series — the **Highgate Clay** — identified during the national soil survey. The Anglican Church also established **St. Cyprian's Preparatory School** in the town.
