# St. Benedict's Mission, Milk River
**Clarendon** · Mission · active

St. Benedict's Mission serves the Milk River community of southern Clarendon, near the famous Milk River mineral spa. Milk River is a coastal community on the south coast of Clarendon, and the mineral spa at Milk River — fed by one of the world's most radioactive mineral springs — is one of the most unusual natural features in Jamaica. The Anglican mission here serves the community around this distinctive location.

## History
Milk River developed as a community around one of the most remarkable natural features in the Caribbean: a mineral spring with extremely high levels of natural radioactivity and dissolved minerals, fed by underground volcanic activity. The Milk River Bath, established in the 18th century, became one of the most celebrated therapeutic destinations in Jamaica for those seeking relief from arthritis, gout, and other conditions. The Anglican Diocese established St. Benedict's Mission to serve the Milk River community alongside St. Saviour's, also recorded here.

## Architecture
St. Benedict's Mission is a masonry church of modest proportions, representative of Anglican rural church building in Clarendon. The structure balances practicality with the dignity appropriate to a place of worship, featuring a straightforward nave with local stone or concrete block construction typical of Jamaica's interior church-building tradition. Set in the rural interior of Clarendon. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
Milk River has been served within the Clarendon deanery. The mission designation suggests St. Benedict's has functioned as a smaller, non-resident ministry point in the community.

## Notable Facts
The Milk River mineral spa is one of the most remarkable natural phenomena in Jamaica. The spring water is reported to have a radioactivity level significantly higher than typical mineral waters elsewhere in the world, attributed to dissolved radium. The spa has been in operation since the 18th century and remains operational, with the hotel still providing baths. The community of Milk River has been shaped by this unique feature — an unusual context for an Anglican mission church.
