# St. Agatha's, Bromley
**St. Mary** · Church · active

St. Agatha’s Anglican Church serves the community of Bromley in the interior of St. Mary parish. The dedication to St. Agatha, the Sicilian martyr associated with courage and healing, is an uncommon choice for a Jamaican Anglican church and gives this mission chapel a distinctive identity within the St. Mary network. Bromley is a small interior farming community, and the church has provided Anglican ministry to local families for several generations.

## History
Bromley developed as a small settlement in the interior hill country of St. Mary, its English name reflecting the colonial naming conventions of the plantation era. The Anglican Diocese established St. Agatha’s as part of its mission outreach into the interior communities of the parish. The unusual dedication may reflect an early clergyman’s particular devotion or a specific founding circumstance now lost to the historical record.

## Architecture
St. Agatha’s is a small masonry chapel in the interior hills of St. Mary. The building is set in the Bromley community, in the hilly farming landscape of central St. Mary. The area’s elevated position and heavy annual rainfall create landslide and road-closure risk during hurricane passages.

## Clergy
Bromley has been served within the St. Mary deanery under shared clergy arrangements. The church’s small size means it depends heavily on dedicated lay leaders for its regular pastoral life.

## Notable Facts
St. Agatha’s unusual dedication is among the more distinctive in the Jamaican Anglican network, reflecting the variety of saints’ names drawn upon when the Diocese was establishing its mission chapels across the interior communities of the island in the 19th century. Hurricane Melissa (October 2025) brought heavy rain and wind to interior St. Mary; the Bromley community was in the affected zone.
