# St. Benedict's Church, Richmond
**St. Mary** · Church · active

St. Benedict’s Anglican Church serves the community of Richmond in St. Mary parish, a small farming district in the interior of the parish. The dedication to St. Benedict, founder of Western monasticism and patron of Europe, is unusual for a small Jamaican rural church and gives this chapel a distinctive identity within the St. Mary Anglican network. The church has served the Richmond community for several generations, providing worship and pastoral care to the farming families of this quiet interior district.

## History
Richmond is a small interior community of St. Mary whose name reflects the colonial naming conventions of the plantation era. The Anglican Diocese established St. Benedict’s as part of its mission network across the interior communities of the parish in the post-Emancipation period. The unusual dedication to St. Benedict may reflect a particular clerical preference or local tradition at the time of the church’s founding. The church has served the community across the long transformation of St. Mary’s interior from plantation agriculture to smallholder farming.

## Architecture
St. Benedict’s is a small masonry chapel typical of Anglican mission buildings in interior St. Mary, set in the hilly terrain of the Richmond district. The church’s interior position gives it some natural shelter from direct coastal storm surge while exposing it to the heavy rainfall and orographic effects that characterise the inner hill country of St. Mary.

## Clergy
Richmond has been served within the St. Mary deanery under shared clergy arrangements. As a small interior chapel, its pastoral life depends on lay leaders sustaining worship between clerical visits.

## Notable Facts
Note that a second church named Church of the Epiphany also lists Richmond, St. Mary as its location, suggesting the community has or had more than one Anglican worship point, or that one entry reflects a different part of what is a dispersed district. St. Benedict’s unusual dedication is one of the more distinctive in Jamaica’s Anglican network. Hurricane Melissa (October 2025) brought heavy rain and wind to interior St. Mary; the Richmond community and its congregation were in the affected zone.
