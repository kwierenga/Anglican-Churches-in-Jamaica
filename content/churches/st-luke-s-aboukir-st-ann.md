# St. Luke's, Aboukir
**St. Ann** · Church · active

St. Luke’s Anglican Church serves the small rural district of Aboukir in central St. Ann. It was established in the late 1800s for the hillside communities formed by freed families after Emancipation in 1838. Despite facing storms and other challenges over the years, the church has remained a vital place of worship. Today, it continues to serve Aboukir as a steadfast symbol of both faith and community history.

## History
St. Luke's Anglican Church was founded in the late 19th century, as freed people established new villages in the hills of the Aboukir district after Emancipation. The area was previously part of a landscape dominated by plantations near Alexandria and Coleyville, where the population was enslaved. As these new communities grew, the Anglican Diocese began sending clergy from Alexandria and Brown's Town to hold services. By the 1880s, a permanent stone chapel was completed and dedicated to St. Luke, the biblical evangelist known for compassion and healing. The church quickly became a central hub for baptisms, education, and community gatherings during Jamaica's transition to a free society.

## Architecture
St. Luke’s is a simple, sturdy church built from local limestone in the rural Gothic Revival style. It features a rectangular nave, a small chancel, and narrow pointed-arch windows with colored glass. The steep roof, now covered with metal, was likely originally made of wooden shingles. Inside, the church has an open-timber roof, wooden pews, and a modest pulpit. The surrounding churchyard, with graves of the community's founding families, contributes to the site's serene and historic atmosphere.

## Clergy
St. Luke’s has always been part of the St. Ann Deanery, sharing clergy with neighboring missions in Alexandria, Madras, and Brittonville. Early priests traveled difficult hillside roads to lead worship and support local education and family life. The church was instrumental in providing basic literacy and religious instruction, which were especially vital in the post-Emancipation era. After Jamaica’s Independence in 1962, the congregation remained active through the Mothers’ Union, youth programs, and community events. Lay readers were essential in maintaining regular services when ordained clergy were serving multiple congregations.

## Notable Facts
St. Luke’s has endured several major hurricanes, including Charlie (1951) and Gilbert (1988), which caused damage to the roof and windows. Each time, the community, with support from the Diocese, carried out the necessary repairs. The sturdy stone structure has also withstood occasional flooding from heavy hillside rains. There are no reports of damage from Hurricane Melissa (2025). Today, St. Luke’s Anglican Church remains an active parish, holding weekly services, weddings, and funerals, standing as a living reminder of Aboukir’s deep community heritage and enduring faith.
