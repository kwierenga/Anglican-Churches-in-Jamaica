# St. Luke's, Smithfield
**Manchester** · Church · inactive

St. Luke's Anglican Church serves Smithfield in Manchester, listed as a co-cure with St. David's Snowdon, St. Jude's Pratville, and St. Patrick's Providence under the **Snowdon (Newport) rectory**. Smithfield is a community in the western interior of Manchester. The cure has been listed as **VACANT** in recent diocesan records, with **Owen Lambert (SM)** and **Doreen Hall (SM)** serving as supplementary ministry assistants.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1814).** Manchester is one of Jamaica's youngest parishes, created by **Act 55 George III. c. 23** of **1814** in response to a petition of 29 November 1814 by the residents of Mile Gully, May Day, and Carpenter's Mountain — who had complained that "the closest public building was 40 miles away." The Act came into effect on **13 December 1814**, taking the "hill country" forming the eastern portion of St. Elizabeth and the western portions of Vere and Clarendon, and constituting it as a separate parish. The new parish was named after the then Governor of colonial Jamaica, **William Montagu, 5th Duke of Manchester** (Governor 1808–1827); the parish capital, **Mandeville**, was named after the Duke's son, **Viscount Mandeville**, on **27 August 1816**. Mandeville was built on 110 acres bought from Robert Crawford's Caledonia Estate at £10/acre.

**Slavery, the parish church, and the Sam Sharpe Rebellion (1831).** Manchester's hill-country economy was built on coffee from the eighteenth century onward (the cool, elevated terrain favoured coffee growing), supplemented by sugar in lower zones; both depended on enslaved African labour. The Mandeville **Court House** was built between 1817 and 1820 of limestone blocks "cut by slaves" (NLJ). **St. Mark's Anglican Church, Mandeville** — the parish church of Manchester — was completed and consecrated in **1820** and for many years was the only church in the parish. Its organ loft was used as a jail during the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, the largest slave uprising in the British Caribbean, in which a Moravian minister was held awaiting court martial. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**Emancipation, 1865, disestablishment.** The British Parliament passed the Slavery Abolition Act in 1833; Emancipation came in 1834, and full freedom followed at the end of the apprenticeship period on **1 August 1838**. Maidstone in Manchester (founded 1840 by the Nazareth Moravian Church on 341 acres of Thomas Frith's coffee estate) was among Jamaica's earliest free villages. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865, Crown Colony rule replaced the planter Assembly (1866); the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of St. Luke's: Nuttall's expansion.** Bishop Enos Nuttall (Bishop of Jamaica 1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. The 1900 Handbook of Jamaica records "Snowdon & Providence" as a cure under Rev. **F. L. King** — placing St. Luke's Smithfield within the orbit of that cure as one of its mission stations.

**Twentieth century: Kendal, bauxite, Independence.** Manchester's twentieth-century history is marked by two great forces. The **Kendal train tragedy** of **1 September 1957** — when a Sunday excursion train returning from Montego Bay derailed — claimed almost 200 lives and injured 600–700, Jamaica's worst rail accident; the majority of casualties were members of a St. Anne's Catholic Church (Kingston) day excursion. The **bauxite industry**, with **Alcan** establishing alumina operations in **1957** and Kaiser arriving by the 1960s, transformed the parish economy and led Mandeville to be described as "a town which grew on bauxite." Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Luke's a settled mission within the Snowdon cure.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of St. Luke's Smithfield after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. Luke's is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural Manchester in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard suited to a rural congregation of modest means.

## Clergy
The Snowdon/Pratville/Providence/Smithfield cure has Owen Lambert and Doreen Hall as supplementary ministry assistants; the cure has been listed as VACANT in recent diocesan records. The 1900 Handbook records Rev. **F. L. King** as rector of "Snowdon & Providence."

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** Smithfield is a common English place name derived from "smooth field" — Smithfield in London was the famous livestock market and execution site. **St. Luke's** feast day (**18 October**) falls in the quieter period after hurricane season; Luke — traditionally the physician among the evangelists — has a particular relevance to communities that have experienced storm damage and recovery.
