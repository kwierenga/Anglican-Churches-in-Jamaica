# St. Mark's, Ham Walk
**St. Catherine** · Church · active

St. Mark's Anglican Church serves Ham Walk, a community in the interior of St. Catherine parish. Ham Walk is a small farming community in the hill country, and the church has provided Anglican ministry to the local community for several generations. Ham Walk's name is one of several evocative Jamaican community names that preserve the memory of colonial estate names or local traditions.

## History
Ham Walk sits in the interior hill country of St. Catherine, in the agricultural landscape between the main north-south road and the western hills. The Anglican Diocese established St. Mark's as a mission chapel to serve this community, dedicated to the evangelist whose Gospel is the shortest and most action-focused of the four. The church has served the Ham Walk farming community through the post-Emancipation period.

## Architecture
St. Mark's is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural St. Catherine in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. Set in the rural interior of St. Catherine. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
Ham Walk has been served within the St. Catherine deanery under shared clergy arrangements.

## Notable Facts
The name Ham Walk is one of many unusual community names in Jamaica's interior that preserve memories of the colonial estate system. The name may derive from a 'walk' — the Jamaican term for a cultivation area on an estate, particularly a banana or coffee walk — combined with a proprietor's name. St. Catherine's interior is rich with such evocative community names reflecting the landscape of the plantation era.
