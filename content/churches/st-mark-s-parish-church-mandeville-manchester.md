# St. Mark's (Parish Church), Mandeville
**Manchester** · Parish Church · active

St. Mark's Anglican Parish Church is the principal Anglican church of **Mandeville**, the capital of Manchester parish and the main urban centre of Jamaica's central highlands. Consecrated in **1820** — just four years after the town's founding and fourteen years before Emancipation — it was built in the Gothic Revival style from local stone on the highest parish capital in Jamaica, at approximately 628 metres (2,061 feet) above sea level. Visitors describe it as a "Mandeville historical treasure" with "stunning architecture — stone walls, pointed arch doors and windows."

## History
**Founding of the parish (1814).** Manchester parish was created by **Act 55 George III, c. 23** — passed in 1814 to take effect in 1815 — in response to a petition of **29 November 1814** from residents of Mile Gully, May Day, and Carpenter's Mountain, who argued that the nearest public building was 40 miles away. Carved out of the older parishes of Clarendon, Vere, and St. Elizabeth, the new parish formally came into being on **13 December 1814** and was named after **William Montagu, 5th Duke of Manchester**, then Governor of colonial Jamaica (1808–1827). The new capital was named **Mandeville** after the Duke's son, **Viscount Mandeville**.

**Building the parish church (1816–1820).** The Manchester Vestry first met on **4 July 1816** and, at a meeting on **27 August 1816**, resolved to build a Court House, Parsonage, Workhouse, and Church, in that order, on 110 acres acquired from Robert Crawford's Caledonia Estate. St. Mark's was consecrated in **1820** as the parish church, built from local stone in the Gothic Revival style fashionable in the early nineteenth century. The first baptism was conducted by the Rev'd **John Trew** in September 1816 even before the church's completion (Jamaica Gleaner, 7 May 2016).

**Slavery and the early clergy.** Manchester's economy was built on slavery. The parish encompassed sugar estates in the lowlands along the Rio Minho, coffee plantations in the hills, and livestock pens across the plateau. The Anglican Church served as the established church of this system — its clergy paid by the colonial government, its vestry composed of the planter class, its parish administration extending to poor relief, roads, and the maintenance of social order. Trew's controversial successor, **George Wilson Bridges** (rector 1817–1823) baptised **9,547 enslaved people** and conducted 2,187 marriages, even as he rented the rectory out as a tavern and defended slavery (Gleaner, 7 May 2016).

**The Sam Sharpe Rebellion (1831).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion** — the largest slave revolt in British Caribbean history — the **organ loft of St. Mark's was pressed into service as a jail** for captured rebels (National Library of Jamaica). **Samuel Sharpe**, the Baptist deacon who led the uprising, was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. The rebellion's brutal suppression was decisive in forcing the British Parliament to abolish slavery the following year.

**Emancipation and the post-1838 expansion.** Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. The freed population of Manchester — who had laboured on the sugar and coffee estates — established free villages and provision grounds across the interior. **Maidstone** in Manchester (1840) was among Jamaica's earliest Moravian-led free villages. The cool highlands, unsuited to large-scale sugar but ideal for small farming, attracted substantial settlement. The Anglican Church expanded its mission to serve these communities, establishing chapels of ease and mission stations across the parish. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**, and Manchester eventually grew to have **32 Anglican churches** — one of the highest concentrations in Jamaica. The 1900 **Handbook of Jamaica** records the Mandeville cure with its full network of mission stations radiating from St. Mark's.

**A British highland town: Mandeville's twentieth century.** Mandeville's elevation and climate attracted a British and later North American residential community, giving it a more "English" character than most Jamaican towns. The mid-nineteenth century also saw a small **garrison** stationed at Mandeville: English troops who died in a yellow-fever outbreak are buried in the eastern portion of the St. Mark's churchyard. The **Kendal train tragedy** of **1 September 1957** at Kendal Manchester claimed almost 200 lives — Jamaica's worst rail accident. The **bauxite industry**, which began operations in Manchester in the early **1950s** with Alcan's Kirkvine works, brought economic transformation and population growth across the parish. The first Suffragan **Bishop of Mandeville**, **Benjamin Vaughan**, was consecrated from this rectory in **1961** (Gleaner, 7 May 2016). Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Mark's at the centre of a parish that had grown from plantation territory into one of Jamaica's most prosperous and distinctive communities.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane **Melissa** made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of St. Mark's Mandeville after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. Mark's is built from local stone with pointed-arch doors and windows in the Gothic Revival tradition. The Gleaner attributes the clerestory and tower to Bridges' tenure (1817–1823), though the JNHT records the chancel and timber clerestory as additions of the late nineteenth century, when the tower was also raised — the sources disagree on the dating. The churchyard contains graves spanning the colonial and post-colonial periods — including the eastern-portion garrison burials of yellow-fever-stricken English troops — making it one of the more historically layered burial grounds in Manchester. The interior includes memorial tablets accumulated over two centuries of parish life.

## Education
The Anglican mission to educate was central to Manchester's post-emancipation development. Church schools were established alongside chapels across the parish, providing literacy and education to communities of freed people. **Bishop Lipscomb's** initiative established over 50 national schools across Jamaica (by 1835 he reported 142 schools with 8,500 scholars). The **Mandeville Court House** (1817–c.1820), built from limestone blocks cut by enslaved labourers, held the first school in the parish on its ground floor.

## Clergy
- **September 1816** — Rev. **John Trew** (first baptism conducted before consecration).
- **1817–1823** — Rev. **George Wilson Bridges** (baptised 9,547 enslaved people; defended slavery; rented the rectory as a tavern; the Gleaner credits him with the clerestory and tower, which the JNHT instead dates to the late nineteenth century).
- **1961** — **Benjamin Vaughan** consecrated as first Suffragan Bishop of Mandeville from the St. Mark's rectory.
- St. Mark's has been the seat of the Manchester Deanery and one of the most important Anglican churches in central Jamaica. Its clergy have served as leaders of the Manchester circuit and as the parent cure for outlying mission stations such as St. Michael & All Angels, New Forest.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to **St. Mark** — the Evangelist, author of the shortest and most vivid of the four Gospels — is commemorated on **25 April**. Mandeville sits at **2,061 feet above sea level**, near the geographical centre of Jamaica. During the Christmas Rebellion of 1831–32, the organ loft of St. Mark's was pressed into service as a jail. The Mandeville Court House, contemporaneous with the church, held the first school in the parish on its ground floor.
