# St. Stephen's, Chantilly
**Manchester** · Church · inactive

St. Stephen's Anglican Church serves Chantilly in Manchester, listed as a co-cure with St. Augustine's Porus and St. James' Toll Gate under the **Porus rectory**. The cure has recently been served by **Charles Danvers (SM)**. Chantilly is a French place name — one of the more unusual community names in Manchester, most likely commemorating the French town of Chantilly (north of Paris) and possibly given by a French Huguenot landowner.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1814).** Manchester is one of Jamaica's youngest parishes, created by **Act 55 George III. c. 23** of **1814** in response to a petition of 29 November 1814 by the residents of Mile Gully, May Day, and Carpenter's Mountain. The Act came into effect on **13 December 1814**, taking the "hill country" forming the eastern portion of St. Elizabeth and the western portions of Vere and Clarendon. The new parish was named after **William Montagu, 5th Duke of Manchester** (Governor of Jamaica 1808–1827); the parish capital, **Mandeville**, was named after the Duke's son, **Viscount Mandeville**, on 27 August 1816.

**Slavery, the parish church, and the Sam Sharpe Rebellion (1831).** Manchester's economy was built on coffee and sugar, both worked by enslaved African labour. **St. Mark's Anglican Church, Mandeville** — the parish church of Manchester — was completed and consecrated in **1820**; its organ loft was used as a jail during the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**. Sharpe, the Baptist deacon who led the rebellion, was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church.

**Emancipation, 1865, disestablishment.** Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Maidstone** in Manchester (1840) was among Jamaica's earliest Moravian-led free villages. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of St. Stephen's: the 1900 Handbook record.** The **1900 Handbook of Jamaica** records the Porus cure under **Rev. W. Lund** as "Porus & Chantilly" — placing St. Stephen's at the heart of the cure rather than as an outlying mission. Bishop Enos Nuttall's tenure (1880–1916) saw the Diocese grow from 26 rural mission stations to **122 by 1911**.

**French Huguenot background.** The Chantilly name in Manchester most likely commemorates the French town of Chantilly, famous for its château, horse racing, and lace. **French Huguenot refugees** settled in Jamaica from the late seventeenth century after the revocation of the Edict of Nantes (1685), and their surnames and occasionally place names survive in the Jamaican landscape. Chantilly's French place name in a Manchester farming district is one of the more unusual onomastic traces of that history.

**Twentieth century: Kendal, bauxite, Independence.** The **Kendal train tragedy** of **1 September 1957** claimed almost 200 lives in central Manchester — Jamaica's worst rail accident. The **bauxite industry** transformed Manchester from 1957 onward. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Stephen's an established mission within the Porus cure.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of St. Stephen's Chantilly after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. Stephen's is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural Manchester in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries.

## Clergy
- **1900** — Rev. **W. Lund** (rector of "Porus & Chantilly").
- Today — the Porus cure has been served by **Charles Danvers (SM)**.

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** **St. Stephen the protomartyr** was the first Christian martyr, deacon of the early Jerusalem church, stoned to death for his preaching as recorded in **Acts 7**. His feast day on **26 December** (the day after Christmas) makes "Boxing Day" a particularly significant date for Stephen-dedicated congregations.

**Huguenot toponymy.** Chantilly's French name belongs to a small but distinct strand of Jamaican toponyms with French origins, dating from the late-seventeenth-century arrival of Huguenot refugees fleeing persecution in France after the revocation of the Edict of Nantes.
