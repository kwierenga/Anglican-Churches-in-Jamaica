# St. Stephen's, Chantilly
**Manchester** · Church · inactive

St. Stephen's Anglican Church serves Chantilly in Manchester, listed as a co-cure with St. Augustine's Porus and St. James' Toll Gate under the Porus rectory. Chantilly is a community in the interior of Manchester. St. Stephen's extends Anglican ministry into this part of the parish alongside the main Porus cure.

## History
Chantilly sits in the interior of Manchester near Porus. The cure is based at the Porus rectory (cell: 349-2271) and covers three churches — Porus, Chantilly, and Toll Gate — with Charles Danvers (SM) assisting.

## Architecture
St. Stephen's is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural Manchester in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. Set in the rural interior of Manchester. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
The Porus cure covering Chantilly, St. Augustine's Porus, and St. James' Toll Gate has been unnamed in the rector position in recent diocesan records, with Charles Danvers (SM) serving.

## Notable Facts
Chantilly is a French place name — one of the more unusual community names in Manchester. It most likely commemorates the French town of Chantilly, famous for its château and horse racing, possibly named by a French Huguenot landowner. French Huguenot refugees settled in Jamaica from the late 17th century after the revocation of the Edict of Nantes (1685) and their surnames and occasionally place names survive in the Jamaican landscape.
