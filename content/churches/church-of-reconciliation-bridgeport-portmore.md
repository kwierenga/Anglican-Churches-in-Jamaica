# Church of Reconciliation, Bridgeport
**St. Catherine** · Church · unknown

The Church of Reconciliation serves Bridgeport in the Portmore deanery, one of the principal Anglican churches of Jamaica's largest dormitory city. Portmore was developed from the 1970s as a planned residential area across the Kingston Harbour causeway from the capital, and grew rapidly to become one of Jamaica's most populous urban centres. The Anglican Diocese established Portmore as its own deanery — distinct from the St. Catherine deanery — in recognition of Portmore's enormous population and its distinct urban character.

## History
Portmore is a planned city on the Hellshire peninsula, developed from the 1970s when government housing schemes expanded Kingston's overflow population across the causeway. The Church of Reconciliation at Bridgeport serves one of the principal residential communities within Portmore, with missions at St. Paul's Greater Portmore extending the Anglican ministry across the deanery.

## Architecture
Church of Reconciliation is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural St. Catherine in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. Set in the coastal landscape of St. Catherine. Its coastal setting brings exposure to salt air and periodic storm surge, demanding robust masonry construction.

## Clergy
The Portmore deanery has multiple lay assistants supporting ministry across the large population: Colleen Tinker-Whyte, Reilette Allen, Sanchia Bent, Rohan Dawkins, and James Oliver are all recorded as Anglican Evangelists serving the Bridgeport cure.

## Notable Facts
Portmore is home to over 250,000 people and is sometimes described as a city without a centre — a sprawling residential landscape of housing schemes with few amenities. The Anglican presence in Portmore through the Bridgeport and Cumberland cures reflects the Diocese's effort to serve the rapidly urbanised population that moved from Kingston across the causeway from the 1970s onward.
