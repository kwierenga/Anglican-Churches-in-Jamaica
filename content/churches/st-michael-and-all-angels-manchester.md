# St. Michael & All Angels, New Forest
**Manchester** · Church · active

St. Michael and All Angels Anglican Church serves New Forest, a community in the southern part of Manchester parish near the Manchester–St. Elizabeth border. The church is administered as a mission of the St. Mark's, Mandeville cure (the Manchester Parish Church), with the cure rectory at Market Street, P.O. Box 217, Mandeville P.O., Manchester (telephone 962-2876).

## History
**Founding of the parish (1814).** New Forest sits in the broken upland country that runs along the southern flank of Manchester, in a landscape marked by red bauxite-bearing soils and small-farmer settlements. Manchester was created by **Act 55 George III. c. 23** of **1814** in response to a petition of 29 November 1814 by residents of Mile Gully, May Day, and Carpenter's Mountain. The Act came into effect on **13 December 1814**, amalgamating portions of St. Elizabeth, Clarendon, and the entire old parish of Vere. The new parish was named after **William Montagu, 5th Duke of Manchester** (Governor 1808–1827); **Mandeville**, the capital, was named after his son, **Viscount Mandeville**.

**Slavery, the parish church, and the Sam Sharpe Rebellion (1831).** Manchester's hill-country economy was built on coffee, supplemented by sugar in lower zones, both worked by enslaved African labour. **St. Mark's Anglican Church, Mandeville** — the parish church and parent cure of New Forest — was completed and consecrated in **1820** and was for many years the only Anglican church in the parish; its organ loft was used as a jail during the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**. Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation, 1865, disestablishment.** Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Maidstone** in Manchester (1840) was among Jamaica's earliest Moravian-led free villages. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica. Bishop Enos Nuttall's tenure (1880–1916) saw the Diocese grow from 26 rural mission stations to **122 by 1911**; mission stations like St. Michael and All Angels at New Forest extended the Mandeville parish church's reach into outlying districts as the diocese's nineteenth-century rural church-planting expansion gathered pace.

**Twentieth century: Kendal, bauxite, Independence.** The **Kendal train tragedy** of **1 September 1957** at Kendal Manchester claimed almost 200 lives — Jamaica's worst rail accident. The **bauxite industry** transformed Manchester from 1957 onward; New Forest's red bauxite-bearing soils placed it directly within the industrial landscape that came to define Manchester's twentieth-century economy. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Michael and All Angels a settled mission within the Mandeville cure.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of St. Michael and All Angels New Forest after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

The dedication to "St. Michael and All Angels" — celebrated on 29 September as the feast of Michaelmas — is one of the most distinctive Anglican dedications, naming not only the archangel Michael (the leader of the heavenly host) but the full company of angels. The choice carries a particularly cosmic and protective character that suits a hill-country community.

## Architecture
St. Michael and All Angels is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for mission stations across the Manchester interior. The hill-country position creates wind exposure during storm passages.

## Clergy
St. Michael and All Angels New Forest is served as a mission of the St. Mark's, Mandeville cure (the parish church) within the Mandeville Region's Manchester Deanery.
