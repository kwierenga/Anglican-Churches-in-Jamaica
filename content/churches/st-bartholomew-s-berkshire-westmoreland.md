# St. Bartholomew's, Berkshire
**Westmoreland** · Church · active

St. Bartholomew's Anglican Church is a small country church in the **Berkshire** district of Westmoreland near Bethel Town. The community emerged from the parish's sugar belt and the post-Emancipation establishment of free villages — Bethel Town itself evolved from a slave village into a free village in the nineteenth century. The church serves the Berkshire community through worship, funerals, and pastoral support, in an area frequently impacted by economic hardship and severe weather.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1703).** Westmoreland was officially formed in **1703** from the western portion of the older parish of St. Elizabeth, named for its position at the western part of the island. The parish capital, **Savanna-la-Mar**, was founded around 1730. In 1723 the new parish of Hanover was carved out of Westmoreland's northern section.

**Slavery and the sugar economy.** Under English rule, sugar and slavery accelerated in Westmoreland: an inventory in **1722** numbered the estates at **73**, making the parish one of the leading sugar producers of the British Caribbean. The Beckford family — Richard, Peter, and William — were among the most prominent planters; modern Petersfield is named for Peter Beckford. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**The Sam Sharpe Rebellion (December 1831).** The Christmas Rebellion of 1831, led by the Baptist deacon **Samuel Sharpe** at the Kensington Estate in St. James on **27 December 1831**, spread rapidly through the western parishes — St. James, Trelawny, Hanover, and Westmoreland — with some 60,000 enslaved people involved. Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation and post-1838 free villages.** The British Parliament passed the Slavery Abolition Act in 1833; Emancipation came in 1834, and full freedom followed at the end of the apprenticeship period on **1 August 1838**. Bethel Town, immediately adjacent to Berkshire, is a documented former slave village that became a **free village** after Emancipation. The Anglican Diocese established St. Bartholomew's as a small mission chapel to serve the freed Berkshire community within this wider free-village landscape. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865, Crown Colony rule replaced the planter Assembly (1866); the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica. Bishop Enos Nuttall's tenure (1880–1916) saw the Diocese grow from 26 rural mission stations to **122 by 1911**.

**Twentieth century, Independence, hurricanes.** Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Bartholomew's a settled rural community church. **Hurricane Melissa (28 October 2025)** made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history — bringing severe damage across the parish. The community's vulnerability was starkly exposed; while specific damage to the church building is not detailed in public reports, its location placed it and its congregation directly within a major disaster zone.

## Architecture
The church with an adjacent cemetery is confirmed by funeral notices. By comparison with other small Anglican churches in Westmoreland, St. Bartholomew's can be inferred as a simple, rectangular masonry chapel with a pitched roof, plain windows, and a surrounding burial ground. The church is most likely sited on stable, raised ground in the hilly Berkshire countryside, a common and prudent location in a region prone to heavy rainfall and landslides.

## Clergy
Within the diocesan structure, St. Bartholomew's belongs to the Montego Bay Region and is grouped with other Westmoreland congregations, such as St. Alban's in Ashton, under a shared clergy team. Its status as an active parish is confirmed by its inclusion in synod reports. Recent obituaries document thanksgiving services and burials at the church for residents of Berkshire and surrounding areas.

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** **St. Bartholomew the Apostle**, one of the twelve, traditionally identified with Nathanael in the Gospel of John. By tradition Bartholomew was martyred by being flayed alive, making him patron of tanners, leatherworkers, and butchers. His feast day is **24 August**.

**The Berkshire / Bethel Town free-village landscape.** Berkshire and the adjacent Bethel Town form one of the documented post-Emancipation free-village landscapes of Westmoreland — communities established by formerly enslaved people purchasing land after 1838 and forming their own self-supporting settlements. Modern media reports highlight the socioeconomic challenges facing the area, including poverty and disasters such as house fires.
