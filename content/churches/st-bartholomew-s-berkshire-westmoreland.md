# St. Bartholomew's, Berkshire
**Westmoreland** · Church · active

St. Bartholomew’s Anglican Church is a small country church in the Berkshire district of Westmoreland, serving the wider Montego Bay Region of the Anglican Diocese. Founded in a parish shaped by sugar plantations and the post-Emancipation establishment of free villages like nearby Bethel Town, the church provides a spiritual and communal anchor for a vulnerable rural community. With its own cemetery and a likely modest structure, it continues to serve families through worship, funerals, and pastoral support, a role underscored by its presence in an area frequently impacted by economic hardship and severe weather, including Hurricane Melissa in 2025.

## History
St. Bartholomew’s Anglican Church is situated in the Berkshire district near Bethel Town in western Westmoreland. The region was a centre of sugar production under British rule, relying on the labour of enslaved Africans until Emancipation in the 1830s. Subsequently, the area transformed as freed individuals established independent communities; Bethel Town itself evolved from a slave village into a free village in the 19th century. Within this historical context, the Anglican Church established rural missions like St. Bartholomew's. Now listed in diocesan records as part of the Montego Bay Region, the church has served generations of local residents through the transition from a plantation economy to a present-day community reliant on small-scale farming and seasonal work.

## Clergy
Within the diocesan structure, St. Bartholomew’s belongs to the Montego Bay Region and is grouped with other Westmoreland congregations, such as St. Alban’s in Ashton, under a shared clergy team. Its status as an active parish is confirmed by its inclusion in synod reports. Recent obituaries document thanksgiving services and burials at the church for residents of Berkshire and surrounding areas, affirming its ongoing role in marking life's milestones. The week-to-week ministry is sustained by a combination of visiting clergy and strong lay leadership from churchwardens, Mothers’ Union members, and other volunteers.

## Notable Facts
The Berkshire district is recognized in census data as a small, economically vulnerable community, contrasting sharply with Jamaica's coastal resort towns. St. Bartholomew’s exists within a landscape steeped in history, near Bethel Town—a documented former slave village that became a free village after Emancipation. Modern media reports highlight the socioeconomic challenges facing the area, including poverty and disasters like house fires. The community's vulnerability was starkly exposed when Hurricane Melissa made landfall in nearby New Hope, Westmoreland, in October 2025, causing severe damage across the region. While specific damage to the church building is not detailed in public reports, its location placed it and its congregation directly within a major disaster zone, underscoring its critical role as a pastoral and communal anchor in times of crisis.
