# St. Francis Church, Hiattsfield
**St. Ann** · Church · active

St. Francis Anglican Church is a small country church in the hilltop hamlet of Hiattsfield, St. Ann, located a short drive inland from Ocho Rios. The hamlet originated from a sugar estate owned by Hon. John Hiatt, a powerful planter, Custos, and slaveholder in the late 18th and early 19th centuries. Today, St. Francis serves as a local worship centre for the St Ann Deanery, ministering to farming families and residents in the hills between Ocho Rios, Breadnut Hill, and Gayle.

## History
Hiattsfield began as a sugar estate, one of many in St. Ann, such as Windsor and Minard, that were dependent on the labour of enslaved Africans. Its owner, John Hiatt, was a senior legal figure and Custos of the parish who held many enslaved people. Historical records from the post-Emancipation apprenticeship period mention women from Hiattsfield being imprisoned and list apprentice labourers living on the estate, illustrating its role as a site of continued control. Over time, the estate was broken up into smaller holdings, and the hamlet of Hiattsfield emerged. Within this changing community, St. Francis Anglican Church was established as a local worship centre, connected to the wider Anglican network based in Ocho Rios.

## Architecture
No detailed architectural survey or photographs of St. Francis Church are available in public collections. Based on the typical design of similar country churches in St. Ann built in the late 19th or early 20th century, it is likely a modest, rectangular chapel constructed from stone or concrete block. Its features probably include a zinc roof, plain windows, and a small porch, set in a churchyard that may contain a few graves. The church is situated approximately 320 metres above sea level, giving it a cool, hilly setting overlooking the coastal plain.

## Clergy
St. Francis is part of the St. Ann Deanery and is served by the same priest in charge of St. John's in Ocho Rios and St. Agnes in Salisbury. The clerical directory lists the Very Rev. Richard Tucker as the responsible minister, with Sunday services held at 8:00 a.m. As a small congregation, the week-to-week life of the church relies heavily on lay leaders—wardens, readers, and Mothers’ Union members—who maintain the building and organize worship. This provides Anglican families in the surrounding hamlets with a local place for prayer, baptism, and burial without needing to travel to the larger coastal towns.

## Notable Facts
The Hiattsfield Estate is a documented example of a classic St. Ann sugar plantation, directly linking the hamlet to the history of slavery through its owner, the prominent official and slaveholder John Hiatt. The estate also appears in records from the apprenticeship period, underscoring the difficult transition to full freedom after Emancipation. Today, Hiattsfield is known as a quiet, hillside community near Breadnut Hill and Orange Hill, with views toward the north coast. In this setting, St. Francis Church continues to serve as a spiritual and historical anchor for the local community.
