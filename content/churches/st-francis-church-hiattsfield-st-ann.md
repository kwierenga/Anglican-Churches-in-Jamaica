# St. Francis Church, Hiattsfield
**St. Ann** · Church · active

St. Francis Anglican Church is a small country church in the hilltop hamlet of **Hiattsfield**, St. Ann, located a short drive inland from Ocho Rios. The hamlet originated from a sugar estate owned by **Hon. John Hiatt**, a powerful planter, Custos, and slaveholder in the late 18th and early 19th centuries. Today, St. Francis serves as a local worship centre for the St. Ann Deanery.

## History
**Pre-English settlement: Sevilla la Nueva (1509–1534).** St. Ann has a deeper colonial past than almost any other Jamaican parish. **Sevilla la Nueva**, the first permanent European settlement in Jamaica and the colony's first Spanish capital, was founded in **1509** by Juan de Esquivel near present St. Ann's Bay and served as Spanish capital until **1534**. **Christopher Columbus** had been marooned at St. Ann's Bay for over a year (1503–1504).

**English rule (1655) and the Hiatt sugar estate.** Under English rule from **1655**, St. Ann became a major sugar and livestock region. **Hiattsfield** began as a sugar estate, one of many in St. Ann (alongside Windsor and Minard) that were dependent on the labour of enslaved Africans. Its owner, **John Hiatt**, was a senior legal figure and **Custos** of the parish who held many enslaved people. Until **1870**, the Church of England was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds; its vestry was composed of the planter class.

**Sam Sharpe Rebellion and the Colonial Church Union (1831–32).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, **Hamilton Brown** of Brown's Town led a Colonial Church Union mob that destroyed the Brown's Town Baptist chapel in 1832 — the **Colonial Church Union** sought to "wipe the non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth." **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation (1838), apprenticeship, and the breakup of Hiattsfield.** Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. Historical records from the post-Emancipation **apprenticeship period** mention women from Hiattsfield being imprisoned and list apprentice labourers living on the estate, illustrating its role as a site of continued control. Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb** (1824–1843) had begun extending Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835. Over time, the estate was broken up into smaller holdings, and the hamlet of Hiattsfield emerged. **Sturge Town** (1839, founded by the English Quaker abolitionist Joseph Sturge) and **Clarksonville** (1835, founded by Baptist missionary Rev. John Clark, named for the abolitionist Thomas Clarkson) were among Jamaica's earliest free villages — both in St. Ann (NLJ, *History of St. Ann*).

**Disestablishment (1870) and the founding of St. Francis.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. Within the changing community at Hiattsfield, St. Francis Anglican Church was established as a local worship centre, connected to the wider Anglican network based in Ocho Rios.

**Twentieth century, tourism, and Independence.** Tourism grew at nearby Ocho Rios from the mid-20th century. Bauxite mining began in St. Ann around **1952** (Reynolds, Kaiser). Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Francis a settled mission of the Ocho Rios cure.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of St. Francis Hiattsfield after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
No detailed architectural survey or photographs of St. Francis Church are available in public collections. Based on the typical design of similar country churches in St. Ann built in the late 19th or early 20th century, it is likely a modest, rectangular chapel constructed from stone or concrete block. Its features probably include a zinc roof, plain windows, and a small porch, set in a churchyard that may contain a few graves. The church is situated approximately **320 metres above sea level**, giving it a cool, hilly setting overlooking the coastal plain.

## Clergy
St. Francis is part of the **St. Ann Deanery** and is served by the same priest in charge of **St. John's in Ocho Rios** and **St. Agnes in Salisbury**. The clerical directory lists the **Very Rev. Richard Tucker** as the responsible minister, with Sunday services held at 8:00 a.m. As a small congregation, the week-to-week life of the church relies heavily on lay leaders — wardens, readers, and Mothers' Union members — who maintain the building and organise worship.

## Notable Facts
The Hiattsfield Estate is a documented example of a classic St. Ann sugar plantation, directly linking the hamlet to the history of slavery through its owner, the prominent official and slaveholder **John Hiatt**. The estate also appears in records from the apprenticeship period, underscoring the difficult transition to full freedom after Emancipation. The dedication to **St. Francis** — most likely St. Francis of Assisi (feast day **4 October**), the medieval friar associated with simplicity and care for creation — is unusual in the Anglican network of rural St. Ann.
