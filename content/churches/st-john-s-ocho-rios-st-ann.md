# St. John's, Ocho Rios
**St. Ann** · Church · active

St. John's Anglican Church serves the coastal town of **Ocho Rios** in St. Ann — Jamaica's premier north-coast resort and cruise port. The church dates back to the 19th century, with roots in Jamaica's colonial past, and remains active as a place of worship for residents and visitors in this tourist region.

## History
**Pre-English settlement: Sevilla la Nueva (1509–1534).** St. Ann has a deeper colonial past than almost any other Jamaican parish. **Sevilla la Nueva**, the first permanent European settlement in Jamaica and the colony's first Spanish capital, was founded in **1509** by Juan de Esquivel near present St. Ann's Bay and served as Spanish capital until **1534**. **Christopher Columbus** had been marooned at St. Ann's Bay for over a year (1503–1504). Ocho Rios — its name a likely English corruption of the Spanish *Las Chorreras*, "the waterfalls" — sits a short distance east along the same north coast.

**English rule (1655) and the planter church.** Under English rule from **1655**, St. Ann became a major **sugar and livestock** region. Ocho Rios and the surrounding area were influenced by the plantation economy during slavery. Until **1870**, the Church of England was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds; its vestry was composed of the planter class.

**Sam Sharpe Rebellion and the Colonial Church Union (1831–32).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, **Hamilton Brown** of Brown's Town led a Colonial Church Union mob that destroyed the Brown's Town Baptist chapel in 1832 — the **Colonial Church Union** sought to "wipe the non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth." **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation (1838) and free villages.** Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. Freed families moved into the area and established farms, trade, and small villages. **Sturge Town** (1839, founded by the English Quaker abolitionist Joseph Sturge) and **Clarksonville** (1835, founded by Baptist missionary Rev. John Clark, named for the abolitionist Thomas Clarkson) were among Jamaica's earliest free villages — both in St. Ann (NLJ, *History of St. Ann*). Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb** (1824–1843) had begun extending Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**Disestablishment (1870) and the founding of St. John's.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. The Anglican Diocese grew its presence at Ocho Rios, and St. John's Church was built for the community's spiritual and educational needs. It appears in old photographs showing graves and churchyard, and is listed in the National Library of Jamaica's digital collection.

**Twentieth century: tourism, bauxite, Independence.** Bauxite mining began in St. Ann around **1952** (Reynolds, Kaiser); tourism grew rapidly at Ocho Rios from the mid-20th century, with the town transformed by hotel construction and the development of cruise-ship facilities. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. John's at the heart of a town becoming Jamaica's principal north-coast resort.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of St. John's Ocho Rios after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
The church is built of stone, with strong walls and simple but dignified design. It has pointed-arch windows, a nave for the congregation, and a chancel toward the front. The roof is pitched to handle tropical weather, and the interior has wooden fittings and pews. The churchyard includes old graves which reflect many generations of local families.

## Clergy
St. John's belongs to the Anglican Diocese and is part of the **St. Ann Deanery** (north coast); the **Very Rev. Richard Tucker** today serves the cure that includes St. John's, **St. Francis Hiattsfield**, and **St. Agnes Salisbury**. It has served as a parish church for many years, supporting Sunday schools, youth gatherings, and community outreach. After Independence in 1962, the church continued its work with lay readers and the Mothers' Union to reach local people, tourists, and workers in the region.

## Notable Facts
**Ocho Rios** developed from a small fishing village into Jamaica's major north-coast tourism centre during the second half of the 20th century, with cruise ships, hotels, and attractions like Dunn's River Falls drawing millions of visitors. St. John's role at the heart of this transformed town gives it a distinctive position among St. Ann's Anglican churches — a community of long-standing residents serving alongside transient workers and tourists. The dedication to **St. John** — most commonly the Evangelist (feast 27 December) — is one of the most common Anglican dedications.
