# St. John's Church, Frankfield
**Clarendon** · Church · active

St. John's Anglican Church serves Frankfield, a community in the northern interior of Clarendon parish in the hill country approaching the central Jamaican ridge. Frankfield is the largest town in interior Clarendon, situated in the upper Rio Minho valley at significant elevation, with a character shaped by highland farming and the cool temperatures of the interior hills.

## History
Frankfield developed as the principal town of the interior highlands of Clarendon, serving the farming communities of the upper Rio Minho valley and the surrounding hills. The Anglican Diocese established St. John's to serve the Frankfield community. A second Anglican church, St. Bartholomew's, is also recorded at Frankfield, reflecting the town's size. The interior position and the cooler climate of Frankfield gave it a distinct character from the lowland communities of southern Clarendon.

## Architecture
St. John's Church is a masonry church of modest proportions, representative of Anglican rural church building in Clarendon. The structure balances practicality with the dignity appropriate to a place of worship, featuring a straightforward nave with local stone or concrete block construction typical of Jamaica's interior church-building tradition. Set in the rural interior of Clarendon. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
Frankfield has been served within the Clarendon deanery. As the main inland town, Frankfield's clergy have traditionally served a circuit of smaller hill communities.

## Notable Facts
Frankfield is notable as one of the communities most associated with Jamaican dancehall culture — the area has produced several significant dancehall artists and is referenced in various Jamaican musical traditions. The contrast between the Anglican church's long institutional history and the vibrant popular music culture of the surrounding area is characteristic of many Jamaican interior communities.
