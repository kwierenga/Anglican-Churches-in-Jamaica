# All Saints', Bellas Gate
**St. Catherine** · Church · active

All Saints' Anglican Church serves **Bellas Gate**, a rural community in the interior hill country of St. Catherine parish, west of Linstead. The church is one of a network of mission stations the Diocese of Jamaica established to serve the agricultural districts of the parish in the late nineteenth century. The Bellas Gate cemetery contains a small body of inscribed memorials catalogued in the Jamaican Ancestral Records project.

## History
**Spanish Villa de la Vega (1534) and English conquest (1655).** The Spanish founded **Villa de la Vega** in 1534 — Jamaica's capital from 1538 until **1872**. Penn and Venables's English expedition captured Jamaica in May **1655**. **Spanish Town** (St. Jago de la Vega) became the colonial capital and seat of the Anglican establishment.

**Founding parishes.** Under English rule from 1655, **St. Catherine** was one of Jamaica's original parishes. The neighbouring early parishes of **St. John, St. Dorothy, and St. Thomas-in-the-Vale** were merged into expanded St. Catherine under **Law 20 of 1867**, when Governor Sir John Peter Grant reduced Jamaica's twenty-two parishes to fourteen.

**Slavery and the planter church.** Sugar estates dominated the coastal plain — Bernard Lodge, Caymanas, Innswood, Worthy Park. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds. **The Diocese of Jamaica** was established by Letters Patent on **24 July 1824** by King George IV; **Christopher Lipscomb** was consecrated first Bishop on 25 July 1824 and enthroned at the Cathedral in Spanish Town on 15 February 1825.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838), Sligoville (1838).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Sligoville** in St. Catherine became Jamaica's **first free village** in 1838, founded with land provided by **Howe Peter Browne, 2nd Marquess of Sligo**, then Governor of Jamaica. Bishop Lipscomb (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

**Foundation of All Saints' Bellas Gate.** Bellas Gate is one of the interior settlements that developed as cultivation moved inland from St. Catherine's coastal plain. The Anglican Diocese established All Saints' as a mission church to serve this farming community within the broader Linstead deanery, following the pattern of Nuttall's late-nineteenth-century rural church-planting.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** Capital moved from Spanish Town to **Kingston in 1872**. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found All Saints' a settled rural mission.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Hurricane Melissa brought wind and rain impacts to the interior of St. Catherine.

## Architecture
All Saints' is a modest masonry chapel, consistent with the small-scale Anglican buildings erected to serve St. Catherine's interior districts in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries: a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard. The interior hill-country position offers some shelter from coastal storm surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
All Saints' has been served within the **St. Catherine portion of the Mandeville Region**, grouped with neighbouring missions under shared clergy arrangements typical of rural Anglican administration in Jamaica.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to **All Saints** — commemorating the full company of Christian martyrs and witnesses on **1 November** — is one of the more common Jamaican Anglican dedications, given to small mission chapels intended to serve a scattered rural community without claim to a single saintly patron.
