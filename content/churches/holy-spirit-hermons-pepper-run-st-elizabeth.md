# Holy Spirit, Pepper / Hermons
**St. Elizabeth** · Church · active

The Church of the Holy Spirit at **Pepper** (also locally known as Hermons / Pepper Run) is an active Anglican church in central St. Elizabeth, served as part of the **St. Matthew's, Santa Cruz Cure** within the St. Elizabeth Deanery of the Diocese of Jamaica and the Cayman Islands. It serves the small farming and pen-keeping communities of the Pepper district in the rolling country between Santa Cruz, Maggotty, and Mountainside.

## History
**English conquest (1655) and St. Elizabeth parish (1675).** Penn and Venables's English expedition captured Jamaica from Spain in May **1655**. The parish of **St. Elizabeth** was created in **1675** under Governor Sir Thomas Lynch, named after Lady Elizabeth Modyford, wife of Sir Thomas Modyford. The parish then covered all of western Jamaica and was the staging ground for the English colonisation of the leeward parishes; Westmoreland, Hanover, and parts of Manchester were later carved out of it.

**Sugar, cattle, and slavery.** Through the eighteenth century, the lowland savannas of southern St. Elizabeth — Pedro Plains, Lacovia, Black River — became cattle pens and provision-ground country, while the higher interior districts produced coffee, pimento, ginger and provisions. The Black River merchant houses traded mahogany and logwood internationally, and St. Elizabeth was second only to Westmoreland in the wealth of its planter class. The **interior pen-and-provision economy** of districts like Pepper, Hermons, Newell, Whitehall, and Mountainside was built almost entirely on the labour of enslaved Africans.

**Diocese, Sam Sharpe, Emancipation, disestablishment.** Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church. The **Diocese of Jamaica** was established by Letters Patent on **24 July 1824**; **Christopher Lipscomb** was consecrated first Bishop in 1824 and began the work of building rural mission stations across the island, including in St. Elizabeth. During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. **Emancipation came in 1834**, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. The collapse of sugar prices and the post-Emancipation flight of freed people from the estates transformed St. Elizabeth's interior into peasant country, and the small chapels of ease and mission stations established by Lipscomb's successors served the new free villages.

**Disestablishment, Bishop Nuttall and the rural mission expansion.** After the **Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865** in St. Thomas and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Disestablishment redirected clergy salary funds to elementary schools; by 1900 there were 263 Anglican schools across the island. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. It is in this expansion that the small interior chapels of St. Elizabeth — Holy Spirit at Pepper among them — took their present form, serving the descendants of the enslaved who had settled the district as small farmers after Emancipation.

**Twentieth century, Independence, and the Cure.** Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found the Holy Spirit settled into its role as a small rural church serving the agricultural communities of the Pepper / Hermons district. The church is now part of the **St. Matthew's Santa Cruz Cure**, paired with St. Matthew's at Santa Cruz and other small congregations in the Santa Cruz mountains.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Melissa cut a destructive path through St. Elizabeth, particularly along the central interior corridor where Holy Spirit, Pepper sits.

## Architecture
The Church of the Holy Spirit at Pepper is a modest masonry chapel of the kind that Bishop Nuttall and his successors raised across rural St. Elizabeth in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries — a single nave with a small chancel, designed to serve a few dozen worshippers from the surrounding farming district. Construction is local stone and lime mortar with a timber roof originally clad in shingles or galvanised sheet, set on a slight rise that drains the nearby pasture. The interior is simple, with plain pews and a wooden altar.

## Clergy
The Church of the Holy Spirit is served within the **St. Matthew's, Santa Cruz Cure** in the St. Elizabeth Deanery, sharing clergy with St. Matthew's, Santa Cruz. The current rector of the Cure (as of the diocese's 2025/26 directory) is **Hilda Vaughan**.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to the **Holy Spirit** — the third Person of the Trinity, whose descent at Pentecost is celebrated fifty days after Easter — is uncommon among rural Jamaican Anglican churches and may reflect a deliberate choice on the part of the founders to mark the chapel as a place of new beginnings, fitting for a congregation drawn from a community of newly-freed peasants. Pentecost is the principal feast.
