# St. Matthew's, Orange Grove
**St. Elizabeth** · Church · active

St. Matthew’s Anglican Church serves the small farming community of Orange Grove, located between Lacovia and Santa Cruz. It was established in the late 1800s for freed families who formed new settlements after Emancipation in 1838. Over the years, the church has weathered hurricanes and other challenges yet has remained an active place of worship. Today, St. Matthew’s continues to be a spiritual anchor for the people of the Orange Grove district.

## History
St. Matthew’s Anglican Church was founded in the late 19th century, as the Anglican Diocese expanded into the rural interior of St. Elizabeth in the post-Emancipation period. Before 1838, the Orange Grove area was largely dominated by sugar estates, and most of the population was enslaved. After freedom, many families moved inland to establish independent farms and communities. Anglican clergy from Lacovia and Middle Quarters began visiting these new settlements to hold services. By the 1880s–1890s, a permanent stone chapel was completed and dedicated to St. Matthew the Apostle, becoming a central hub for worship, baptisms, and community life in the growing free villages.

## Architecture
St. Matthew's is built from local limestone blocks in the simple Gothic Revival style common among rural Jamaican churches. It features a rectangular nave, a small chancel, and a modest bell-cot on the western gable. The steep roof, now covered with metal, is designed to shed the heavy seasonal rains. Inside, the church has an open-timber roof, wooden pews, and pointed windows with colored glass. The surrounding churchyard contains the graves of generations of local families, marking the long history of the community.

## Clergy
St. Matthew’s has always been part of the Black River Deanery, served by priests who also ministered in nearby missions like Gilnock, Burnt Savannah, and Slipe. Early clergy, such as Rev. H. C. Wharton and Canon W. R. Campbell, traveled a wide circuit to serve these rural congregations. The church once spearheaded a small school, providing essential education to local children when formal schooling was scarce. After Jamaica’s Independence in 1962, the church continued its work through youth groups and the Mothers’ Union. Lay readers were essential in leading services when a full-time ordained priest was not available.

## Notable Facts
St. Matthew’s has withstood several major hurricanes, including Charlie (1951) and Gilbert (1988), which both damaged the roof and windows. Each time, the congregation and diocese worked together to make repairs. Further restoration work was done in the early 2000s to maintain the building. There are no reports of damage to the church from Hurricane Melissa (2025). Today, St. Matthew’s remains active, holding weekly services, weddings, funerals, and harvest festivals, continuing its role as a stable symbol of faith and community in Orange Grove.
