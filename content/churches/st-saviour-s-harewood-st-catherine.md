# St. Saviour's, Harewood
**St. Catherine** · Church · active

St. Saviour's Anglican Church serves **Harewood**, a small farming community in the hill country interior of St. Catherine. **St. Saviour's holds a particular distinction**: it is recognised by the Diocese as the **first Anglican church consecrated in Jamaica** following the diocese's establishment in 1824. For the diocese's **200th anniversary in 2024**, the Postal Corporation of Jamaica issued a commemorative stamp featuring St. Saviour's Harewood.

## History
**Spanish Villa de la Vega (1534) and English conquest (1655).** The Spanish founded **Villa de la Vega** in 1534 — Jamaica's capital until **1872**. Penn and Venables's English expedition captured Jamaica in May **1655**.

**Founding parishes.** Under English rule from 1655, **St. Catherine** was one of Jamaica's original parishes. The neighbouring early parishes of **St. John, St. Dorothy, and St. Thomas-in-the-Vale** were merged into expanded St. Catherine under **Law 20 of 1867**.

**Slavery and the planter church.** Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**The Diocese of Jamaica founded (1824) and St. Saviour's consecrated.** **The Diocese of Jamaica** was established by Letters Patent on **24 July 1824** by King George IV; **Christopher Lipscomb** was consecrated first Bishop and arrived in Jamaica in February **1825**. **St. Saviour's, Harewood** is recognised by the Diocese as the **first Anglican church consecrated in Jamaica following the diocese's establishment** — the colonial-era parish churches that pre-dated the diocese had not been consecrated under a Jamaican bishop.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838), Sligoville.** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Sligoville** in St. Catherine became Jamaica's **first free village** in 1838, founded with land provided by **Howe Peter Browne, 2nd Marquess of Sligo**, then Governor of Jamaica. Bishop Lipscomb (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. The 1900 **Handbook of Jamaica** records **G. C. Linton** serving Harewood and St. Faith's during this period. Capital moved from Spanish Town to **Kingston in 1872**.

**Twentieth century, the 200th anniversary, and Independence.** Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Saviour's a settled rural mission. The diocese's 200th anniversary in **2024** brought renewed attention to Harewood as the site of the first post-1824 consecration; the Postal Corporation of Jamaica issued a commemorative stamp featuring the church.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Hurricane Melissa brought wind and rain to interior St. Catherine.

## Architecture
St. Saviour's is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural St. Catherine in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
- **1900** — **G. C. Linton** serving Harewood and St. Faith's (1900 Handbook of Jamaica).
- Today — Harewood has been served within the **St. Catherine deanery** under shared clergy arrangements.

## Notable Facts
**St. Saviour's, Harewood** is recognised by the Diocese as the **first Anglican church consecrated in Jamaica following the diocese's establishment in 1824**. The **"Saviour"** dedication is one of the most directly Christological church names in Anglican tradition, explicitly naming Jesus in his redemptive role rather than invoking a saint. The church features on a commemorative stamp issued by the Postal Corporation of Jamaica for the diocese's 200th anniversary in 2024.
