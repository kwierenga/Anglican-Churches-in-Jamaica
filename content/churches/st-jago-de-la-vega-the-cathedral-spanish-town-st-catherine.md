# St. Jago de la Vega (The Cathedral), Spanish Town
**St. Catherine** · Cathedral · active

The Cathedral of St. Jago de la Vega is the Anglican Cathedral of Jamaica, located in Spanish Town — Jamaica's former colonial capital — and the most historically significant Anglican church on the island. Confirmed at its location on White Church Street, Spanish Town, the cathedral is described by reviewers as the oldest church in Jamaica, with its first structure dating to 1538–39 built by the Spanish, and the current building dating from 1714. Reviewers note 'the floor is made of graves' and describe stepping through its doors as 'like stepping back in time' — a building that embodies the full span of Jamaican history from Spanish colony to British rule to independence.

## History
The site of St. Jago de la Vega has been a place of Christian worship since the Spanish founded the town of Villa de la Vega (later Santiago de la Vega, and finally Spanish Town) in the early 16th century. The first church on this site was built by the Spanish in 1538–39 as a Catholic church. When the British captured Jamaica in 1655, the church was converted to Anglican use and eventually rebuilt. The current structure dates from 1714 and represents the third church built on this historic ground. Spanish Town served as the capital of Jamaica from the Spanish period until 1872, when the capital was moved to Kingston. As the cathedral church of the Diocese of Jamaica and the Cayman Islands, St. Jago de la Vega is the mother church of Anglicanism in Jamaica. The building has witnessed the signing of treaties, the administrations of governors, the passing of the Abolition of the Slave Trade, Emancipation, and ultimately Jamaican independence.

## Architecture
The cathedral is a substantial stone structure on White Church Street, Spanish Town. The interior floor is composed of grave slabs — the burial ground of colonial-era governors, officers, merchants, and prominent families whose memorial stones form the actual floor of the building. This creates a literally layered experience of Jamaican history underfoot. The building's architecture reflects its 1714 construction and subsequent centuries of maintenance and restoration. A reviewer notes a visit by the Bishop of Dover, UK, reflecting the cathedral's continued connection to the wider Anglican Communion.

## Clergy
The Cathedral of St. Jago de la Vega is the seat of the Bishop of Jamaica and the Cayman Islands, the diocesan bishop of the Anglican Diocese. The cathedral has been the principal church of the Diocese since the colonial period, and its dean and chapter represent the highest ecclesiastical authority in Jamaica's Anglican structure. Successive bishops have administered the Diocese from this Spanish Town location even after Kingston became the capital in 1872.

## Notable Facts
St. Jago de la Vega holds the distinction of being the oldest continuously used Christian church site in Jamaica, with worship on this spot spanning nearly five centuries from 1538–39 to the present. The floor of burial slabs containing the remains of colonial governors, military officers, and prominent families makes it one of the most historically significant interior spaces in the Caribbean. The move of Jamaica's capital from Spanish Town to Kingston in 1872 left the cathedral in a former capital — an unusual arrangement that has given Spanish Town a cathedral character distinct from its current administrative status as a secondary city. The cathedral was specifically cited by reviewers as 'an invaluable piece of not just religious history but Jamaica's socio-economic and political history over the period from slavery to independence.'
