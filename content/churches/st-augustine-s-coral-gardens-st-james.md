# St. Augustine's, Coral Gardens
**St. James** · Church · active

St. Augustine's Anglican Church serves Coral Gardens, an established residential community in the Montego Bay area of St. James. The church stands at the corner of Sugar Mill Road and Melrose Terrace, Coral Gardens, Montego Bay.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1664).** St. James was one of the second batch of Jamaican parishes formed after the English conquest, established around **1664** by Sir Thomas Modyford and named after **James, Duke of York** (later King James II). At its formation it was much larger than today, including the territory of present-day Trelawny and Hanover. For many years the north side of the island was sparsely settled: in **1673** only **146 persons** resided in the entire parish.

**Eighteenth-century Montego Bay.** Montego Bay was made a free port in **1738**, and Charles Square was laid out by James Lawrence (custos) in **1755**, named after Sir Charles Knowles (Governor 1752–56). Cudjoe, Maroon leader of western Jamaica, signed a peace treaty with the British in **1739**, and the Maroons were given almost 1,500 acres of land at **Trelawny Town** in St. James. The Second Maroon War of **1789** saw 300 Maroons fight 1,500 English soldiers. By the 1780s Montego Bay was, next to Kingston, "the most flourishing town in the island" (NLJ).

**Slavery and the Sam Sharpe Rebellion (December 1831).** Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds. The **Christmas Rebellion** of 1831, led by the Baptist deacon **Samuel Sharpe** at the Kensington Estate in St. James on the night of **27 December 1831**, was one of the largest slave rebellions in the British Caribbean. Sharpe was hanged in **Montego Bay marketplace** on **23 May 1832**, on what is now **Sam Sharpe Square**. Sharpe is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation, 1865, disestablishment.** Within two years the British Parliament passed the Slavery Abolition Act (1833); Emancipation came in 1834, and full freedom followed on **1 August 1838**. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865, Crown Colony rule replaced the planter Assembly (1866); the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of St. Augustine's: a twentieth-century urban mission.** Coral Gardens is one of the residential and commercial districts that developed in Montego Bay during the parish's mid-twentieth-century tourism-and-suburban expansion, on the western flank of the city. The Anglican Diocese established St. Augustine's to serve this growing residential community, following the pattern of mission planting in new urban neighbourhoods that characterised the diocese's twentieth-century work in Montego Bay alongside Holy Trinity Westgate, Mary Magdalene Granville, St. Francis Glendevon, and Holy Cross Mount Salem.

**The 1963 Coral Gardens incident.** Coral Gardens is also the name of a notorious incident in modern Jamaican history: the **Coral Gardens incident** of **April 1963**, when state security forces conducted a violent crackdown on the Rastafarian community in the area. The incident was recognised in 2017 by the Jamaican government with a formal apology to the Rastafarian community. Ministry in Coral Gardens has continued through that history, alongside the parallel Anglican presence in the surrounding Montego Bay communities.

**Independence and the modern era.** Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found Coral Gardens a developing residential community. Montego Bay received city status by act of Parliament on **9 October 1980** and officially became Jamaica's second city in 1982. The St. James Parish Church in central Montego Bay had been wrecked by the **1957 earthquake** but restored "with few changes from the original" (NLJ). Major hurricanes have repeatedly struck the parish — Gilbert (1988), Ivan (2004), and Melissa (October 2025).

## Architecture
St. Augustine's is a functional Anglican church building in the Coral Gardens residential district. The Montego Bay area is exposed to the major hurricanes that periodically strike Jamaica's western coast.

## Clergy
St. Augustine's Coral Gardens is served within the Montego Bay Region's St. James Deanery; the church maintains a public Facebook page.

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** **St. Augustine of Hippo** — the great fourth- and fifth-century theologian whose *Confessions* and *City of God* shaped Western Christianity, with a feast day on **28 August** — is one of the more theologically substantial Anglican dedications, distinct from St. Augustine of Canterbury (commemorated 26 May).

**Hurricane Melissa (28 October 2025)** made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Across the Diocese, some **50 church buildings were destroyed or significantly damaged**. The specific status of St. Augustine's after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.
