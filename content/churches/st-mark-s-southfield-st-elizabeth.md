# St. Mark's, Southfield
**St. Elizabeth** · Church · active

St. Mark's Anglican Church stands at Southfield Square in the Mayfield district of Southfield, a farming town on the southern ridge of the Santa Cruz Mountains at nearly 1,900 feet elevation, overlooking the Caribbean Sea. Consecrated in 1903, the church serves a community shaped by the plantation economy that preceded it — Southfield Pen, where the Great House stones were "cut from the natural rock by the slaves that belonged to the Parchment and Bromfield families," and neighbouring Yardley Chase, whose enslaved lovers Mizzy and Tunkey gave their name to Lovers' Leap. The current rector is Rev. Olando Gayle.

## History
The south coast of St. Elizabeth was plantation country long before St. Mark's was built. Southfield Pen was founded around 1740 by the Parchment family and passed through the Bromfields and Chisholms before the Hodgsons took ownership after Emancipation. In 1796, Andrew Bromfield was recorded as proprietor with 80 enslaved Africans. The adjoining Yardley Chase was a coffee and cotton plantation of up to 1,500 acres with 80 enslaved people, owned by John Bent. It is remembered today for the legend of Lovers' Leap — the dramatic 1,600-foot cliff, now a Protected National Heritage Site, where two enslaved lovers, Mizzy and Tunkey, chose to jump rather than be separated when their plantations changed hands.

After Emancipation in 1834 and full freedom on 1 August 1838, the south coast saw the emergence of free settlements as formerly enslaved people purchased or were granted subdivided lands from declining estates. Southfield grew as a farming community rooted in Black land ownership and self-sufficiency — a pattern characteristic of the post-emancipation south coast, where the drier, more rugged terrain was less suited to large-scale sugar but ideal for the independent smallholding that freed families built. The Hodgsons, who took ownership of Southfield Pen after emancipation, were reportedly keen supporters of the anti-slavery movement.

The Anglican presence in the area predates the permanent church. By the 1900 Handbook of Jamaica, Rev. C. T. Rickard was serving "Plains, Mayfield, and 8 mission stations" — a substantial network of worship points across the south coast. St. Mark's was consecrated in 1903, replacing an earlier mission chapel and giving the community a permanent stone church. The church celebrated its 75th anniversary in 1978 and its centenary in 2003.

Jamaica's independence on 6 August 1962 brought new nationhood to a community that had already been building its own institutions for over a century. Southfield was upgraded from village to town status in 2018, reflecting its growth as a commercial hub for the surrounding farming districts — Mayfield, Yardley Chase, Seaview, Queensbury, Bellevue, Seniors Run, Congo Hole, Round Hill, and Powell Town.

## Architecture
St. Mark's is a well-maintained stone church on the main road at Southfield Square, described by visitors as "a beautiful church in a perfect location" with a "comfortable, welcoming atmosphere." The building dates from 1903 and reflects the solid masonry construction typical of early twentieth-century Jamaican Anglican churches. Given the local tradition of cutting stone from natural rock — as documented at the Southfield Great House — the church was likely built using similar local materials.

## Education
Mayfield All Age School, located in the Mayfield district of Southfield, accommodates approximately 324 children and is active in community engagement including Read Across Jamaica Day and distributing care packages to elderly residents. The 1900 Handbook's listing of "8 mission stations" under the Plains/Mayfield cure suggests that some of these stations may have had associated schools, following the Anglican pattern of combining worship with education in rural communities.

## Clergy
Rev. Olando Gayle currently serves the Southfield Cure, which includes St. Mark's Mayfield and St. Mary's Southfield. Rev. C. T. Rickard served at the time of the church's consecration in 1903. More recently, Rev. Basil McLeod (c. 2009–2012) was credited with bringing "positive change" to the Anglican churches in southern St. Elizabeth.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to St. Mark the Evangelist — traditionally the author of the shortest, most vivid, and likely earliest of the four Gospels — connects this south-coast church to one of the foundational figures of Christian scripture. Mark's Gospel was written primarily for Gentile converts and is characterised by direct, bold proclamation. His symbol is the winged lion, and his feast day is 25 April. Visitors from as far as New York have noted that worship at St. Mark's Southfield feels familiar to Anglicans worldwide — a testament to the international character of the Communion. The Sunday Eucharist is at 7:30 AM. Nearby Lovers' Leap, where enslaved lovers chose death over separation, stands as one of the most powerful memorials to the human cost of slavery in Jamaica.
