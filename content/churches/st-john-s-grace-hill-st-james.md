# St. John's, Grace Hill
**St. James** · Church · active

St. John’s Anglican Church is a small country church located at Grace Hill, off the Johns Hall main road in rural St. James. Founded as a 19th-century mission station serving the interior estates between Montpelier and Johns Hall, it continues today as a quiet Anglican centre for local farming families and commuters. With its own churchyard cemetery, it remains a vital site for worship and community rituals, particularly funerals, connecting the present community to a long history of Anglican mission work in the region.

## History
Within the current diocesan structure, St. John’s – Grace Hill is part of the Montego Bay Region and is grouped with other small congregations, indicating it shares clergy resources. Its status as an active, organized parish is confirmed by its inclusion in Jamaica Church Missionary Society financial reports. The church’s most visible role today is as a venue for funerals; recent death announcements specifically cite St. John’s for thanksgiving services followed by burial in its cemetery. This underscores its importance as a key gathering place for local families during significant life events, a function supported by a combination of visiting clergy and dedicated lay leadership.

## Architecture
A 1931 photograph from the National Library of Jamaica’s collection depicts St. John’s as a small, functional rural church built from stone or masonry, situated on gently rising ground. While no full architectural survey exists, the image confirms a modest, rectangular chapel with a simple profile, lacking a large tower. Based on comparison with similar country churches in St. James from that period, its likely features include a pitched zinc or shingle roof, plain or louvered windows, and a modest porch or bell-cote, set within a fenced yard that contains the church cemetery—a classic layout for Jamaican Anglican missions.

## Clergy
St. John's is part of the Montego Bay Region of the Diocese of Jamaica and shares clergy resources with other small congregations in the area. ## Notable Facts
St. John's serves a district with deep connections to the interior plantation economy of St. James. The Johns Hall and Montpelier areas were the site of significant sugar and livestock estates in the 18th and 19th centuries, and the congregation descends largely from those who remained in the area as small farmers after Emancipation. A 1931 National Library of Jamaica photograph confirms the church's modest rural character in the early 20th century. The church continues to serve as a community gathering point, with funeral and burial notices regularly citing St. John's as the venue for thanksgiving services.
