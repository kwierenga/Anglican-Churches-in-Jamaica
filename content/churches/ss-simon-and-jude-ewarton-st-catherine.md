# SS Simon and Jude, Ewarton
**St. Catherine** · Church · active

SS Simon and Jude Anglican Church serves **Ewarton**, a community in northern St. Catherine parish near the Bog Walk area and the **Jamalco alumina plant**. The joint dedication to the apostles **Simon the Zealot** and **Jude Thaddaeus** is one of the most distinctive in Jamaica's Anglican network (joint feast day **28 October**).

## History
**Spanish Villa de la Vega (1534) and English conquest (1655).** The Spanish founded **Villa de la Vega** in 1534 — Jamaica's capital until **1872**. Penn and Venables's English expedition captured Jamaica in May **1655**.

**Founding parishes.** Under English rule from 1655, **St. Catherine** was one of Jamaica's original parishes. The neighbouring early parishes of **St. John, St. Dorothy, and St. Thomas-in-the-Vale** were merged into expanded St. Catherine under **Law 20 of 1867**. Ewarton sat in the old **St. Thomas-in-the-Vale** parish before the 1867 merger.

**Slavery and the planter church.** Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds. **The Diocese of Jamaica** was established by Letters Patent on **24 July 1824**; **Christopher Lipscomb** was consecrated first Bishop in 1824.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838), Sligoville.** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Sligoville** in St. Catherine became Jamaica's **first free village** in 1838, founded with land provided by **Howe Peter Browne, 2nd Marquess of Sligo**, then Governor of Jamaica. Bishop Lipscomb (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. Capital moved from Spanish Town to **Kingston in 1872**.

**Foundation of SS Simon and Jude.** Ewarton is a community in northern St. Catherine known particularly for the **Jamalco alumina plant** in the vicinity, which processes bauxite mined from the red soil of the interior. The Anglican Diocese established SS Simon and Jude to serve Ewarton's community, choosing the joint feast day of two of the less-documented apostles. The church has served the community through the industrial transformation of the Bog Walk/Ewarton area in the 20th century.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** Bauxite mining transformed the Jamaican economy from the 1950s onward, and Ewarton became one of the alumina-processing towns. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found SS Simon and Jude a settled town parish.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Hurricane Melissa brought wind and rain to interior St. Catherine.

## Architecture
SS Simon and Jude is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural St. Catherine in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means.

## Clergy
Ewarton has been served within the **St. Catherine deanery**. The church's small membership and calm atmosphere reflect a tightly knit rural congregation that has maintained its character over several decades. A reviewer's testimony recalls the church helping shape his values during his teenage years in the early 1980s — including values of "honesty, integrity, and ethical behavior."

## Notable Facts
The joint dedication to **Simon and Jude** is one of the more unusual apostolic pairings in Jamaica's Anglican network. Both apostles are relatively obscure in the New Testament — **Simon the Zealot** is distinguished from Simon Peter only by his political designation, and **Jude Thaddaeus** is the subject of very few Biblical references. Their joint feast day (**28 October**) is one of the lesser-observed apostolic commemorations.
