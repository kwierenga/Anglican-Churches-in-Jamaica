# SS Simon and Jude, Ewarton
**St. Catherine** · Church · active

SS Simon and Jude Anglican Church serves Ewarton, a community in northern St. Catherine parish near the Bog Walk area and the alumina industry. Confirmed at its Ewarton location by Google Places, the church is described by a reviewer as a place whose congregation helped shape his values during his teenage years in the early 1980s — including values of 'honesty, integrity, and ethical behavior' that served him in a career in the Constabulary Force. The joint dedication to the apostles Simon the Zealot and Jude Thaddaeus is one of the most distinctive in Jamaica's Anglican network.

## History
Ewarton is a community in northern St. Catherine known particularly for the Jamalco alumina plant in the vicinity, which processes bauxite mined from the red soil of the interior. The Anglican Diocese established SS Simon and Jude to serve Ewarton's community, choosing the joint feast day of two of the less-documented apostles — commemorated together on 28 October in the Anglican calendar. The church has served the community through the industrial transformation of the Bog Walk/Ewarton area in the 20th century.

## Architecture
SS Simon and Jude is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural St. Catherine in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. Set in the rural interior of St. Catherine. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
Ewarton has been served within the St. Catherine deanery. The church's small membership and calm atmosphere reflect a tightly knit rural congregation that has maintained its character over several decades.

## Notable Facts
The joint dedication to Simon and Jude is one of the more unusual apostolic pairings in Jamaica's Anglican network. Both apostles are relatively obscure in the New Testament — Simon the Zealot is distinguished from Simon Peter only by his political designation, and Jude Thaddaeus is the subject of very few Biblical references. Their joint feast day (28 October) is one of the lesser-observed apostolic commemorations. The reviewer's personal testimony about the church's formative role in his moral development — 'I owe my grandaunt, Mrs Hopian Cato who is resting peacefully behind the church for all these values' — is a vivid human document of the church's community impact.
