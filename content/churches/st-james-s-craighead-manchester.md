# St. James', Craighead
**Manchester** · Church · inactive

St. James' Anglican Church serves Craighead in northern Manchester, part of the Albert Town cure circuit. Craighead is a community in the hills near Albert Town, in the farming landscape on the edge of the Cockpit Country. The church extends the Albert Town cure's reach into the surrounding rural communities.

## History
Craighead is a Scottish place name — 'craig' (crag/rock) being common in Scottish topography — reflecting the Scottish landowners who named properties in this part of Jamaica. The community is part of the Albert Town cure centred in northern Manchester.

## Architecture
St. James' is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural Manchester in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. Set in the rural interior of Manchester. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
The Albert Town cure covering Craighead is served from the Albert Town rectory.

## Notable Facts
The 'craig' prefix in Craighead connects it to the many Scottish-named communities in Jamaica's interior — reflecting the significant Scottish presence in colonial plantation ownership, particularly in Manchester and Trelawny. St. James is the patron saint of pilgrims and is associated with the great pilgrimage route to Santiago de Compostela, a connection that has unexpected resonance in the remote hill communities of northern Jamaica.
