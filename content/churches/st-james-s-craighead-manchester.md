# St. James', Craighead
**Manchester** · Church · inactive

St. James' Anglican Church serves Craighead in northern Manchester, part of the **Albert Town cure circuit**. Craighead is a community in the hills near Albert Town, in the farming landscape on the edge of the Cockpit Country. (The Albert Town cure spans the Manchester–Trelawny border, and the **1900 Handbook of Jamaica** records the cure as "Albert Town & 4 Mission Stations" under **Rev. J. R. M. Cass**.)

## History
**Founding of the parish (1814).** Manchester was created by **Act 55 George III. c. 23** of **1814** in response to a petition of 29 November 1814 by residents of Mile Gully, May Day, and Carpenter's Mountain. The Act came into effect on **13 December 1814**, taking the "hill country" of eastern St. Elizabeth and the western portions of Vere and Clarendon. The new parish was named after **William Montagu, 5th Duke of Manchester** (Governor 1808–1827); **Mandeville**, the capital, was named after his son, **Viscount Mandeville**.

**Slavery, the parish church, and the Sam Sharpe Rebellion (1831).** Manchester's hill-country economy was built on coffee, supplemented by sugar in lower zones; both depended on enslaved African labour. **St. Mark's Anglican Church, Mandeville** — the parish church — was completed and consecrated in **1820**, and its organ loft was used as a jail during the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**. Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation, 1865, disestablishment.** Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Maidstone** in Manchester (1840) was among Jamaica's earliest Moravian-led free villages. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of St. James': the Albert Town cure.** Craighead is a **Scottish place name** — *craig* (crag/rock) being common in Scottish topography — reflecting the Scottish landowners who named properties in this part of Jamaica. The community is part of the Albert Town cure that links northern Manchester to the Cockpit Country edge in southern Trelawny. **Bishop Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. By the 1900 Handbook, the cure was formally constituted as "Albert Town & 4 Mission Stations" under Rev. **J. R. M. Cass**.

**Twentieth century: Kendal, bauxite, Independence.** The **Kendal train tragedy** of **1 September 1957** at Kendal Manchester claimed almost 200 lives — Jamaica's worst rail accident. The **bauxite industry** transformed Manchester from 1957 onward. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. James' a settled rural mission within the Albert Town cure.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of St. James' Craighead after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. James' is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural Manchester in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries. The Diocese maintains a directory image of the church (attachment_id 900).

## Clergy
- **1900** — Rev. **J. R. M. Cass** (rector of "Albert Town & 4 Mission Stations").
- Today — served from the Albert Town rectory.

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** **St. James the Greater**, brother of John the Evangelist and patron saint of pilgrims (associated with the great pilgrimage route to Santiago de Compostela), with a feast day on **25 July**.

**Scottish heritage.** The "craig" prefix in Craighead connects it to the many Scottish-named communities in Jamaica's interior — reflecting the significant Scottish presence in colonial plantation ownership, particularly in Manchester and Trelawny.
