# St. Lawrence's, Devon
**Manchester** · Church · active

St. Lawrence's Anglican Church serves Devon, a community in the interior of Manchester parish. Devon is a farming community in the hill country of Manchester, its name — like Kendal — drawn from an English county. St. Lawrence's has served the Devon community for several generations. The dedication to St. Lawrence, the 3rd-century deacon-martyr famous for his courage at execution, is one of the more striking in Jamaica's Anglican network.

## History
Devon sits in the interior of Manchester, in the farming landscape of the parish. The English county name follows the pattern of colonial estate naming. The Anglican Diocese established St. Lawrence's to serve this community.

## Architecture
St. Lawrence's is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural Manchester in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. Set in the rural interior of Manchester. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
St. Lawrence's Devon is administered as a mission of the St. Barnabas' Mile Gully cure, with the cure rectory at Mile Gully P.O., Manchester (telephone 965-4527). The cure has been listed as **VACANT** in recent diocesan directories.

## Notable Facts
St. Lawrence was a 3rd-century Roman deacon martyred in 258 AD by being roasted alive on a gridiron. The tradition holds that he said to his executioners: 'Turn me over, I'm done on this side' — a display of courage and dark humour that has made him a beloved figure. He is patron of cooks and comedians. His feast day (10 August) falls in the height of hurricane season in Jamaica.
