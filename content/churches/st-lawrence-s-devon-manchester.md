# St. Lawrence's, Devon
**Manchester** · Church · active

St. Lawrence's Anglican Church serves Devon, a community in the interior of Manchester. Devon is a farming community in the hill country of Manchester, its name — like Kendal, Battersea, and other Manchester districts — drawn from an English county. St. Lawrence's is administered as a mission of the **St. Barnabas' Mile Gully cure**, with the cure rectory at Mile Gully P.O., Manchester (telephone 965-4527). The cure has been listed as **VACANT** in recent diocesan directories.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1814).** Manchester was created by **Act 55 George III. c. 23** of **1814** in response to a petition of 29 November 1814 by residents of Mile Gully, May Day, and Carpenter's Mountain. The Act came into effect on **13 December 1814**, taking the "hill country" of eastern St. Elizabeth and the western portions of Vere and Clarendon. The new parish was named after **William Montagu, 5th Duke of Manchester** (Governor 1808–1827); **Mandeville**, the capital, was named after his son, **Viscount Mandeville**.

**Slavery, the parish church, and the Sam Sharpe Rebellion (1831).** Manchester's hill-country economy was built on coffee, supplemented by sugar in lower zones; both depended on enslaved African labour. **St. Mark's Anglican Church, Mandeville** — the parish church of Manchester — was completed and consecrated in **1820**, and its organ loft was used as a jail during the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**. Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation, 1865, disestablishment.** Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Maidstone** in Manchester (1840) was among Jamaica's earliest Moravian-led free villages. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of St. Lawrence's: the Mile Gully cure.** Bishop Enos Nuttall's tenure (1880–1916) saw the Diocese grow from 26 rural mission stations to **122 by 1911**. The 1900 Handbook of Jamaica records "Mile Gully" as a cure under **Rev. E. Clarke**, with St. Lawrence's Devon as one of its mission stations. The Mile Gully cure also encompasses St. Peter's Whitby, the Harry Watch chapel of ease, and the famous abandoned **St. George's Anglican Church** ("Duppy Church"), a national monument.

**Twentieth century: Kendal, bauxite, Independence.** The **Kendal train tragedy** of **1 September 1957** at Kendal Manchester claimed almost 200 lives — Jamaica's worst rail accident. The **bauxite industry** (Alcan from 1957, Kaiser later) employed over 3,000 by the 1960s. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Lawrence's a settled mission within the Mile Gully cure.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of St. Lawrence's Devon after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. Lawrence's is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural Manchester in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries. The Jamaican Ancestral Records project documents an associated cemetery as the "Devon Missionary Church" cemetery.

## Clergy
- **1900** — Rev. **E. Clarke** (rector at Mile Gully).
- Today — administered as a mission of the St. Barnabas' Mile Gully cure (currently vacant).

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** **St. Lawrence** was a third-century Roman deacon martyred in 258 AD by being roasted alive on a gridiron. Tradition holds that he said to his executioners, "Turn me over, I'm done on this side" — a display of courage and dark humour that has made him a beloved figure. He is patron of cooks and comedians. His feast day (**10 August**) falls in the height of hurricane season in Jamaica.
