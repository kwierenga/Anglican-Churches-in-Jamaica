# St. Boniface, Stony Gut / vicinity
**St. Thomas** · Mission · unknown

St. Boniface Anglican Church is associated with the Stony Gut area of St. Thomas, a community of profound historical significance in Jamaican history. Stony Gut is the community most closely identified with Paul Bogle, the National Hero and leader of the 1865 Morant Bay Rebellion, who was a Native Baptist deacon in the area. The presence of an Anglican church named for St. Boniface — the missionary monk who Christianised Germany — in the vicinity of Stony Gut represents an interesting intersection of ecclesiastical and political history in one of Jamaica's most historically charged landscapes.

## History
Stony Gut was the home community of Paul Bogle, the deacon and activist who led the Morant Bay Rebellion of October 1865. Bogle organised his congregation at Stony Gut and led the march on the Morant Bay courthouse that triggered the uprising. The colonial government's brutal suppression killed over 430 people and led to the execution of Bogle and Gordon. The area around Stony Gut has been a site of Jamaican historical memory ever since. The Anglican church established in the vicinity represents the Diocese's mission presence in this historically significant landscape, though the relationship between the established Anglican church and the Native Baptist tradition of Paul Bogle reflects the complex denominational politics of 19th-century St. Thomas.

## Architecture
St. Boniface is a masonry church of modest proportions, representative of Anglican rural church building in St. Thomas. The structure balances practicality with the dignity appropriate to a place of worship, featuring a straightforward nave with local stone or concrete block construction typical of Jamaica's interior church-building tradition. Set in the coastal landscape of St. Thomas. Its coastal setting brings exposure to salt air and periodic storm surge, demanding robust masonry construction.

## Clergy
The Stony Gut area has been served within the St. Thomas deanery under shared clergy arrangements from Morant Bay. The presence of an Anglican church in the vicinity of Stony Gut represents the Diocese's attempt to maintain a pastoral presence in a community most closely associated with the Native Baptist tradition.

## Notable Facts
Paul Bogle was declared a National Hero of Jamaica in 1969, one of only seven individuals to receive this honour. His image appears on the Jamaican fifty-cent coin. Stony Gut itself is a place of national pilgrimage for Jamaicans commemorating the Rebellion and its legacy. The Morant Bay Rebellion is considered a pivotal moment in Jamaican history, directly leading to the abolition of the Jamaican House of Assembly and the establishment of Crown Colony government, which eventually created the conditions for the 20th-century movement toward independence. Hurricane Ivan (2004) caused widespread damage across St. Thomas.
