# St. Peter's, Whitby
**Manchester** · Church · active

St. Peter's Anglican Church serves **Whitby**, a community in the rural interior of Manchester parish. The church is administered as a mission of the **St. Barnabas', Mile Gully** cure, alongside the chapel of ease at Harry Watch and the cure church St. Barnabas'. The cure rectory is at Mile Gully P.O., Manchester (telephone 965-4527); the cure was recently listed as **VACANT**.

## History
**Founding of the parish (1814).** Manchester was created by **Act 55 George III. c. 23** of **1814** in response to a petition of 29 November 1814 by residents of **Mile Gully**, May Day, and Carpenter's Mountain. The Act came into effect on **13 December 1814**, taking the "hill country" of eastern St. Elizabeth and the western portions of Vere and Clarendon. The new parish was named after **William Montagu, 5th Duke of Manchester** (Governor 1808–1827); **Mandeville**, the capital, was named after his son, **Viscount Mandeville**.

**Slavery, the parish church, and the Sam Sharpe Rebellion (1831).** Manchester's hill-country economy was built on coffee, supplemented by sugar in lower zones, both worked by enslaved African labour. **St. Mark's Anglican Church, Mandeville** — the parish church — was completed and consecrated in **1820**; its organ loft was used as a jail during the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**. Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation, 1865, disestablishment.** Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Maidstone** in Manchester (1840) was among Jamaica's earliest Moravian-led free villages. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of St. Peter's: the Mile Gully cure.** Whitby is a small upland farming district in the rolling country around Mile Gully — one of several Manchester communities whose names were transferred from English towns by colonial-era estate proprietors. **Whitby** is a fishing port on the Yorkshire coast, famously associated with **Captain James Cook** (whose ships *Endeavour* and *Resolution* were Whitby-built colliers) and the historic **St. Hild's Abbey**. The Diocese of Jamaica established St. Peter's at Whitby as one of the small mission outposts of the **St. Barnabas' Mile Gully cure**, which serves the dispersed Anglican congregations of the Mile Gully hinterland and includes the famous abandoned **St. George's Anglican Church** — the "Duppy Church" — that stands roofless near the town and is a Jamaica National Heritage Trust site. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

**Twentieth century: Kendal, bauxite, Independence.** The **Kendal train tragedy** of **1 September 1957** at Kendal Manchester claimed almost 200 lives — Jamaica's worst rail accident; Kendal lies on the rail line that ran through the Mile Gully country. The **bauxite industry** transformed Manchester from 1957 onward. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Peter's a settled rural mission.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of St. Peter's Whitby after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. Peter's Whitby is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for mission stations across the Manchester interior. The hill-country position creates wind exposure during storm passages.

## Clergy
St. Peter's Whitby is served as a mission of the St. Barnabas', Mile Gully cure within the Mandeville Region's Manchester Deanery. The cure has recently been listed as vacant.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to **St. Peter the apostle** — chief of the twelve, traditionally identified as the first bishop of Rome, with a feast day on **29 June** (shared with St. Paul) — is one of the most common Anglican dedications, found at parish churches and mission stations across the diocese. The Yorkshire-coast origin of the name "Whitby" places this Manchester community within the long-running Jamaican pattern of transferred English place-names: from Cumbria-named Sedburgh in nearby Christiana, to Yorkshire-named Whitby here, to Wales-named Snowdon further south.
