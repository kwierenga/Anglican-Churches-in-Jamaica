# St. Peter's, Whitby
**Manchester** · Church · active

St. Peter's Anglican Church serves Whitby, a community in the rural interior of Manchester parish. The church is administered as a mission of the St. Barnabas', Mile Gully cure, alongside the chapel of ease at Harry Watch and the cure church St. Barnabas'. The cure rectory is at Mile Gully P.O., Manchester (telephone 965-4527); the cure was recently listed as **VACANT**.

## History
Whitby is a small upland farming district in the rolling country around Mile Gully — one of several Manchester communities whose names were transferred from English towns by colonial-era estate proprietors (Whitby being a fishing port on the Yorkshire coast, famously associated with Captain James Cook and the historic St. Hild abbey).

The Diocese of Jamaica established St. Peter's at Whitby as one of the small mission outposts of the St. Barnabas' Mile Gully cure, which serves the dispersed Anglican congregations of the Mile Gully hinterland. The cure is part of the broader rural mission network the Diocese extended across Manchester after the parish's creation in 1814 and especially during Bishop Enos Nuttall's late-nineteenth-century rural church-planting expansion. The Mile Gully cure also contains the famous abandoned St. George's Anglican Church — the "Duppy Church" — that stands roofless near the town and has become a national monument and well-known site of local folklore.

The dedication to St. Peter the apostle — chief of the twelve, traditionally identified as the first bishop of Rome, with a feast day on 29 June (shared with St. Paul) — is one of the most common Anglican dedications, found at parish churches and mission stations across the diocese.

## Architecture
St. Peter's Whitby is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for mission stations across the Manchester interior. The hill-country position creates wind exposure during storm passages.

## Clergy
St. Peter's Whitby is served as a mission of the St. Barnabas', Mile Gully cure within the Mandeville Region's Manchester Deanery. The cure has recently been listed as vacant.
