# St. George's, Red Hills (Clarendon)
**Clarendon** · Church · active

St. George's Anglican Church serves **Red Hills** in Clarendon parish, a community in the interior of the parish in the hill country north of May Pen. (This Red Hills should not be confused with the **Red Hills** community in St. Andrew.) The dedication is to **St. George** — patron saint of England, whose **23 April** feast day was a marker of English Anglican settlement across the colonial Caribbean.

The church is a mission of the **St. Bartholomew's Frankfield** cure, with the cure rectory at Frankfield P.O., Clarendon (telephone 904-4565); the Frankfield cure was recently listed as **VACANT**. (Note: this church appears in some local references as "St. Gregory's"; the diocesan directory consistently records it as **St. George's**, which we have adopted following the diocesan listing.)

## History
**Founding of the parish (1664).** Clarendon parish was created in **1664**, nine years after the English seizure of Jamaica from Spain (**1655**), and named for **Sir Edward Hyde, 1st Earl of Clarendon** (1609–1674), Lord High Chancellor of England under Charles II. The original parish church was the **Church of the White Cross** at "The Cross." **St. Paul's Chapelton** was built as a chapel of ease in **1666** and became the parish church when the Cross Church was destroyed in **1815**. **Vere** was carved out as a separate sugar-plain parish in **1673** and was reabsorbed into Clarendon in **1867**; **Law 20 of 1867** designated **May Pen** as chief town.

**Slavery, Sam Sharpe, and Emancipation.** Clarendon was one of Jamaica's great sugar parishes — the fertile Rio Minho plain held the great estates of the Suttons, Pennants, Ballards, and Dawkins, and the wealth of the parish was built on the labour of enslaved Africans. One of Jamaica's earliest slave rebellions erupted at **Suttons Plantation in 1690**: some 500 enslaved people rose in revolt, and many of those who escaped joined the Maroon communities eventually led by **Cudjoe** into the Cockpit Country. During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**, a vigilante alliance that sought to "wipe the non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of the Red Hills mission.** Red Hills in Clarendon sits in the interior farming landscape north of May Pen. The Anglican Diocese established St. George's as a mission of the St. Bartholomew's Frankfield cure to serve this community, alongside the other missions of the cure (St. Luke's Sanguinetti, St. Matthew's Aenon Town, Holy Trinity Park Hall, and Peckham). The St. George dedication is shared with several other Jamaican Anglican churches — from larger town churches like St. George's Buff Bay (Portland) to mission stations across the rural deaneries. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

**Twentieth century: bauxite, May Pen, Independence.** Chapelton lost its status as parish capital in **1938**, with administrative functions transferring to **May Pen**. **Alcoa** began bauxite operations in Clarendon in **1959**; the **Jamalco** alumina refinery at Halse Hall opened in **1972**. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. George's a settled mission of the Frankfield cure.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of St. George's Red Hills after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. George's is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural Clarendon in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries: a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard. The hilly interior position channels wind during storm passages and makes good drainage essential to the building's longevity.

## Clergy
St. George's Red Hills is served as a mission of the St. Bartholomew's Frankfield cure. The cure rectory in Frankfield (currently vacant) is the contact point for this church and the other missions of the cure.
