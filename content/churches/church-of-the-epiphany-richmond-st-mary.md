# Church of the Epiphany, Richmond
**St. Mary** · Church · active

The Church of the Epiphany is an Anglican church serving the **Richmond** community in St. Mary parish, one of two Anglican worship points associated with Richmond in the dataset. The **Epiphany** dedication — marking the manifestation of Christ to the Gentiles at the visit of the Magi — is a relatively unusual church name in the Jamaican Anglican network. The church serves the Richmond farming community in the interior hills of St. Mary.

## History
**Pre-English era and the Battle of Rio Nuevo (1658).** **Christopher Columbus** sighted Jamaica in 1494 on his second voyage; the north-eastern coast of present St. Mary lay on the path of his exploration. The **Battle of Rio Nuevo (June 1658)**, fought near the mouth of the Rio Nuevo on the St. Mary/St. Ann boundary, saw British forces under **Sir Edward D'Oyley** decisively defeat a Spanish expedition attempting to recapture Jamaica — effectively ending Spanish resistance to British rule.

**English rule (1655) and the planter church.** Under English rule from **1655**, St. Mary became a major sugar-producing parish, its coastal plain and river valleys dense with estates that depended on enslaved labour. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds and centrally implicated in the plantation system.

**Tacky's Revolt (1760).** On Easter Monday, **7 April 1760**, **Tacky's Revolt** broke out on **Frontier and Trinity estates** near Port Maria. Tacky — a **Fante royal** from the Gold Coast of West Africa — led a rising of self-emancipated **Coromantee** people intended to overthrow British colonial rule on the whole island. Over **500 Black men and women** were killed in battle, executed, or took their own lives — the most significant slave rebellion in the British West Indies between the 1733 St. John insurrection and the 1791 Haitian Revolution.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union** to "wipe non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb** (1824–1843) had begun extending Anglican schools — by 1835 he reported 142 schools with 8,500 scholars across Jamaica.

**Metcalfe parish (1841–1867) and disestablishment (1870).** A separate parish of **Metcalfe** was created in **1841** by petition of freeholders of eastern St. Mary and adjoining St. George, named for **Sir Charles Metcalfe**, then Governor of Jamaica; **Annotto Bay** was its capital. **Law 20 of 1867** reduced parishes from 22 to 14, reuniting Metcalfe with St. Mary, with Port Maria restored as parish town. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

**Foundation of the Epiphany church.** The Church of the Epiphany was established as part of the Diocese's mission network in interior St. Mary, serving the post-Emancipation farming communities of the Richmond area. The presence of both St. Benedict's and the Church of the Epiphany in the dataset suggests the Richmond district has historically supported two Anglican worship points, or that one entry reflects a different part of a dispersed district.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** The **banana trade** transformed the St. Mary economy in the late 19th and 20th centuries, with Annotto Bay and Port Maria as leading shipping points. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found the Church of the Epiphany a settled mission within the Diocese.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Hurricane Melissa brought heavy rain and wind to interior St. Mary; the Richmond community and its congregations were in the affected zone.

## Architecture
The Church of the Epiphany is a small masonry chapel in the interior hills of St. Mary. The building sits in the hilly terrain of the Richmond district, subject to the heavy annual rainfall of this part of the parish. The church's modest construction is characteristic of Anglican interior mission buildings across Jamaica.

## Clergy
Richmond's churches have been served within the St. Mary deanery under shared clergy arrangements, with the two Anglican worship points in the district supported by the same circuit clergy.

## Notable Facts
The **Epiphany** dedication is noted in the Anglican calendar as falling on **6 January**, and churches with this dedication were sometimes founded on or near that date. The Richmond district's interior position places it in the rainfall-intensive hill country of St. Mary.
