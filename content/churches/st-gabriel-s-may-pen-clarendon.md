# St. Gabriel's, May Pen
**Clarendon** · Parish Church · active

St. Gabriel's Anglican Church is the principal Anglican church of May Pen, the capital town of Clarendon and the largest urban centre on Jamaica's south-central plain. Standing at 10 Church Street, it is dedicated to the Archangel Gabriel — a relatively uncommon dedication in Jamaica's Anglican network — and serves a congregation drawn from across the Rio Minho valley and the surrounding parish. The church is a twentieth-century parish centre in a town that became Clarendon's capital only in 1938, though the Anglican presence in the parish stretches back to the seventeenth century.

## History
Clarendon parish was created in 1664, nine years after the English seized Jamaica from Spain, and named for Sir Edward Hyde (1609–1674), 1st Earl of Clarendon and Lord High Chancellor of England. Over the colonial period the parish was reshaped several times, eventually absorbing the older parishes of St. Dorothy and Vere to reach its present boundaries; Chapelton was its capital before May Pen.

Clarendon's economy was built on slavery. The parish's coastal plain, drained by the Rio Minho and the Milk River, contained dozens of sugar estates, pens, and coffee properties that depended on enslaved labour. Until 1870, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds; its vestry was composed of the planter class. The old parish churches were at Alley (St. Peter's, serving Vere) and Chapelton (St. Paul's), which anchored the Anglican ministry for most of the colonial period.

Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on 1 August 1838. The freed population of Clarendon established free villages and provision grounds across the interior, and the Anglican Church expanded its mission to serve these communities.

May Pen itself grew through the nineteenth century as a market settlement, beginning — according to local tradition — as two inns on the banks of the Rio Minho. The town takes its name from the Rev. William May (born England 1695), who served first as Rector of Kingston Parish Church before being transferred to Clarendon, where he was rector for 32 years and whose estate at this location gave the district its name. Before a bridge was built, travellers east and west had to lodge at May's inns when the Rio Minho was in spate; Dr. Samuel Glaister Bell, a renowned parish physician, drowned crossing the river, and the May Pen Clock Tower later honoured him. Law 20 of **1887** first formally designated May Pen as the chief town of the merged Clarendon, which at that point absorbed Vere (its southern-plain neighbour) into a single parish under the Clarendon name. May Pen was granted mayoral status in 1962, the year of independence. St. Gabriel's, at Church Street, serves as the principal Anglican church of the capital. The twentieth century saw May Pen's rapid growth, accelerated by the bauxite and alumina industry that transformed the parish's economy: Alcoa began bauxite operations in 1959, the first shipment left in 1963, and the Jamalco alumina refinery at Halse Hall opened in 1972.

Jamaica's independence on 6 August 1962 found St. Gabriel's at the civic centre of one of Jamaica's most populous and economically significant parishes.

## Architecture
St. Gabriel's is a masonry parish church in the tradition of Jamaican town churches, with a nave proportioned to serve a substantial congregation and an adjoining hall with kitchen and facilities used for community events. The churchyard contains graves spanning the colonial and post-colonial periods, with 567 memorial records documented.

## Education
The Anglican mission to educate expanded across Clarendon after Emancipation, part of the Diocese's wider effort to provide schools for the freed population. St. Gabriel's continues this tradition through Sunday schools and partnerships with Anglican schools in May Pen.

## Clergy
May Pen's Anglican ministry is centred on St. Gabriel's, which coordinates outreach across a parish dense with smaller Anglican churches and chapels. A reviewer notes the traditional Anglican form of worship — open-eyed prayer from a prayer book — as distinctive and sincere.

## Notable Facts
The Archangel Gabriel's dedication is relatively uncommon in Jamaica's Anglican network — this is one of only a small number of churches dedicated to an archangel rather than an apostle or saint. Gabriel, the messenger angel who announced the Incarnation to Mary, is honoured on 29 September (Michaelmas) jointly with Michael and Raphael. May Pen was made the capital of Clarendon only in 1938, making St. Gabriel's a comparatively young parish centre in a parish whose older Anglican presence is at Alley (1671) and Chapelton. Hurricane Melissa (28 October 2025) tracked across south-western Jamaica; Clarendon's interior and coastal communities sustained impact, and May Pen's Anglican network was part of the diocesan recovery effort.
