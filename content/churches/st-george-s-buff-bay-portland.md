# St. George's, Buff Bay
**Portland** · Church · active

St. George's Anglican Church is the historic Anglican church of **Buff Bay**, a town on the north coast of Portland at the mouth of the **Buff Bay River**. Buff Bay is one of Portland's larger coastal communities, historically important as a commercial and transport hub for the western part of the parish. Reviewers praise its impressive stone architecture as giving the town "a bit medieval" character that "just stands out as something ancient but strong."

## History
**English rule (1655) and the founding of Portland (1723).** Under English rule from **1655**, the future Portland coast remained largely Maroon territory. **Portland parish was created in 1723** by order of the then Governor, **Henry Bentinck, 1st Duke of Portland** (Governor 1722–1726), and named in his honour. The British built **Fort George** at Port Antonio in 1729.

**The First Maroon War (1728–1739) and Nanny.** The **Windward Maroons** under **Quao** and **Nanny** controlled the Blue Mountains and John Crow Mountains. The **1740 treaty** granted **Nanny a 500-acre land grant** that forms the present site of **Moore Town**. **Nanny of the Maroons** became Jamaica's only female National Hero in October 1975.

**Slavery and the planter church.** Sugar estates dominated the fertile coastal lowlands and river valleys; coffee thrived in the hills. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union** to "wipe non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Christ Church Port Antonio** was completed around **1840** under Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb**.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall, and the banana revolution.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. In **1870**, **Captain Lorenzo Dow Baker** purchased bananas in Port Antonio market; the first shipment of **1,450 stems** reached Jersey City in **1871**, launching the international banana trade. Baker's Boston Fruit Company became **United Fruit Company in 1899**.

**Foundation of St. George's.** Buff Bay developed as one of the main towns of Portland's north coast, with the river providing fresh water and a natural focal point for settlement. The Anglican church here has a long history connected to the town's commercial and planting class. St. George's was established as a substantial church in Buff Bay — its stone architecture is notable enough to draw particular comment from visitors who describe it as awe-inspiring and a testament to the Anglican Diocese's heritage in the area. The church's name, dedicated to the patron saint of England, reflects the colonial cultural framework within which it was built.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** The **1903 hurricane** damaged Christ Church Port Antonio and devastated the banana industry. Tourism grew at Port Antonio. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. George's a settled town parish.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. **Hurricane Gilbert (1988)** had earlier struck Buff Bay severely. Hurricane Melissa tracked along the north coast; Buff Bay was in the storm's path and the congregation experienced direct impact.

## Architecture
St. George's is a stone-built church on St. George's Street in Buff Bay, confirmed by Google Places. Reviewers consistently note the impressive character of the building: "The architectural design of this building was superb. It just stands out as something ancient but strong in the town." Another describes the atmosphere as giving "a bit medieval" feel. The church sits near the **Buff Bay River** mouth, with the river creating compound flooding risk alongside storm surge during hurricane passages.

## Clergy
St. George's serves as the principal Anglican church of Buff Bay and has been the hub of the **western Portland deanery circuit**, with clergy covering the mission network in the western part of the parish. The church has maintained an active congregation drawing from the town's community and the surrounding farming and fishing districts.

## Notable Facts
Buff Bay's position at the mouth of the **Buff Bay River** makes it one of the more flood-prone communities on Portland's north coast. The river can overflow during the extraordinary rainfall events that Portland experiences during hurricane passages. The dedication to **St. George** — patron saint of England, with a feast day on **23 April** — was a marker of English Anglican settlement across the colonial Caribbean.
