# St. John's, Sligoville
**St. Catherine** · Church · active

St. John's Anglican Church at Sligoville holds a particular historical significance in Jamaica: Sligoville was the first free village established in Jamaica after Emancipation in 1838, founded on land provided by the Marquess of Sligo, then Governor of Jamaica. The church confirmed at its Sligoville Road location is described by a reviewer as 'a place of historic significance' — with a caution to watch for wasps in the eaves, bell tower, and around the tombstones. This is a church rooted in the earliest chapter of Jamaican freedom.

## History
**Spanish Villa de la Vega (1534) and English conquest (1655).** The Spanish founded **Villa de la Vega** in 1534 — Jamaica's capital from 1538 until **1872**. Penn and Venables's English expedition captured Jamaica in May **1655**. Under English rule from 1655, **St. Catherine** was one of Jamaica's original parishes; the neighbouring early parishes of **St. John, St. Dorothy, and St. Thomas-in-the-Vale** were merged into expanded St. Catherine under **Law 20 of 1867**.

**Slavery and the planter church.** Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds. **The Diocese of Jamaica** was established by Letters Patent on **24 July 1824**; **Christopher Lipscomb** was consecrated first Bishop in 1824. During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union**; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Foundation of Sligoville (1838) — Jamaica's first free village.** Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Sligoville was established in 1838 as the first free village in Jamaica**, created when **Howe Peter Browne, 2nd Marquess of Sligo and Governor of Jamaica**, provided land in the hills of St. Catherine for freed people to settle on their own terms. The village became a symbol of post-Emancipation aspiration — a demonstration that freed Jamaicans could organise a settled, self-supporting community outside the plantation system. The Anglican church at Sligoville served this pioneering free community, which was also home to Baptist and other nonconformist congregations. The name Sligoville directly commemorates the Marquess whose support made the settlement possible. Bishop Lipscomb (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. Capital moved from Spanish Town to **Kingston in 1872**.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. John's an active free-village parish.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Hurricane Melissa brought wind and rain to interior St. Catherine.

## Architecture
St. John's is The church sits in the hill country of St. Catherine, at elevation above the coastal plain. The reviewer's note about wasps in the eaves and bell tower, and around the tombstones, paints a picture of a historic building with an old churchyard in a quiet hill setting. The church's age means it has stood through the full history of post-Emancipation Jamaica, from the first days of freedom to the present.

## Clergy
Sligoville has been served within the St. Catherine deanery. The church's connection to the first free village gives it a particular pastoral identity — a congregation whose origins lie in the most hopeful moment of Jamaican social history.

## Notable Facts
Sligoville's status as Jamaica's first free village is one of the most significant facts in the social history of the post-Emancipation Caribbean. The Marquess of Sligo was an unusually progressive colonial governor who used his personal resources and position to facilitate the creation of the village. Sligoville became a model for subsequent free village development across Jamaica and is commemorated as a site of national heritage significance. The church here has served the descendants of Jamaica's first free community for nearly two centuries.
