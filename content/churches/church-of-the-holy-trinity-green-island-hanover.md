# Church of the Holy Trinity, Green Island
**Hanover** · Church · active

The Church of the Holy Trinity serves the community of Green Island on Hanover's western coast, the parish's second Anglican parish seat alongside Lucea. Green Island Harbour was a shipping point for the sugar estates of western Hanover during the plantation era, and the Anglican community here was already established by the mid-nineteenth century. By 1865 Green Island had its own rector on the same salary as the Island Curate (£340), and by 1891 the congregation numbered over 300 registered members.

## History
Hanover parish was carved out of St. James in 1723. Until 1870, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds. The Anglican presence at Green Island developed alongside the sugar economy of the western coast: the harbour served as a loading point for estates whose enslaved workforce produced the wealth that flowed through Hanover to Lucea and out to the Atlantic.

Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on 1 August 1838. By the 1860s the Anglican presence at Green Island had grown into a full parochial cure. The 1865 Jamaica Almanac records Rev. Henry Gibbes Lawson as Rector at Green Island on a salary of £340 — equal to that of the Island Curate — placing Green Island on a footing with the larger parish centres of western Jamaica. Lawson was still rector in 1870.

Disestablishment in 1870 withdrew state funding from the Church of England in Jamaica and reconstituted it as a self-supporting Diocese. Green Island continued as a parochial centre through the transition. The 1891 Handbook records Rev. J. K. Collymore as clergyman at Green Island, with 306 registered members, 87 baptisms, and 10 marriages in that year — a congregation second only to Lucea among Hanover Anglican churches. By 1900 the incumbent was Rev. J. N. Somerville.

Jamaica's independence on 6 August 1962 found Holy Trinity an established community church within the western Hanover deanery, serving a town whose economy had shifted over a century from sugar-port commerce to small-scale fishing and farming.

## Architecture
Holy Trinity is a masonry church of modest proportions suited to a coastal community. The churchyard contains burials spanning the later nineteenth and twentieth centuries. Green Island sits on a low-lying coastal position, sheltered to some degree by the headlands of the harbour but exposed to surge and wind during storms that strike western Jamaica.

## Clergy
Recorded incumbents include Rev. Henry Gibbes Lawson (Rector, 1865 and 1870), Rev. J. K. Collymore (1891), and Rev. J. N. Somerville (1900). Since the twentieth century, Holy Trinity has been grouped within the Montego Bay Region and the western Hanover ministry.

## Notable Facts
By 1891, Green Island's 306 registered Anglicans made it the second-largest Anglican congregation in Hanover after Lucea Parish Church. The harbour gave the settlement an unusual degree of natural shelter on a generally exposed coast — an asset in commerce that has not fully protected it from major hurricanes. Hurricane Melissa (28 October 2025) made landfall a short distance to the south at New Hope, Westmoreland; western Hanover was in the direct impact zone and the 100-year-old Ramble Anglican Church nearby was reported demolished by the Jamaica Gleaner. Green Island and the surrounding Anglican communities were part of the diocesan recovery response coordinated by the Diocese of Jamaica and the Cayman Islands.
