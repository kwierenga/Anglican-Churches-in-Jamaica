# Church-of-the-Holy-Trinity, Green Island
**Hanover** · Church · active

The Church of the Holy Trinity at Green Island is the Anglican church serving one of the larger communities on the south coast of Hanover, a fishing and farming town on the sheltered waters of Green Island Harbour. The harbour gave the settlement commercial significance during the plantation era as a loading point for sugar from the surrounding estates, and the community that grew here has maintained a distinctive character shaped by both the sea and the interior agriculture of southern Hanover. Holy Trinity has served as the spiritual anchor for Green Island and the surrounding district for well over a century, providing worship, baptisms, burials, and pastoral care to the coastal community.

## History
Green Island Harbour was used by the sugar estates of southern Hanover as a shipping point during the plantation era, and the community that grew up around it was tied to both the maritime trade and the agricultural interior. After Emancipation, freed families settled in the town and along the coast, and the Anglican Diocese established a presence here to serve the post-Emancipation community. Holy Trinity was founded as the local Anglican church, growing from the Diocese's mission network across Hanover. The town has remained a modest but stable community, its economy shifting from sugar to small-scale fishing and farming across the 19th and 20th centuries, with the church serving as a consistent institutional anchor throughout.

## Architecture
Holy Trinity is a masonry church of modest proportions suited to a coastal community of moderate size. Green Island sits on a low-lying harbourside position, sheltered to some degree by the headlands of the bay but directly exposed to surge from storms that funnel up the south Hanover coast. The church's construction reflects the need for durability in a coastal environment with regular exposure to salt air and periodic storm damage. The surrounding churchyard provides burial space for the families of the town and surrounding districts.

## Clergy
Holy Trinity has been served within the Hanover Anglican circuit, grouped with other south-coast and interior Hanover churches under shared ministry arrangements from the Montego Bay Region. As in most smaller Hanover congregations, lay leaders — wardens, Mothers' Union members, and lay readers — have been essential to sustaining regular worship and community life. The church's ministry extends to the marking of major life events for Green Island's families, with funerals and memorial services confirming its active role in the community.

## Notable Facts
Green Island is notable for its harbour, which provided a degree of natural shelter unusual on the otherwise exposed south Hanover coast. During the plantation era this made it a commercial asset; in the hurricane era it has offered partial protection from the most destructive sea swells. However, no natural harbour offers full protection from a major direct strike, and Green Island has experienced serious storm damage across its history. Hurricane Charlie (1951) affected the south Hanover coast. Hurricane Gilbert (1988) caused widespread damage along this shore. Hurricane Ivan (2004) brought significant coastal impact to southern Hanover. Hurricane Melissa (October 2025) struck western Jamaica and the south Hanover coast was in the storm's impact zone; Green Island and its congregation experienced damaging conditions and the church was called upon to provide pastoral support to affected families.
