## History
Constant Spring developed as a suburban district along the main road leading north from Half-Way-Tree toward the Blue Mountains, growing in the post-independence era as one of St. Andrew's principal residential and commercial zones. The Anglican Diocese established the Church of the Good Shepherd to serve this expanding community. The Constant Spring area takes its name from a local spring, and the district has developed as one of the more prosperous northern suburban communities.

## Architecture
Church of the Good Shepherd is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural St. Andrew in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The structure is functional rather than ornate — a simple rectangular nave, a modest entrance, and a small churchyard — suited to a rural congregation of modest means. Set in the rural interior of St. Andrew. The interior position offers some shelter from coastal surge while remaining exposed to wind and heavy rainfall during major storm events.

## Clergy
Constant Spring has been served within the northern St. Andrew deanery circuit. The church's position in a prosperous suburban community supports an active congregation with substantial lay leadership.

## Notable Facts
Constant Spring Road is one of the main arteries of northern St. Andrew, connecting the urban centre to the hill communities and serving as the gateway to the Blue Mountains for visitors and residents alike. The Church of the Good Shepherd sits on this important road corridor. The Good Shepherd image has particular resonance in a church that serves the gateway community between the city and the mountains, calling to mind the shepherd who knows the terrain and seeks out those who are lost.
