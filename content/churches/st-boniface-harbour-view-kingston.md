# St. Boniface, Harbour View
**Kingston** · Church · active

St. Boniface Anglican Church stands on St. Thomas Main Road in Harbour View, Kingston 17, serving one of Jamaica's most distinctive post-independence communities at the eastern gateway to Kingston Harbour. The church is the main cure of a circuit that includes St. Peter's, Port Royal and the mission of St. Martin's, Bull Bay — a geographically remarkable grouping that spans modern suburbia, a historic fishing village with over three centuries of history, and a rural coastal settlement.

## History
Harbour View was developed in 1960 as the largest single housing scheme ever undertaken by an organisation in the West Indies at that time. Built by West Indies Home Contractors (WIHCON), the company created in 1956 by the Matalon family under managing director Owen Matalon — known as Jamaica's "housing man" — the scheme comprised approximately 1,800 dwelling units using Matalon's proprietary mass construction system. The development came just two years before Jamaica's independence in 1962, and the new community quickly attracted young families seeking affordable homes on Kingston's eastern edge.

The land on which Harbour View was built has a much older military history. Fort Nugent, originally constructed by Spanish slave agent James Castillo to defend his residence, was later strengthened by Governor George Nugent around 1806 to guard the eastern approach to Kingston Harbour. A Martello tower from this period — probably built between 1806 and 1811 at a cost of £12,000 — survives as a protected heritage site within the community.

St. Boniface was established in the early 1960s to serve the new housing scheme, following a pattern across the Diocese of Jamaica in which churches were planted in newly developed communities. The church does not appear in the 1900 Handbook of Jamaica or in J. B. Ellis's 1913 history of the Diocese, confirming it as a mid-twentieth century foundation rather than a colonial-era church. Its establishment likely falls within Bishop Percival Gibson's episcopate (1956–1967), a period of rapid church expansion in Kingston and St. Andrew.

Harbour View's geography makes it one of the most exposed communities in Kingston. Situated where the Palisadoes tombolo meets the mainland, with Kingston Harbour to the north and the Hope River flowing through, the area is vulnerable to both storm surge and river flooding. Residents recall that soon after the housing scheme was completed, roads and houses began flooding during storms due to inadequate drainage. Hurricane Melissa in October 2025 damaged both the community and the church.

Despite these challenges, Harbour View has developed a strong community identity. It is said to have been the first community in Jamaica to produce its own community newspaper and among the first to host street dances. The Harbour View Citizens' Association has long been active in community affairs.

## Architecture
St. Boniface is a mid-twentieth century church building, consistent with the functional modernist style typical of Anglican churches erected to serve Jamaica's post-war housing developments. Built in the early 1960s for a new suburban community rather than as a Victorian-era chapel, its design reflects the practical priorities of the period. The church's coastal position on St. Thomas Main Road brings exposure to salt air, storm surge, and the periodic flooding that affects the wider Harbour View area.

## Community
St. Boniface has served as a community hub beyond its Sunday worship. The church has hosted health fairs in partnership with the Rotary Club of Kingston East and Port Royal and the Shipping Association of Jamaica, offering residents free health screenings, diabetes and hypertension checks, optical screening, and medication. The church's Sunday School ministry has served generations — among its longest-serving members is Enid Bernard, who at age 100 in 2024 was still attending St. Boniface and was recognised for her faithful and dedicated service to the Sunday School.

The cure links three very different communities under a single pastoral charge: Harbour View's suburban streets, Port Royal's historic fishing village at the end of the Palisadoes — where St. Peter's Church has stood since 1725 — and the rural coastal settlement of Bull Bay to the east.

## Clergy
The current rector is Kirk Brown. The rectorship encompasses all three communities of the cure.

## Notable Facts
Harbour View's most famous son is Donald Quarrie, the legendary Jamaican sprinter who won the Olympic 200-metre gold medal at the 1976 Montreal Games. The local secondary school, originally Harbour View Secondary, was renamed Donald Quarrie High School in his honour when it opened in January 1977.

St. Boniface of Crediton (c. 675–754), the church's patron saint, was an Anglo-Saxon Benedictine monk who became the great missionary to the Germanic peoples — the "Apostle of the Germans." Born Winfrid in Devon, he is remembered for felling the sacred Thunder Oak of the Norse god Donar near Fritzlar, an act of defiance that led to mass conversions. He was martyred in Frisia in 754. His feast day is 5 June. The dedication connects a twentieth-century Caribbean housing community to the English church's own missionary heritage.
