# St. Andrew's, Bamboo
**St. Ann** · Church · active

St. Andrew’s Anglican Church serves the small roadside community of Bamboo, located on the historic route between Ocho Rios and Brown’s Town. It was established in the late 1800s for the communities built by freed families after Emancipation in 1838. Despite facing storms and changing times, the church has remained a constant and active presence in the town. Today, St. Andrew’s continues to be a cornerstone for worship and community life in Bamboo and the surrounding districts.

## History
St. Andrew’s Anglican Church was founded in the late 19th century, as the district of Bamboo grew with families moving inland from coastal estates after Emancipation. Before 1838, the region's population was largely enslaved on plantations near St. Ann’s Bay. The post-Emancipation era saw the growth of small farming villages along the roadways, with Bamboo emerging as a local trading stop. The Anglican Diocese sent clergy from Brown’s Town and St. Ann’s Bay to serve this new settlement, and by the late 1800s, a permanent stone chapel was completed. Dedicated to St. Andrew, it provided the community with a vital center for baptisms, marriages, and spiritual support.

## Architecture
St. Andrew’s is a simple limestone church built in the rural Gothic Revival style. It features a rectangular nave, a small chancel, and pointed-arch windows with colored glass. The steep roof, now covered with metal, was designed to handle the heavy rainfall common in the area. Inside, the church has an open-timber roof, wooden pews, and a modest pulpit. The surrounding churchyard, with graves of long-standing local families, contributes to the site's peaceful and historic character.

## Clergy
St. Andrew’s has always been part of the St. Ann Deanery, sharing clergy with churches in Brown’s Town, Moneague, and Claremont. Early clergy traveled the main road to lead services and support local education and family life. The church was instrumental in promoting literacy and religious instruction, which were especially vital in the decades after Emancipation. After Jamaica’s Independence in 1962, the congregation remained active through the Mothers’ Union, youth groups, and community outreach. Lay readers were essential in leading services when ordained clergy were serving multiple parishes.

## Notable Facts
St. Andrew’s has endured several major hurricanes, including Charlie (1951) and Gilbert (1988), which caused damage to the roof and windows. Each time, the congregation and diocese carried out the necessary repairs. Further restoration work in the late 20th and early 21st centuries helped maintain the building's structure. There are no reports of damage from Hurricane Melissa (2025). Today, St. Andrew’s Anglican Church remains an active parish, holding weekly services, weddings, and community celebrations, serving as an enduring symbol of faith and heritage in Bamboo.
