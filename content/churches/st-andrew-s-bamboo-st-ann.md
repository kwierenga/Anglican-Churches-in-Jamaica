# St. Andrew's, Bamboo
**St. Ann** · Church · active

St. Andrew's Anglican Church serves the small roadside community of **Bamboo**, located on the historic route between Ocho Rios and Brown's Town. It was established in the late 1800s for the communities built by freed families after Emancipation in 1838. Today, St. Andrew's continues to be a cornerstone for worship and community life in Bamboo and the surrounding districts.

## History
**Pre-English settlement: Sevilla la Nueva (1509–1534).** St. Ann has a deeper colonial past than almost any other Jamaican parish. **Sevilla la Nueva**, the first permanent European settlement in Jamaica and the colony's first Spanish capital, was founded in **1509** by Juan de Esquivel near present St. Ann's Bay. It served as Spanish capital until **1534** when relocated to Villa de la Vega (Spanish Town). **Christopher Columbus** had been marooned at St. Ann's Bay for over a year (1503–1504) on his fourth voyage.

**English rule (1655) and the planter church.** Under English rule from **1655**, St. Ann became a major **sugar and livestock** region — the coastal plain to sugar estates and the interior plateau to "**pens**" (livestock properties). Before 1838, the region's population was largely enslaved on plantations near St. Ann's Bay. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**Sam Sharpe Rebellion and the Colonial Church Union (1831–32).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, **Hamilton Brown** of Brown's Town led a Colonial Church Union mob that destroyed the Brown's Town Baptist chapel in 1832 — the **Colonial Church Union** sought to "wipe the non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth." **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation (1838) and free villages.** Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. The post-Emancipation era saw the growth of small farming villages along the roadways, with **Bamboo** emerging as a local trading stop. Other free villages of St. Ann include **Sturge Town** (1839, founded by the English Quaker abolitionist **Joseph Sturge**) and **Clarksonville** (1835, founded by Baptist missionary Rev. John Clark and named for the abolitionist Thomas Clarkson) — see NLJ *History of St. Ann*. Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb** (1824–1843) had begun extending Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**Disestablishment (1870) and the founding of St. Andrew's.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. The Anglican Diocese sent clergy from Brown's Town and St. Ann's Bay to serve this new settlement at Bamboo, and by the late 1800s a permanent stone chapel was completed. Dedicated to **St. Andrew**, it provided the community with a vital centre for baptisms, marriages, and spiritual support.

**Twentieth century: bauxite, tourism, Independence.** Bauxite mining began in St. Ann around **1952** (Reynolds, Kaiser); **Discovery Bay** became a major bauxite port. Tourism grew at **Ocho Rios** as a key north-coast resort. **Hurricane Charlie (1951)** and **Hurricane Gilbert (1988)** caused damage to the roof and windows of St. Andrew's; each time, the congregation and Diocese carried out the necessary repairs. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Andrew's a settled rural town parish.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. There are no separate reports of damage to St. Andrew's Bamboo in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. Andrew's is a simple limestone church built in the rural Gothic Revival style. It features a rectangular nave, a small chancel, and pointed-arch windows with coloured glass. The steep roof, now covered with metal, was designed to handle the heavy rainfall common in the area. Inside, the church has an open-timber roof, wooden pews, and a modest pulpit. The surrounding churchyard, with graves of long-standing local families, contributes to the site's peaceful and historic character.

## Clergy
St. Andrew's has always been part of the **St. Ann Deanery**, sharing clergy with churches in Brown's Town, Moneague, and Claremont. Early clergy travelled the main road to lead services and support local education and family life. After Jamaica's Independence in 1962, the congregation remained active through the Mothers' Union, youth groups, and community outreach. Lay readers were essential in leading services when ordained clergy were serving multiple parishes.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to **St. Andrew the apostle** — patron saint of Scotland and brother of Simon Peter, with a feast day on **30 November** — is found in several Jamaican Anglican churches and reflects the significant Scottish presence in the colonial-era plantation economy.
