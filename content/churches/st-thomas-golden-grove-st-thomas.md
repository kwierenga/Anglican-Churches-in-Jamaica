# St. Thomas', Golden Grove
**St. Thomas** · Church · active

This St. Thomas' Anglican Church at **Golden Grove** represents a second Anglican presence in the Golden Grove area of St. Thomas, alongside St. Andrew's also recorded in this community. Golden Grove is in the eastern coastal plain of St. Thomas in the **Plantain Garden River** area.

## History
**English rule (1655) and the founding parish.** Under English rule from **1655**, St. Thomas was one of Jamaica's original parishes — originally **"St. Thomas-in-the-East"** with the first rector appointed in **1666**.

**Sugar economy and slavery — the Plantain Garden River valley.** The **Plantain Garden River valley** of eastern St. Thomas was among the most productive sugar districts in 18th-century Jamaica. **Golden Grove estate** was one of the significant properties in this valley. Great estates of the wider parish included Stokes Hall, Albion, and Holland. All were worked by enslaved Africans throughout the colonial period. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union** to "wipe non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb** (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**The Morant Bay Rebellion (11 October 1865).** On **11 October 1865**, **Paul Bogle** — a small farmer and Native Baptist deacon of **Stony Gut** — led a protest march to the Morant Bay courthouse. Governor **Edward John Eyre's** brutal response: **439 executed**, **600+ flogged**, **1,000+ homes burned**. Bogle was hanged on **24 October 1865**; **George William Gordon** was hanged on **23 October 1865**. **Both were declared National Heroes in 1965**.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall.** Jamaica became a **Crown Colony in 1866**. Law 20 of 1867 absorbed **St. David** into St. Thomas. The Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

**Foundation of St. Thomas'.** Golden Grove's position in the eastern St. Thomas sugar district means it historically had a substantial population of estate workers and later smallholder farmers. The presence of two Anglican entries in this area may reflect the Diocese's practice of maintaining multiple worship points across a large estate district, or may represent a historical uncertainty in the precise location of church buildings in this part of the parish.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** Internal self-government came in **1944**. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** fulfilled the aspirations for which Bogle and Gordon died. The area was devastated by **Hurricane Ivan (2004)**, which made landfall near this part of the coast.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Hurricane Melissa brought further impacts to eastern St. Thomas.

## Architecture
St. Thomas' is a masonry church of modest proportions, representative of Anglican rural church building in St. Thomas. The structure balances practicality with the dignity appropriate to a place of worship, featuring a straightforward nave with local stone or concrete block construction typical of Jamaica's interior church-building tradition. Its coastal setting brings exposure to salt air and periodic storm surge, demanding robust masonry construction.

## Clergy
Golden Grove's Anglican worship points have been served within the **St. Thomas deanery** under shared clergy arrangements, connected to the Port Morant circuit.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to **St. Thomas the Apostle** (feast day **21 December** in the Anglican calendar; **3 July** in some traditions) — the apostle who doubted then proclaimed "My Lord and my God" — is shared with the parish itself. The Plantain Garden River valley's intensive sugar cultivation made it one of the most productive plantation landscapes in the British Caribbean.
