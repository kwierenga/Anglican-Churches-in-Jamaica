# St. George's, Georges Valley
**Manchester** · Church · active

St. George's Anglican Church serves Georges Valley, a community in the rural interior of Manchester parish. (Note: the Diocese of Jamaica's online region directory does not separately list "Georges Valley" among its Manchester deanery entries reviewed; the church appears in our inventory but its current cure attachment requires diocesan confirmation.)

## History
Georges Valley is one of the small upland farming districts of Manchester, in the cool interior country that supports coffee and small-scale farming. The community's name preserves the wider Anglican naming pattern in which English saint dedications and English place names became attached to interior settlements during the late-eighteenth- and nineteenth-century plantation period; "Georges Valley" most directly evokes the dedication to St. George that the church carries.

Manchester parish itself was created by an Act of the House of Assembly on 13 December 1814, formed by amalgamating portions of St. Elizabeth, Clarendon, and the entirety of the old parish of Vere, and named after William Montagu, 5th Duke of Manchester, then Governor of Jamaica. The Anglican network across the parish has grown through cure churches like St. Mark's Mandeville (consecrated 1820) and Christ Church Christiana, with mission stations and small chapels extending into surrounding districts. St. George's at Georges Valley belongs to that broader pattern of upland mission outreach.

The dedication to St. George — patron saint of England, with a feast day on 23 April — is found at several Jamaican Anglican churches; George was a Romano-British soldier-martyr whose legend made him one of the most internationally recognised soldier-saints of the medieval Christian tradition.

## Architecture
St. George's is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for mission stations across the Manchester interior. The elevated position creates wind exposure during storm passages.

## Clergy
St. George's Georges Valley has been served within the Manchester Deanery; its specific cure attachment is not separately published in the diocesan online directory entries reviewed.
