# St. George's, Georges Valley
**Manchester** · Church · active

St. George's Anglican Church serves **Georges Valley**, a small upland farming district in the rural interior of Manchester parish. (The Diocese of Jamaica's online region directory does not separately list "Georges Valley" among its Manchester deanery entries reviewed; the church appears in our inventory but its current cure attachment requires diocesan confirmation.)

## History
**Founding of the parish (1814).** Manchester was created by **Act 55 George III. c. 23** of **1814** in response to a petition of 29 November 1814 by residents of Mile Gully, May Day, and Carpenter's Mountain. The Act came into effect on **13 December 1814**, taking the "hill country" of eastern St. Elizabeth and the western portions of Vere and Clarendon. The new parish was named after **William Montagu, 5th Duke of Manchester** (Governor 1808–1827); **Mandeville**, the capital, was named after his son, **Viscount Mandeville**.

**Slavery, the parish church, and the Sam Sharpe Rebellion (1831).** Manchester's hill-country economy was built on coffee, supplemented by sugar in lower zones, both worked by enslaved African labour. **St. Mark's Anglican Church, Mandeville** — the parish church — was completed and consecrated in **1820**; its organ loft was used as a jail during the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**. Sharpe was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica.

**Emancipation, 1865, disestablishment.** Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Maidstone** in Manchester (1840) was among Jamaica's earliest Moravian-led free villages. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica.

**Foundation of St. George's.** Georges Valley is one of the small upland farming districts of Manchester, in the cool interior country that supports coffee and small-scale farming. The community's name preserves the wider Anglican naming pattern in which English saint dedications and English place names became attached to interior settlements during the late-eighteenth- and nineteenth-century plantation period; "Georges Valley" most directly evokes the dedication to **St. George** that the church carries. The Anglican network across the parish has grown through cure churches like St. Mark's Mandeville (1820) and Christ Church Christiana, with mission stations and small chapels extending into surrounding districts. St. George's at Georges Valley belongs to that broader pattern of upland mission outreach. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

**Twentieth century: Kendal, bauxite, Independence.** The **Kendal train tragedy** of **1 September 1957** at Kendal Manchester claimed almost 200 lives — Jamaica's worst rail accident. The **bauxite industry** transformed Manchester from 1957 onward. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. George's a settled rural mission.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of St. George's Georges Valley after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. George's is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for mission stations across the Manchester interior. The elevated position creates wind exposure during storm passages.

## Clergy
St. George's Georges Valley has been served within the Manchester Deanery; its specific cure attachment is not separately published in the diocesan online directory entries reviewed.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to **St. George** — patron saint of England, with a feast day on **23 April** — is found at several Jamaican Anglican churches; George was a Romano-British soldier-martyr whose legend made him one of the most internationally recognised soldier-saints of the medieval Christian tradition.
