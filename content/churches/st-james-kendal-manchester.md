# St. James', Kendal
**Manchester** · Church · active

St. James' Anglican Church serves Kendal, a community in the rolling hills of north-central Manchester whose name was transferred from Kendal in Westmorland (now Cumbria), England, by colonial-era estate proprietors. **Kendal Manchester is the site of one of the most consequential events in Jamaica's twentieth-century history**: the **Kendal train wreck of 1 September 1957**, in which a Sunday excursion train returning from Montego Bay derailed at Kendal Station, claiming **almost 200 lives** and injuring **600–700 passengers**. It remains Jamaica's worst rail accident and one of the most devastating peacetime rail disasters in world history. (Note: an earlier draft of this narrative incorrectly placed the wreck in St. Ann; the National Library of Jamaica's *History of Manchester* explicitly identifies Kendal Manchester as the site.)

## History
**Founding of the parish (1814).** Manchester is one of Jamaica's youngest parishes, created by **Act 55 George III. c. 23** of **1814** in response to a petition of 29 November 1814 by the residents of Mile Gully, May Day, and Carpenter's Mountain — who had complained that "the closest public building was 40 miles away." The Act came into effect on **13 December 1814**, taking the "hill country" forming the eastern portion of St. Elizabeth and the western portions of Vere and Clarendon. The new parish was named after **William Montagu, 5th Duke of Manchester** (Governor of Jamaica 1808–1827); the parish capital, **Mandeville**, was named after the Duke's son, **Viscount Mandeville**, on 27 August 1816.

**Slavery, the parish church, and the Sam Sharpe Rebellion (1831).** Manchester's hill-country economy was built on coffee from the eighteenth century onward, supplemented by sugar in lower zones; both depended on enslaved African labour. The Mandeville **Court House** was built between 1817 and 1820 of limestone blocks "cut by slaves" (NLJ). **St. Mark's Anglican Church, Mandeville** — the parish church of Manchester — was completed and consecrated in **1820** and for many years was the only church in the parish; its organ loft was used as a jail during the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**. Until 1870 the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church.

**Emancipation, 1865, disestablishment.** Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. **Maidstone** in Manchester (1840) was among Jamaica's earliest Moravian-led free villages. After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870** and reconstituted as the self-supporting Diocese of Jamaica. Bishop Enos Nuttall's tenure (1880–1916) saw the Diocese grow from 26 rural stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

**Foundation of St. James'.** The Diocese of Jamaica retains an image of "St. James Church Kendal Mandeville, Manchester" (attachment_id 903). The Jamaican Ancestral Records project maintains an index of inscribed memorials at "St. James' Anglican, Manchester" — most likely referring to Kendal.

**The Kendal train tragedy (1 September 1957).** On Sunday 1 September 1957, the Kendal train returning to Kingston from a Montego Bay day excursion organised largely by St. Anne's Catholic Church in Kingston derailed at the Kendal Station as it descended the hill from Greenvale. An inquiry attributed the derailment to **excessive speed coming down the hill from Greenvale** combined with **defective braking caused by the closing of a brakeline cock that had been faultily installed**. **Almost 200 passengers were killed** and 600–700 were injured. The Kendal disaster remains, as the NLJ records, "Jamaica's worst railway accident and one of the gravest in world railway history." A community of barely a few hundred people became the site of national mourning. Many of those killed were Roman Catholic excursionists, and St. James' Anglican Church served alongside other parish institutions in the immediate aftermath.

**Twentieth century, bauxite, Independence.** The **bauxite industry** transformed Manchester from 1957 onward, with **Alcan** establishing alumina operations the same year as the Kendal wreck. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** brought new nationhood. The **Pioneer Chocolate Factory** at nearby Williamsfield and the **High Mountain Coffee Factory** continue traditions of Manchester's specialty agriculture.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. The specific status of St. James' Kendal after Melissa has not been separately reported in public sources reviewed.

## Architecture
St. James' is a modest masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Anglican Diocese across rural Manchester in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries. The Diocese maintains a directory image of the building (attachment_id 903).

## Clergy
Kendal has been served within the Manchester deanery under shared clergy arrangements.

## Notable Facts
**The dedication.** **James the Greater**, brother of John the Evangelist and patron saint of pilgrims, with a feast day on **25 July**. James was, according to Acts 12, the first of the apostles to be martyred. The dedication is shared with Jamaica's parish church of St. James (Montego Bay, 1775–1782).

**The English Kendal.** The English Kendal — now in Cumbria — was a significant wool-trading town in the Lake District, known for "Kendal Green" cloth. The Jamaican Kendal's name preserves this connection across three centuries.

**The 1957 disaster.** The Kendal train wreck of 1 September 1957 remains Jamaica's worst railway accident, its memory still vivid in Manchester. The site is sometimes confused with the Kendal in St. Ann; the National Library of Jamaica's *History of Manchester* explicitly places the wreck in the Kendal of Manchester parish, north of Mandeville.
