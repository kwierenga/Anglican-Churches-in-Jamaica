# St. Barnabas', Enfield
**St. Mary** · Church · active

St. Barnabas’ Anglican Church serves Enfield, a small interior community in St. Mary parish in the hill country of the central parish. Enfield is a quiet agricultural district, and the church has provided Anglican ministry to the local farming families for several generations. The dedication to St. Barnabas, the apostle celebrated for his generous and encouraging character, is shared with several other Jamaican Anglican churches across the island.

## History
Enfield is a small interior community of St. Mary whose name reflects the English estate-naming conventions of the plantation era. The Anglican Diocese established St. Barnabas’ as part of its mission outreach into the interior communities of the parish, serving the freed families who settled in the area after Emancipation. The church has served the Enfield community through the transformations of the 19th and 20th centuries.

## Architecture
St. Barnabas’ is a small masonry chapel characteristic of Anglican mission buildings in interior St. Mary. The building sits in the hilly terrain of the Enfield district, in a landscape subject to heavy rainfall and occasional landslide risk during major storm events.

## Clergy
Enfield has been served within the St. Mary deanery under shared clergy arrangements. Lay leaders sustain the church’s regular pastoral life between clerical visits.

## Notable Facts
Enfield’s interior position in St. Mary places it in the rain-shadow country that produces some of the highest annual rainfall totals in Jamaica. Hurricane passages bring heavy rain and wind to these interior communities, with road damage and landslides periodically isolating them from the coastal towns. Hurricane Melissa (October 2025) brought serious rain impacts to interior St. Mary; the Enfield community and congregation were in the affected zone.
