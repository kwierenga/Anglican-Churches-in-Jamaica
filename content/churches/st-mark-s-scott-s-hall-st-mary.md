# St. Mark's, Scott's Hall
**St. Mary** · Church · active

St. Mark’s Anglican Church serves Scott’s Hall, a community in the southern interior of St. Mary parish in the hill country approaching the Blue Mountains. Scott’s Hall is notable as one of the communities near the historic Maroon territories of St. Mary, and the church here serves an agricultural community with deep roots in the post-Emancipation settlement of the parish interior. The dedication to St. Mark the Evangelist is shared with several other Jamaican Anglican churches.

## History
Scott's Hall — known locally as "Katch Hall" — is a Maroon community in the hills along the Wag Water and Flint Rivers in southern St. Mary, and is recorded in Edward Long's *History of Jamaica* (1774) as the only town then existing in the parish. It is the smallest of the surviving treaty Maroon communities, alongside Moore Town, Charles Town, and Accompong, and was formed by a splinter group of the Windward Maroons after the 1739 peace treaty. Early surveys distinguished the "Negro Town" of the Maroons from an adjacent settlement of whites called Scott's Hall; in later usage the whole area came to be known as Scott's Hall, and is today led by its own Maroon Colonel. The Anglican chapel was established as part of the Diocese's post-Emancipation mission network in the southern interior of the parish.

## Architecture
St. Mark’s is a small masonry chapel in the southern interior hills of St. Mary, at relatively high elevation in terrain that rises toward the Blue Mountain ridge. The building’s elevated position creates wind exposure during hurricane passages and increases the risk of landslides on the surrounding slopes.

## Clergy
Scott’s Hall has been served within the southern St. Mary circuit of the Diocese deanery under shared clergy arrangements. The remote location means lay leaders play a central role in sustaining the church’s regular pastoral life.

## Notable Facts
Scott’s Hall’s proximity to the historic Maroon territories of St. Mary gives the surrounding landscape a distinctive historical significance. The area south of Annotto Bay was a zone of both plantation agriculture and Maroon resistance in the 18th century. The church’s elevated position in the southern hills means it is in the zone of highest rainfall in the parish. Hurricane Melissa (October 2025) brought serious rain impacts to the southern interior of St. Mary; the Scott’s Hall community was in the affected zone.
