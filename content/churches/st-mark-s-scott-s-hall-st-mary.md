# St. Mark's, Scott's Hall
**St. Mary** · Church · active

St. Mark's Anglican Church serves **Scott's Hall** — known locally as **"Katch Hall"** — a Maroon community in the southern interior of St. Mary parish in the hill country approaching the Blue Mountains. **Scott's Hall is one of the four surviving treaty Maroon communities** of Jamaica, alongside Moore Town, Charles Town, and Accompong, and is the smallest of the four.

## History
**Pre-English era and the Battle of Rio Nuevo (1658).** **Christopher Columbus** sighted Jamaica in 1494 on his second voyage. The **Battle of Rio Nuevo (June 1658)** saw British forces under **Sir Edward D'Oyley** decisively defeat a Spanish expedition attempting to recapture Jamaica — effectively ending Spanish resistance to British rule.

**English rule (1655) and the Maroon Wars.** Under English rule from **1655**, St. Mary became a major sugar-producing parish. The **First Maroon War (1728–1739)** ended with the **1739 peace treaty** between the British and the Windward Maroons led by Quao (with the Leeward Maroons led by Cudjoe signing a parallel treaty). Scott's Hall was formed by a splinter group of the **Windward Maroons** after the 1739 treaty. Early surveys distinguished the "Negro Town" of the Maroons from an adjacent settlement of whites called Scott's Hall; in later usage the whole area came to be known as Scott's Hall, and is today led by its own **Maroon Colonel**. **Edward Long's *History of Jamaica* (1774)** records Scott's Hall as the only town then existing in St. Mary parish.

**Tacky's Revolt (1760).** On Easter Monday, **7 April 1760**, **Tacky's Revolt** broke out on **Frontier and Trinity estates** near Port Maria. Tacky — a **Fante royal** from the Gold Coast of West Africa — led a rising of self-emancipated **Coromantee** people intended to overthrow British colonial rule. The rebellion was ultimately suppressed only with the assistance of the Windward and Leeward Maroons, who had been obliged by the 1739 and 1740 treaties to aid the British in putting down slave revolts — a treaty obligation that placed Scott's Hall directly in the suppression of Tacky's Revolt. Over **500 Black men and women** were killed in battle, executed, or took their own lives — the most significant slave rebellion in the British West Indies between the 1733 St. John insurrection and the 1791 Haitian Revolution.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union** to "wipe non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb** (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall, and the founding of St. Mark's.** After the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 and Crown Colony rule (1866), the Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**. The Anglican chapel at Scott's Hall was established as part of the Diocese's post-Emancipation mission network in the southern interior of the parish.

**Twentieth century and Independence.** The **banana trade** transformed the St. Mary economy in the late 19th and 20th centuries. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** found St. Mark's a settled mission in one of Jamaica's four treaty Maroon communities.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Hurricane Melissa brought serious rain impacts to the southern interior of St. Mary; the Scott's Hall community was in the affected zone.

## Architecture
St. Mark's is a small masonry chapel in the southern interior hills of St. Mary, at relatively high elevation in terrain that rises toward the Blue Mountain ridge. The building's elevated position creates wind exposure during hurricane passages and increases the risk of landslides on the surrounding slopes.

## Clergy
Scott's Hall has been served within the **southern St. Mary circuit** of the Diocese deanery under shared clergy arrangements. The remote location means lay leaders play a central role in sustaining the church's regular pastoral life.

## Notable Facts
**Scott's Hall** is recorded in Edward Long's *History of Jamaica* (1774) as the only town then existing in St. Mary parish. As one of the four surviving **treaty Maroon communities** — alongside Moore Town, Charles Town, and Accompong — it has its own **Maroon Colonel**, an elected community leader continuing a tradition that dates from the 1739 treaty. The dedication to **St. Mark** — the Evangelist, author of the shortest of the four Gospels (feast day **25 April**) — is shared with the Manchester parish church at Mandeville.
