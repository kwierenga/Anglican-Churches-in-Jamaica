# St. John's Church (Old), Leith Hall
**St. Thomas** · Church · ruin

The Old St. John's Church at **Leith Hall** is a historic Anglican building — possibly a ruin or disused structure — at Leith Hall, a community on the eastern coast of St. Thomas parish near Port Morant. The 'Old' designation suggests this is a historic or possibly ruinous building, distinct from the active St. Barnabas' Anglican Church also in the Port Morant/Leith Hall locality.

## History
**English rule (1655) and the founding parish.** Under English rule from **1655**, St. Thomas was one of Jamaica's original parishes — originally **"St. Thomas-in-the-East"** with the first rector appointed in **1666**.

**Sugar economy and slavery.** The Leith Hall and Port Morant area of eastern St. Thomas was historically one of the most productive sugar districts in Jamaica. The **Plantain Garden River valley** behind Port Morant supported extensive sugar cultivation, and the Anglican church here served the planter class and their estates. Great estates included **Stokes Hall, Golden Grove, Albion, Holland**. Until **1870**, the Church of England in Jamaica was the established state church, supported by colonial government funds.

**Sam Sharpe (1831), Emancipation (1838).** During the **1831 Sam Sharpe (Christmas) Rebellion**, planters and Anglican clergy formed the **Colonial Church Union** to "wipe non-conformist preachers off the face of the earth"; **Sam Sharpe** was hanged in Montego Bay on **23 May 1832** and is today a National Hero of Jamaica. Emancipation came in 1834, with full freedom on **1 August 1838**. Bishop **Christopher Lipscomb** (1824–1843) extended Anglican schools — 142 schools with 8,500 scholars by 1835.

**The Morant Bay Rebellion (11 October 1865).** On **11 October 1865**, **Paul Bogle** — a small farmer and Native Baptist deacon of **Stony Gut** — led a protest march to the Morant Bay courthouse. Governor **Edward John Eyre's** brutal response: **439 executed**, **600+ flogged**, **1,000+ homes burned**. Bogle was hanged on **24 October 1865**; **George William Gordon** was hanged on **23 October 1865**. **Both were declared National Heroes in 1965**.

**Disestablishment (1870), Nuttall.** Jamaica became a **Crown Colony in 1866**. Law 20 of 1867 absorbed **St. David** into St. Thomas. The Anglican Church was disestablished in **1870**. Bishop **Enos Nuttall** (1880–1916) extended the rural mission network from 26 stations in 1880 to **122 by 1911**.

**Foundation, decline, and abandonment.** Leith Hall is a community on the eastern coast of St. Thomas, in the sugar-producing district around Port Morant and the Plantain Garden River valley. The Anglican church here is one of the oldest ecclesiastical sites in this part of St. Thomas, predating the current St. Barnabas' church in the area. The 'Old' designation in the dataset record suggests this building may have been superseded by a newer church, fallen into disuse, or partially ruined, while retaining its identity as a historical landmark.

**Twentieth century.** **Hurricane Ivan (2004)** struck the south-east coast of Jamaica with catastrophic force, causing severe damage across the coastal communities of St. Thomas — accelerating the decline of any structures already weakened by age. Jamaica's independence on **6 August 1962** had earlier found this site already largely a heritage relic.

**Hurricane Melissa (October 2025).** Hurricane Melissa made Category 5 landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland on **28 October 2025** — the strongest hurricane to strike Jamaica in recorded meteorological history. Hurricane Melissa brought further coastal impacts to eastern St. Thomas.

## Architecture
The Old St. John's is recorded as a historic structure in the coastal community of Leith Hall. The building's age and condition are indicated by the 'Old' designation. The coastal plain of eastern St. Thomas around Port Morant is directly exposed to the Caribbean Sea, making it one of the more hurricane-vulnerable stretches of the St. Thomas coast. The Plantain Garden River valley behind Leith Hall also creates flooding risk during heavy rainfall.

## Clergy
As an old or ruined building, St. John's Leith Hall is no longer served by active clergy. In its operational years it was served within the **St. Thomas deanery**, likely linked to the Port Morant circuit.

## Notable Facts
The dedication to **St. John** — most commonly the Evangelist (feast 27 December), occasionally the Baptist (24 June) — is one of the most common Anglican dedications. The Plantain Garden River valley that surrounds Leith Hall is among the most historically productive sugar landscapes in the British Caribbean.
