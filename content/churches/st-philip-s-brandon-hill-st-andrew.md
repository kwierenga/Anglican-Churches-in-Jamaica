# St. Philip's, Brandon Hill
**St. Andrew** · Church · active

St. Philip's Anglican Church serves Brandon Hill, a community in the upland country of rural St. Andrew. The Diocese of Jamaica's directory lists St. Philip's at Brandon Hill within the Kingston Region, distinct from a separate (and inactive) "St. Gabriel's, Brandon Hill" entry in our inventory — the diocese itself records only one church at this location, and the diocesan name is **St. Philip's**.

## History
Brandon Hill is one of the upland communities in the rural part of St. Andrew, in the broken hill country that extends north of the Liguanea plain into the Blue Mountain foothills. St. Andrew Parish was created in 1664 — four years after Britain established civil administration following the conquest of Jamaica from Spain — and is named after the apostle Andrew, patron saint of Scotland. Until the disestablishment of the Anglican Church in 1870, the parish church at Half Way Tree was the only Anglican church in the parish and bore responsibility for the spiritual welfare of all parishioners.

After disestablishment, the Diocese was organised under its own canons and resources and could expand its mission network through deanery and cure structures. Mission chapels and cures grew up across the parish's rural districts to serve communities that had previously had to travel to the parish church for ministry. St. Philip's at Brandon Hill belongs to that wider rural extension.

The dedication to St. Philip the Apostle — who, in the Gospel of John, brought Nathanael to Christ and asked Jesus to "show us the Father" — is one of several Anglican dedications to the lesser apostles. His feast day (1 May) is shared with St. James the Less.

## Architecture
St. Philip's is a small rural masonry chapel of the kind erected by the Diocese for mission stations across the St. Andrew uplands. The hill-country position creates wind exposure during storm passages.

## Clergy
St. Philip's Brandon Hill is served within the Kingston Region's St. Andrew Deanery.
