# JNHT (Jamaica National Heritage Trust) Anglican Church Research Report

Source: https://www.jnht.com/ — primary index for declared national monuments and heritage sites.
Research date: 2026-04-23.

## Site structure overview

- Main churches index: `https://www.jnht.com/churches.php` — mixed denominations.
- Individual site pages follow the pattern `https://www.jnht.com/site_<slug>.php`.
- Image assets live under `http://www.jnht.com/_images/` (occasionally `/IMAGES/`). All inline images on JNHT pages are served over HTTP, not HTTPS.
- Historic district pages (Spanish Town, Port Royal, Falmouth) provide additional context on Anglican churches inside those districts but do not host independent church pages.
- Foundation pages and tour pages only re-list the same site URLs visible in the main churches index — no extra Anglican church entries were discovered there.
- The churches.php index appears to *omit* a few Anglican parish-church pages that exist on the site (e.g. St. Andrew Parish Church) — those pages still respond at predictable URLs.

The JNHT churches.php list contains 34 churches across all denominations. Filtering to Anglican only (parish churches plus pages titled "Anglican") yielded **18 Anglican entries**, all of which already correspond to churches in our 306-row inventory. No Anglican church on JNHT is missing from our inventory.

Notes on denomination filtering:

- "Holy Trinity Cathedral" (Kingston) at `/site_holy_trinity_cathedral.php` is the **Roman Catholic** cathedral on North Street (designed by Raymond F. Admiral, 85-ft copper dome). It is not Anglican; the JNHT page text mislabels it but the architect, dome, and Byzantine-revival style identify it as the RC cathedral. **Excluded** from this report.
- The Anglican cathedral in Jamaica is St. Jago de la Vega in Spanish Town — covered separately below.
- "Trelawny Parish Church" and "Falmouth Parish Church" both refer to the same building: St. Peter's, Falmouth. The `site_trelawny_parish_church.php` page is live; `site_falmouth_parish_church.php` returns 404.

## Already in our inventory

### `st-peter-s-parish-church-falmouth-trelawny` — St. Peter's, Falmouth (Trelawny Parish Church)

- JNHT URL: https://www.jnht.com/site_trelawny_parish_church.php
- Image: http://www.jnht.com/_images/falmouth_parish_church.jpg
- Built 1795; oldest public building in Falmouth. Land donated by Edward Barrett (of the Moulton-Barrett family — 7,800 sq ft at Duke and Pitt Streets, per the Falmouth historic-district page). Brick and stone, vernacular architecture derived from classical forms. **Extended in 1842**, making it among the largest Anglican churches on the island. Two interior galleries (north and west) carried by four monumental timber columns. Organ donated by John Tharpe Esq. of Good Hope Estate. Wall monuments date to early 1800s; churchyard has graves spanning 200+ years.

### `holy-trinity-retreat-st-mary` — Holy Trinity, Retreat (St. Mary)

- JNHT URL: https://www.jnht.com/site_holy_trinity_anglican.php
- Image: http://www.jnht.com/_images/HolyTrinityAnglican.jpg
- Erected on land donated by **Henry Rigg in 1828** (English sugar planter, b. Bowness, Westmoreland 1791; lived 35 years at Retreat; d. 1869). Site formerly part of a sugar estate. Construction: brick, cut stone, concrete and wood. Described as "a very beautiful historic Georgian" structure.

### `kingston-parish-church-st-thomas-the-apostle-kingston-parade-king-st-kingston` — Kingston Parish Church (St. Thomas the Apostle)

- JNHT URL: https://www.jnht.com/site_kingston_parish_church.php
- Image: http://www.jnht.com/_images/kingston_parish_church.jpg
- **Current building erected c. 1911** on the foundations of the previous church destroyed by the **1907 Kingston earthquake**. The 1911 design mirrors the original. **Clock tower added after World War I** as a memorial to the war dead — gives rise to the colloquial saying that native Kingstonians are "born under the clock." Interior monuments include Admiral John Benbow (stationed at Port Royal, 1702) and John Wolmer (Kingston goldsmith whose bequest founded Wolmer's school). Altar features a statue of St. Mary donated by the Chinese community and a statue of St. Thomas from the Syrian community.

### `christ-church-parish-church-port-antonio-portland` — Christ Church, Port Antonio (Portland Parish Church)

- JNHT URL: https://www.jnht.com/site_portland_parish_church.php
- Image: http://www.jnht.com/_images/portland_parish_church.jpg
- Land **indentured 3 September 1836** by John Dean and Jane Brown for the church. Predominantly brick construction with stone and masonry decorative elements. Three-story rectangular nave with internal stairs to a secondary entrance; **four-story tower** housing the bell and clock.

### `st-peter-s-port-royal-kingston` — St. Peter's, Port Royal

- JNHT URL: https://www.jnht.com/site_st_peters_church.php
- Image: http://www.jnht.com/_images/st_peters_church2.jpg
- Original church destroyed in the **1692 Port Royal earthquake**; replacement destroyed by **fire in 1703**. Vestry plaque records that the present church was **rebuilt 1725–1726**. Walls are now cement-rendered and scored to imitate stone blocks, hiding the original brick. **Original black-and-white floor tiles still visible in the aisles.**

### `st-jago-de-la-vega-the-cathedral-spanish-town-st-catherine` — Spanish Town Cathedral (St. Jago de la Vega)

- JNHT URL: https://www.jnht.com/site_spanish_town_cathedral.php
- Image: http://www.jnht.com/_images/spanish_town_cathedral.jpg
- Anglican; "the oldest Cathedral in the British Caribbean." Built in the second half of the 17th century after the British 1655 conquest, on the foundations and using materials from the **Spanish Chapel of the Red Cross (1525)**. **Destroyed by hurricane in 1712, rebuilt 1714.** **Tower added 1817** — crowned with one of the few steeples in the Caribbean. Designated cathedral of the Jamaican Diocese in **1843**. Cruciform plan; mixture of round-headed and pointed arches, classical quoins, medieval buttresses. Holds the oldest baptismal and marriage records on the island. Burials/monuments include Sir Thomas Modyford (Governor 1664–1671) and the Earl of Effingham (Governor 1790–1791). Numerous sculpted monuments by **John Bacon**. Marble headstone memorials to British colonists going back to the 1700s.

### `st-ann-parish-church-st-ann-s-bay-st-ann` — St. Ann Parish Church, St. Ann's Bay

- JNHT URL: https://www.jnht.com/site_st_ann_parish_church.php
- Image: http://www.jnht.com/_images/st_ann_parish_church.jpg
- Dedication: "Church of St. Anne." **Completed September 1871; consecrated 20 September 1871** by Rt. Rev. Reginald Courtney, Bishop of Kingston. Construction contract awarded to Climie, a Colonial Engineer. Cut-stone Gothic Revival; pointed-arch windows separated by buttresses; semi-octagonal apse with corner buttresses and pointed-arch stained-glass windows (largest at the east end).

### `st-dorothy-s-church-old-harbour-st-catherine` — St. Dorothy's, Old Harbour

- JNHT URL: https://www.jnht.com/site_st_dorothys_anglican.php
- Image: http://www.jnht.com/_images/dorothys_anglican_church.JPG
- Built on land donated by **Colonel Thomas Fuller and his wife Catherine Fuller in 1681**. The historic parish of St. Dorothy is the source of the name — Old Harbour was once part of that parish. Construction: cut stone, brick, mortar and wood.

### `st-john-s-parish-church-black-river-st-elizabeth` — St. John the Evangelist, Black River

- JNHT URL: https://www.jnht.com/site_st_elizabeth_parish_church.php
- Image: http://www.jnht.com/_images/st_elizabeth_parish_church.jpg
- Dedication: **St. John the Evangelist**. Origins date to c. 1700; current main fabric possibly erected c. 1774 (timeline ambiguous). **Tower foundation laid in 1837** (per inscription). Yellow-brick construction with limestone detailing; design merges Gothic and classical elements reminiscent of medieval English parish churches. Tower has corner quoins, battlements, and corner pinnacles.

### `st-george-s-buff-bay-portland` — St. George's, Buff Bay

- JNHT URL: https://www.jnht.com/site_st_georges_anglican.php
- Image: http://www.jnht.com/_images/st_george_anglican.jpg
- **Built 1814** of cut stone — the stone reportedly arrived from England as ship ballast. Originally the parish church of the former parish of St. George; when Governor Sir John Peter Grant **reduced Jamaica's parishes from 22 to 14 in 1867**, St. George was absorbed into Portland. Stained-glass panel windows. Solid raised panelled door at the base of a tall castellated clock tower. Described as the oldest building in the town of Buff Bay.

### `st-james-parish-church-sam-sharpe-square-st-james` — St. James Parish Church, Montego Bay

- JNHT URL: https://www.jnht.com/site_st_james_parish_church.php
- Image: http://www.jnht.com/_images/st_James_parish-chur.png
- Erected **1775–1782**, white limestone. Greek-cross plan with western bell tower. Large round-headed sash windows; an elaborate **Palladian window** sits behind the altar at the west end. Significantly damaged by the **1957 earthquake** (the JNHT page actually says "1951"; this almost certainly refers to the 1957 Mona/Montego earthquake — caveat on use). Restoration produced only minor departures from the original design. Dedication is to St. James the Great, patron saint of Spain.

### `st-mark-s-parish-church-mandeville-manchester` — St. Mark's, Mandeville

- JNHT URL: https://www.jnht.com/site_st_marks_anglican_church.php
- Image: http://www.jnht.com/_images/st_marks_anglican_church.jpg
- **Completed and consecrated 1820**, stone construction. **Late-19th-century additions:** chancel and timber clerestory; tower raised. Classic Gothic details — pointed-arch doors and window openings.

### `st-mary-s-montpelier-st-james` — St. Mary's, Montpelier

- JNHT URL: https://www.jnht.com/site_st_marys_anglican.php
- Image: http://www.jnht.com/_images/st_marysanglican_church.jpg
- Cornerstone dated **1847**, cut-stone construction. Pronounced pointed-arch stone architrave at the entrance; pointed-arch (Gothic) windows; outstanding multi-partite pointed-arch window at the rear. Stands on Old Montpelier Estate land — one of the estates burnt by enslaved people during the **Western Jamaica Slave Rebellion of 1831–1832** (the "Christmas Rebellion" / Sam Sharpe Rebellion).

### `st-peter-s-church-alley-clarendon` — St. Peter's, Alley

- JNHT URL: https://www.jnht.com/site_st_peters_church_alley.php
- Image: http://www.jnht.com/_images/st_peters_church_alley_.jpg
- Founded **1671** as the parish church of the historic parish of Vere. **Current structure built c. 1715** on the foundations of the original. Brick and mortar; tower entrance outlined by quoins with castellated crown; **slate roof — one of the few remaining in Jamaica.** Houses the **organ of 1847** — said to be the **oldest organ in the Commonwealth Caribbean**. Bell **cast 1857 by Mears of Whitechapel, London** (the same foundry that cast Big Ben). Interior monuments to the Morant, Gale, and Sutton families (members of the colonial Council and Assembly).

### `christ-church-morant-bay-st-thomas` — Christ Church, Morant Bay (St. Thomas Parish Church)

- JNHT URL: https://www.jnht.com/site_st_thomas_parish_church.php
- Image: http://www.jnht.com/_images/st_thomas_parish_chur.jpg
- **Built 1865**, brick construction. Replaced an earlier church at "Church Corner" that was abandoned because of dilapidation. **The east wall has a brick with the date 1865 baked into it** as a permanent record of the construction year. (Note: the church is contemporary with the 1865 Morant Bay Rebellion — JNHT does not connect the two but the date is striking.)

### `hanover-parish-church-lucea-hanover` — Hanover Parish Church, Lucea

- JNHT URL: https://www.jnht.com/site_hanover_parish_church.php
- Image: http://www.jnht.com/IMAGES/HanoverParishChurch.jpg
- Also known historically as **St. Mary's Anglican** (per JNHT). Dates to **c. 1725** — first baptism 1725, first burial 1727, first marriage 1749. Originally T-shaped brick (now rendered). Square battlemented western tower; **tower and spire damaged by the 1957 earthquake** and later restored. **North Transept added 1837.** Pipe organ installed **1891** (still functional). Two notable rectors — **D.W. Rose (1806–1824)** and **John Stainsby (1833–1853)** — recognised for advocating justice for enslaved people. Original altar replaced by one gifted from Savanna-la-Mar Church. **Major refurbishment in 2002** (roof and ceiling).

### `st-andrew-parish-church-half-way-tree-st-andrew` — St. Andrew Parish Church, Half-Way-Tree

- JNHT URL: https://www.jnht.com/site_st_andrew_parish_church.php
- Images: http://www.jnht.com/IMAGES/st_andrew_parish_church.jpg ; http://www.jnht.com/_images/st_andrew_parish_church_000.jpg
- (Note: this page is not linked from churches.php but is live.) **Founded 1664** — one of the oldest churches in Jamaica. Dedication to **St. Andrew the Apostle, patron saint of Scotland**. First building on the present site dated to the 1680s but was destroyed in the **1692 earthquake**; rebuilding began the same year and the new structure was completed in **1700**. **Extensively renovated and enlarged in 1879**. Major renovation in **1907** following the Kingston earthquake of that year. Church Office added **1974**. Stained-glass windows installed after 1879. The current organ (the fourth in the church's history) was installed in **1978**. Cemetery contains burials of prominent historical figures. **Declared a national monument 3 April 2003.**

### `church-of-the-good-shepherd-constant-spring-st-andrew` — Church of the Good Shepherd, Constant Spring

- JNHT URL: https://www.jnht.com/site_church_of_the_good_shepherd.php
- Images: http://www.jnht.com/IMAGES/church_of_good_shepherd2.jpg ; http://www.jnht.com/IMAGES/sheperd_4_000.jpg
- Origin as a **mission school for East Indian children at Constant Spring**, started by **Maurice Wortley** (killed in WWI, 1915). His sister **Dorothy Wortley** continued the work; **bamboo schoolhouse opened February 1916** with eight pupils on land donated by **John Farquharson**. **Canon E. J. Wortley** (Maurice's father) and his wife Bertha founded a Children's Home in 1918, later moved to "Maurice Hill" at Constant Spring. **In 1926, with five pounds in hand, Canon Wortley began building the church**; **completed debt-free 1927** and consecrated. Canon Wortley designed the church himself; built with voluntary labour and community contributions (he refused to use unpaid materials). 1928 — Canon Wortley died; the East Indian community erected a memorial stone. Three stained-glass windows above the altar. **Renovated 1951 after hurricane damage.** **Restored 1978 by the Anglican Diocese**, rededicated by **Bishop Edmondson** and opened to the wider community. **1984 — administration of the Wortley Home transferred to St. Jude's Anglican, Stony Hill.**

## Other relevant non-church Anglican sites (rectories, related buildings)

JNHT does **not** maintain stand-alone pages for Anglican rectories, parish halls, churchyard walls, or graveyards. The cemeteries.php category lists only Bedward's Tomb, the old Jewish Cemetery at Hunt's Bay, the Lacovia Tombstones, and Carmel Moravian Cemetery — none Anglican.

Useful Anglican-adjacent context appears inside the **historic district** pages, which each cover an Anglican church we already have:

- **Spanish Town historic district** — https://www.jnht.com/site_spanish_town.php — declared a National Monument **29 December 1994**. Reinforces the Cathedral of St. Jago de la Vega as the focal heritage building of the district (cruciform plan, 1525 Spanish chapel foundations, 1817 tower, 1843 cathedral designation, John Bacon monuments). Useful narrative material for `st-jago-de-la-vega-the-cathedral-spanish-town-st-catherine`.
- **Falmouth historic district** — https://www.jnht.com/site_falmouth.php — gives the precise land-grant detail for St. Peter's: *"In 1794, Moulton Barrett donated 7,800 square feet of land along the intersection of Duke Street and Pitt Street for the construction of an Anglican church, the St. Peters Anglican Church"* — earlier than the 1795 build date on the parish-church page. Useful supplementary fact for `st-peter-s-parish-church-falmouth-trelawny`.
- **Port Royal historic district** — https://www.jnht.com/site_port_royal.php — repeats the 1692 / 1703 / 1725–26 story of St. Peter's; no new facts but supports `st-peter-s-port-royal-kingston`.
- **Titchfield Peninsula** and **Black River** historic-district pages do not add Anglican-specific content.
